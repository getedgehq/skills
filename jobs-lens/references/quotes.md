# Steve Jobs: verified public quotes

Short excerpts from public launches, interviews, letters and filings, each with its source. Excerpts were matched against the linked source when collected; links can rot, so re-check one before relying on it. Excerpts are short on purpose: quote only these words, never the paraphrase around them. Numbers are referenced from SKILL.md.

Independent reference list. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Steve Jobs, his estate or Apple.

## How he opens, financing and negotiation

38. “It is now 1984. It appears IBM wants it all.” The enemy before the product. Date: October 1983. Occasion: Apple's sales conference, where Jobs introduced the 1984 Macintosh advertisement. His presentation begins with a chronological account of IBM's missed opportunities and growing power, before introducing the commercial. Source: [Steve Jobs Archive, original speech transcript](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/). Status: VERIFIED.
39. “We have to let go of this notion that for Apple to win Microsoft has to lose.” Abandon the false zero-sum narrative. Date: August 6, 1997. Occasion: Macworld Boston keynote, immediately after Bill Gates's appearance announcing the Microsoft partnership and $150 million investment. Source: [Full keynote transcript and video](https://mac-history.net/macworld-boston-1997-steve-jobs-returns-bill-gates-appeares-on-screen/). Status: VERIFIED.
40. “Apple had to remember who Apple was because they’d forgotten who Apple was.” Define your own company rather than the competitor. Date: May 30, 2007. Occasion: D5 conference, joint interview with Bill Gates, recalling Apple's turnaround. Source: [AllThingsD's professionally prepared transcript](https://allthingsd.com/20070531/d5-gates-jobs-transcript). Status: VERIFIED.
41. “Post PC products now 66% of our revenues” Market transition expressed as actual revenue. Date: October 24, 2010. Occasion: Jobs's internal Top 100 - A strategy agenda, later introduced as Defendant's Exhibit 489.245 in Apple litigation. This is a written agenda item, not a public keynote statement. Source: [Scanned original litigation exhibit, page 1](https://sriramk.com/memos/jobs-top-100.pdf#page=1). Status: VERIFIED against the exhibit image.
42. “As I see it, HC has the following choices:” Frame a negotiation as explicit alternatives. Date: January 24, 2010. Occasion: Jobs's email to News Corp executive James Murdoch concerning HarperCollins ebook pricing. He follows with three numbered alternatives. This is evidence of his negotiating structure, not an endorsement of the conduct examined in the ebook antitrust proceedings. Source: [U.S. Department of Justice, Trial Exhibit PX-0032, page 1](https://www.justice.gov/atr/cases/apple/exhibits/px-0032.pdf#page=1). Status: VERIFIED against the original email scan. *(source is a scanned exhibit; wording not checked by script, read the scan before quoting)*
43. “By almost any definition, Flash is a closed system.” An explicit technical argument against the incumbent. Date: April 29, 2010. Occasion: Jobs's published Thoughts on Flash letter, arguing against Flash support on iPhone and iPad. Source: [Contemporaneous full-text reproduction, Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2010-04-29-steve-jobs-publishes-some-thoughts-on-flash-many-many-thou.html). Status: VERIFIED.
44. “Things are packages of emphasis.” Product strategy means deliberate omissions. Date: June 1, 2010. Occasion: D8 interview, responding to criticism of Apple's product decisions. Jobs follows by describing what a product emphasizes, deprioritizes or excludes. Source: [Transcript excerpt maintained by Apple engineer Ken Kocienda](https://gist.github.com/kocienda/44f9e299b7771fdbfec2ddb4fab39c36). Status: VERIFIED against the published transcription.
45. “Consumers don’t want to be treated like criminals and artists don't want their valuable work stolen.” Describe the competing needs of both sides. Date: April 28, 2003. Occasion: iTunes Music Store launch. Jobs explains why legal music purchasing needs to work for listeners and rights holders simultaneously. Source: [Apple's original iTunes Music Store announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2003/04/28Apple-Launches-the-iTunes-Music-Store/). Status: VERIFIED, company-published Jobs quotation.
46. “There have only been two milestone products in our industry” Make the launch a historical milestone before naming the product. Date: January 24, 1984. Occasion: Original Macintosh unveiling at Apple's annual shareholders meeting. Jobs compares the Apple II and IBM PC before presenting Macintosh as the third milestone. Source: [PBS, Triumph of the Nerds, transcript containing the original launch footage](https://www.pbs.org/nerds/part3.html). Status: VERIFIED, recorded speech reproduced in the documentary transcript.

## Never quote these as his

1. “Stay hungry. Stay foolish.” VERIFIED as spoken by Jobs, but not his original wording. On June 12, 2005, he explicitly attributed it to the closing message of the Whole Earth Catalog. Correct attribution: Jobs repeated the Catalog's phrase in his Stanford address. [Stanford transcript](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says).
2. “Here's to the crazy ones.” VERIFIED as Apple advertising copy, but not as a sentence Jobs originally wrote. The words come from the 1997 Think Different campaign. Jobs recorded an unused narration; former agency executive Rob Siltanen describes the collaborative creation and writing of the advertisement. Correct attribution: the Apple/TBWA campaign, not an original Jobs aphorism. [Siltanen's first-hand account](https://www.forbes.com/sites/carminegallo/2011/12/13/steve-jobs-didnt-write-the-words-heres-to-the-crazy-ones/).
3. “The best way to predict the future is to invent it.” MISATTRIBUTED when credited as an original Jobs quotation. The documented attribution is to Alan Kay, with a longer history of related formulations. Do not label this “Steve Jobs said” without a separate recording of him quoting Kay. [Quote Investigator's source chronology](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2012/09/27/invent-the-future/).
4. “We're here to put a dent in the universe.” LIKELY, but not independently verified here from a contemporaneous Jobs recording. The expression appears in recollections of Jobs, including accounts associated with John Sculley. Retrospective testimony can establish that someone recalls the remark, not necessarily a perfectly transcribed sentence. [Sculley interview](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/john-sculley-on-steve-jobs-and-the-future-of-media-marketing-fintech-and-health-care/).
5. “Real artists ship.” VERIFIED as a near-primary eyewitness recollection, not as an identified recorded keynote quotation. Macintosh engineer Andy Hertzfeld attributes the expression to Jobs in his account of finishing the original Macintosh. Correct citation: Jobs, as recalled by Hertzfeld. [Folklore.org, Real Artists Ship](https://www.folklore.org/Real_Artists_Ship.html).
