# Worked examples

Credit: Illustrations applying the synthesis of Arian Ruß's public method. All clients, creators, plans, and outcomes below are invented. They are not swinx case studies or evidence of expected performance. Rules refer to sections of [method.md](../references/method.md).

## Illustration 1: Cybersecurity webinar for IT leaders

### Weak first draft

Client: fictional security vendor ShieldPath.

Book the creator with 80,000 followers. Publish: Join our revolutionary platform demo and discover the best cybersecurity solution. Tag 30 CIOs. Spend EUR 9,400 and guarantee 200 leads at EUR 47 each. Count LinkedIn event responses as registrations. Let sales handle follow-up whenever convenient.

### Improved result

**Audience:** DACH heads of IT and security at firms with 200 to 2,000 employees, facing ransomware recovery planning.

**Candidate decision:** Prefer fictional former CISO Lena Berg, with 9,000 followers, evidence of relevant professional responsibility, and substantive discussions with security leaders. Ask for aggregate audience-fit evidence before confirming the booking. The 80,000-follower candidate primarily reaches job seekers and general creators. Lena is provisional; her availability and fee are unknown.

**Offer:** Ransomware recovery: three decisions IT leaders should test before an incident. Lena contributes an experience-based recovery exercise, supplied by her and checked for permission to share. The client contributes a practical checklist and answers questions without making the session a product pitch.

**Booking brief:** Proposed live speaker role, one educational post, relevant event invitations within agreed capacity, and an approved personalized message draft. Confirm preparation time, fee, recording rights, date, and cancellation terms before commitment.

**Draft post:** A recovery plan can exist on paper and still fail during an incident. In this session we will test three decisions: who authorizes recovery, which systems restart first, and how teams validate a clean environment. I will walk through a tabletop exercise with ShieldPath's security team. Register at [verified webinar link].

This is proposed copy, not a claim about Lena's actual experience or endorsement.

**Economics:** Assume a EUR 9,400 campaign budget excluding VAT and internal client labor. Planning scenarios use 100, 160, and 200 verified registrations, producing EUR 94, EUR 58.75, and EUR 47 cost per registration. These counts are hypotheses to validate after the audience and booking review. The optimistic scenario coincides with the published IT average; that coincidence supplies no evidence for the forecast.

**Illustrative observed result:** 280 LinkedIn event responses, 160 unique webinar registrations, 120 qualified registrations, and 64 live attendees. Cost per registration is EUR 58.75; cost per qualified registration is EUR 78.33; cost per attendee is EUR 146.88; show rate is 40%. Later meetings are tracked separately. The client campaign owner sends the promised resource within two working days and routes requested consultations to an assigned sales owner.

### Rules that changed the decision

- Section 3 replaced follower volume with relevant authority and audience access.
- Sections 5 and 6 replaced generic product promotion and mass tagging with useful expert content.
- Section 7 replaced a guaranteed benchmark and mixed denominators with assumptions and separate funnel counts.
- Sections 4 and 8 made booking and sales follow-up concrete responsibilities.

## Illustration 2: HR webinar with narrow audience and a booking conflict

### Weak first draft

Client: fictional language-training provider FluentWorks.

Book a famous HR creator. Advertise AI will transform every HR department. Require three scripted product endorsements. Guarantee EUR 41 per registration. When the creator cannot join live, leave them listed as a live speaker anyway. Send the same message to everyone in their network.

### Improved result

**Audience:** HR and learning leaders in German firms with multilingual frontline teams, 500 or more employees, and a need to assess workplace communication barriers.

**Candidate review:** Fictional HR consultant Tom Weiss has 12,000 followers and relevant learning-leader interactions. A larger general leadership creator lacks demonstrated audience overlap. Tom's live availability conflicts with the event. Proposed solution: an accurately advertised recorded contribution plus a live client practitioner and moderator. Confirm Tom's promotional capacity and recording terms. If he cannot contribute credibly, select another candidate or revise the date.

**Offer:** Where language gaps slow frontline onboarding: a diagnostic for HR and learning teams. Tom supplies a short recorded framework. The practitioner walks through an invented onboarding scenario explicitly labeled as an illustration. Attendees receive a diagnostic worksheet.

**Draft personal invitation:** Hi [name], your work on [verified relevant responsibility] may make this session useful. We will explore a practical way to identify language barriers in frontline onboarding, with a recorded contribution from Tom Weiss and a live practitioner discussion. Details and registration: [verified link].

Send only where the contextual reference is true and the invitation is authorized. Do not insert fictional personalization into a real message.

**Economics:** Assume EUR 8,200 excluding VAT and internal labor. At 100, 150, or 200 verified registrations, cost per registration would be EUR 82, EUR 54.67, or EUR 41. These are arithmetic scenarios pending evidence, not performance promises. Use the EUR 41 figure only as the historical HR context in [S1].

**Illustrative observed result:** 150 verified registrations, 105 qualified registrations, 60 new qualified contacts, and 45 live attendees. Cost per registration is EUR 54.67, cost per qualified registration is EUR 78.10, show rate is 30%, and new-contact share is 57.1%. If the main goal is net-new buyers, assess the 60 new qualified contacts rather than calling all 150 registrants new leads. Review audience fit, timing, and reminders before increasing promotion volume.

### Rules that changed the decision

- Sections 2 and 3 required evidence of the specific buyer community.
- Sections 4 and 5 turned an availability conflict into an honest role and a relevant educational offer.
- Sections 6 and 7 replaced generic blasts and benchmark guarantees with contextual invitations and transparent measurement.
- Section 8 required diagnosis of weak attendance and contact novelty before scaling.
