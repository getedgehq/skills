# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations. Brands, creators, rates, results and schedules are hypothetical. They are not Greger or Bloom clients. Rule citations refer to the public source register; budgets and operational choices are this package's implementation guidance.

## Illustration 1: A meal brand enters Germany

### Situation

Fictional brand AbendBox wants recurring German-language paid social assets. It has a marketing owner, product samples, some campaign data and a monthly production budget of EUR 8,000. Its current freelancer purchases repeatedly produce the same generic unboxing.

### Weak first draft

Hire two popular creators for twenty polished videos. Send the logo guide and ask them to make something authentic. Pay EUR 300 per video. Hold two weekly status calls. Rewrite their scripts until every line matches the brand voice. Choose the best videos by views and promise the team a 50% cost saving.

### Improved result

**Squad:** Five German-market creator slots covering a busy parent, office worker, shift worker, commuter and solo household. Select using relevant samples, credible product use, communication, editing and recurring availability. These are recruiting profiles, not claims that actual candidates are booked.

**Immersion:** A two-hour workshop covers audience needs, positioning, actual preparation, supported benefits and lessons from existing campaigns. Each creator demonstrates a meal and pitches an everyday use case. Ship meals and company merchandise, with an operational companion describing contacts, message boundaries and deliveries. Avoid inventing health claims.

**Retainers:** Hypothetically, five creators receive EUR 1,000 each per month for eight edited assets delivered through two sprints. This produces forty planned assets. Budget EUR 1,000 for coordination, EUR 300 for shipping, EUR 300 for finishing and EUR 400 for weekly and monthly bonuses. Planned production spend is EUR 7,000, leaving EUR 1,000 contingency. The plan specifies revisions and proposed paid usage for agreement; it assumes no rights are already secured.

**Creative allocation:** Each sprint contains four assets per creator: two brand briefs and two creator concepts. Across the month that is twenty in each lane. A brand brief tests a preparation demonstration because prior demonstrations appeared to convert better than unboxings. Creator concepts explore late shifts, a shared fridge and arrival home after a commute.

**Concrete brand brief:** Open with the creator returning home after a late shift. Supported benefits in this hypothetical product brief are quick preparation, portion information and home delivery. Essential shots show the package, preparation and finished meal. The creator chooses natural phrasing. The conversion hypothesis is that seeing the full preparation sequence reduces uncertainty about convenience.

**Weekly learning:** One thirty-minute session returns spend and purchases by asset, discusses the strongest and weakest demonstrations, and chooses next angles. Views are context, not the only outcome. Bonuses recognize documented learning and reliable delivery under declared criteria.

**Three-month review:** Keep, revise or expand the operation after reviewing reliable delivery, accepted assets, business outcomes and management load. A reliable creator who can coach others becomes a candidate for a paid coordination role.

### Rules that made the difference

- Local squad, immersion and team membership replace repeated cold sourcing. [S1]
- Hook, supported benefits and essential scenes make the brand brief executable. [S2]
- Creator ownership survives alongside brand direction. [S1]
- The German food case supports attention to localization and claims. [S8]
- Reliable output and learning guide the review; cost savings remain a hypothesis. [S1]

**Illustrative review outcome:** If only thirty-five of forty assets are accepted, cost per accepted asset is EUR 7,000 / 35 = EUR 200. Reporting EUR 175 using planned output would hide the rejection cost. Diagnose the five rejected assets before adding creators.

## Illustration 2: A consumer app gets views without clear learning

### Situation

Fictional app StadtPlan has six existing local creators. The team calls every asset creator-led, but its founder rewrites every concept. Several videos spike while an app promotion runs. Management wants ten more creators and daily posts on dedicated accounts.

### Weak first draft

Create sixty videos from the founder's winning script. Attribute the entire download spike to the highest-view video. Open ten more accounts next week. Give a bonus to whoever has the most views, and ask the best performer to manage the others for free.

### Improved result

**Diagnosis:** This squad needs an operating reset before recruitment. Preserve the existing six-person relationship and run a three-hour workshop around the product experience, audience, conversion goal and boundaries. Send missing product access and merchandise. Do not treat workshop attendance alone as proof of understanding.

**Sprint:** For a hypothetical monthly retainer, each creator delivers ten assets across two five-asset sprints. Sprint one assigns three brand briefs and two creator ideas per creator; sprint two assigns two brand briefs and three creator ideas. The sixty monthly assets balance to thirty per lane. The odd-sprint convention is package guidance.

**Brand brief:** Open on the app's map while choosing a nearby activity. Three hypothetical supported benefits are location filtering, saved plans and shareable suggestions. Essential scenes show a real filter, a saved activity and the shared suggestion. The hypothesis is that a visible interaction communicates usefulness more clearly than a spoken list.

**Creator concept:** One creator proposes a rainy-day challenge filmed around local venues. The brand corrects an inaccurate feature claim but leaves the story and setting intact. If the founder replaces it with the map script, classify it as brand-led and restore the lost creator concept slot.

**Measurement:** Assign IDs and timestamps to assets; compare momentum with branded searches, app store visits, downloads and first-use activation. The promotion and overlapping uploads mean the spike is directional evidence. Do not assign all downloads to one video. Test related hooks again during a documented comparison window.

**Cadence:** One thirty-minute weekly meeting covers business signals, uncertainty, learning and creator pitches. Individual edits move to asynchronous feedback. Declare weekly and monthly bonus budgets, with reliability and useful experiments alongside performance evidence.

**Expansion decision:** At the three-month review, assess whether the six-person system is sustainable. A reliable creator may take a paid manager role with defined time and fewer production duties. Dedicated accounts are a separate proposed extension under TechUGC or Botschafterprogramme, requiring publishing ownership and an agreed cadence. Ten additional creators are not yet justified by the available evidence.

### Rules that made the difference

- Retainers and deep brand training make creators ongoing partners. [S1][S3]
- A genuine 50/50 split creates new angles rather than sixty copies of a founder script. [S1]
- Business signals inform organic learning; coincident spikes remain uncertain. [S5]
- Successful organic formats can inform paid tests without guaranteeing paid performance. [S4][S6]
- The three-month review values reliability before management promotion. [S1]

**Illustrative review outcome:** Two comparable tests show increased app store visits but unchanged first-use activation. Keep testing acquisition hooks while investigating activation friction. More videos alone do not resolve that uncertainty.
