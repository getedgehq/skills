# Method and decision rules

Credit: Based on Andrew Foxwell's public material and Foxwell Digital publications, with Paul Fairbrother, Courtney Fritts, Alex Afterman, Harry Delmage/Delmege and Mark Pereyra credited where relevant. Source details are in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Scope and evidence

This skill reconstructs a public decision framework, not a private course or a single proprietary formula. Its organizing principle is fit: choose the testing system that repeatedly discovers creatives worth scaling under the account's economics, production capacity, data density and risk constraints. Reassess the system when it ceases to work. [S1][S2]

The four-method taxonomy is from Courtney Fritts's agency article. The later Foxwell/Fairbrother article foregrounds two mainstream structures, while retaining hybrids. They describe different levels of detail rather than mutually exclusive doctrines. [S1][S2]

Source-backed guidance is cited below. Statements marked **Package adaptation** translate that guidance into a usable plan; they are not attributed numerical rules from Foxwell. Algorithm explanations and performance claims remain the authors' observations, not established causal findings.

## Intake and economics

Collect:

- Daily total spend, separate testing allowance and maximum tolerable loss.
- Weekly genuinely new concepts, variants per concept and production cost.
- Target CAC or CPA, observed conversion costs, conversion delay and data volume.
- AOV, contribution margin, return/refund exposure and the business's payback assumptions.
- Cold versus existing-customer results, attribution basis and first-time buyer revenue.
- Existing campaign structure, creative concentration and operator capacity.

Budget alone is insufficient. A small account with one unfamiliar concept needs a different learning plan from a mature account producing many proven-angle iterations. Predictable conversion patterns favor efficiency filters; uncertain angles and longer purchase cycles need more protected learning. [S2]

Scaling depends on margin, creative supply and infrastructure together. Foxwell recommends starting with AOV and contribution margin because affordable acquisition cost shapes auction tolerance. Bundles, complementary products and useful starter kits can create room to acquire customers, provided they improve actual economics rather than simply discounting revenue. [S3]

**Package adaptation:** Calculate first-order acquisition room as net order revenue minus non-ad variable costs minus required contribution. Explicitly state whether contribution margin is measured before or after advertising. Include repeat-purchase value only when the user supplies evidence and a payback policy. Do not infer profitability from gross ROAS alone, and do not treat a higher AOV as automatically higher margin.

Separate harvesting existing demand from growing new demand. Review new-customer acquisition cost, first-time buyer revenue and prospecting stability as spend rises. Strong retargeting can conceal weak acquisition. Use audience reporting where reliable; separate the measurement even when separate campaigns are unnecessary. [S3]

## Framework selection

| Framework | Choose when | Delivery and learning trade-off | Main failure to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| ABO testing, then scale in place or graduate | Few concepts, sufficient learning budget, need to understand new angles, operator can manage tests | Allocates budget by ad set; concept batches get exposure, but individual ads can still receive uneven spend | Fragmentation, costly losers, weak transfer into scaling |
| CBO testing with ad-set minimum spends | More concepts, enough total budget for meaningful minimums, willing to trade precision for allocation efficiency | Minimums support concept exposure while remaining budget moves toward early performers | Early allocation bias and concepts stuck near their minimum |
| Direct competition in existing campaigns | Constrained separate-test budget, high output, mature account, or iterations of proven creative | Consolidates learning and discovers ads that earn delivery in the intended environment | Incumbents starve new ads; sudden spend on a poor newcomer |
| Manual bid testing | Stable cost benchmarks, predictable conversion, efficiency valued over maximum delivery | Cost or bid controls filter delivery and may support broad creative supply | Tight controls starve tests; delayed conversions make early filtering unreliable |

The four rows and their conditional fit come from [S2]. [S1] emphasizes moving toward main-campaign testing as output grows, because creating a separate ad set for every brief becomes cumbersome and fragments learning.

Do not label direct competition risk-free. [S2] describes it optimistically as avoiding separate testing waste, but [S1] explicitly warns that a new ad can quickly absorb budget and produce poor returns. It provides simpler allocation, not immunity from losses. [S1][S2]

Do not present bid caps and cost caps as identical. [S2] groups them within manual bid testing. **Package adaptation:** Specify the actual control, verify its current behavior before implementation, and measure realized results. A bid cap limits bidding rather than guaranteeing a realized CPA, and a cost goal is not a hard ceiling on every conversion. Delivery by itself is not proof of profitability.

### Conditional source examples

These numbers are examples or heuristics, never mandatory thresholds:

- [S2] suggests that below $100/day, direct competition or manual controls may be the feasible options. Lack of predictable costs can still make manual controls inappropriate.
- [S2] contrasts one to three weekly concepts, potentially suited to ABO, with ten or more concepts that require considerable ABO funding and may favor CBO.
- [S2] illustrates four or five CBO concept ad sets with minimums near 10% of campaign budget each. At $500/day, five $50 minimums account for $250, leaving $250 for flexible allocation.
- [S1] describes batches of roughly one to ten ads tied to one brief, and roughly ten to twenty active ads per ad set for direct testing. These are context-specific working ranges.
- [S1] gives production examples of weekly batches for newer advertisers, forty to fifty monthly ads for mid-sized advertisers, and one hundred or more for larger advertisers. Cost per acquisition and win rate affect useful throughput.

[S3] separately mentions three to six hypothesis-led creatives per ad set in a common scaling structure. Do not blend that with [S1]'s direct-testing range into one universal count. Choose an appropriate workload and explain the source context. [S1][S3]

### Hybrids and evolution

Use protected learning for a genuinely new product, unfamiliar angle or consequential offer, and direct competition for business-as-usual refreshes. Alternatively use CBO minimums normally and a limited ABO lane when a particular concept requires a clearer read. Give each lane a purpose and funded capacity. [S1][S2]

New accounts may benefit from structured learning but may lack the funding for many concurrent ad sets. High-volume accounts may benefit from consolidation but still require oversight for sudden losses. Seasonal auction pressure can change the preferred balance. A framework is justified by useful discoveries and successful scaling, not by popularity. [S2]

## Budget and exposure

[S2] recommends planning around approximately a week for ordinary tests, while recognizing that clear signals can appear earlier. It illustrates fixed daily ABO allocations and, for scaling in place, increases of roughly 20% to 30% every few days if performance holds. These are starting points, not statistical guarantees. [S2]

**Package adaptation:** For ABO, planned outlay equals the sum of daily ad-set allocations multiplied by planned days. For CBO, fund minimums within the total campaign budget, leaving meaningful flexible allocation. Do not add minimums on top of the campaign total. Check production costs separately and avoid spending the same allowance in two lanes.

A guaranteed ad-set allocation is not equal delivery to every asset. A week's elapsed time without conversions or meaningful delivery is not automatically a conclusive test. [S1][S2]

**Package adaptation:** Agree on maximum loss, monitoring cadence, conversion-lag allowance and evidence requirements before launch. Use historical account variation and event density to judge uncertainty. State whether the task seeks practical allocation decisions or causal inference; ordinary Meta delivery is not a randomized creative experiment. If a test is underfunded, run fewer briefs, extend its planned window where affordable, or report that the result is inconclusive.

## Creative research and diversification

Start with actual customer situations, objections, reviews, founder insight and observed winning creative. [S5] recounts Delmege's preference for manual investigation and human ideation, with AI helping execute, format, mine reviews and iterate. Retain specific customer detail and human QA; generic persona summaries are weak substitutes. Do not reconstruct his existing AI skills, which are neither fetched nor part of this package. [S5]

Preserve the persuasive psychology of a working angle while changing how someone encounters it. A founder explanation, customer testimonial, conversational Yapper, mechanism demonstration and authority-led explanation can reach different entry points into the same proposition. [S3]

Useful format families include product renders, lifestyle and flat-lay photography, founder or employee videos, partnership ads, unboxing, testimonials, native social video and appropriate AI-generated imagery. Select based on customer evidence and message requirements, not a quota. [S1]

Source quality gate: assess whether an asset plausibly has the potential to absorb $10,000 in spend before accepting it into production. This challenges weak, purposeless output. It is not a requirement to spend that amount on a small account or a prediction of performance. Low-production execution can satisfy the gate if the idea is strong. [S1]

Distinguish two types of work:

- **Exploration:** A new angle or format seeks a new viable entry point. Several properties may change; label it as concept discovery.
- **Iteration:** Keep the proven body while changing a hook, or keep the hook while changing the following hold scene. Test length or creator separately when attribution of learning matters. [S4]

[S4] describes the hook around seconds one to three and the hold scene around seconds three to six, alongside several length variants. Treat these as production starting points, not fixed timing laws for every format. Use retention metrics to find friction, then validate against conversion outcomes. Testimonial statics can be another execution of proof, using real permitted evidence. [S4]

**Package adaptation:** Truthful proof and permission are prerequisites for a usable brief. Emotional specificity is not permission to invent professional endorsements, medical outcomes or customer experiences. Keep unverified statements as research questions or production placeholders.

## Yappers

[S3] credits a Founders contributor, spelled Harry Delmage on that page, for the format structure. [S5]'s recap names Harry Delmege and describes long-form, story-led Yapper work. Credit the community contribution rather than claiming Andrew invented it.

A Yapper is a conversational, low-production video delivered like a personal explanation. [S3] describes natural single takes without studio lighting, graphics or cuts. It is more specific than the category UGC: a montage with voiceover does not meet this described execution, although it may be a useful separate format. [S3]

Four-part brief:

1. **Attention:** Open with a surprising or counter-conventional observation relevant to the customer.
2. **Recognition:** Describe a concrete lived difficulty that someone can recognize in their day.
3. **Bridge:** Introduce the product as a natural response to that difficulty, after establishing the situation.
4. **Invitation:** Close with a gentle next step consistent with the story.

The source warns against broad problems, prematurely foregrounding the product and switching into rehearsed commercial delivery. Authenticity and trust are the authors' explanation for the format's usefulness on cold audiences; lower CPMs are not promised outcomes. [S3]

Brief a speaker who can truthfully express the experience. Include the situation, substantiated mechanism or proof, a conversational body and several hooks to test. Recording separate single-take variants can preserve the native feel while varying the opening. Do not require a universally short duration: [S5] also describes long-form story-led execution. [S3][S5]

**Package adaptation:** For an agent-generated script, mark it as proposed copy requiring speaker validation. Use founder observations or demonstrable product features when no genuine customer account is available. Write for a person to say naturally, not for a synthetic testimonial presented as real.

## Winners, iteration and scaling

[S2] describes a testing winner as meeting account KPIs over a sufficient period, or improving on existing scale ads when none meet the KPI. Distinguish those cases: an improvement above an unacceptable baseline is a useful lead, not automatically permission to expand losses. [S2]

[S1] adds spend capacity to ROAS, CPA and creative diagnostics. An attractive ratio on a few purchases may have less scaling value than an economically acceptable asset that reliably earns meaningful delivery and generates many purchases. Evaluate both efficiency and usable volume. [S1]

**Package adaptation:** Use four decision labels:

| Label | Meaning | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Insufficient exposure | Too little comparable delivery or conversion evidence | Hold judgement; change exposure only within the test allowance |
| Failed under tested conditions | Adequate practical evidence and unacceptable results | Pause or revise the hypothesis; record the conditions |
| Relative improvement | Better than the current baseline but below economics | Continue bounded development; do not claim profitable scale |
| Qualified scaling candidate | Meets economics with useful delivery and sufficient observation | Validate scale in place or at the destination |

Keep low-delivery ads distinct from proven losers. Main-campaign selection tells you that an asset did not earn allocation in that environment; it does not isolate why the creative failed. [S1][S2]

Scale in place, graduate to a main campaign, or combine the two. An asset can perform well in its testing ad set and then lose allocation among stronger incumbents. [S1][S2] [S1] reports a Meta-representative suggestion of fifty conversions before moving an ad, but describes it as rarely practical; it is not a platform requirement for this skill.

Monitor dependence on a few creatives. [S3] flags cases where one or two ads consume more than 70% of spend as concentration risk. That is a diagnostic prompt for diversification, not an instruction to disable profitable ads to force equal spending.

Use AAA sequencing: assessment of economics and stability, action on conversion, creative and structure, then ascension through justified expansion. Diversify visual expression, protect winning psychology and monitor acquisition as spend increases. [S3]

## Edge cases and warnings

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Many variants, few original ideas | Count concepts separately; do not mistake asset count for strategic diversity. [S1][S5] |
| Small budget, weak historical evidence | Limit concurrency; prefer a conditional learning plan over automatic manual caps. [S2] |
| Long consideration period or variable AOV | Allow the appropriate delay and economic segmentation; early cap-based selection can mislead. [S2] |
| No delivery under caps | Check cap calibration and uncertainty before labelling all ideas failures. [S2] |
| Strong hook rate, poor sales | Diagnose the product bridge, proof, offer and landing experience; attention is diagnostic, not the final objective. [S1][S4] |
| New creative absorbs excessive main-campaign spend | Apply the pre-agreed loss rule and inspect realized economics. [S1] |
| Warm audiences support all apparent growth | Validate new-customer performance before scaling the blended result. [S3] |
| AI output expands faster than quality | Restore specific research and human review; use AI to execute chosen ideas. [S5] |
| Framework no longer discovers scalable ads | Compare an alternative for a bounded period or small parallel lane; document the result. [S2] |

[S5] reports one operator's observation that synthetic UGC can fatigue faster than real-person content. Treat it as a hypothesis to monitor in the account, not a universal lifespan rule. Never replace quality with a format superstition. The durable principle across the sources is a funded, documented, adaptable creative system. [S1][S2][S5]
