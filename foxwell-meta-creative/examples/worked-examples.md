# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations. Brands, budgets, scripts, evidence and results are fictional. They demonstrate application of the public principles, not Foxwell case studies or forecasts. Source IDs refer to [sources.md](../references/sources.md).

## Illustration 1: Small account with sparse data

### Situation

FoldKit sells a compact desk organizer. Total Meta budget is $90/day. AOV is $72, non-ad variable costs are $38, and the required first-order contribution is $9. Affordable acquisition cost is therefore $25. Current new-customer CPA is $32, with few weekly conversions and a two-day conversion delay. Production capacity is two new concepts per week. There is no separate funded testing allowance.

### Weak first draft

Create five ABO test ad sets at $30/day each. Run twenty variants for forty-eight hours, pick the highest CTR, and move it to a scaling campaign. Use a polished Yapper opening: "Introducing FoldKit, the ultimate solution for everyone who wants productivity. Buy now." Raise its budget by 100% immediately.

### Why it fails

The proposed $150/day exceeds the entire account budget. Sparse data and conversion delay undermine the two-day verdict. CTR does not resolve acquisition economics. The script starts with the product, uses an unspecific problem and sounds like a commercial. Moving an ad provides no assurance it will keep spending. [S1][S2][S3]

### Improved result

**Framework:** Direct competition within the existing $90/day campaign, introducing one brief at a time. This follows [S2]'s constrained-budget option while acknowledging incumbent allocation bias. Avoid manual caps initially because the account has unstable costs and limited evidence. Do not promise equal delivery.

**Economics and review:** $25 is the qualified acquisition target; $32 is the baseline to improve. Review after a planned week, allowing another two days for delayed conversion reporting before the final read. Seven days represents up to $630 total campaign allocation, not $630 incremental testing spend. The founder monitors daily. As an invented local risk rule, pause a newcomer if it reaches $75 without a purchase after allowing for reporting lag; this rule limits loss and does not prove statistical failure. If new creative receives too little delivery, classify it as insufficient exposure.

**Hypothesis:** A specific end-of-work cleanup problem will make the organizer easier to understand than generic productivity language. The proposed speaker is the founder describing their own desk, with the actual folding feature shown only after the problem is established.

**Proposed Yapper body, requiring speaker validation:**

"My desk wasn't messy all day. It got messy in the last five minutes, when I shoved everything aside to close my laptop. I kept leaving the cable and notebook in different places. That's the little problem I built this for. Everything sits here while I'm working, then this folds away together when I'm done. If your desk has the same end-of-day pile, the page shows how it folds."

Record a natural single take, without studio graphics or scripted commercial delivery. Alternate opening for a separate take: "The worst bit of my desk was what I did to it after work." Keep the body and invitation stable to learn about the hook. A third concept waits until the existing brief has a useful read or the next review resets capacity.

**Illustrative decision:** Suppose the ad later produces four purchases on $112, a $28 CPA. It beats the $32 baseline but misses the $25 economic target. Label it a relative improvement. Continue bounded development; do not call it a profitable scaling winner.

### Rules that made the difference

- Framework fits constrained budget and unstable historical data. [S2]
- Affordable CAC is calculated before scaling. [S3]
- Product arrives after a recognizable lived problem in the Yapper. [S3]
- Hook iteration preserves the body and economic evaluation outranks attention metrics. [S4][S1]
- The maximum-loss rule, calculation and explicit decision labels are package adaptations, not Foxwell thresholds.

## Illustration 2: High output with a risky new launch

### Situation

An invented outdoor accessories brand spends $6,000/day on Meta. Weekly production includes twelve new concepts and twenty-four proven-angle variants. An acceptable new-customer CPA is $40. Existing acquisition CPA is $36. A single testimonial ad earns 74% of spend. The brand wants to launch a new travel kit while continuing established-product growth. Its authorized testing allowance is $600/day within the existing total, with an approved learning-loss allowance of $1,200 for the launch week.

### Weak first draft

Put all thirty-six assets into one main ad set immediately. Treat any ad with no spend after one day as a failed concept. Scale the first ad with a 6x ROAS even if it has only two purchases. Produce thirty more near-identical testimonial edits to solve concentration. Call all conversational videos Yappers, including highly edited montages.

### Why it fails

A large campaign can over-allocate to a poor new ad. No spend is not a creative verdict. A ratio on two purchases says little about spend capacity. Cosmetic edits preserve dependence on essentially the same entry point. A montage does not match the Yapper execution described in [S3]. [S1][S3]

### Improved result

**Framework:** Hybrid with two explicit jobs. Established-angle iterations enter the consolidated main campaign in manageable waves. Three unfamiliar launch briefs receive protected CBO testing with minimum spends. The $5,400/day main lane plus $600/day launch lane equals the unchanged $6,000/day total. Use no extra unfunded ABO lane.

**Allocation:** Give each launch ad set a $60/day minimum, reserving $180 in total and leaving $420/day for flexible CBO allocation. Planned launch outlay is at most $4,200 over seven days; minimums are included in that total. Confirm current control availability before launch. These are invented account settings, guided by [S2]'s example allocation logic.

**Learning and risk:** Each launch brief contains two to four assets and one explicit concept hypothesis. The operator checks delivery and economics twice daily because allocation can move quickly. A local learning-loss measure compares paid spend to purchases valued at the $40 allowable CPA, after conversion lag. Suspend the lane for review if excess spend reaches the approved $1,200 limit. This is an operating guardrail, not an incrementality estimate or sourced universal rule.

**Production:** Preserve the working angle that a compact kit keeps essential items easy to find. Express it as a founder demonstration, a specific packing testimonial and a Yapper about searching inside a bag at the station. Each uses real product evidence. Carry out hook-only variants separately from entirely new concepts so the learning log distinguishes exploration and iteration.

**Proposed Yapper opening and bridge, requiring speaker validation:**

"I didn't need a bigger bag. I needed to stop emptying it on the station bench to find one cable. That was the annoying part: everything fit, but none of it stayed where I could find it. This kit keeps those small things together, so I can lift out one piece instead of unpacking my whole bag. If that sounds familiar, there's a packing demo on the page."

Shoot as a natural single take. Test another opening with the same body and genuine speaker context. Do not add fake customer outcomes or claim that the format guarantees cheaper impressions.

**Illustrative evaluation:** Over the agreed window, ad A spends $80 and records two purchases with 6x ROAS. Ad B spends $1,200 and records thirty-two new customers, a $37.50 CPA. Ad A has promising but sparse evidence; it needs further observation. Ad B is a stronger scaling candidate because it combines acceptable acquisition cost with useful delivery. An ad that spends $12 remains insufficiently exposed.

**Scaling:** Keep B active in the launch test while validating it in the main campaign, within the total spending allowance. If it loses delivery there, inspect competition rather than erasing the testing evidence. Check the new-customer CPA and concentration weekly. Diversify the 74% dependency through additional formats without abruptly disabling the profitable incumbent.

### Rules that made the difference

- Hybrid protects unfamiliar launch learning while consolidating familiar iterations. [S1][S2]
- Minimums receive real funding and leave flexible allocation. [S2]
- Spend capacity supplements efficiency and small-sample ROAS. [S1]
- Winning psychology is retained across distinct visual expressions. [S3]
- Monitoring cadence, learning-loss arithmetic and approval limits are illustrative package adaptations.
