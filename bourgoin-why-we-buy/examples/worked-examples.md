# Worked examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations, not customer evidence or Bourgoin case studies. All buyer statements, service capabilities, audiences, and proposed test plans below are fictional. They demonstrate the rules; a live task must replace them with actual records. No measured results are claimed.

## Illustration 1: bookkeeping after a missed filing

### Weak first draft

> The ultimate bookkeeping solution for ambitious founders. Our innovative dashboard saves time and guarantees tax compliance. Book now before disaster strikes. Only three spots left!

This copy assumes capabilities, scarcity, and catastrophe. It has no buyer moment, ignores the current workaround, and mistakes broad adjectives for proof.

### Illustrative input and extraction

A recent customer reports missing a filing while checking invoices in a spreadsheet. She began searching the next morning, tried a general accounting app, and still had to check deadlines herself. The fictional service can review a records workflow and create a responsibility-and-deadline map; it cannot promise universal compliance.

| Evidence ID | Invented reported observation | Resulting insight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | Searching began after the missed filing | Trigger: a concrete failure changed readiness |
| E2 | Spreadsheet and app still required manual deadline checks | Alternative pain: responsibility stayed with the owner |
| E3 | She wanted evenings back and confidence with her accountant | Job and personal desire: organize responsibility and recover control |

The primary job is to establish a dependable bookkeeping routine after a missed deadline. Control is supported by the illustrative story rather than assigned because it sounds persuasive. These extractions follow the cornerstone framework. [S1]

### Improved result

**Headline:** Missed a filing while juggling invoices?

**Body:** Start by finding where deadline tracking breaks down. Our records-workflow review maps what needs doing, who owns it, and when it is due, so you can discuss a clear plan with your accountant. Bring your current spreadsheet or app setup.

**Offer:** A scoped review of the bookkeeping workflow, with a written responsibility-and-deadline map.

**CTA:** Request a records-workflow review.

**Fit statement:** This review organizes your process. It does not replace your accountant's tax advice.

The psychological move is specific pain followed by a feasible route to relief, supported by an explicit process rather than an invented guarantee. [S5][S7]

### Trigger-test brief

- **Objective:** Increase qualified review requests from owners struggling with missed bookkeeping deadlines.
- **Candidates:** A search landing page for relevant help queries; an accountant's post-filing resource email; a practical bookkeeping recovery article with a review CTA.
- **First recommendation:** The search landing page, assuming the service already has an authorized search campaign. It reaches a plausible help-seeking moment and is small enough to implement. [S1]
- **Hypothesis:** Naming the missed-deadline context and explaining the review will produce more qualified requests than generic software claims.
- **Comparison:** Alternate the current truthful general copy and the revised copy within comparable traffic allocation; keep offer, targeting, and form consistent. The weak draft above is diagnostic, not a valid control to publish.
- **Primary measure:** Requests from owners reporting a deadline-tracking problem divided by unique landing-page visitors. Define qualification before counting.
- **Observation plan:** Two weeks, subject to existing budget limits. If too few requests occur, report the test as inconclusive rather than claiming a lift.
- **Next question:** Do other recent buyers begin searching after a missed filing, or do most act before an upcoming deadline?

The comparison and metric conventions make the test interpretable; they are additions by this package.

### Rules that made the difference

The trigger replaced a generic founder persona; actual workarounds replaced an imagined software battle; personal relief followed evidence; process detail replaced an unsupported guarantee; and the first test connected a reachable moment to a clear action. [S1][S5][S7][S8]

## Illustration 2: a freelancer preparing a client strategy

### Weak first draft

> Personas are dead. Smart marketers use our AI research platform. Unlock revolutionary insights and impress every client. Click to discover the secret nobody is telling you.

This insults the buyer's current practice, invents a universal outcome, and opens a curiosity loop without specifying the answer. It assumes the buyer wants a new platform rather than progress on a current project.

### Illustrative input and extraction

A freelance marketer bought a research-planning kit after a new client asked why its campaign should appeal to customers. The marketer first searched for a persona template, then tried a blank worksheet, but could not tie its fields to real purchase decisions. The buyer wanted a defensible recommendation and less anxiety in the strategy meeting.

The fictional kit contains prompts for interviewing recent buyers, an evidence worksheet, and a worked synthesis. It does not automatically supply customer research or guarantee client approval.

| Field | Interpretation of the invented story | Evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | A new client requested a rationale for campaign direction | Reported in the illustration |
| Primary job | Prepare a customer-backed recommendation before the strategy meeting | Interpretation of reported actions |
| Alternative pain | Blank persona worksheets lacked buying evidence | Reported in the illustration |
| Personal desire | Feel confident explaining the recommendation | Reported in the illustration |

This separates a behavior sequence from the inference used to design the offer. Recent actions matter more than a statement that someone would like better research. [S1][S2]

### Improved result

**Subject:** The missing evidence in your next persona worksheet

**Opening and promised answer:** A persona worksheet can organize what you know. It cannot tell you what made someone start shopping. Before presenting a campaign recommendation, add a recent buyer's trigger, alternatives tried, and desired progress. Those details give you a reason for the message you propose.

**Offer:** The buyer-story planning kit includes interview prompts, an evidence worksheet, and an example synthesis. Preview the worksheet to see the work involved.

**CTA:** Preview the buyer-story worksheet.

**Fit statement:** You still need buyer conversations or existing buyer records. The kit helps organize evidence; it does not generate evidence for you.

The message respects the existing worksheet while explaining what it lacks. The curiosity question is answered in the opening. The asset contents and limitation make the offer credible. [S4][S5][S6]

### Trigger-test brief

- **Objective:** Generate relevant worksheet previews that lead to kit purchases.
- **Candidates:** A search article for persona-template queries; a guest tutorial in a freelancer community; a partner newsletter aimed at marketers starting client engagements.
- **First recommendation:** A partner newsletter placement if audience fit and permission are established. Its readers may be preparing the relevant deliverable, but their timing remains a hypothesis. [S1]
- **Hypothesis:** Explaining the gap between worksheet fields and buying evidence earns more qualified interest than attacking personas.
- **Smallest implementation:** One email excerpt and a preview page showing the real worksheet.
- **Primary measure:** Kit purchases divided by unique visitors from the placement. Preview completion is a secondary diagnostic, not proof of demand.
- **Comparison and period:** Observe the placement cohort for 14 days. Without comparable prior traffic or a randomized control, report exploratory purchase behavior rather than causal lift.
- **Next question:** Are purchases prompted by a new client engagement, a failed campaign, or a routine planning cycle? Split messages if these correspond to different jobs.

### Rules that made the difference

A specific project replaced a broad professional identity. A respectful belief update replaced humiliation. The hook delivered its answer. Real kit contents replaced inflated outcomes. The test distinguishes useful curiosity from purchase behavior. [S1][S4][S5][S6]
