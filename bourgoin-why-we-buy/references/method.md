# Method: buyer evidence, message, and trigger test

Based on Katelyn Bourgoin's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Katelyn Bourgoin.

## Reading map and attribution

Use the sections on evidence and interviews for research, cornerstone insights for synthesis, psychological presentation for copy, and trigger tests for experiments. The core is Bourgoin's public Trigger Technique: gather recent buying stories, extract actionable insights, design growth ideas around trigger moments, and explain the story to the team before choosing one test. The other sources deepen message design. [S1]

Evidence labels, metric specifications, review gates, and scope adaptations below are operational conventions added by this package. They implement the public ideas without implying Bourgoin published this exact workflow. A citation identifies the underlying source, not proof that a proposed campaign will work. Source anecdotes and marketing statistics are not performance forecasts for a new client.

## 1. Start with a decision and actual buyers

Choose a concrete outcome that determines which ideas are useful: qualified demos, relevant traffic, purchases, or fewer abandoned checkouts. A traffic objective can be appropriate when the page already converts; it is insufficient when the real bottleneck is buyer qualification. Bourgoin places the growth objective before test ideation. This package records it at intake as well to prevent scope drift. [S1]

A useful buyer already recognized the problem, paid to address it, and obtained meaningful value. Favor recent purchasers because they can recover the sequence more accurately. A company role, age group, or industry is only recruitment context; it does not explain the event that made someone act. [S1]

If no customers exist, study people who recently paid a competitor or are actively trying to buy a solution. A currently searching participant can illuminate triggers and alternatives but cannot provide evidence of a completed purchase from your product. Bourgoin explicitly distinguishes actual or current buyers from people who merely match an ideal-customer profile. [S2]

## 2. Reconstruct events without selling a solution

Begin near the purchase, then work back to the earliest moment when the current situation became unacceptable or a new opportunity mattered. Rebuild the forward sequence afterward. Seek concrete context, actions, approximate timing, and changes in urgency. The trigger explains entry into a buying journey; a later advertisement may explain discovery of a particular seller. Do not automatically treat those as the same event. [S1]

Useful paraphrased prompts:

- What was happening when you first started looking for a different approach?
- What happened immediately before that? How were you handling the issue until then?
- What did you do next? What did you search for, and whom did you ask?
- What did you try, consider, buy, postpone, or rule out?
- What was difficult about each approach? What made changing feel risky?
- What finally made this purchase make sense at that moment?
- What were you hoping would change in your own working day or life?

These prompts operationalize the recent-behavior focus. They are not a reproduction of Bourgoin's paid interview scripts. [S1][S2]

Bourgoin's explicit interview warnings are to avoid asking customers to invent solutions, asking them to predict future use or payment, relying heavily on opinions, recruiting the wrong people, and interviewing at the wrong time. Ask about recent actions and existing struggles instead. Opinion can explain meaning, but it cannot substitute for observed buying behavior. [S2]

Package evidence convention: record an identifier, source type, date or approximate period, action, wording, and relevance to the decision. Use three labels:

- **Evidence:** Something a buyer reported or an artifact records. A reported event is not independently verified merely because it is specific.
- **Interpretation:** Your explanation of why an event matters.
- **Hypothesis:** A claim about another buyer, channel, message, or outcome awaiting testing.

Keep uncertainty attached to the claim. If the record says only that someone likes simplicity, a stressful deadline and desire for status remain unknown. Do not fill them with persuasive inventions.

## 3. Extract the four cornerstone insights

| Insight | Write it as | Decision it informs | Reject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | A specific event or accumulating struggle that changed readiness | When to reach a similar buyer | A generic funnel label |
| Primary job | Progress sought in a particular situation | Promise and offer | A feature list or demographic category |
| Pain with other solutions | Friction in an approach actually considered or used | Contrast and switching support | An invented rival weakness |
| Personal desire | A supported improvement in the buyer's own life | Emotional or social relevance | An emotion assigned by the marketer |

These are Bourgoin's cornerstone categories. Her broader extraction also asks about goals and the solutions considered, tried, or purchased before narrowing to a primary job. [S1]

Describe a job as situation plus desired progress: when a client asks for a defensible campaign plan, understand what their customers respond to so the marketer can recommend a direction confidently. Function, feeling, and social context can coexist. Pick the job that best explains the purchase rather than putting every benefit into a single message. [S1]

The personal desire is not a moral judgment. It names the individual's stake, such as relief, control, or recognition, only when the story supports it. Official business outcomes and personal motivations may differ. Keep both visible rather than making the product promise solely about one. [S1]

Alternatives include spreadsheets, manual work, asking a colleague, tolerating the problem, and category outsiders. Ask what buyers actually did instead of collecting vendors known to the seller. Bourgoin warns that founders can mistake attention to their own category for attention from buyers. [S8]

Package decision rule: if stories describe different jobs or moments, split them into provisional segments. If several events occurred, distinguish the first search trigger from the final purchase catalyst. If change was gradual, describe the accumulation and the point action began; do not force a dramatic incident.

## 4. Choose a psychological presentation after synthesis

Psychology changes how a supported benefit is presented. It does not generate the benefit, establish product capability, or prove buyer motivation. Choose a principal barrier and explain why the presentation fits it.

| Buyer condition | Presentation | Practical rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Message sounds unlike the buyer | Distinctive buyer language | Find compact expressions in real data and preserve their meaning | [S3] |
| Buyer shares the premise but doubts the purchase | Relevant reassurance | Use truthful evidence that validates this decision | [S4][S5] |
| Adoption conflicts with an established belief | Respectful belief update | Explain changed conditions or a credible learning journey | [S4] |
| Provider credibility is uncertain | Visible competence and honesty | Show relevant expertise, process, outcomes, and limitations | [S5] |
| Important content is being ignored | A relevant information gap | Signal the topic and resolve the promised question | [S6] |
| Buyer describes a concrete cost of staying stuck | Pain followed by relief | Name the real cost and give a believable next step | [S7] |
| Purchase carries embarrassment or judgment anxiety | Discretion, normalization, or visualization | Reduce the specific anxiety described by buyers | [S8] |

### Language and proof

Buyer-isms are short, distinctive expressions from satisfied buyers. Gather them from interviews and reviews and use them to improve clarity, memorability, or naming. Bourgoin also illustrates checking a name with the audience rather than assuming familiar marketing advice determines the winner. This does not make a poll proof of purchase demand. [S3]

Package integrity rule: preserve actual quotations separately from paraphrases. Do not present invented draft wording as a testimonial or create a composite speaker. Confirm that a phrase represents the intended context and that any factual promise is supported. Public-source text in this package is paraphrased; source citations are distinct from customer evidence identifiers.

Bourgoin describes credibility through expertise, trustworthiness, likability, and similarity of experience or values. Place relevant evidence where people assess you, such as a profile, product explanation, or purchase decision. A limitation stated honestly can help establish trust; explain how it affects fit and what the buyer can reasonably expect. [S5]

Package rule: do not fabricate credentials, testimonials, outcome numbers, or implied endorsements. If proof is missing, flag the gap and use a verifiable description of the actual process if available. A credible tone does not replace evidence.

### Belief, curiosity, and pain

Bourgoin recommends reaching people who already share a relevant belief and giving them material they can share with other decision-makers. If a buyer must revise an identity-linked belief, confronting them with more facts can provoke resistance. Show how circumstances changed or how someone similar revised their view without humiliation. This is not permission to reinforce a false claim. [S4]

An information gap works when the missing answer matters to the reader. Give enough detail to establish relevance and then answer the question opened by the headline or subject line. Bourgoin explicitly warns that clickbait and unresolved loops damage trust. A clear direct headline can be preferable when the buyer already has immediate purchase intent. [S6]

Avoidance framing highlights a concrete cost of inaction, then offers a path forward. Bourgoin explicitly cautions against fearmongering and repeats the instruction to close any mental loop opened. Do not amplify a minor inconvenience into disaster, or import a loss estimate the seller cannot support. Her illustrative results do not establish a universal advantage for negative headlines. [S7]

### Buyer attention and anxiety

Buyers do not follow the business as closely as its operators do. State what improves for them and explain enough context for a first encounter. Repeating an important message with different wording is reasonable. Do not assume brand announcements are inherently interesting. [S8]

For sensitive purchases, consider discreet checkout or packaging, useful normalization, or previews that reduce uncertainty about use. Base such changes on the actual concern. Any normalization statistic must be verified separately rather than borrowed from an unrelated example. [S8]

## 5. Design a small trigger test

Bourgoin's test-design sequence identifies the target buyer, target moments, possible marketing ideas, a compelling promise, and a clear call to action. Connect the moment to places buyers seek help and sources they trust. A channel is useful because it reaches the right situation, not because it is fashionable. [S1]

Brainstorm several possibilities from the cornerstone insights. For a team workshop, Bourgoin suggests a short timed brainstorm, presenting three ideas, and asking the team to choose one first test. Present the story before the ideas so the reasoning is understandable. Invite enthusiasm, concerns, and contributions rather than treating stakeholder agreement as buyer validation. [S1]

Package experiment specification:

- State the buyer hypothesis and the behavior expected if it is right.
- Name the smallest asset and audience that can answer the learning question.
- Select one primary metric with a denominator and a qualification rule where relevant.
- Specify a baseline or comparable control if available, observation period, and practical constraints.
- Define what would support, weaken, or leave the hypothesis unresolved.
- Keep other material variables comparable when testing a copy change.
- If testing a whole campaign concept, acknowledge that results cannot isolate the effect of its headline, channel, and offer separately.

Truthful urgency may follow from the buyer's timing or a genuine offer condition. Fabricated deadlines and unsupported scarcity are prohibited by this package. A timely, useful action is sufficient without pressure.

Deliver the buying story, ledger, cornerstone insights, presentation rationale, finished message, three candidates where useful, and one selected test. Plan within the brief; publishing, spending, recruiting, or contacting others requires the relevant user authorization.

When results become available, compare them with the objective. A click can indicate curiosity while failing to establish qualified demand. Low-volume results may remain inconclusive. Revisit the story, audience, reach, proof, and offer before declaring the buyer psychology wrong or the channel ineffective.

## 6. Edge cases and stopping rules

- **Only one story:** Use it for a hypothesis and a small test. Do not claim segment prevalence or a predictable conversion lift.
- **Reviews without chronology:** Extract language and alternative pains; mark the trigger unknown until a sequence can be recovered. [S3]
- **Only opinions or future intentions:** Produce a provisional brief and specify the recent-action question needed. Do not disguise weak evidence as a buying story. [S2]
- **Several stakeholders:** Keep user, champion, approver, and payer roles separate. Use shared material to help the champion explain the decision. The role separation is a package convention; shareable reassurance comes from Bourgoin. [S4]
- **Contradictory stories:** Preserve the disagreement and compare contexts. Do not average incompatible jobs into a generic persona.
- **No reach into the trigger moment:** Choose a feasible proxy or label the channel unvalidated. Do not assert targeting capability that the channel lacks.
- **Narrow edit request:** Improve the supplied message with available evidence and return unresolved questions briefly. Do not require an interview project before making a reversible edit.
- **Unsupported product promise:** Stop short of that claim, narrow the promise to verified capability, and mark missing proof. The buyer's desire does not establish that the product delivers it.
- **Sensitive context:** Reduce documented anxiety rather than applying stigma to an entire audience. [S8]

The conventions above keep the public method useful when evidence or scope is limited. Unknowns are acceptable output. Concealed uncertainty is a method failure.
