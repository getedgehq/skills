# The B2B creator blueprint in depth

Credit: This package is based on Brendan Gahan's public method and Creator Authority's public positioning. It is an independent synthesis, not an official framework or internal agency manual. Source identifiers resolve in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Scope and evidence

The central mechanism is to combine a credible industry person's perspective with video and professional audience targeting. The person supplies expertise and familiarity; video makes their judgment and personality legible; paid distribution can extend that trusted voice to relevant buyers. Gahan describes this combination directly in his posts and in his contribution to LinkedIn's article. [S1] [S2] [S3]

This skill's numbered structure is an editorial organization. It is not a claim that Gahan publicly named a framework with this exact title or sequence. Sections distinguish **public principle** from **package adaptation**. [S7] is an interview-based publisher account, not a transcript independently checked against the underlying recording. [S4] confirms agency service positioning. [S6] describes operations through a vendor case study. Keep these evidence levels separate.

Treat historical survey results as rationale for testing, not causal proof or current performance promises. [S3] describes a survey of 1,500 senior B2B marketers in six countries, with expert-network and panel sampling. Its comparisons are reported associations. The CTR figures in [S1] and [S2] differ; the fetched posts do not establish a common comparison baseline. Do not merge them into a universal expected uplift. Vendor economics in [S7] are likewise context-dependent. [S1] [S2] [S3] [S7]

## 1. Buyer research before casting

**Public principle:** Start with customer, brand, and category understanding. Insights about motivations and differentiation shape the concept and creator strategy. Selecting personalities before establishing that foundation reverses the process described in the interview coverage. [S7]

**Package adaptation:** Build a compact buyer map: professional role, purchase influence, trigger, obstacle, alternative, and evidence source. Include technical evaluators and budget approvers when relevant. State one campaign insight in this form: this buyer wants a specific improvement but hesitates because of a specific risk; this verified product capability helps address it.

Use customer interviews, sales objections, win/loss notes, and product evidence supplied by the client. A competitive differentiator needs support. If exclusivity cannot be demonstrated, use a narrower defensible benefit rather than claiming nobody else offers it. If research is missing, label the insight provisional and propose the smallest inquiry that could disprove it.

## 2. One objective per activation

**Public principle:** The interview account prioritizes a single campaign goal. Lead-oriented activity can use jointly produced resources, webinars, or relevant product announcements. Awareness can use educational, distinctive, or entertaining concepts. Full-funnel agency services do not mean every activation must do everything. [S7] [S4]

**Decision rule:** Select the metric from the required buyer response before choosing the format. An awareness campaign should build recognition among relevant professionals; a lead campaign should motivate a useful, appropriate next step. Consideration can be a separate activation within the program, consistent with the broader buyer-journey discussion in [S3].

**Package adaptation:** Define one primary metric, a baseline if available, review date, and pass threshold. Keep reach, clicks, and completions as diagnostics when the objective is qualified opportunities. Keep immediate demo requests secondary when the campaign is explicitly for awareness. Do not punish a campaign for missing an objective it was never designed to serve.

## 3. Useful concepts with creator contribution

**Public principle:** Form a rough campaign concept after the goal. For lead generation, the creator should contribute substantive expertise to the resource. Their identity on a promotional thumbnail is insufficient. The resource must have independent value to the audience. Awareness concepts can be novel or entertaining when appropriate to the brand and buyer. [S7]

**Decision rule:** Ask what useful thing the creator can teach, demonstrate, diagnose, or challenge that the buyer would want even without a brand mention. Then identify where the product legitimately belongs in that account. If removing the logo removes all value, rebuild the concept.

**Package adaptation:** Describe the buyer tension, creator insight, demonstration, product role, and next step in five sentences. For leads, specify the resource itself and the creator's contribution before drafting promotional copy. For awareness, choose a distinctive idea tied to a relevant professional truth rather than adding a gated download by habit.

## 4. Creator fit and evidence

**Public principle:** The interview coverage puts real industry expertise ahead of follower count and treats impressions as budget context. The vendor case study reports profiling, campaign-specific filtering, network analysis, and content review as parts of Creator Authority's discovery workflow. [S7] [S6]

Assess the following dimensions separately:

| Dimension | Evidence to seek | Decision consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category authority | Relevant operating experience and technically sound public work | Reject unsupported expertise, regardless of popularity |
| Buyer relevance | Supplied audience analytics, professional discussion, relevant community ties | Mark inferred fit as uncertain until corroborated |
| Content and voice | Recent substantive posts, videos, prior sponsored work | Match the creator's natural strengths to the concept |
| Authenticity | Specific professional exchanges and credible account history | Investigate repetitive or suspicious engagement |
| Brand alignment | Product familiarity, relevant convictions, meaningful concept feedback | Require a human vetting conversation |

This table is a package adaptation, not Gahan's published scorecard. Tool scores can help triage but do not prove expertise or fraud. Favikon promotes its own scoring and workflow tools in [S7]; neither the vendor nor a paid subscription is mandatory. Manual review can implement the same decisions. [S6] [S7]

A small practitioner audience can be valuable when expertise is strong and paid targeting supplies scale. A large audience can still be useful when it is relevant. Do not introduce arbitrary follower minimums or maximums. Expertise-based selection and commercial fee drivers are separate: audience size can affect price without deciding credibility. [S7]

## 5. Outreach and human vetting

**Public principle:** Keep the invitation concise and explain the proposed paid collaboration and relevance. A call establishes campaign context, creator plans, schedule, and enthusiasm. Heath's interview summary also emphasizes real conversation and genuine excitement about the brand. [S7] [S5]

**Package adaptation:** Draft an invitation with one specific observation about their work, the buyer problem, paid scope, and a low-friction question. Prepare a call agenda covering product experience, their honest interpretation of the concept, conflicting commitments, delivery capacity, and proposed creative contribution.

Ask what they would change. Thoughtful disagreement can signal valuable judgment. If the creator cannot explain why the product or idea matters to their audience, revisit the fit or concept. If they have not used the product, offer a genuine evaluation stage; do not write first-person experience for them.

[S7] advocates repeated follow-up and contains vendor promotion of mass outreach. This package does not treat bulk automation as a necessary part of Gahan's principle. Drafting is the default; send only within the user's authorization. If outreach is authorized, use the agreed follow-up limit and stop on a decline or opt-out.

## 6. Budget and commercial scope

**Public principle:** Begin with business economics, ask for relevant historical performance, and obtain the creator's price for the actual activation. Rates vary with expertise, engagement, audience, cross-platform profile, and the work required. A jointly produced research asset involves different effort from one post. [S7]

**Package adaptation:** Separate creator compensation, production, usage rights, paid media, and measurement costs. Specify revision limits, deliverables, deadlines, paid usage duration, and whether exclusivity is required. These commercial fields make the plan executable; the sources do not prescribe a standard contract or fee schedule.

For a lead goal, calculate an allowable acquisition cost using the client's economics. Show media CPL separately from total campaign CPL: total included campaign cost divided by qualified leads. Define qualification and the cost window. Use historical data only if audience, offer, and attribution are comparable. A forecast without evidence is a scenario, not a promise.

## 7. A clear brief with creative freedom

**Public principle:** The interview coverage describes a concise brief covering goal, audience, reasons to believe, deliverables, boundaries, and logistics including disclosure, tracking links, and hashtags. Supply context without scripting every line. [S7]

**Package adaptation:** Give three verified proof points where available, with provenance and permitted wording. If only one is supported, use one and identify the gap; never manufacture evidence to fill the brief. Define non-negotiable factual boundaries separately from optional creative suggestions.

The creator owns their viewpoint and natural expression. The brand reviews product accuracy, disclosure, and agreed deliverables. Avoid edits that turn a practitioner into a corporate narrator. Specific limitations and honest tradeoffs can preserve credibility when they are accurate and relevant. The brief should permit those judgments rather than require unqualified praise.

## 8. Video as a trust medium

**Public principle:** Gahan links video with human familiarity and the influence of creator relationships. LinkedIn's article discusses short social video, storytelling, testimonials, and demonstrations; these formats are broader article context, not all prescriptions uniquely attributable to Gahan. [S2] [S3]

**Package adaptation:** Structure a video around a buyer-recognizable problem, an expert perspective, observable evidence, a useful qualification, and one appropriate next step. Show the person making a decision or demonstrating a workflow rather than reciting unsupported adjectives. Clear audio and readable captions help the audience access that expertise.

No fetched source establishes one mandatory length, opening formula, editing style, cadence, or camera orientation. Choose production choices as hypotheses for the audience and placement. An illustrative 45-second demonstration is a test variant, not a Gahan rule. Human credibility does not require poor production, and polish does not compensate for weak insight.

## 9. Targeted distribution

**Public principle:** Gahan combines influencer content with targeted media and highlights Thought Leader Ads. His blueprint post also mentions BrandLink and disclosure tools as part of the opportunity. These platform names do not make every tool necessary for every campaign. [S1] [S2] [S3]

**Package adaptation:** Specify the organic post, relevant professional targeting, authorized amplification asset, rights window, paid ceiling, and tracking. Before implementation, check current official LinkedIn documentation for supported formats, permissions, targeting, disclosure options, and account eligibility. The fetched historical posts are not current product instructions.

If paid media is unavailable, plan organic distribution and creator collaboration while lowering confidence in predictable reach. Do not sell that alternative as the same scale mechanism. If a format is ineligible for amplification, adapt the asset or select an available authorized distribution route. If targeting is too narrow to deliver, widen adjacent relevant roles or geographies with an explicit relevance rationale.

## 10. Measure, learn, and reallocate

**Public principle:** The interview coverage separates creator performance, creative performance, and message or call-to-action performance. Evaluate outcomes in the context of paid spend. Once winners emerge, concentrate investment and ask strong creators for further work rather than spreading spend evenly indefinitely. [S7]

**Package adaptation:** Maintain one row per creator, asset, message, audience, and spend window. Record creator fee allocation, media cost, objective outcome, quality, and caveats. Do not rank raw outcomes without exposure context. A better-funded creator may have more leads and worse efficiency.

Use matched or reasonably comparable audience and spend conditions when feasible. Check sample size, tracking errors, offer differences, and downstream qualification before declaring a winner. Set the decision window and minimum evidence in advance. Scale when goal attainment and efficiency hold; revise when the content interests the right people but the next step fails; stop when fit or authenticity fails. These evidence safeguards are package adaptations, not a statistical framework published by Gahan.

Trust is a strategic objective, but clicks alone do not measure it. Use brand research, buyer feedback, relevant professional conversation, and sales feedback where available. Name each as a direct measure or a proxy. Long sales cycles may justify leading indicators and later pipeline review, not invented instant ROI. [S2] [S3]

## Warnings and edge cases

- **Empty reach:** Casting for fame while ignoring expertise undermines the trust mechanism. Return to professional relevance and proof. [S7]
- **Overloaded goals:** Trying to accomplish every commercial task in one activation weakens the concept and evaluation. Choose one primary outcome. [S7]
- **Superficial co-creation:** A creator endorsement cannot rescue a resource that offers no useful expertise. Give the creator a substantive role. [S7]
- **Transactional fit:** Heath's summary attributes genuine brand enthusiasm to Gahan. [S3] also warns about purely transactional partnerships through another contributor, Drew Neisser. Do not present Neisser's warning as Gahan's direct quote. [S5] [S3]
- **Overcontrol:** A rigid corporate script removes the expert's contribution. Supply evidence and boundaries, then preserve the voice. [S7]
- **Unequal media:** Total clicks or leads alone can misidentify a winning creator. Compare against the spend supporting each result. [S7]
- **Unproven claims:** Historical uplift and vendor cost claims are context, not forecasts. Set a bounded test using client economics. [S1] [S2] [S7]
- **Sensitive product claims:** As a package adaptation, require supported wording and a designated reviewer for financial, health, security, or regulatory assertions. When evidence is missing, narrow the claim or omit it.
- **No suitable external creator:** As a package adaptation, consider a credible practitioner, customer, or employee with transparent affiliation. State that this changes the source of authority and may change audience access.
- **Unavailable analytics:** As a package adaptation, keep the candidate provisional and use a small evidence-gathering collaboration if authorized. Do not replace missing data with confident estimates.
