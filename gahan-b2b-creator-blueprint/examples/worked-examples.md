## Illustration 1: Qualified leads for an operations platform

### Situation and assumed evidence

QueuePath sells workflow software to customer-support operations teams at mid-sized software companies. In this illustration, customer interviews indicate that operations managers struggle to see where handoffs fail. Assume the product team verified that QueuePath preserves a timestamped ownership history. The team has EUR 8,000 for a four-week pilot and wants relevant evaluation requests.

### Weak first draft

Hire the largest business creator available. Have them read a 60-second script describing QueuePath as the ultimate AI platform that transforms every support team. Add a download link to the existing sales brochure. Spend the entire budget boosting the post to everyone interested in business. Report impressions and total leads as proof of success.

### Why it fails

The casting has no evidence of support-operations authority. The claim exceeds the assumed product evidence. The resource adds little independent value. Broad distribution ignores the buyer. The review conflates exposure, unqualified submissions, and actual business interest.

### Improved result

**Primary goal:** Generate qualified evaluation requests from support-operations managers in the selected market. For this illustration, qualified means the requested role, company size, and a stated handoff problem. The target is 20 qualified requests at no more than EUR 400 total campaign cost per request. This is a pilot target, not a forecast.

**Buyer insight:** Managers need to locate ownership gaps before recommending another tool. Show a diagnostic approach before asking them to evaluate software.

**Creator selection:** Shortlist two former support-operations leaders who publish practical workflow analyses. Verify their experience, recent content, and supplied audience analytics. Invite the stronger match to discuss the concept and test the product. No follower minimum applies. Selection remains provisional until that conversation and evaluation occur.

**Concept:** Co-create a handoff audit worksheet. The creator contributes diagnostic questions and a worked process example. QueuePath appears in one implementation section showing the assumed verified history feature. The worksheet remains useful to teams using another tool.

**Video outline, with an illustrative 45-second duration:**

- Opening: A fictional expert asks, "Can you show who owned your last escalated ticket at every handoff?"
- Teaching: They explain how to map responsibility, timing, and missing ownership.
- Demonstration: A clearly labeled sample ticket shows the timestamped history.
- Qualification: Visibility helps diagnosis; it does not automatically repair the process.
- Next step: Download the audit worksheet and request an evaluation if the exercise reveals a relevant gap.

The wording is an invented script suggestion. The actual creator must adapt it to their honest experience. Disclose the paid relationship plainly.

**Budget and distribution:** EUR 2,500 creator fee including the agreed worksheet contribution, EUR 500 production, and EUR 5,000 media. Any paid usage rights must fit the fee agreement; otherwise revise the allocation. Plan Thought Leader Ads to relevant support leadership roles after checking current eligibility and securing permission. Use tracked links and a consistent lead-qualification definition.

**Decision:** Review qualified requests, media CPL, total CPL, and sales feedback. If all EUR 8,000 is spent and 25 qualified requests arrive, total CPL is EUR 320. If only five requests qualify, total CPL is EUR 1,600 regardless of how many raw form submissions occurred. Both are hypothetical calculations.

### Rules that made the difference

Buyer research and one goal grounded the concept. Expertise replaced popularity as the casting criterion. Substantive co-creation made the resource valuable. Creative freedom preserved the expert voice. Targeted amplification supplied relevant reach, and fully defined costs and qualification made the outcome reviewable. See method sections 1 through 10, especially 2, 3, 4, 7, and 10. [S7] [S2] [S3]

## Illustration 2: Awareness among industrial reliability managers

### Situation and assumed evidence

BearingBridge offers machine-maintenance analytics. The fictional company wants recognition among plant reliability managers before the next planning cycle. Assume its engineering team verified that its dashboard supports inspection prioritization, with no evidence that it predicts every failure. The pilot lasts six weeks and has EUR 12,000.

### Weak first draft

Hire a general technology celebrity to announce that BearingBridge eliminates downtime. Demand three videos with identical product slogans. Require viewers to book a demo in every video. Rank the creators by clicks, then give more money to whoever generates the largest total.

### Why it fails

The expertise and product claim are unsupported. A premature conversion ask conflicts with the awareness objective. Identical scripts erase the creator's perspective. Raw clicks privilege whoever receives more paid distribution and do not establish recognition.

### Improved result

**Primary goal:** Build recognition within the reliability-management audience. If a suitable research panel and budget are available, compare awareness before and after exposure using a stated study design. Otherwise use verified relevant reach and buyer interviews as provisional indicators, explicitly acknowledging that reach does not establish awareness lift.

**Creator selection:** Evaluate a reliability engineer who explains inspection decisions and a maintenance trainer who demonstrates diagnostic tools. Review content accuracy, relevant professional responses, audience evidence, and genuine interest. Ask each to identify a misconception they would challenge. Avoid writing customer testimonials for people who have not used the product.

**Concept:** Two creator-led videos unpack a mistaken maintenance assumption. One demonstrates why a single unusual reading needs context. The other teaches how to prioritize inspections across machines. Each integrates BearingBridge's verified dashboard role where it helps the explanation. Use a low-friction next step to explore the inspection guide; demo requests are secondary diagnostics.

**Sample improved opening:** "An unusual reading is a reason to investigate. Here are the three checks I would make before escalating it." The creator chooses their own defensible checks. Neither the brand nor this package invents technical advice on their behalf.

**Budget:** Allocate EUR 4,000 to creator work and agreed usage, EUR 1,000 to production, and EUR 7,000 to paid distribution. Target relevant industrial functions and markets, with feasibility checked before launch. Use the same audience criteria where possible across assets.

**Hypothetical learning:** Creator A receives EUR 4,200 in media and 420 clicks. Creator B receives EUR 2,800 and 350 clicks. A has more total clicks, but media CPC is EUR 10 for A and EUR 8 for B. Neither figure proves greater trust or awareness. Examine audience delivery, substantive buyer responses, and the primary awareness evidence before reallocating. Equalizing future exposure can clarify the comparison.

**Next decision:** Expand the stronger awareness concept only if relevance and primary evidence support it. If the correct audience sees the content but interviews reveal no recognition, revise the product connection and creative idea. If the audience is wrong, correct distribution first.

### Rules that made the difference

One objective prevented a forced demo pitch. Category expertise and verified claims protected credibility. Human video turned a slogan into professional teaching. Media context corrected a misleading raw ranking. The evaluation separated measured awareness from proxies. See method sections 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, and 10. [S1] [S2] [S3] [S7]
