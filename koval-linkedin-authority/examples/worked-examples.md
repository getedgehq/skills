## Illustration 1: Reply then connect, supported by a client journey

### Situation

Mira runs an onboarding consultancy for small B2B software companies. Her fictional internal project record shows that one client reduced median time to first setup from 12 to 7 days over six weeks. She has permission in this scenario to share a redacted timeline. An operations lead, Sam, has replied to her email asking how she measures setup time.

### Weak first draft

Post: We get amazing results for SaaS companies. Here are five tips to improve onboarding. Like this post and book a call today.

Email response: Thanks. Our system guarantees better activation. Please connect with me, subscribe, and book a call so I can explain the metric.

Connection note: I help companies scale. Want a free consultation?

This draft hides the intervention, invents a guarantee, withholds the answer, and repeats a generic pitch across channels.

### Improved result

Profile direction: Describe the buyer as an operations lead at a B2B software company and the offer as diagnosing onboarding handoff delays. Feature a redacted project timeline instead of an unsupported revenue promise.

Illustrative post draft:

> Our client's median time to first setup was 12 days.
>
> The team thought it needed a bigger onboarding team. Reviewing the timeline showed something else: accounts waited between the kickoff call and assignment of a setup owner.
>
> We assigned the owner during kickoff and added a handoff check. Six weeks later, the median was 7 days. This measures first setup, not retention or revenue.
>
> The redacted timeline shows the change. I turned our handoff checks into a worksheet. Comment SETUP if you want it.

Email response to Sam:

> Hi Sam, we measured calendar days from account creation to the first completed setup event. We compared medians over the two review periods and checked for a change in customer mix. I can send the event definition if that helps your team.

Connection note after the human reply:

> Sam, good exchanging notes about setup metrics over email. Happy to connect here too.

Resource handoff: Deliver the worksheet requested through the chosen delivery path. If a newsletter is offered, describe it separately and let Sam choose whether to subscribe. Do not make a call or subscription a condition added after the resource offer.

Record: Route 1; trigger is Sam's human question; email answer drafted; connection request drafted; no send action performed; meeting invitation pending evidence of a relevant need.

### Rules that made the difference

- [S1]: An existing email exchange supplies the context for a connection, while the email remains relevant to the question.
- [S3]: Before state, mistaken diagnosis, actual changes, after state, documented resource, and visual proof make the journey concrete.
- [S4]: Firsthand project experience replaces generic advice.
- Adaptation: Metric scope, permission, subscription choice, and an explicit state prevent inflated claims and accidental handoffs.

Success is a qualified conversation or suitable next step, not the number of SETUP comments. No outcome is asserted for these invented drafts.

## Illustration 2: Accept then email, without assuming purchase intent

### Situation

Jon sells procurement workflow reviews to manufacturing operations directors. In this fictional scenario, Lea has accepted his LinkedIn connection. Her company has publicly announced a second production site. Jon has not observed her reading his content and has no prior email conversation.

### Weak first draft

Email: You accepted my request, so I know you're interested in our services and saw my case study. We can save you 30%. Book a call tomorrow.

LinkedIn DM: Just emailed you. Can you book that call now?

The draft turns acceptance into buying intent, invents content consumption and savings, and duplicates pressure across channels.

### Improved result

Selected route: Route 2 because acceptance is recorded. No reply prerequisite is needed.

Original illustrative email:

Subject: Purchase approvals for the second site

> Hi Lea, thanks for connecting on LinkedIn. I saw your company's announcement about the second production site.
>
> I review how manufacturers assign purchase approvals across sites. A useful first check is whether the same owner approves an order at both locations or the responsibility changes during handoff.
>
> Is that a workflow your team is reviewing, or is it already settled?

LinkedIn action: No simultaneous pitch. Keep the connection as a credibility surface and track the email response before deciding the next step.

Record: Acceptance verified; content views unknown; email address requires verification before sending; email drafted; route held if no usable address is available. A refusal ends the sales sequence. Lack of acceptance would have left this route pending before the email step.

### Rules that made the difference

- [S1]: Connection acceptance precedes a relevant email.
- [S1]: The channels perform different jobs instead of carrying identical messages.
- Adaptation: Acceptance is a relationship event, not evidence of content consumption or intent.
- Adaptation: The draft asks one question grounded in public context instead of making an unsupported outcome promise.

## Illustration 3: Email first, then connections for the highest-value 10%

### Situation

A fictional analytics consultancy has 127 unique candidate buyers. Seven have requested no contact and are removed before ranking. The remaining 120 are eligible for the campaign. The team has capacity for eight new connection invitations during this review period.

### Weak first draft

Send an email and connection request to all 127 today. Prioritize whoever liked the most posts. Tell everyone we can reproduce the revenue numbers in Koval's examples.

This ignores suppression, wastes invitations, confuses social activity with value, and borrows another person's outcome as proof.

### Improved result

Local ranking adaptation: Score verified fit out of 5, commercial problem value out of 3, and current relevance out of 2. Unknown attributes earn no assumed points and are flagged for research. Break score ties by stronger documented fit, then stable record ID. These weights are invented planning choices, not Koval's formula.

Allocation: N = 120. Top-decile allocation = floor(120 / 10) = 12 records. The twelve selected buyers receive relevant email outreach before any connection request. Three are already connected, leaving nine candidates for new invitations. Available capacity permits eight invitations now; the ninth stays queued. No lower-ranked records are promoted to use capacity.

Example selected record: Ana, a finance director overseeing a recently announced reporting-system consolidation, scores 9/10 with linked evidence. A founder who frequently likes posts but has no relevant reporting problem scores 3/10 and stays outside the decile.

Original illustrative email to Ana:

> Hi Ana, your company's reporting consolidation caught my attention. I help finance teams check whether metric definitions match across systems before migration. Would a short definition-audit worksheet be useful for that review?

Subsequent illustrative connection note:

> Ana, I sent a note about metric definitions during your reporting consolidation. I share practical audit observations here and would be glad to connect.

Overlap handling: If Ana replies before the request, use the reply context and record the handoff under route one. Send one invitation. A buyer outside the top decile who sends a genuine relevant reply can also enter route one; the decile restriction applies to route three's list allocation.

### Rules that made the difference

- [S1]: Email outreach precedes selective LinkedIn connections to high-value leads.
- [S1]: The selection is based on value, not an acceptance-rate target or popularity.
- Adaptation: An explicit eligible denominator, rounding choice, tie rule, and capacity cap make the allocation auditable.
- [S2] [S5]: Qualified conversations and commercial outcomes replace activity counts as the primary review focus.
