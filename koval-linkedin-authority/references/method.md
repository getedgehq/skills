# Method and implementation reference

## 1. Scope and evidence discipline

The public LinkedIn Authority System overview joins content, lead magnets, inbound conversations, and measurement into a commercial funnel. A separate post supplies three precise ways to combine LinkedIn and email. Another overview includes email capture and conversion. Together they support an authority-to-conversation workflow, not a complete reproduction of an unpublished course or private playbook. [S1] [S2] [S5]

Read citations as attribution to the specific public posts listed in sources.md. Sections explicitly called **Implementation adaptation** are this package's practical design choices. They must not be described as Koval's exact process. The posts disclose no universal posting frequency, follow-up interval, lead scoring formula, DM script, email nurture length, or guaranteed conversion rate. [S1] [S2] [S5]

Use the method for a defined B2B buyer and a credible offer. The output is a tailored plan, messages, and measurement rules. Do not fabricate firsthand experience or turn Koval's results into the user's evidence. Report his results only as attributed, unverified examples, when relevant. [S2] [S3] [S4] [S5]

## 2. Core principles

### Give the channels different functions

Koval associates LinkedIn success with trust and credibility, and email success with relevance and speed. A connection can add context after an email reply; acceptance can provide familiarity before an email; selective connections can concentrate scarce relationship capacity on valuable prospects. [S1]

The lesson is channel complementarity. It is not a license to broadcast identical pitches twice. A LinkedIn connection supplies a credible person and an ongoing content surface, while an email can address a specific commercial problem directly. [S1]

### Optimize the system for commercial movement

The overviews criticize heavy cold-DM activity, prolonged commenting, generic event or quotation posts, and buying writing tools as substitutes for qualified meetings. They favor lead magnets, authentic content, inbound conversion, and tracking that improves performance. [S2] [S5]

Do not interpret this criticism as a blanket prohibition on all outbound activity, comments, tools, or impressions. [S1] explicitly uses outbound email and connection requests. Reach is useful evidence of distribution; the mistake is treating it as proof of commercial success. [S1] [S2] [S5]

### Earn authority through specific experience

Koval favors sharing what happened in actual work over generic prescriptions or detached opinions. Experience is harder to substitute with a language model and provides distinctive material. [S4]

He also favors client journeys over unsupported result announcements: identify the before state, mistaken explanation, actual intervention, after state, and repeatable components, with visual evidence and a recognizable client where appropriate. [S3]

## 3. Offer and profile preparation

**Implementation adaptation:** Define the ideal customer profile, or ICP, as a buyer role, business situation, problem, ability to benefit, and commercially plausible offer. Establish exclusions and the next action before producing a list or a lead magnet.

Prepare a profile that helps the prospect understand who is speaking and why the offer is credible. Suggested fields are buyer-specific headline, problem and approach in the summary, substantiated proof in featured material, and one clear next action. This profile structure is an adaptation of the credibility principle, not a disclosed profile formula. [S1]

If proof is absent, use documented process observations or a transparent demonstration. Do not invent a client journey to meet a content format. If the profile and email describe different buyers or outcomes, repair that mismatch before adding channel volume.

## 4. The three channel routes

The following sequence directions are explicitly supported by [S1]. The gates and exception handling are implementation adaptations.

| Route | Ordered actions | Trigger for the handoff | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reply then connect | Send relevant email, receive a reply, then request a LinkedIn connection | A human reply that leaves a relationship open | Build on an existing small interaction with visible credibility |
| Accept then email | Request a connection, observe acceptance, then send relevant email | Recorded connection acceptance | Make the email follow recognition of the sender |
| Connect only the top 10% | Use email across an eligible list, reserve subsequent connection requests for its highest-value 10% | Documented rank in that list's top decile, after email outreach | Spend limited connection capacity on the most valuable relationships |

### Route 1: Reply then connect

Koval treats an email response as an initial commitment that makes connecting feel natural. The connection adds credibility and ongoing context. [S1]

**Implementation adaptation:** Continue handling the email question on email. A connection request may acknowledge the exchange without repeating the full offer. A genuine question, interest, or referral can be suitable context. An unsubscribe, hostile rejection, or explicit request to stop is not a warming signal. An automatic absence notice is not a human commitment. Record these separately and do not trigger a connection from them.

No reply means the route's connection trigger has not occurred. Choose any later email follow-up according to the user's existing sequence, or leave the record pending. Do not label an email open or inferred profile visit as a reply.

### Route 2: Accept then email

Koval's rationale is that accepting a connection exposes the prospect to the sender's profile and potentially content, reducing the unfamiliarity of the later email. He presents this as easier to receive than a cold LinkedIn DM. [S1]

**Implementation adaptation:** Acceptance confirms a connection, not content consumption, buying intent, or newsletter permission. Reference the connection modestly, then explain relevance to the prospect. Do not claim the recipient read a post unless they said so. If acceptance is pending, leave the handoff pending. An already connected prospect can enter the email step when the relationship is verified, without another invitation.

### Route 3: Email, then connections for the top 10%

Koval reserves connection requests for the highest-value portion of an email list because LinkedIn relationship capacity is constrained compared with email reach. The 10% refers to leads selected for connections in this route, not a 10% acceptance target or a segment of people who replied. [S1]

**Implementation adaptation:** Use this auditable selection procedure:

1. Name the cohort and freeze its review date. Remove duplicates, explicit stop requests, and clearly ineligible buyers before ranking.
2. Rank the remaining leads by documented buyer fit, value of the problem, purchasing capacity, and relevance of the offer. Define weights if scoring is useful; weights belong to the local plan.
3. Let N be the eligible cohort size. Use floor(N / 10) as a conservative invitation allocation. State this rounding choice explicitly. For N below 10, select none by default or obtain a stated small-cohort exception from the plan owner.
4. Break ties using a declared criterion such as stronger verified fit; do not expand the decile simply because several records tie.
5. Select the top-ranked records and show evidence for each. Cap new invitations by actual account capacity. Already connected top-decile records require no new invitation; do not promote lower-ranked leads merely to consume the unused allocation.
6. Preserve the order: email outreach first, then connections to selected leads. A reply is not required for route three; if one occurs, coordinate with route one and send only one invitation.

The top-decile rule is specific to this outbound allocation strategy. Do not impose it on every inbound lead or an active email relationship using route one. A route-three lead outside the decile can legitimately qualify for route one after a suitable reply, with the route change recorded.

[S1] mentions a numeric weekly invitation limit and describes email as highly scalable. Treat those as the author's statements at publication, not permanent limits or permission for unlimited sending. Use the user's known account capacity and delivery constraints; if unknown, leave volume unspecified rather than inventing a current limit.

## 5. Content and lead magnets

### Experience-led drafting

Start with a real observation, decision, conversation, or outcome. State what happened and what it taught the operator about the buyer's problem. Generic advice can explain the experience, but should not replace evidence. AI can organize and edit supplied facts while retaining the user's voice; the overviews promote authentic AI-assisted content without disclosing exact prompts. [S4] [S2] [S5]

### Client journey structure

Preserve Koval's public progression: specific before state; the client's mistaken belief; concrete changes made; specific after state; documented system offered to readers; visual evidence and client identity when available. The resource request can use a comment keyword. Avoid reducing this to a large result plus a sales link. [S3]

**Implementation adaptation:** Verify time periods and metric definitions, obtain permission for client assets, and show context that materially affects the result. Without permission, anonymize honestly. Without evidence, write a process post rather than a testimonial. Keep invented examples visibly labeled.

### Resource-to-email handoff

The overviews identify lead magnets that attract clients, DM conversion, and email capture and conversion as system components. They do not disclose the full collection flow or nurture schedule. [S2] [S5]

**Implementation adaptation:** Make the resource solve one bounded ICP problem that naturally connects to the paid offer. Specify request action, delivery path, information collected, and what happens next. If the plan adds ongoing email nurture, make that expectation explicit; a comment asking for a resource alone does not establish subscription permission.

An original nurture outline can deliver the resource, explain one implementation issue, provide relevant proof, and invite a suitable next step. The number and timing of emails are local choices. Do not identify this outline as Koval's unpublished email sequence.

### Three-stage content boundary

[S5] advertises a three-stage system but does not enumerate its full stages. **Implementation adaptation:** If a complete plan needs content categories, use attention from firsthand experience, credibility from documented methods and journeys, and action through a relevant resource or offer. These are useful organizing functions, not verified proprietary stage names or a prescribed posting ratio. Do not attribute additional details from unfetched profile excerpts.

## 6. Conversations, exceptions, and measurement

**Implementation adaptation:** A resource request is an observable interest in the resource, not automatically a qualified sales opportunity. Deliver what was requested, establish fit with a relevant question when needed, and invite a meeting when there is a problem the offer can address. Do not force a meeting to release an advertised free resource. The fetched posts advertise inbound scripts but do not supply their wording. [S2] [S5]

Keep one person-level record across channels with route, latest event, current relationship, email status, next action, and stop conditions. If routes overlap, use the latest real interaction and suppress duplicate invitations or parallel pitches. If the person asks for email only, respect that preference. If there is no usable email address, hold the email route or choose a user-approved alternative without pretending the specified handoff occurred.

Track distribution and commercial movement separately. Koval emphasizes qualified meetings and improving through KPIs, and contrasts impressions with revenue. [S2] [S5]

**Implementation adaptation:** A workable funnel includes relevant profile visits when available, resource requests, valid opt-ins, qualified conversations, meetings booked and held, opportunities, signed contract value, and cash collected. Define a qualified conversation in terms of buyer fit and a relevant problem, not just any response. Count a person once across channels. Record route and cohort to understand which handoff produced the result.

Acceptance rate uses accepted invitations divided by sent invitations within a stated window. Reply rate uses human replies divided by delivered emails, with positive replies tracked separately. State denominators and observation dates. Do not combine different cohorts or equate booked meetings with held meetings. Contract value and collected cash measure different things.

Review the first weak transition and its evidence before increasing activity: relevance if replies are poor, credibility if connections lack context, resource fit if requests are unqualified, and conversation handling if interested buyers do not advance. Posting frequency, review cadence, and numerical targets are chosen for the business, not promised by this public method.

## 7. Warnings and stopping rules

Koval warns against treating LinkedIn and email identically, spending connections on all leads, generic guru-style advice without lived experience, vague client-result posts, and optimizing activity or impressions without commercial outcomes. [S1] [S2] [S3] [S4] [S5]

**Implementation adaptation:** Stop a proposed handoff when its trigger is absent, identity is uncertain, proof is invented, the recipient has requested no contact, or sending is outside the user's granted scope. Return the missing fact or next permissible action. Never imply that connecting guarantees feed visibility, email acceptance, or a purchase. The method is a decision framework supported by public posts, not a guaranteed revenue system.
