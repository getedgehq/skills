# Worked examples

These are invented illustrations, not Marina Zayats's clients, actual Schaffensgeist projects, or reported outcomes. All first-person incidents and organizational facts below exist only within the fictional briefs. In real work, obtain and verify equivalent client input before drafting.

## Illustration 1: Industrial CEO seeking skilled applicants

### Fictional brief and input

Elena Braun leads a fictional industrial equipment company. She wants experienced technicians to consider joining. Her internal communication team can collect input weekly; she can join a review meeting every two weeks. Verified facts within this illustration: she previously worked in maintenance, attended a recent mentoring discussion, and approved describing a trainee's need for protected learning time without identifying anyone.

### Weak first draft

> Proud to lead a world-class organization driving innovation, sustainability, diversity, and excellence. Our people are our greatest asset. Join us as we revolutionize the future!

The audience, contribution, and proof are missing. The draft could belong to almost any executive. Its unsupported superlatives and broad theme list obscure why a technician should care.

### Improved positioning and strategy result

- Primary objective: Help experienced technicians understand whether the workplace supports learning and responsible work.
- Zielgruppe: Maintenance and production specialists considering a career move.
- Superkräfte-Portfolio: Maintenance experience, attention to operational constraints, and a habit of discussing tradeoffs with teams.
- Personal Branding Pitch: I lead an equipment business with a maintenance practitioner's perspective. I want technical people to see how we make room for learning and reliable work when production pressure rises.
- Core themes: Technical learning; responsible production decisions.
- Core message: Development needs protected working time, not just a promise in a recruitment brochure.
- Public boundary: No named trainee, private performance assessment, or unapproved safety detail.
- Channel decision: LinkedIn posts and relevant technician conversations, supported by the same message during recruitment events.

### Improved post draft

> A trainee asked me a question I could not answer with a recruitment brochure: When production gets busy, does learning time disappear?
>
> I used to work in maintenance. I know how easily the urgent job wins.
>
> In our mentoring discussion, I asked us to treat planned learning time as a production decision, with a named owner and an explicit reason whenever it changes.
>
> That is the standard I want us to work toward. A development promise should survive a difficult Tuesday, not just sound good in an interview.
>
> What helps your team protect time for technical learning?

### Editorial and review result

The team prepares a mentoring draft and a second draft on an operational tradeoff. In the two-week CEO Redaktionssitzung, Elena checks the anecdote, confirms the wording describes a desired standard rather than a completed company-wide policy, and approves version 2. The internal owner publishes that approved version. Elena receives proposed response points and participates in the resulting conversation. No account credentials go to the adviser.

Suggested measurement: Record relevant applicant enquiries and questions about learning conditions. Mark recruitment attribution unknown unless the applicant or recruiting team provides evidence. No results are asserted by this illustration.

### Rules that made the difference

The roadmap connects supported strengths, goals, and stakeholders before messaging. [S4] The CEO process establishes positioning and strategy before editorial execution, with two-week sign-off and no external takeover. [S1] Agency guidance focuses themes on a specific audience and rejects treating the personal account as recycled corporate publicity. [S8] The supported anecdote and relevant discussion question implement the input and writing approach. [S7]

## Illustration 2: Founder moving from recruitment software to workforce planning

### Fictional brief and input

Samir Khan founded a fictional recruitment-software business and now advises operations leaders on workforce planning. The account's history centers on hiring. Within this illustration, he has approved sharing that software adoption discussions exposed gaps in role design and training time. He has no evidence for claiming that he has transformed dozens of organizations. He prefers written explanations to personal video and keeps family life private.

### Weak first draft

> I am now a leading workforce transformation visionary. Follow me for recruitment, AI, wellbeing, leadership, climate, finance, entrepreneurship, and my personal journey. Big changes are coming!

The draft asserts unverified authority, gives the audience too many associations, and does not explain why his previous work connects to his new direction.

### Improved positioning and roadmap result

- Primary goal: Build conversations with operations leaders deciding how to improve capacity before opening more vacancies.
- Supported strength: First-hand experience of recruitment-software adoption and the operational questions it surfaced.
- Personal Branding Pitch: I help operations leaders ask better workforce-planning questions before adding another vacancy. My recruitment-software background taught me to look at role design and learning time alongside hiring.
- Narrative connection: The work has expanded from filling roles to examining why organizations need those roles and whether people can learn them.
- Core themes: Workforce assumptions; practical skill development.
- Channels: Written LinkedIn explanations and small industry discussions. Video and private family stories are unnecessary.
- Next actions: Refresh the profile, publish a transition explanation, and revisit the new message through useful examples over subsequent cycles.

### Improved transition post draft

> I used to begin workforce conversations with the vacancy.
>
> While helping teams adopt recruitment software, I kept hearing questions the software could not answer: Had the role changed? Could an existing colleague learn the work? Was there time to train them?
>
> Those questions are now the focus of my work with operations leaders.
>
> I will still talk about hiring. I will also examine the decisions that come before it: role design, skills, and the assumptions behind a headcount request.
>
> Before opening the next vacancy, which of those questions does your team need to answer?

### Review and exception result

The strategy packet marks the revised pitch proposed until Samir reviews it. The editorial packet separates the transition post from later topical drafts. When he misses the scheduled two-week review, the team holds unapproved drafts and arranges a replacement meeting. At that meeting he approves the transition post and requests an example for the skills draft. The agent does not treat the missed meeting as consent.

Suggested measurement: Track conversations with relevant operations leaders, invitations to workforce-planning discussions, and enquiries that mention the new positioning. Reach remains a supporting indicator. Compare signals over time rather than claiming the rebrand succeeded after one announcement.

### Rules that made the difference

The draft uses real strengths and an audience-linked pitch rather than inventing a future reputation as an existing achievement. [S4] Zayats's focus post supports a small message set and repeated explanations when roles change; the podcast description corroborates a coherent transition narrative. [S10] [S13] Context-sensitive personality and privacy choices allow a written, professional presence without personal disclosure. [S9] Approval states and the missed-meeting handling are package implementation conventions that preserve CEO sign-off. [S1]
