# CEO branding deliverable template

Use this template for a positioning and editorial package. Bracketed fields are instructions to replace. Leave missing facts explicitly unresolved. This layout, its approval states, and its record fields are implementation conventions; the sourced method is explained in [../references/method.md](../references/method.md).

## 1. Brief and evidence status

- Executive and current role: [Name, role, organization, and effective date.]
- Requested scope: [New positioning, refresh, profile strategy, editorial cycle, or combination.]
- Inputs available: [Interviews, voice notes, profile, existing strategy, approved facts, stakeholder feedback.]
- Important gaps: [Missing input, consequence for drafting, and person who can resolve it.]
- Evidence legend: [Confirmed client fact, public source, assumption, or proposal.]
- Publication boundary: [CEO or designated internal publisher. The agent has no account takeover role.]

Guidance: Public method citations establish process principles. They do not verify this executive's biography. Link client claims to client evidence without unnecessarily exposing confidential material.

## 2. Positioning session record

- Primary company objective: [Desired effect, stakeholder decision, and observable signal.]
- Supporting company objective: [Include only if compatible with the primary objective.]
- Personal objective: [Career or influence goal. Mark confidential if appropriate.]
- Priority Zielgruppe: [Specific stakeholder group, current problem, and why it matters.]
- Secondary audience: [Optional; state how it changes examples or channels.]
- Desired association: [What the audience should remember about the person.]
- Current impression: [What the audit and outside feedback actually suggest.]
- Gap to close: [Difference between current impression and desired association.]

Guidance: Replace abstract aspirations such as visibility with a useful consequence. Keep confidential personal motivations in a restricted working record rather than automatically including them in the circulated paper.

## 3. Superkräfte-Portfolio

| Strength or resource | Evidence and feedback | Demonstrated, emerging, or aspirational | Audience value | Public permission |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Capability, knowledge, experience, personality, or resource] | [Specific event, artifact, or feedback] | [Status] | [Problem it helps address] | [Approved, restricted, or unresolved] |

Guidance: List only strengths that influence positioning. Avoid turning aspirations into achievements. Capture outside feedback as well as the executive's own view.

## 4. Positionspapier

- Personal Branding Pitch: [In two or three plain sentences, connect the priority audience, useful contribution, supported strengths, and distinct perspective. This length is a template convention.]
- Narrative connection: [Why the selected themes belong together in this person's role and story.]
- Core values and public facets: [Values, behaviors that demonstrate them, and personality facets suited to this setting.]
- Private boundaries: [Subjects, people, and experiences excluded from public use.]
- Voice guidance: [Vocabulary, sentence style, degree of formality, recognizable expressions, and examples approved by the executive.]
- Differentiation: [Concrete perspective that separates this contribution from generic leadership commentary.]
- Topics outside scope: [Unrelated interests, unsupported expertise, and restricted subjects.]

| Core theme, normally two or three | Message or position | Audience question | Supporting proof | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Specific subject] | [What the CEO wants people to understand] | [Real question] | [Approved experience or evidence] | [What must not be claimed or shared] |

Guidance: A theme names the subject. A message states the point. Write the strategy before filling a posting calendar.

## 6. Profile and presence recommendations

- Headline direction: [Role plus recognizable contribution, grounded in current evidence.]
- About section draft: [Pitch, relevant proof, selected themes, and an appropriate way to connect.]
- Visual direction: [Appearance consistent with the desired impression and the person.]
- Analog alignment: [Speaking, camera, meeting, or interview behavior that should convey the same positioning.]
- Gaps requiring client action: [Missing approved photograph, factual confirmation, or training need.]

Guidance: Provide drafts and recommendations for review. Do not independently edit a live account.

## 8. Draft content packet

### Draft [identifier and version]

- Audience question: [What the reader needs to understand.]
- Message: [Single point this draft communicates.]
- Evidence: [Approved anecdote or supporting material; mark gaps.]
- Opening: [A relevant and truthful reason to keep reading.]
- Body: [Clear explanation in the executive's recognizable voice.]
- Takeaway: [Something useful to understand or apply.]
- Optional invitation: [Natural discussion question or next step, only if suitable.]
- Visual: [Purpose, caption, source or ownership, and review status.]
- Review note: [Questions the CEO must answer before sign-off.]

Guidance: Do not invent first-person experiences or add performance claims for persuasive effect. Labels such as hypothetical or proposed belong outside any publish-ready draft when unresolved content remains.

## 9. Publication and community handoff

- Approved item and version: [Exact text and asset identifiers.]
- CEO approval: [Person, date, and recorded decision.]
- Publication owner: [CEO or explicitly designated internal owner.]
- Relevant conversations: [Accounts, questions, or discussions connected to the positioning.]
- Suggested contributions: [Useful draft comments for human review.]
- Response ownership: [Who handles routine questions and who reviews sensitive replies.]
- Follow-up window: [Agreed time to read responses and identify new editorial input.]

Guidance: Hand off content and response suggestions. Do not collect passwords or assume authority to post or message on the executive's behalf.
