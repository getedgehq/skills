## Roadmap foundations

The public diagram brings together three foundations: Superkräfte-Portfolio, Ziele, and Zielgruppe. Their intersection leads to Positionierung, expressed through a Personal Branding Pitch. The roadmap then connects Themen & Botschaften, themes and messages, to Content and Kanäle, channels. It includes both digital and analog visibility. Content and channel fit must be considered together, rather than turning any fashionable platform into an automatic destination. [S4] Figure 1, printed p. X.

Apply these components as follows:

| Component | Practical discovery | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Superkräfte-Portfolio | Capabilities, knowledge, experience, personality, and other relevant resources | Select strengths that are real and useful to the audience. |
| Ziele | Career direction and desired professional effects | State what visibility is intended to make possible. |
| Zielgruppe | People who benefit from those strengths and matter to the goals | Describe whose problem or decision the brand addresses. |
| Positionierung and Personal Branding Pitch | Concise expression of the overlap | Make the person's contribution and distinct perspective recognizable. |
| Themes and messages | Subject areas plus what the person believes or recommends within each | Connect them to positioning rather than accumulating unrelated interests. |
| Content | Suitable ways to express those messages | Package the argument so the audience can understand and use it. |
| Channels | Appropriate digital and analog settings | Choose for the person, content, and audience together. |

These are paraphrased components, not a reproduction of the diagram's artwork or wording. The preface emphasizes reflection, real attributes, selecting manageable visibility options, and combining outside feedback with self-understanding. It does not require an extroverted personality or sacrificing privacy. [S4] Printed pp. VII-X.

**Implementation convention:** Maintain a strengths register with the claim, supporting incident or artifact, stakeholder feedback, and permission to share. Tag each item as demonstrated, emerging, or aspirational. Only demonstrated facts can support confident achievement claims; an emerging capability can be discussed honestly as learning.

## Positioning session

Start with intended company outcomes, such as employer attractiveness, reputation, customer relationships, or change communication. Establish the CEO's personal objectives too. Agency guidance notes that some personal motives may be disclosed more readily to an external adviser than to employees; preserve agreed confidentiality rather than publishing the interview notes. [S1] [S8]

Audit how the person already appears, which channels they use, and whether the impression matches lived experience. Obtain outside feedback because seniority can reduce access to candid responses. Identify the person's concerns, story, and Superkräfte instead of treating the account as another distributor for company announcements. Choose priority stakeholders; trying to address everyone produces vague messages. [S8]

Useful discovery prompts, offered here as implementation conventions:

- What decision should the right stakeholder feel better equipped to make after encountering you?
- Which experience can you discuss in detail without borrowing someone else's expertise?
- What would employees recognize in your communication, and what would surprise them for the wrong reason?
- What do you want people to associate with you after several months of consistent communication?
- Which topics, people, and experiences must stay outside the public narrative?

If goals conflict, make the tradeoff explicit. A recruitment objective might prioritize employee experience; a customer objective might prioritize industry decisions. Choose a primary objective and a supporting objective instead of requiring every post to achieve everything. This prioritization rule is an implementation convention.

## Strategy session

Agree on core subjects, communication guidelines, messages, a Positionspapier, and the intended profile presentation. This follows the positioning session rather than replacing it with an immediate batch of posts. [S1]

Use two or three core themes as the default focus. Agency guidance recommends topics at a level appropriate to the executive's knowledge and role. A CEO can discuss a strategic operational tradeoff without impersonating a technical specialist. Distinguish a theme, such as industrial skills, from a message, such as protecting mentoring time when production pressure rises. [S8]

The pitch and profile should articulate a recognizable present contribution. The agency's newsletter retrospective treats a good pitch as clear, personal, and grounded in value, while the personal-branding service connects it to selected channels and relevant contacts. [S6] [S11]

**Implementation convention:** The Positionspapier should contain objectives, audience priorities, supported strengths, the pitch, themes, core messages, supporting proof, voice examples, public boundaries, profile direction, channels, and editorial governance. Record open strategic decisions separately from approved choices.

## Voice, boundaries, and audience service

Digital and live impressions should be closely aligned. Experience and anecdotes help supported writing stay near the executive rather than becoming an invented persona. [S1]

Alignment permits context-sensitive expression. Zayats's authenticity post asks which personality facets belong in which setting, what stays private, and which values should show through. Reflection and honest feedback matter; rude behavior is not justified by claiming authenticity. [S9]

Public communication can feel personal through an opinion, encounter, or professional lesson without exposing private life. The newsletter retrospective makes this distinction explicitly. [S11]

Center audience usefulness. Zayats's interview warns against self-promotion without a takeaway, copied formulas, buzzwords, and replacing substance with algorithm tactics. A clearer explanation of a real audience problem beats a clever but empty performance. [S3]

**Implementation convention:** Ask whether the executive could explain and defend the draft in a live conversation. If not, verify the claim, interview again, or remove it. Preserve recognizable phrasing when useful, but do not invent catchphrases or imitate someone else's successful post.

## Editorial execution

Use recurring Redaktionssitzungen with internal communication to keep planned topics connected to the CEO. The OMR account specifies CEO participation every two weeks for content approval. This is an approval cadence, not a requirement to publish only once every two weeks. [S1]

The current agency ghostwriting page describes capturing ideas through conversation, voice or written messages, and structured interviews. Writing follows strategy and input capture. It calls for a relevant opening, understandable structure, an appropriate voice, useful visual treatment, and an optional call to action. It also describes monitoring discussions where the client's participation would be useful. [S7]

**Implementation convention:** Prepare each meeting with candidate topics, recent stakeholder questions, source notes, drafts, and previous results. Ask the CEO to confirm the experience, argument, wording, and public boundaries. Keep an idea store for future posts and a decision log for approved, revise, and hold outcomes.

For each draft record the version, approver, approval date, and approved assets. Changed factual claims or substantive edits require renewed approval. If the CEO misses the two-week meeting, hold unapproved items and arrange a replacement review. Previously approved material may proceed through its agreed internal publication process if it remains accurate and appropriate.

A weekly post can be a manageable starting point in the current ghostwriting offer, but capacity and relevance should govern the actual rhythm. Do not turn Zayats's own professional output into a mandatory client workload. [S7] [S3]

## Account control and community work

The OMR interview explicitly says the agency does not take the CEO's credentials and independently run the profile. Its rationale is closeness to the person's experiences and voice. Later agency guidance discusses internally managed accounts and favors hybrid support involving the CEO, ghostwriter, and communication team. This package retains the requested no-account-takeover boundary. [S1] [S8]

Drafting, review, and research can be delegated. The CEO or an explicitly designated internal owner controls publication. Do not request credentials or independently post, send messages, accept connections, or impersonate the executive. Preparing an approval packet does not authorize those external actions. These agent execution rules implement the no-takeover boundary.

Publication does not finish the work. Train the executive and communication team to respond to comments; suggest relevant conversations and contributions. The agency guidance describes hands-on executive participation, supported by internal communication when volume or criticism rises. Treat its suggested daily time as an example, not a universal requirement. [S1] [S8]

## Measurement and iteration

Connect review to the agreed objectives. Agency sources identify qualified enquiries, applicants, speaking invitations, partnerships, and relevant audience growth alongside reach and engagement. The newsletter retrospective rejects one universal success threshold because goals differ. [S7] [S11]

Use the Christian Fuchs case as an illustration: agency reporting connects a consistent series featuring colleagues with recruitment and speaking outcomes. Those are reported results from one corporate-influencer context, not guaranteed CEO-branding performance or a requirement to copy its series. Avoid importing its platform timing claims as durable method rules. [S12]

**Implementation convention:** Set a review date, an initial baseline, a small set of goal-linked indicators, and a way to record how enquiries arose. Classify attribution as reported, corroborated, or unknown. Evaluate content relevance and process reliability before increasing volume.

## Edge cases and warnings

| Situation | Action and rationale |
| --- | --- |
| No real anecdote available | Ask for an observation or use a clearly framed general explanation. Never manufacture first-person experience. This implements the proximity principle in [S1] and real attributes in [S4]. |
| Executive wants complete detachment | Explain the required input and approval commitment. Offer a smaller realistic scope and hold the workflow if meaningful participation is unavailable. [S1] [S8] |
| Introversion or privacy concern | Select a comfortable format and professional facets; family disclosure and stage performance are optional. [S4] [S9] |
| Many interests or a portfolio career | Connect selected topics through one intelligible contribution; defer unrelated ideas. [S3] [S10] |
| New role or topic direction | Explain the transition and repeat the revised narrative over time. Do not assume one announcement updates the audience's perception. [S10] [S13] |
| Employer departure | Revisit company-dependent claims and preserve a contribution grounded in the person. Agency discussion notes that a brand built solely on company news is fragile. [S11] |
| Sensitive announcement or crisis | Route the draft through the client's existing communication and specialist review before approval; pause stale routine content when necessary. This is an implementation convention informed by the agency's warning about consequences of poorly considered executive statements. [S8] |
| Generic promotion or borrowed persona | Return to the supported contribution, audience problem, and recognizable voice. Rewrite with concrete substance instead of copying a formula. [S1] [S3] |
| Demand for fast virality | Reconfirm the business objective and horizon. Focus and repeated messages build recognition over years; a viral result is not a positioning strategy. [S10] |

Do not attribute every warning in the OMR article to Zayats. Other interviewees' personas, performance claims, influencer offers, and criticisms are separate material. Likewise, case-study participants' personal routines are not the author's universal method. [S1] [S12]
