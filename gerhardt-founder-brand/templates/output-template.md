# Founder-brand package template

This is a practical deliverable template derived from the public method, not a Gerhardt-authored form. Use all sections for a strategy. For a single post, retain the audience, evidence, draft, and relevant open questions. Bracketed fields are instructions to replace, not facts to invent. Consult [method.md](../references/method.md) for the rules.

## 1. Assignment and evidence

- **Deliverable:** [Narrative, strategy, post, or series; specify scope and format.]
- **Audience:** [Customer role, business context, and recurring problem; avoid everyone.]
- **Product status:** [Available capabilities, planned capabilities, and important limits.]
- **Founder inputs:** [Interview, voice note, existing writing, or confirmed history; record provenance.]
- **Participation:** [How the founder supplies ideas and checks drafts; realistic time available.]
- **Open questions:** [Missing facts that change the story or promise; explain their effect.]

Guidance: Separate confirmed facts, assumptions, and unanswered questions. Use founder material before composing personal claims. [S1] [S2]

## 2. Expertise and founder narrative

**Expertise sentence:** For [specific audience], [founder] helps with [topic/problem], drawing on [relevant experience].

**Backstory:** [A specific experience relevant to the audience.]

**Problem encountered:** [What happened, to whom, and why it mattered.]

**Inadequate approach or villain:** [A process, assumption, or status quo the audience recognizes.]

**Decision to build:** [The factual connection between experience and company purpose.]

**Customer improvement:** [The better experience possible within the product's actual limits.]

**Narrative paragraph:** [Connect the fields into a concise story; do not add invented drama.]

Guidance: Keep human texture when natural, but make relevant credibility carry the argument. [S2] [S3]

## 3. Beliefs and alignment

Repeat this block for two or three substantive beliefs:

- **Belief:** [An actual founder position that changes a customer decision.]
- **Supporting observation:** [Specific experience or verified evidence, with provenance.]
- **Audience implication:** [What the customer should reconsider or try.]
- **Product connection:** [How the company supports the position; a teaching point need not include a pitch.]
- **Claim limits:** [Where the claim stops; whether any capability is planned.]

Guidance: If no supporting observation exists, return to the interview rather than polishing a generic opinion. Product behavior must support the company promise. [S1]

## 4. Distribution and audience benefit

- **Existing assets:** [Founder following, customer newsletter, event reach, or other relevant distribution.]
- **Initial channel:** [One primary route and the evidence that intended customers use it.]
- **Format:** [Writing, conversation, solo lesson, or speaking; explain founder fit.]
- **Audience benefit:** [What people learn or accomplish even if they never buy.]
- **Series promise, if needed:** [A repeatable benefit, not simply company news.]
- **Expansion condition:** [What audience signal or capacity would justify another format.]

Guidance: Begin with available ingredients. Do not require a fixed sequence or a hosted community. [S1] [S2] [S5]

## 5. Founder-voiced starter content

Repeat for each requested draft:

- **Topic and belief:** [Connect to the niche and one position above.]
- **Underlying material:** [Locate the founder input supporting the draft.]
- **Opening observation:** [The specific thing noticed or learned.]
- **Context:** [Enough detail to make the observation understandable and credible.]
- **Useful implication:** [A customer action, distinction, or question.]
- **Finished draft:** [Natural founder language adapted to the chosen format.]
- **Review notes:** [Facts to verify, voice uncertainty, and claim limits.]

Guidance: Editing and AI may help structure the material; they must not supply a fictional founder history or generic substitute personality. [S4]
