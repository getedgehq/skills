# Zero-click deliverable template

Use the fields that fit the request. For one post, deliver a compact brief, final draft, optional next action, and review note. For a campaign, include distribution, derivatives, and measurement. For an audit, place the existing draft, diagnosis, and revision in the draft section. This field structure is a package adaptation of the public method, not a template attributed to Natividad.

Replace bracketed fields before delivery. Keep internal reasoning separate from publishable copy. Source IDs support method decisions; citations for substantive claims in the marketing itself must come from the user's actual evidence, not from these marketing-method articles.

## 1. Audience and impact brief

- **Requested deliverable:** [Post, newsletter, promotion, sequence, or audit.] State the format and quantity the user requested.
- **Audience:** [Role, situation, and relevant knowledge.] Identify who needs the insight, rather than a broad demographic alone. [S4]
- **Audience change:** [What the person should understand, do, recognize, or remember.] Choose one primary effect the artifact can plausibly create. [S7]
- **Business contribution:** [Specific business purpose.] Explain how that effect could support the organization; avoid a generic revenue claim. [S3]
- **Constraints:** [Length, tone, channel, timing, approved claims, required action.] Preserve the assignment's requirements.
- **Program premise, if recurring:** [Subject, distinctive viewpoint, reason to subscribe.] Make this an editorial quality standard. [S3]

## 2. Trigger and evidence

- **Audience trigger:** [Question, friction, misconception, decision, or change.]
- **Evidence:** [Supplied observation or public source, date, and relevant context.] Explain what it shows. [S6]
- **Evidence status:** [Observed, inferred, or assumed.] Separate the factual basis from interpretation.
- **Validation:** [Existing validation or proposed small check.] Use an audience question, social test, search interest, or competitive gap. [S6]
- **Selection rationale:** [Why this idea is worth doing now.] Consider need, business relevance, gap, and lasting usefulness. [S6]

## 3. Destination and complete insight

- **Channel and native format:** [Where the audience encounters it and in what form.]
- **Channel rationale:** [Evidence of audience presence or labeled assumption.] Explain the fit before selecting a source asset. [S4]
- **Complete insight:** [The useful conclusion, demonstration, or recommendation delivered locally.] Write the answer rather than a promise to reveal it. [S1]
- **Treatment:** [Compact argument, substantive bullets, or emotion-grounded opening.] Pick the structure suited to the idea. [S1]
- **Essential context:** [Definitions, evidence boundaries, prerequisites, or exceptions.] Include anything whose absence would make the native insight misleading.

## 4. Ready-to-use draft

[Write the entire publishable artifact here, including its opening, explanation or demonstration, resolution, and optional next action. For email, include a subject line and complete body. For video copy, include the spoken or visible explanation needed for local comprehension. Do not leave the actual insight in the brief alone.] [S1] [S3]

**Audit only:** [Identify the failed rule in the existing draft, then supply the repaired version.]

## 5. Optional next action

- **Action:** [Read further, subscribe, register, reply, or explore the offer.]
- **Additional benefit:** [What the next experience adds beyond this complete insight.]
- **Route:** [Approved URL, profile destination, or contact path.] Keep it easy to find. An absent URL in the user's inputs is a missing detail, not permission to invent one. [S2]
- **Program cadence, if needed:** [How useful native pieces and explicit asks fit together.] Treat suggested value-to-ask ratios as heuristics. [S5]

## 6. Derivatives and sequence, if requested

| Destination | Complete local takeaway | Native treatment | Additional depth or action | Sequence rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Channel] | [Answer provided here] | [Form] | [Optional extension] | [Why this comes now] |

Use a row for each meaningful derivative. Do not copy one headline and URL into every channel. Build understanding progressively and respect audience format preferences. [S4] [S6]

## 7. Measurement and learning, if requested

- **Primary business indicator:** [Outcome linked to the specific purpose.]
- **Audience indicators:** [Relevant reach, replies, sharing, growth, or comprehension evidence.]
- **Reuse indicator:** [Number of meaningful channel applications, if relevant.] [S5]
- **Baseline and review period:** [Available comparison period and planned review date.]
- **Confounders and limits:** [Seasonality, concurrent campaigns, missing referrals, and unavailable data.] State what the evidence cannot establish. [S2]
- **Learning question:** [What decision will the review inform?]
- **Feedback capture:** [Where new questions or misunderstandings will be recorded for the next cycle.] [S6]

## 8. Review note

- **Link-removal result:** [State the value that remains when all invitations and URLs are removed.] [S1]
- **Checklist result:** [Pass, or failed items with repairs.] Use [review-checklist.md](../checklists/review-checklist.md).
- **Remaining assumptions:** [Only material uncertainty requiring attention.]
