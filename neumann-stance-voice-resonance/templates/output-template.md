# Stance, Voice, Resonance deliverable

This is an agent-adapted template, not a reproduction of Builderz's internal documents. Replace brackets with supported information. Mark unknowns explicitly. Omit fields unnecessary for the brief; never fill gaps with invented proof. Preserve section names when delivering a full system.

## 1. Brief and status

- Subject: [Name, role, organization]. Identify whose judgment and voice the work represents.
- Requested scope: [Strategy, one post, weekly batch, distribution plan, or review]. State the actual assignment.
- Intended audience: [Role, market, decision context]. Specify who needs this judgment.
- Business purpose: [Useful conversation or recognized expertise sought]. Avoid follower growth as the only purpose.
- Current perception and gap: [Observed baseline, evidence, and unknowns]. Separate facts from hypotheses.
- Materials received: [Interview notes, samples, profile, approved records]. Identify dates and provenance.
- Status: [Provisional, reviewed, awaiting subject approval, or approved]. Distinguish editorial readiness from permission to publish.

## 2. Stance

Positioning sentence: For [audience facing decision], [subject] is the person to ask about [market question], because [supported experience or judgment].

- Audience tension: [Decision, risk, or trade-off]. Make it recognizable to the reader.
- Distinct judgment: [What the subject recommends and why]. Include material conditions.
- Evidence: [Source IDs from the subject's evidence inventory]. Do not use method sources as proof of the subject's achievements.
- Boundaries: [Claims, topics, or audiences outside scope]. Explain what the position cannot credibly cover.
- Profile recommendations: [Headline, About, Featured changes]. Show how each supports the position.

## 3. Topic architecture

Repeat this block for each useful pillar:

- Market question: [A repeatable decision question]. Avoid a broad category without a decision.
- Why it matters: [Reader consequence]. Connect it to the primary audience.
- Subject's judgment: [Defensible answer]. Record conditions and alternatives.
- Proof available: [Situation, number, or decision with source]. Mark permission to disclose.
- Next angles: [Distinct supported follow-ups]. Do not generate new experiences from the same thin anecdote.

## 4. Voice card

- Observed traits: [Rhythm, vocabulary, directness, reasoning style]. Link each trait to a sample.
- Preferred expressions: [Subject-owned wording]. Preserve recognizable speech naturally.
- Rejected expressions: [Words or claims the subject would not use]. Include learned corrections.
- Stance boundaries: [What the subject does and does not believe]. Prevent stylistic editing from changing beliefs.
- Provisional assumptions: [Traits needing calibration]. Keep them separate from observations.

## 6. Draft cards

Repeat for each draft:

- Topic and audience: [Pillar and reader decision]. Show strategic fit.
- Core judgment: [One defensible point]. Avoid several disconnected lessons.
- Support: [Evidence IDs]. Trace material factual claims.
- Draft: [Complete post text]. Use a structure suited to the material.
- Reader value: [Decision the reader can improve]. Make value independent of buying.
- Uncertainties and disclosure changes: [Outstanding issue]. Resolve before release.
- Subject corrections: [Edits and learning]. Update the voice card where appropriate.
- Approval: [Who approved which version, when, or pending]. No approval inferred from silence.
