# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations. Every person, company, situation, figure, and sample sentence below is fictional. These are package adaptations for teaching the method, not Builderz cases, client testimonials, or attributed Neumann quotations. Their drafting structures are optional.

## Illustration 1: A manufacturing executive with generic posts

### Invented input

Mara runs a precision-parts manufacturer. She wants procurement leaders at equipment manufacturers to consult her before accepting fragile delivery promises. Her interview describes a recent order: an incoming drawing revision left a fixture unvalidated. She delayed the commitment until engineering and production resolved the risk. The shipment then met the revised date. She has permission to discuss the process without identifying the customer. No cost savings were calculated. Her observed voice is calm, specific, and skeptical of grand claims.

### Weak first draft

> Leadership is all about innovation and customer centricity. At our company, we always go the extra mile to deliver excellence. Proud of our amazing team for another successful project! Get in touch if you need a reliable manufacturing partner.

This could belong to almost any business. It gives procurement no decision rule and supplies no evidence of Mara's judgment.

### Improved Stance and topic

Position: Mara helps procurement leaders distinguish a defensible delivery commitment from a date unsupported by production readiness.

Repeatable topic question: What must be verified before promising a ship date after a drawing change?

Profile alignment: Replace a generic leadership headline with a clear manufacturing role and delivery-risk focus. Feature an approved process explanation rather than an invented performance claim.

### Improved post

> We changed a promised delivery date before the order reached production.
>
> A drawing revision had changed the part. The fixture still matched the previous version.
>
> I asked engineering and production to resolve that mismatch before we confirmed the new date. We shipped on the date we then agreed.
>
> Keeping the original promise would have meant treating an unvalidated fixture as ready.
>
> When a specification changes, I want procurement to ask one extra question: what assumption behind this delivery date has changed too?
>
> A date becomes useful when the work behind it is credible.

### Resonance and review

Propose a comment on a relevant procurement discussion explaining which readiness assumption to check. Answer technical follow-ups within Mara's knowledge and offer a conversation only when requested or appropriately authorized. Track qualified profile interest and inquiries that reference delivery judgment, not just reactions.

Rules that made the difference: Stance selected a concrete buyer decision; the evidence inventory supplied a real choice within the illustration; Voice removed generic enthusiasm; the Proof Post principle connected the claim to a situation. No financial result was added. The review gate still requires Mara's approval before publication.

## Illustration 2: A specialist tempted by provocation

### Invented input

Jonas is an independent data-migration specialist serving finance leaders at mid-sized distributors. His notes describe a test migration where total record counts matched, but twelve customer balances were allocated to the wrong accounts. The team paused acceptance and fixed the mapping. Jonas distinguishes completeness checks from correctness checks. His voice is analytical, concise, and willing to state limits. The fictional client approved anonymized discussion of this test; there is no evidence that all large consultancies mishandle migrations.

### Weak first draft

> Big consultancies are destroying ERP projects. Most migrations are a disaster, and companies waste millions because they trust the wrong experts. Hire someone who actually knows what they are doing. Agree?

The accusation exceeds the evidence, changes Jonas's stance, and seeks broad anger rather than helping a finance leader assess acceptance.

### Improved Stance and topic

Position: Jonas helps finance leaders decide which reconciliation evidence is needed before accepting a migration.

Topic question: What does a matching record count fail to prove?

### Improved post

> The record count matched. Twelve customer balances were still on the wrong accounts.
>
> In a test migration I reviewed, the first check asked whether every record had arrived.
>
> That check passed.
>
> The account-level reconciliation asked whether each balance belonged where it had landed. It found the mapping error. We paused acceptance and corrected it.
>
> I want both checks before I recommend acceptance. Completeness cannot stand in for correctness.
>
> The exact reconciliation depends on the data and its use. For customer balances, the total alone is not enough.
>
> What evidence does your acceptance checklist require beyond the count?

### Resonance and review

Prepare a substantive response for an ERP acceptance discussion: explain the difference between count checks and account-level reconciliation. Do not tag unrelated firms or turn the example into an accusation. A relevant finance reader's question about acceptance is more useful than unrelated viral attention.

Rules that made the difference: Niche depth replaced obligatory provocation; evidence limited the claim to the test described; the person's conditional judgment survived editing; the closing question serves the buyer decision. Relevant inquiries can test whether the position resonates, while a future technical question supplies material for the next conversation. This example does not promise reach or leads.
