# Detailed method

Based on Emily Kramer's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Emily Kramer.

## Reading and attribution

This is an operational synthesis of the public sources listed in [sources.md](sources.md). Framework definitions and expert warnings have citations. Evidence registers, readiness gates, explicit experiment thresholds, and the template layout are package implementation choices. They support the method without claiming to reproduce MKT1's subscription templates.

The central principle is continuity: audience research and company economics inform strategy; strategy informs the work portfolio; briefs connect that portfolio to production and distribution; measurement informs the next decision. Marketing should function as a multidisciplinary builder of durable growth, with a roadmap and foundational investment, rather than merely fulfilling sales requests. [S1] [S4]

## 1. Scope and strategy drivers

Start with the three drivers: audience and market research, business model and GTM motion, and marketing advantages. A channel can work for one company and fail for another because contract size, buying process, product maturity, and customer workflow differ. Company-specific inputs should explain the recommendation before tactics enter the discussion. [S1]

Capture:

- Decision and horizon: annual strategy, quarterly allocation, one campaign, content roadmap, or team design.
- Audience evidence: interviews, customer behavior, objections, buying roles, and information sources.
- Economics and motion: self-serve or sales-led purchase, typical contract value, time to value, purchase cycle, and expansion route.
- Baseline and constraints: present initiatives, lifecycle conversion, available people, spend, tools, and launch dependencies.

As a package convention, label inputs as observed, inferred, or unknown. Tie important facts to user-provided records; [S#] references substantiate the method, not facts about the startup. If research is missing, propose a provisional direction and the evidence required to choose confidently.

Scope rule: summarize existing strategy for a small brief. Do not create an annual plan just to answer a narrow campaign request. Conversely, do not call a list of channels and lead targets an annual strategy. A real plan includes the work that can change growth, operational prerequisites, and a credible forecast. [S4]

## 2. Audience and ecosystem

Define the total addressable market, then narrow to ideal customer profiles. Include both company-level fit and the people involved in purchasing or using the product. Record relevant characteristics, workflows, unmet needs, decision criteria, budget, influence, and sources of trust. Personas that stop at titles or demographic labels do not provide enough information to guide marketing. [S2]

Distinguish the core ICP, scaling ICPs with supporting evidence, testing ICPs needing validation, and audiences deliberately deprioritized. These are investment decisions, not a claim that other people can never buy. A new audience should earn investment through fit and learning, rather than receive an equal share automatically. [S2] [S5]

Map the ecosystem beyond competitors. Include integrations, communities, service providers, influential practitioners, complementary products, and potential partners. A player may be a partner in one workflow and a competitive threat in another. Evaluate audience overlap, credibility, and the value each side would receive from collaboration. [S2]

Practical decision rule: choose an engine only after identifying a credible route to this audience. If a partner's distribution is merely hoped for, describe it as a dependency to test, not an established advantage.

## 3. Marketing advantages

An advantage is a favorable dynamic in the product, business, team, or story that marketing can accelerate. It must create a growth mechanism that the company can activate differently or more effectively than alternatives. Generic product quality and a competent team alone do not establish an advantage. [S1] [S3]

Useful candidates from the public method include:

- Product sharing, network effects, a useful free plan, or a wedge that helps entry into adjacent markets.
- Integrations, co-marketing, channel partners, and market or regulatory urgency.
- Underserved demand for education or community, credible founder relationships, or a defensible category story. [S3]

For each candidate, document the evidence, growth mechanism, activation investment, limitations, and whether it already exists or must be built. An integration is not automatically distribution. A free trial is stronger when users can reach value and the purchase trigger makes sense. A wedge fails as an advantage if every expansion requires starting over. Channel partners need incentives and enablement. [S3]

Kramer explicitly cautions against forcing a founder story or category creation. Founder credibility requires a founder who can and wants to tell the story. A substantially improved version of an existing product does not automatically justify the cost of introducing a new category. Some advantages can be developed through product or GTM changes; natural fit matters more than a fashionable label. [S3]

When no clear advantage exists, identify a product, business, or GTM hypothesis to investigate. Do not invent one merely to complete the dashboard. [S1] [S3]

## 4. Revenue levers

Rank the four routes to revenue growth for the current quarter:

1. More customers in the core audience.
2. Customers from a new audience.
3. Greater customer value through expansion, pricing, or customer mix.
4. Greater efficiency through better conversion, lower acquisition cost, or less wasted effort. [S1]

Explain the ranking using saturation, customer economics, conversion constraints, product readiness, and available capacity. Marketing may support a pricing or product change without owning the decision. Obtain cross-functional agreement on dependencies rather than claiming unilateral control.

Optional effort allocations should total 100 percent of the planning capacity being allocated. Treat them as a planning hypothesis, not a permanent identity. Check that the portfolio actually reflects the ranking: an efficiency-first plan dominated by net-new traffic purchases is inconsistent unless the rationale demonstrates otherwise. [S5]

The expert warning is against defaulting to more top-of-funnel volume forever. As the market saturates, conversion and customer value can become more useful routes to growth. [S1]

## 5. Positioning and story

Positioning condenses audience, market, and product research into who the product is for, what it is, and its most meaningful differentiation relative to a clearly chosen alternative. An alternative may be a competitor, a manual process, or another way of solving the problem. Where several comparisons are plausible, examine them before choosing the one that makes the product's value most legible to buyers. The homepage hero should be able to convey the three core elements. [S2]

Keep the current positioning separate from a future vision. Describe what buyers can obtain today, while explaining aspirations elsewhere. A long positioning document that cannot produce a plain statement is unfinished. [S2]

The Story Stack connects brand, company story, product story, and positioning. Brand covers what people think and feel about the company. Story includes mission, vision, and the product narrative; positioning is one component of that product narrative. Content should also address audience problems, market insight, and the company's point of view. The public guide's illustrative product-content proportion is adjustable, not a mandatory quota. [S2]

Perceptions are three to five beliefs the audience should repeat about the company. Gather raw inputs across founders, marketing, product, sales, and customer success; marketing synthesizes the final set. Write from the audience perspective, cover both company and product story, and check whether competitors could say the same thing. [S2]

Content pillars organize subject matter. Perceptions establish the beliefs running through those subjects. A pillar such as security is not itself a differentiating Perception. Reconsider major work that supports none of the selected narratives. [S2] [S6]

## 6. Decision dashboard and KPO goals

Keep a compact, current Marketing Decision Dashboard containing prioritized ICPs, advantages, Perceptions, ranked revenue levers, and high-level goals. Repeat these fields in roadmaps and briefs so the annual strategy influences daily choices. Format matters less than continued use. [S1] [S5]

Set three goal types:

| Type | What it captures | Review evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KPI | Business or lifecycle outcome | Baseline, target, measurement definition, date |
| Project | High-impact initiative or milestone | Useful completed scope and completion date |
| Ops | Research, hiring, tooling, analytics, or other foundation | Readiness condition and accountable owner |

Goals should be measurable and time-bound, with the reason for each goal, supporting strategy, risks, dependencies, and non-goals. A project completion is not proof of business impact; a KPI does not explain how the team will achieve it. Account for both. [S1] [S4]

If the KPI baseline is unknown, make measurement setup an Ops commitment and mark the outcome target provisional. This is an implementation convention to avoid false precision.

## 7. Portfolio prioritization

Inventory existing initiatives before adding work. Decide which to stop, maintain, or scale. Kramer warns against continuously adding activities and spending all the team's attention repairing weak programs while neglecting strong ones. [S1] [S5]

Organize the portfolio into big bets, core work, Ops and foundation, and not now. A deferred idea should retain its reason and, where useful, the condition that would make it worth reconsidering. Categories may change when the scope changes; they are aids to decisions, not fixed labels. [S5]

Attach strategic metadata first: ICP, Perception, revenue lever, advantage, primary engine, and relevant goal. Then size effort, impact, cash cost, maturity, and owner requirements. Avoid wasting estimation time on work already misaligned with the strategy. Compare similar work within categories rather than pretending a tracking repair and a major launch compete on one perfectly objective score. [S5]

Balance proven work with tests, current results with future growth, quick wins with big bets, and coverage with depth. The public prioritization article suggests giving depth substantial capacity, roughly comparable to breadth combined. Its illustrative bucket allocations are starting points, not universal staffing mandates. The practical requirement is to show that the plan fits actual capacity and deepens its best opportunities. [S5]

Urgency is not strategic justification. A founder's exciting tactic or an internal request still needs a fit with audience, priorities, and evidence. When a better opportunity appears, explicitly replace lower-priority work instead of silently adding it. [S1] [S8]

## 8. Fuel, engine, and lifecycle

Fuel is what the audience reads, hears, or sees: messaging, content, creative, and useful assets. Engine covers the channels, distribution processes, tools, and metrics that deliver it. Plan them together. Distribution capacity should shape asset creation; audience needs should shape both. [S7]

When content performs poorly, inspect whether the intended audience encountered it and had an appropriate next action before commissioning replacement content. Likewise, a sophisticated automation system cannot compensate for irrelevant creative. Hire and plan against the actual imbalance. [S7]

Compare six engine families: inbound, outbound, product virality, events, ecosystem, and lifecycle. Self-serve and sales-led motions suggest different starting mixes, but neither is an automatic channel prescription. Test before scaling, concentrate enough effort to learn, and consider mutually reinforcing channels. Check cross-channel effects before discontinuing a channel. Kramer rejects copying fashionable channels and excessive dependence on a single engine. [S2]

For account-driven GTM, tier accounts by fit, value, and intent. Give stronger-fit accounts appropriate higher-touch treatment, with playbooks and signals matched to tier. Track companies and contacts separately: company progression and an individual's readiness are related but distinct. Define qualification signals, next actions, owners, and handoffs, then measure stage volume, conversion, and elapsed time. [S2]

Kramer explicitly discourages team-branded qualification stages because they encourage territorial ownership. Prefer customer-oriented stages agreed across GTM teams. Existing CRM labels need not be changed merely to write a plan; describe the proposed mapping and rules if implementation is outside scope. [S2]

## 9. GACCS, content, and Mileage

Write the brief before producing the asset. The older GACC article describes four components; the newer Field Guide adds Stakeholders. Use the five-part version here: Goals, Audience, Creative, Channels, Stakeholders. The two Cs identify creative/fuel and channels/engine, regardless of the order displayed in a source. [S1] [S5] [S6]

Start with a compact alignment brief, then expand into production details. Use the agreed brief as the review rubric. Identify the goal, specific audience, useful and distinct creative contribution, distribution and follow-up, and accountable collaborators. Briefing should improve choices, rather than add paperwork after decisions are already made. [S1] [S6]

Treat content as a product: it should solve an audience problem and have a recognizable value proposition. Prefer differentiated anchor assets, recurring series, tools, or templates to a quota of interchangeable posts. Map a custom journey from discovery to consumption to the next useful action, including relevant nurture, sales, or onboarding follow-up. [S2] [S7]

Mileage means extending the value of research and assets through both repurposing and distribution. Repurposing generates related formats; distribution gets them consumed. Give each derivative its own audience-context, route to reach, and next step. Plan reuse upfront and schedule revisiting successful older work, rather than forgetting it after publication. Repetition of strong Perceptions is useful. [S2]

For early-stage learning, the next action may be another resource, an event, or a useful diagnostic. Do not force every interaction into a demo request. The action should fit the audience's readiness and the campaign objective. [S2] [S7]

## 10. Team capacity and forecast

Each campaign has one directly responsible individual coordinating creative, distribution, handoffs, and feedback. Campaign discipline applies to product launches, events, content launches, and demand programs because each combines fuel and engine. Reassess quarterly campaign priorities monthly and deepen successful campaigns instead of assuming novelty is required. [S8]

Assess coverage across product marketing as foundation, content/brand as fuel, and growth marketing as engine. For early teams, Kramer favors marketers with depth in two areas and working knowledge of the third. Where coordination is the constraint, a marketing producer should work across the functions and report to the marketing leader. Fit hiring to the gap; the framework does not require three departments in a tiny startup. [S1]

When revenue or budget is in scope, build a bottom-up forecast with finance from reachable accounts or traffic, historical conversions, deal value, and expected timing. Factor in planned changes only with explicit assumptions. Compare it with the top-down target and iterate on budget and activities. Choose acquisition efficiency measures suited to the business, rather than blindly using a percentage of revenue. [S2] [S4]

Implementation checks: use consistent time periods, distinguish pipeline from booked revenue, avoid counting the same account twice across engines, and show uncertainty around untested lifts. Sales-cycle delays may put revenue outside the campaign window. If the forecast falls short, show the shortfall and the changes needed; never fabricate a conversion improvement to satisfy the target.

## Edge cases and completion

| Situation | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| One-person team | Keep all three functions visible, combine roles, and reduce concurrent commitments. [S1] |
| No historical conversions | Mark forecast assumptions unvalidated; fund instrumentation and a bounded test before scale. Implementation choice informed by [S2]. |
| Multiple products or ICPs | Prioritize deliberately and check narrative and capacity coverage; do not divide effort equally by default. [S5] |
| Sudden market opportunity | Reassess fit, replace lower-priority work, and keep an owner and brief. [S8] |
| Asset is good but results weak | Inspect distribution, lifecycle friction, and measurement before remaking the asset. [S7] |
| Existing campaign succeeds | Increase reach, reinforce the story, or adapt the asset rather than chase novelty. [S2] [S8] |
| Requested channel lacks strategic fit | Explain the mismatch, defer it, or define a small test answering the key uncertainty. [S1] [S5] |
