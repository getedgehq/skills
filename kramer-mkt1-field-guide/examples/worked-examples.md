# Worked examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. Company facts, numbers, dates, and targets are fictional teaching inputs, not evidence of actual performance or promises. Citations explain the rules applied. The examples use the package's own deliverable format.

## Illustration 1: LedgerNest quarterly efficiency plan

### Situation

LedgerNest sells close-management software to finance teams at growing SaaS companies. In this illustration, interviews show controllers want practical ways to diagnose close bottlenecks. Two accounting partners have agreed to a small co-marketing pilot. Forty engaged core-ICP accounts are stalled before discovery. The team has one marketer and fractional design support, with six campaign days available this month after core work.

### Weak first draft

> Publish four generic finance blog posts every week, start a founder podcast, run ads to finance professionals, and host a webinar. Target 500 leads and a 30 percent pipeline increase this quarter. Repurpose the webinar into clips. Sales will follow up when leads look qualified.

The draft supplies volume without a mechanism, treats all finance roles as one audience, and claims a pipeline lift without a baseline. It adds channels without capacity estimates and gives clips no distribution or useful next step.

### Improved result

**Decision:** Spend this quarter improving progression among engaged core-ICP accounts, then expand acquisition through the confirmed partner pilot. Defer the podcast because it consumes the same capacity without an evidenced audience or distribution advantage.

**Dashboard:** Core ICP is controllers at growing SaaS companies with a multi-person close process; CFOs are stakeholders, not interchangeable targets. The advantage is an evidenced need for close education plus two partners with audience overlap. Position LedgerNest as close-management software for lean SaaS finance teams that helps expose ownership and workflow bottlenecks relative to spreadsheet coordination. A campaign Perception is: LedgerNest helps lean finance teams diagnose close delays before adding headcount. A full quarterly strategy would include a broader set of three to five Perceptions. **Revenue-lever allocation:** Efficiency first, 50 percent; core acquisition second, 30 percent; customer value third, 15 percent; new ICP acquisition fourth, 5 percent for research. These fictional allocations describe intent and must be checked against the actual work portfolio. [S1] [S5]

**KPO commitments:**

- KPI: By quarter-end, progress 12 of the 40 currently engaged accounts to a held discovery meeting, versus the fictional prior-quarter baseline of eight. Treat the increase as a test target, not a forecast guarantee.
- Project: Launch one close-readiness diagnostic and two partner workshops by the end of week four.
- Ops: Agree account qualification, routing, and follow-up rules with sales before the first workshop, and validate tracking with test records. [S4]

**GACCS brief:** The goal is qualified-account progression, with diagnostic completions and workshop participation as leading signals. The audience is controllers already aware of the product. The creative is a practical diagnostic with guidance on ownership gaps, handoff delays, and avoidable rework. Channels are confirmed partner newsletters, workshops, and a lifecycle sequence to relevant engaged accounts. The marketer is DRI; partners own invitations, design support produces the diagnostic, and a named sales lead owns requested consultations. [S1] [S7] [S8]

**Capacity and Mileage:** Use two days for the diagnostic, one for partner coordination, one for workshop preparation, one for routing and measurement, and one for distribution/review. Each workshop yields a checklist for partner newsletters and a short problem-specific clip for existing email subscribers. Each format links to the diagnostic or another useful resource; only contacts requesting advice enter the consultation follow-up. Revisit the anchor asset in six weeks. [S2]

**Learning:** Review partner reach and qualified-account progression monthly. If reach is low, inspect partner execution before remaking content. If accounts consume the diagnostic but stall, examine the next action and handoff. Expand only after the pilot demonstrates relevant engagement and the team can support follow-up. [S7] [S8]

### Rules that made the difference

The revenue-lever ranking displaced a top-of-funnel volume target with work on the actual bottleneck. ICP and advantage evidence justified the creative and partner engine. KPO goals made tracking and routing visible commitments. A single DRI and explicit capacity estimate turned a wish list into a deliverable plan. Mileage paired every derivative with distribution. ## Illustration 2: SignalDock campaign for a self-serve developer product

### Situation

SignalDock is an invented API monitoring tool with a self-serve free plan. Users can reach a useful diagnosis within a session, but the marketer does not know whether new signups activate. The technical founder is respected among existing users but does not want to become a regular social creator. A successful integration tutorial exists; no partner has yet committed to promote it. Leadership requests a category launch and a revenue forecast.

### Weak first draft

> Create the category of Autonomous Reliability Intelligence. Have the founder post daily, buy a conference booth, and publish 20 SEO articles. Increase signup-to-paid conversion to 10 percent so 2,000 new signups produce 200 customers this month. Hire a content specialist. Use the integration tutorial everywhere.

The draft forces a category and founder advantage, treats a forecast assumption as a fact, ignores time to paid conversion, and assumes a new hire solves an undiagnosed bottleneck.

### Improved result

**Decision:** Run a four-week activation campaign for the core self-serve audience before expanding acquisition. Preserve the successful tutorial, investigate lifecycle friction, and test one ecosystem route. Defer category creation, the booth, and broad SEO production. [S1] [S3] [S5]

**Strategic context:** Core users are backend engineers responsible for diagnosing failed API requests. Position SignalDock as an API monitoring tool that helps these engineers trace failure causes relative to checking disconnected logs manually. The relevant Perception is that engineers can obtain a useful diagnosis without building their own monitoring workflow. The free plan is a candidate advantage because it allows value before purchase; its activation and upgrade triggers need validation. Founder credibility is useful for occasional technical review, but is not a dependable publishing engine in this scenario. [S2] [S3]

**KPO commitments:** Instrument first diagnostic completion by the end of week one. Refresh the anchor tutorial and launch one behavior-based onboarding sequence by week three. Report activation and time to first value by week four; set a paid-conversion improvement target only after the baseline is trustworthy. The sequence is a Project commitment, instrumentation an Ops commitment, and activation the KPI under investigation. [S4] [S7]

**GACCS brief:** Goal is helping eligible trial users reach their first useful diagnosis. Audience is newly signed-up backend engineers who have not completed setup. Creative is an example-driven walkthrough and troubleshooting checklist based on recurring setup questions. Channels combine the in-product setup entry point with permission-appropriate onboarding email; the integration community is a small distribution test pending confirmation. The marketer is DRI, an engineer checks technical accuracy, product implements event tracking, and support owns unresolved setup questions. [S1] [S2] [S7]

**Mileage and capacity:** Adapt the tutorial into a concise setup email, an in-product help excerpt, and a community-specific answer. Each has its own distribution owner and returns users to the relevant setup step. Use a short ops engagement to establish instrumentation before committing to a specialist hire; reassess whether the remaining gap is fuel production or engine operation. [S1] [S2]

**Forecast:** Suppose the fictional reachable cohort is 500 signups and historical paid conversion, once validated, is 4 percent. That yields 20 expected paid customers over the observed conversion window, not automatically this month. At an illustrative monthly price of $100, the cohort contributes $2,000 in new MRR once converted. A leadership request for $5,000 would require 50 paid customers, equivalent to 1,250 signups at the same conversion rate. Show the reach and timing gap. Keep any activation-driven uplift as an unvalidated scenario until results support it. [S2]

**Learning:** After four weeks, compare first-value completion and support friction with the established baseline. If activation improves but paid conversion does not, investigate purchase triggers and readiness. Scale the reinforcing routes that reach the right users; do not assume every signup source contributes independent demand. [S2] [S7]

### Rules that made the difference

The advantage test removed forced category creation and an unavailable founder engine. Fuel and lifecycle were designed together, and a focused test reused proven work. KPO goals exposed missing measurement. Bottom-up forecasting surfaced the revenue gap instead of filling it with an invented conversion rate. Capability assessment tied resourcing to the observed constraint. [S1] [S2] [S3] [S4] [S7]
