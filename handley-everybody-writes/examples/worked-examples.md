# Worked examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. Businesses, facts, experiences, and draft copy below are teaching material, not testimonials or claims about real organizations. Source citations explain the applied method.

## Illustration 1: bicycle repair booking email

### Scenario and supplied facts

A neighborhood shop wants more weekday bookings from commuter cyclists. It services commuter bicycles and offers reservable weekday appointments. The owner's invented brief notes that riders worry about losing their transport to work. There is no evidence of same-day repairs or a guaranteed completion time.

### Weak first draft

Subject: Revolutionary booking is here!

We're excited to announce our cutting-edge booking platform. Our reliable, friendly team offers seamless weekday service that gets you back on the road the same day. We're proud to put customer satisfaction first. Book now and transform your commute!

### Diagnosis and working decisions

The draft starts with the business's excitement, offers generic voice attributes, and makes an unsupported turnaround promise. The reader needs to plan around being without their bicycle, not admire booking software. Reframing connects reservable appointments to planning a repair around a commute. The evidence supports scheduling, not repair completion. [S1] [S3]

A proposed voice is practical, neighborhood-minded, and unhurried. In sentences, that means naming the commute, offering direct help, and avoiding manufactured urgency. The cyclist plans; the shop supports that choice. [S4] [S5]

### Improved result

Subject: Plan your bike repair around your commute

Your bike gets you to work. When it needs attention, a little planning helps.

Reserve a weekday service appointment and tell us what needs fixing. Ask about repair timing when you book so you can plan your journeys while your bike is with us.

Choose your weekday appointment: [booking link]

### What changed and why

- The subject and opening deliver the same reader consequence: planning around a necessary repair. [S1] [S3]
- The copy removes same-day service and generic innovation claims because the brief cannot support them. This is the package's evidence-handling adaptation. [S1]
- Concrete, calm language makes the proposed voice observable instead of naming virtues that any competitor could claim. [S4]
- The ending gives a specific action instead of repeating the business's enthusiasm. [S1]

Review note: Replace the booking-link placeholder and confirm how the shop handles questions about repair timing. Until then, this is proposed copy with a clear operational gap.

## Illustration 2: founder story for an About page

### Scenario and supplied facts

An invented founder of a meeting-notes tool supplies this invented account: while coordinating a volunteer project, she used three documents to track decisions. One volunteer arrived with an old task list. The team spent their first ten minutes reconciling it. The tool now puts decisions and assigned actions in one shared note. No productivity study or customer outcome data is supplied.

The desired reader is a small-team coordinator comparing tools. The brand's proposed voice is observant, practical, and lightly wry. The page should connect a specific origin incident to the reader's task without claiming the product eliminates every coordination problem.

### Weak first draft

We are visionaries redefining the future of collaboration. Our founder has always been passionate about innovation and recognized a universal problem affecting every team. Our revolutionary platform guarantees perfect alignment and ten hours of weekly savings. Join the movement and unlock your team's full potential.

### Diagnosis and working decisions

The usable human detail has disappeared. The founder is presented as exceptional without showing anything meaningful; the reader is asked to admire a movement; the product makes unsupported guarantees. The structure needs the incident, the recognizable consequence, and the idea it revealed. [S1] [S6] [S7]

The working reader consequence is simple: make decisions and assigned actions easier to find in one place. The origin incident can illustrate the need, but cannot establish a universal time-saving claim. [S1] [S3]

### Improved result

Title: One meeting. Three documents. Two versions of the plan.

During a volunteer project, I kept decisions in one document and task lists in two others. A volunteer arrived with an older list. We spent the first ten minutes working out which plan we meant to follow.

If you've coordinated a small team, you may recognize the problem: a decision was made, but finding it is another task.

That morning changed what I wanted from a meeting note. It needed to hold the decision and the assigned action together, somewhere everyone could find them.

That's what our tool does: one shared note for decisions and assigned actions. It won't make the decisions for you. It gives your team a place to return to them.

See how a shared note works: [example link]

### What changed and why

- The writer becomes understandable through a specific, imperfect experience. The revision preserves supplied human thinking instead of replacing it with generic founder praise. [S6] [S7]
- The reader can recognize a situation without being told that every team has the same problem. The idea emerges from the incident rather than an unrelated lecture. [S6]
- Product language stays within the supplied capability. The ten-hour savings and perfect-alignment claims are removed. [S1]
- A lightly wry title expresses the proposed voice and accurately previews the story. The next action helps a comparing reader evaluate the product. [S1] [S4] [S8]

Review note: In real use, confirm the founder's wording and incident before presenting it in first person. Replace the example link and verify the described capability. These invented details must not be reused as an actual founder story.
