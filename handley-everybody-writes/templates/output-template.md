# Writing deliverable template

Use this as a working scaffold, then return the copy in the user's requested format. Keep internal reasoning outside the finished prose. Remove unused fields rather than forcing an email, landing page, or button into an article structure. The scaffold is an agent adaptation of the public writing sequence. [S1] [S2]

## Working brief

**Deliverable and constraints:** [Format, channel, length, deadline, required information]
Guidance: Record where the reader encounters the copy. Preserve supplied constraints; use a provisional assumption only when it does not materially change the work.

**Business purpose:** [What this piece should accomplish]
Guidance: Describe an outcome, not merely the production of content. [S1]

**One reader:** [Person's situation, knowledge, concern, and decision]
Guidance: Use a concrete audience situation without inventing biographical facts or assuming ignorance means low intelligence. [S1] [S2]

**Reader consequence:** [One plain sentence about why this matters]
Guidance: Question the business benefit until it reaches a meaningful, supportable consequence. Keep speculative stakes out of the promise. [S1] [S3]

**Human input:** [Notes, observation, perspective, draft, or missing experience]
Guidance: Identify the thought you must preserve. Mark a proposed angle as proposed; never generate purported lived experience as factual filler. [S7]

**Claim support:** [Claim | supporting fact or source | limitation]
Guidance: Repeat for material assertions. Separate illustrative anecdotes from measured outcomes. Narrow claims with insufficient support. [S1]

**Customer material, if used:** [Contributor | provenance | permission status | attribution]
Guidance: Record reuse permission separately from public availability. Do not present uncleared material as ready for republication. [S5]

**Voice choices:** [Established guide, or three provisional attributes with sentence examples]
Guidance: Include company and reader pronouns, useful specialist terms, and language to avoid. Define observable choices rather than generic virtues. [S4]

**Structure:** [Reader question -> useful answer -> support -> next step]
Guidance: Adapt the order to the task. For a story, note what it reveals about the writer, reader, and idea without making those labels compulsory headings. [S1] [S6]

## Finished copy

**Title, subject, or label:** [Selected wording]
Guidance: Promise what the body actually supplies. Omit this field when the requested format does not use it. [S1]

**Optional preview or subheading:** [Useful supporting line]
Guidance: Add information or orient the reader; do not merely repeat the title.

**Body:** [Final prose in the requested format]
Guidance: Lead with the reader's need, organize support coherently, and preserve meaningful voice. Use paragraphing and lists appropriate to the surface. Resolve rough-draft placeholders before calling this finished. [S1] [S2] [S4]

**Next action:** [Specific action or application, with verified destination if relevant]
Guidance: Match the action to what the piece enables. Use an explicit placeholder when a link is unavailable and flag it below. [S1] [S3]

## Review notes

**Status:** [Ready for requested use / Proposed draft with listed gaps]
Guidance: Describe the actual state, not an unsupported approval claim.

**Open items:** [Exact unsupported claim, missing link, experience check, or reuse permission]
Guidance: List only material gaps, and say what resolves each. Keep notes separate from the copy.

**Revision rationale, if useful:** [Reader consequence and one or two consequential changes]
Guidance: Explain decisions concisely; method citations can appear here when requested.

**Review performed:** [Substantive rewrite, voice review, mechanics, format check; independent editor if actually used]
Guidance: Self-review must not be described as independent editorial approval. [S1] [S2]
