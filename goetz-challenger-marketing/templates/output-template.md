# Challenger activation proposal

Use this template as the deliverable structure. Replace bracketed fields; preserve uncertainties as explicit assumptions. Do not invent source evidence, partner agreement or campaign results. Rule labels refer to [method.md](../references/method.md).

## 1. Recommendation

**Proposed activation:** [One sentence describing the idea, intended audience and product contribution.]

**Why this should work:** [A short causal explanation from product truth to attention, trial and business value. Avoid promising virality.]

**Decision needed:** [The specific choice the user must make, if any. Distinguish proposing an action from authority to execute it.]

## 2. Brief and constraints

- Brand and offer: [What is sold, at what price, through which channels.]
- Commercial outcome: [One primary objective and timeframe.]
- Audience and occasion: [Who is choosing, using or sharing the product, and when.]
- Incumbent advantage: [Reach, familiarity, distribution or another concrete strength.]
- Constraints: [Cash ceiling, team hours, available stock or service capacity, dates and geography.]
- Assumptions: [Missing information, validation owner and what depends on it.]

## 3. Product truth and category reversal

**Product difference:** [An experiential benefit with evidence. Apply P1.]

**Proof available:** [Tests, operational facts or documented customer experience; identify claim limitations.]

**Category convention:** [What competitors commonly do in the actual buying environment.]

**Proposed reversal:** [How the idea differs while retaining comprehension. Apply P2.]

**Existing surface:** [Packaging, checkout, receipt, shop or another touchpoint, with visibility and incremental cost. Apply P3.]

## 4. Cultural and partner rationale

**Community and shared ritual:** [A specific culture, occasion or reference, not a broad demographic label. Apply P4.]

**Reason to belong:** [What the brand contributes and what people get.]

**Partner options:** [Compare two or three plausible candidates on audience trust, co-creation, distribution, rights, cost and workload. Unknowns should stay unknown.]

**Recommended collaboration:** [Partner contribution + brand contribution + co-created product or experience. Apply P5.]

**Status and dependencies:** [Proposed, contacted or agreed; factual availability, licensing and capacity requirements.]

## 5. Creative execution

**Three original routes:** [Three different observations, each tied to the same product truth.]

**Selected headline:** [Welcoming humour with a clear product connection. Apply P8.]

**Plain benefit line:** [A substantiated explanation someone can understand without the cultural reference.]

**Purchase instruction:** [Accurate location, date, price or booking action.]

**Voice invariants:** [What remains recognisable across the pilot and follow-ups. Apply P6.]

## 7. Forciertes Glück response card

- Opportunity signal: [An observable event suggesting fit or demand.]
- Interpretation: [Why it matters; alternative explanation.]
- Next action: [One useful response and deadline.]
- Owner and authority: [Who decides and within which limit.]
- Ready assets: [Copy, contact, stock information and purchase path.]
- Capacity and reserve: [Maximum cash, hours and deliverable volume.]
- Stop condition: [What ends the response or requires revising it.]

This response card is a package adaptation of P7, not Götz's published form.

## 8. Measurement and decision

**Baseline:** [Comparable prior period, outlet or another feasible reference; identify confounders.]

**Attention:** [Qualified exposure, discussion or search evidence.]

**Demand:** [Relevant enquiries, retailer interest, bookings or trial.]

**Business:** [Paid purchases, contribution and repeat behaviour where observable.]

**Proposed thresholds:** [Numbers agreed for this brief, explicitly labelled as targets.]

**Scale / revise / stop:** [Separate conditions for each decision, including delivery quality and economics.]
