# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations. Brands, partners, prices, budgets and targets are hypothetical. They are not Gustavo Gusto campaigns or evidence of Michael Götz's results. Rule identifiers refer to [method.md](../references/method.md).

## Illustration 1: A bakery meets a tabletop gaming club

### Brief

Night Crumb sells baked-to-order bread through two local collection points. Its demonstrable difference is a crisp crust at collection, supported by staggered baking slots. It has EUR 600 cash and 12 staff hours for a two-week pilot. The desired outcome is 40 incremental paid orders with positive contribution after variable costs. Local evening gamers are a proposed audience, to be checked with the club organiser.

### Weak first draft

Buy a generic influencer post: "The best bread in town! A revolution in sustainability. Available everywhere. Order now before it disappears." Print a game character on the bags and promise a viral launch.

Problems: no substantiation for superiority or sustainability, no availability accuracy, no relationship to the influencer's audience, unconfirmed character rights, and no mechanism connecting attention with bread orders.

### Improved result

Co-create a Saturday session break with a local tabletop club. Offer preordered, shareable bread portions for collection at the nearby bakery before the session. The club contributes knowledge of the break ritual and distributes one invitation through its existing member channel. The bakery contributes fresh bread and a collection slot. Ask the organiser to confirm that food is welcome at the venue before scheduling.

Use original bag copy: "A crisp opening move." Add a plain factual line: "Baked for your 18:00 collection. Order by Friday 16:00." Keep ingredients and necessary product information visible. Use an original illustrated bread shape rather than licensed game art.

Allocate EUR 180 to bag labels and a prototype batch, EUR 120 to a small tasting, EUR 150 to partner hosting and EUR 150 as a reserve. Record 12 hours separately. These are proposed spending limits; the owner must verify supplier costs. Paid orders must cover their own variable product costs.

Run one collection window per week for two weeks. Compare paid orders with the average of the prior four comparable Saturdays, and tag club referrals with a simple optional order code. Review whether ordinary orders were displaced. Measure margin and collection reliability alongside referrals; do not call all tagged sales incremental.

Prepare for forciertes Glück: if a second club makes a qualified enquiry, the bakery owner may use up to EUR 100 of the reserve for another prototype, subject to baking capacity. If demand exceeds capacity, state the next available slot. Stop expansion if collection quality fails or variable contribution turns negative.

### Rules that changed the work

P1 replaced vague superiority with a verifiable fresh-baking benefit. P3 made the bag and collection page useful media. P4 and P5 turned a decorative cultural reference into a shared occasion and co-created experience. P7 supplied a bounded response to unexpected interest. P8 kept the humour welcoming and product-linked.

## Illustration 2: A repair shop collaborates with an amateur orchestra

### Brief

Second Turn repairs clothing in a town centre. Its product truth is a fitting appointment before alteration and a clearly scoped price quote. A local amateur orchestra needs practical fixes to performance clothing before a concert. The shop has EUR 300 cash, eight marketing hours and six spare fitting slots over ten days. The goal is four paid repair jobs from a suitable new audience while preserving service quality.

### Weak first draft

Launch a citywide billboard saying "Your clothes are embarrassing. Upgrade your look." Pay a famous musician to share it. Offer unlimited same-day repairs and announce a permanent orchestra partnership before discussing it with the group.

Problems: expensive reach, insulting tone, no link to the service difference, invented partnership and an impossible capacity promise.

### Improved result

Propose a small fitting evening with the orchestra organiser, timed before a rehearsal. The organiser identifies practical garment problems and invites interested players. The shop explains which repairs it can perform and offers six bookable fitting slots. The proposed partner has not agreed yet, so the plan remains a proposal until the organiser accepts.

Create an original appointment card: "For trousers that keep missing their cue." Follow with: "Book a fitting. We confirm the repair and price before work begins." Do not claim to improve musical performance. A short original demonstration uses a spare garment, with no recording of musicians or copyrighted repertoire required.

Budget EUR 60 for appointment cards, EUR 80 for demonstration materials, EUR 60 for a modest organiser fee if agreed, and EUR 100 reserve. Track eight hours separately and price repairs using the shop's usual economics. State the six-slot limit accurately, not as invented scarcity.

Track enquiries, bookings, completed paid jobs, contribution and whether agreed completion dates were met. The proposed learning question is whether the pre-rehearsal occasion attracts repair needs that the ordinary shop window misses. A successful pilot could justify a second evening, not automatically a permanent partnership.

Forciertes Glück trigger: another local performance group asks for help. The shop owner responds with a scoped fitting offer within two working days, using the reserve only if capacity and margin remain acceptable. If people ask for jobs outside the shop's competence, refer them honestly rather than turning the attention into promises it cannot keep.

### Rules that changed the work

P1 made the fitting and quote the promise. P2 challenged generic fashion advertising through a relevant occasion. P5 replaced celebrity reach with a partner's practical contribution. P6 limited the intervention to available capacity. P7 converted unexpected interest into a useful next action. P8 replaced customer mockery with affectionate situational humour.
