## 6. Production brief for each selected concept

- **Concept ID and working title:** [Link to the table row.] Keep identity stable across executions.
- **Persona and trigger:** [Who is in a buying situation and why now.] Give production enough context to choose credible scenes.
- **Idea:** [One or two sentences explaining the persuasive premise.] Make the buyer's reason to care explicit.
- **Awareness and belief:** [Existing knowledge; belief to reinforce or change.] Avoid assuming brand familiarity at a broad entry point.
- **Messaging option 1:** [Opening direction.] State whether it varies a hook or changes the angle.
- **Messaging option 2:** [Opening direction.] Preserve the intended test distinction.
- **Messaging option 3:** [Opening direction.] Do not call cosmetic differences new concepts.
- **Proof assets:** [Actual files, demonstrations, verified specifications, or authentic testimony.] List missing proof before production.
- **Objection to address:** [Specific uncertainty and its credible answer.] Match the answer to evidence.
- **Creator and credibility:** [Talent or speaker, why appropriate, availability.] Do not fabricate experience or endorsement.
- **Format and visual beats:** [Execution, setting, opening, demonstration or proof, close.] Explain how the form supports the angle.
- **Offer and call to action:** [Accurate offer and next action.] Match the landing destination.
- **Production requirements:** [Assets, practical limitations, owner and timing if known.] Flag unknown assignments.
- **Test question:** [What this execution should teach.] Note which variables change.

The persona, short idea, and three messaging options reflect Denney's roadmap structure. Additional proof and learning fields make the brief reviewable. [S3]

## 7. Roadmap and measurement

Guidance: Quarterly coverage, monthly selection, and weekly production are planning layers, not mandatory project durations. Keep unselected ideas in a backlog with rationale for deferral. Commercial measures identify performance; diagnostic measures help explain it. Record exposure, attribution window, offer, and delivery differences that limit comparison. Do not invent universal thresholds. [S2] [S3] [S6]
