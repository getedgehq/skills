# Worked Examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. Their brands, customer signals, assets, and outcomes are hypothetical. They demonstrate the method, not Denney's client work or expected performance. Source citations identify the rules applied, not evidence for the fictional products.

## Illustration 1: Refillable soap for a crowded bathroom

### Assignment and fictional evidence

A refillable hand-soap brand wants its next monthly concept plan. Its current ads emphasize sustainable living and attractive packaging. In this illustration, inspected customer reviews describe moving into small apartments, bulky replacement bottles, and crowded sink edges. Other reviews ask whether mixing the refill is inconvenient. The hypothetical product has a reusable dispenser and a compact refill pouch, and the team can film the real preparation process.

### Weak first draft

Persona: environmentally conscious women aged 25 to 40.

Concept: commission a renter creator, a product grid, and a carousel. All use the same broad claim about a better planet and offer a discount. Choose the format with the highest click-through rate.

This draft defines the audience demographically, mistakes executions for strategic variety, misses the observed buying trigger, and uses an engagement measure to declare commercial success. [S2] [S3] [S6]

### Improved result

**Evidence distinction:** The small-space pattern comes from the invented brand's own review sample. It is a research signal, not a claim that every renter has this problem. The refill objection is recorded separately.

**Buyer versus creative map:** Buyers describe storage and sink clutter after downsizing; current ads mostly invite identification with sustainable living.

**Diagnosis:** The account's current messaging emphasizes environmental identity while the inspected buyer sample describes a practical storage problem. Test a small-space convenience angle and address preparation uncertainty before commissioning more lifestyle variations. [S3]

| Field | Selected concept |
| --- | --- |
| Persona and trigger | A renter who has just moved into a smaller bathroom and needs to reduce sink and cupboard clutter. |
| Angle | Keep hand soap available without storing another bulky replacement bottle. |
| Messaging lenses | Convenience and calm as desires; renter context affects the demonstration. |
| Awareness | Problem aware: the buyer recognizes the clutter but may not know refill options. |
| Belief to address | Refills may seem like extra work with little practical benefit. |
| Proof | A truthful side-by-side storage demonstration using the actual pouch, replacement bottle, and cupboard. |
| Objection | Preparation effort and how much soap the pouch makes. |
| Creator and format | A credible renter demonstration, with a separate static storage comparison planned only if the angle merits extension. |
| Hypothesis | The storage angle will generate stronger purchase performance than the current environmental message under a comparable setup. |
| Priority | First exploratory concept because the review pattern aligns with a visible creative gap and available assets. |

**Production brief:** Show the crowded sink and storage cupboard, demonstrate preparation without hiding meaningful steps, then show the actual footprint of the refill pouch. Use three hook options: a small-bathroom observation, the inconvenience of storing replacement bottles, and the practical setup after moving. These are opening variations within the same angle, not three independent persona tests. [S3]

**Measurement and next decision:** Review purchases and cost per purchase against the account's target and current-message comparator. Use viewing and comments to investigate preparation confusion. Because the current ads and new demonstration may differ in execution, the first result evaluates a concept bundle; it does not isolate angle causality. If purchase evidence supports the concept, test its static expression. If comments repeatedly reveal an unclear preparation step, revise that proof before expanding.

### Rules that made the difference

- Trigger-based segmentation replaced a broad demographic and changed the creative premise. [S6]
- Buyer-versus-ad comparison produced a specific diagnosis rather than an arbitrary format list. [S3]
- The angle remained recognizable in both a demonstration and a possible static expression. [S2] [S7]
- Commercial outcomes identified performance; diagnostic signals explained potential friction. [S2]

## Illustration 2: A new commuter backpack without customer history

### Assignment and fictional evidence

A new backpack brand wants a first production roadmap but has no customers or account results. In this illustration, comparable backpacks' lower-rated public reviews complain about unpacking everything to retrieve a laptop during a commute. The new product has an accessible laptop compartment, demonstrated with a device of known dimensions. Other reviews mention strap discomfort, but the brand has no comfort testing or customer proof.

### Weak first draft

Persona: busy professionals.

Concept: a founder video saying the bag is the most comfortable backpack for every commuter, plus three fashionable edits copied from a competitor whose ads have run for months. Launch them all and keep whatever receives the most views.

The draft invents a superlative claim, treats competitor longevity as validation, ignores the specific access problem, and provides no distinct learning question. [S3] [S6]

### Improved result

**Evidence distinction:** Competitor reviews suggest a market need; they do not establish this brand's buyer profile. Label the persona and its priority provisional. Do not reuse the strap complaint as proof of the new product's comfort. [S3]

**Creative map:** There are no live ads. Instead, tag the planned founder concepts as general professional identity and note that there is no observed buyer-versus-ad gap yet.

**Diagnosis:** The provisional research suggests frustration with retrieving devices from crowded bags. Lead with an observable access demonstration, then use early customer feedback to decide whether this situation deserves a broader creative pillar. This is a research-led launch hypothesis, not an established account diagnosis.

| Field | Selected concept |
| --- | --- |
| Persona and trigger | A commuter who repeatedly needs a laptop during travel and dislikes unpacking the bag. |
| Angle | Reach the laptop compartment while keeping the rest of the packed bag in place. |
| Awareness | Solution aware: the buyer knows commuter backpacks but needs to assess the access design. |
| Customer signal | Repeated access complaints in the invented comparable-product review sample. |
| Proof | An uncut product demonstration with a stated compatible laptop size and normal packing. |
| Objection | Device fit and whether access still works when the bag is packed. |
| Creator and format | A demonstrator explaining the design in a realistic commute setting; a founder is acceptable if credible and available. |
| Hypothesis | The access premise will attract qualified purchase interest better than a general style premise. |
| Confidence | Provisional audience evidence, strong observable product proof, no account performance evidence. |
| Priority | A feasible first test that does not depend on unsupported comfort claims. |

**Three messaging options:** retrieving a laptop on a train, avoiding a full bag unpack at a workspace, and showing the compartment design. The first two vary the situation within the access premise; the third foregrounds the mechanism. If the team wants to test a separate comfort angle, it must first obtain appropriate proof and assign a distinct concept ID.

**Roadmap:** Plan an initial access-versus-style comparison within the approved production capacity. Record the lack of direct buyer evidence. Put device-fit reassurance and another access execution in the backlog. Revisit quarterly persona coverage after the brand has meaningful first-party feedback rather than filling a quarter with invented segments. [S3] [S6]

**Measurement and next decision:** Use the account's purchase objective and agreed exposure criteria; review product-page objections and comments about fit as diagnostics. If sales are too sparse, retain an inconclusive status and seek more direct customer evidence. Do not crown a winner based on views. A supported access angle can later move into a static compartment diagram or authentic customer demonstration. [S2] [S7]

### Rules that made the difference

- Lower-rated competitor research generated provisional hypotheses for a brand without customer history. [S3]
- A portable angle came before creator and format selection. [S2]
- Competitor visibility became inspiration rather than supposed performance proof. [S3] [S7]
- Package safeguards removed unsupported superiority claims and allowed an inconclusive result instead of false certainty.
