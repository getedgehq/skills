# The Method in Depth

Based on Dara Denney's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Dara Denney.

## Scope and attribution

This method turns customer evidence into a creative strategy, then into concepts and production briefs. Denney's public approach emphasizes research, content analysis, and persona coverage rather than selecting formats in isolation. The Motion profile summarizes that emphasis; the public talks and posts provide the working details. [S1] [S2] [S3]

Sections below paraphrase her guidance. Items explicitly labelled **Package safeguard** are implementation rules for making an agent's deliverable traceable and interpretable, not additional claims about what Denney said. The five awareness stages are presented as she uses them in public talks; this package does not reproduce book text. Source definitions and links appear in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Evidence before ideas

Gather the offer, positioning, actual product capabilities, customer reviews, comments, interviews, support questions, relevant social discussions, current ads, historical performance, and competitor creative. Denney describes creative strategy as the work preceding production and places research at its center. Avoid treating brainstorming as a substitute for understanding customers. [S3] [S4]

Read some material manually before asking AI to extract patterns. Denney warns that familiarity with the underlying market is what lets a strategist recognize misleading AI analysis. Use AI to organize evidence and assist briefing, then inspect and edit its output. [S3]

Capture each signal with a locator, the customer's situation, trigger, pain, desired outcome, objection, and confidence in the interpretation. Preserve differences between segments instead of collapsing all complaints into a generic inconvenience.

**Package safeguard:** Maintain separate labels for observed statements, interpretations, and untested hypotheses. Count frequency within the inspected sample, with its denominator and limitations. A review sample is not an estimate of the entire market. Do not turn a customer's belief into verified product efficacy.

For a new brand, Denney recommends comparable brands' lower-rated reviews, especially ratings from one to three stars, to identify unresolved needs. Treat the resulting personas as provisional and replace assumptions with the new brand's own feedback as it arrives. [S3]

## 2. Personas and coverage

A useful persona describes the event, change, or problem that puts someone into a buying state. Age, gender, role, and hobbies can enrich that situation, but cannot replace it. Denney explicitly warns against broad demographic personas when trigger-based segments would lead to different creative decisions. [S6]

Build two maps:

- **Buyer evidence:** Situations described in actual customer material, including motivations and objections.
- **Creative evidence:** People and situations implied by the premise, message, creator, imagery, and setting of current ads. [S2] [S3]

Compare these maps. An underserved buying trigger can be a better opportunity than producing another variation for the audience already represented. Rank buyer situations separately by frequency and emotional intensity; a smaller segment can warrant investigation when its problem is unusually urgent or emotionally charged. [S3]

**Package safeguard:** Explain emotional rankings using concrete stakes or customer language, not invented scores. Preserve overlap: one buyer may occupy several situations. An ad's implied audience is an interpretation, not proof of delivered demographics or purchase behavior. Mark ambiguous targeting unknown.

## 3. Diagnosis before the roadmap

A diagnosis explains why the current strategy needs a particular next test. Tie a customer need or belief to an observable coverage gap. For example: buyer evidence repeatedly describes storage difficulty, while current ads emphasize environmental identity; test space-saving convenience before commissioning more identity-led executions. This follows Denney's gap-to-diagnosis approach. [S3]

Reject diagnoses that could justify any set of ads, such as needing fresher creative. Name the underserved trigger, misunderstood objection, narrow awareness entry, or missing content pillar.

Denney also asks whether the account expands into new demand, maintains a coherent creative ecosystem for its strongest personas, and connects paid content pillars to landing pages, email, and creator content. Check those dependencies when they affect the proposed concept. [S8]

**Package safeguard:** Report funnel mismatches as findings within scope. Do not assume permission to rewrite other channels. If evidence contradicts the proposed gap, revise the diagnosis rather than defending a preferred idea.

## 4. Messaging lenses

An angle is the persuasive idea: a claim, tension, explanation, or transformation relevant to a buyer. A format is its execution. A grid or testimonial describes presentation; the reason the buyer should care describes the angle. Denney distinguishes these in her analysis and Q&A. [S2]

Use four lenses to discover or classify persuasive routes. They can overlap; they are not a requirement to create four concepts for every persona. [S2]

| Lens | Question | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human desire | What emotional outcome does this buyer seek? | Connect calm, belonging, romance, power, or another desire to evidence and product use. |
| Demographic context | Does age, role, hobby, or job change the situation? | Retain context when it changes the message, demonstration, or credibility needed. |
| Strategic angle | What framing makes the problem or belief compelling? | Consider personal history, negative framing, taboo discussion, or recognizable talent when the framing serves the premise. |
| User journey | What does the buyer already understand? | Set the entry point and explanation to the awareness stage. |

For each angle, write the customer signal, belief to address, main promise, available proof, objection, and awareness stage. An angle should remain recognizable if the format changes. Three opening lines that express the same promise are hook variations, not necessarily three new angles.

**Package safeguard:** Emotional framing must still fit the offer. Negative framing cannot manufacture a complaint, distort a review, or rely on unsupported accusations. Celebrity, founder, or personal-history treatments require appropriate available assets and a credible speaker; do not invent endorsement or lived experience.

## 5. Awareness and entry points

Denney uses five awareness stages to explain message fit and the tradeoff between broad entry points and purchase readiness. In her makeup case study, a routine-oriented message drew from existing UGC and outperformed other messages in the tested grid execution. That illustrates a method of discovering messages, not a prediction for every account. [S2] [S5]

| Stage | Existing knowledge | Useful message direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware | Not actively concerned about the problem or seeking a solution | A relevant routine, relatable situation, education, or curiosity that introduces the need. |
| Problem aware | Recognizes the problem | Describe the situation and introduce a credible path toward improvement. |
| Solution aware | Understands possible solution categories | Explain a relevant tradeoff, mechanism, or differentiator. |
| Product aware | Knows this product and considers its fit | Address comparison, suitability, objections, and believable proof. |
| Most aware | Knows the offer and is close to deciding | Clarify the offer, purchase terms, remaining reassurance, or timely reason to act. |

If current ads all assume familiarity with the brand, investigate broader entries. Do not discard purchase-ready creative simply because broader awareness can offer expansion. The suggested formats in Denney's awareness charts are examples, not exclusive mappings. [S2] [S5]

**Package safeguard:** Tag the intended entry point rather than claiming all viewers share one mental state. Evaluate expansion through the account's actual objective; broader reach alone does not establish profitable demand.

## 6. Execution after message

Choose format, creator, imagery, and setting to make the angle understandable and believable. Denney's content analysis treats these as distinct dimensions converging on the intended persona. A creator's credibility or context may explain apparent script or format success. [S2]

Use demonstrations for observable use cases, testimonials for authentic experience, comparisons for relevant tradeoffs, and static graphics for concise visual proof when appropriate. These are production choices, not guaranteed winners. Carry a supported angle into other executions, adapting the wording and visual logic rather than assuming a video line will work unchanged as a static headline. [S2] [S5] [S7]

Denney recommends studying what a brand's operating approach teaches rather than merely copying a successful-looking ad. Her examples include creator operations and replicating angles across executions. [S7]

**Package safeguard:** Identify which variables change in each execution. If creator, angle, and format all change, the test evaluates the whole concept; it cannot isolate the effect of format. Select talent for credible fit rather than a demographic stereotype.

## 7. Roadmap and briefs

Keep ideas in a central backlog, assess evidence and confidence, and prioritize feasible production with room for an occasional high-upside experiment. Denney cautions against treating competitors' activity or long-running ads as sufficient reasons to prioritize an idea. [S3]

Her described cadence combines quarterly planning, monthly roadmaps, and weekly production. Adapt that structure to the user's planning horizon and capacity without reverting to disconnected weekly reactions. [S3] [S6]

Each selected roadmap concept contains a persona, an idea in one or two sentences, and three messaging options that can be hooks or variations. Add creator decisions and production requirements so the idea is actionable. [S3]

**Package safeguard:** A compact brief also records proof assets, the hypothesis, primary learning question, comparison, owner or timing if known, and a next decision. Explain priority in words using evidence, confidence, potential impact, and production effort; no fixed scoring formula or quota is attributed to Denney. Missing owners, budgets, or thresholds remain open fields.

## 8. Results and feedback

Denney's four-part analysis sequence is metrics, content, comparison, and feedback. Commercial measures such as spend, results, cost per result, and return help identify performance; click-through, viewing, sharing, and comments help investigate the story behind it. Content analysis is essential to interpreting what actually changed. [S2]

Compare against the account's other creatives and broader performance pattern, then relevant competitor or industry patterns. Read comments for objections and language absent from the dashboard. External creative is inspiration and context; its public visibility does not disclose its economics. [S2] [S3]

Record whether evidence supports extending the angle, changing its expression, addressing an objection, or revising the persona hypothesis. Continue developing productive angles across executions while new evidence warrants it. [S7]

**Package safeguard:** Spend distribution alone is insufficient evidence of profitable performance. Use the objective and account targets, and report insufficient exposure, inconsistent attribution windows, or changes in offer and delivery as limitations. A weak result with several changed variables does not disprove a persona. Do not impose universal sample sizes or significance thresholds; obtain appropriate account-specific criteria.

## 9. Explicit warnings and edge cases

Denney explicitly warns against broad personas, endless format testing without strategy, and a missing roadmap that leaves teams reacting weekly. [S6] She also emphasizes manual familiarity before trusting AI, substantive editing of AI-assisted work, and evidence-based prioritization rather than copying competitors. [S3] Her strategic questions warn against isolated ad production without a coherent hypothesis, expansion plan, and supporting content ecosystem. [S8]

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| No customer evidence | Use comparable lower-rated reviews, label hypotheses provisional, and plan to collect direct feedback. [S3] |
| Strong emotion but low frequency | Consider a bounded test and explain the stakes; do not equate sample intensity with market size. [S3] |
| Broad persona and trendy format | Rebuild from a concrete trigger and persuasive idea before assigning execution. [S6] |
| High click-through, weak purchases | Investigate message, audience, and post-click objections; do not declare a commercial winner from engagement. [S2] |
| Competitor ad runs for months | Study the hypothesis it suggests; do not infer profit or copy it as evidence. [S3] [S7] |
| Polished AI brief with weak grounding | Revisit original evidence and edit the interpretation before production. [S3] |
| Winner across mixed changes | Extend cautiously and design a clearer follow-up; attribution remains unresolved. Package safeguard. |
| Unverified product or health claim | Separate market belief from valid proof, remove unsupported claims, and choose a supportable premise. Package safeguard. |
| User requests only an audit | Deliver diagnosis and recommended tests at audit depth; do not invent a production commitment. Package safeguard. |
