# Worked examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. Company names, responses, counts, product capabilities, and artifacts are fictional teaching inputs. They are not Wynter results or evidence of conversion improvement. Method citations identify the applied rules, not support for the invented business claims.

## Illustration 1: Fix category confusion before polishing value

### Brief and weak first draft

ClearLedger sells payroll correction software to HR operations leads at companies with 500 to 2,000 employees operating across multiple US states. The decision is whether to launch its new homepage. Assume the exercise brief verifies that its product checks corrections against payroll rules and exports approved changes to an existing payroll system.

**Hero headline:** The future of workforce agility.

**Subheading:** Unlock seamless operations with one intelligent platform built for tomorrow.

**CTA:** Discover the future.

**Page outline:** Vision statement, three repeated sections on regional teams, employee growth, and operational transformation, then a payroll correction explanation near the bottom.

### Illustrative evidence and diagnosis

In a fictional 15-prospect test, nine initially describe staffing or scheduling software; six find the payroll explanation only lower on the page. Several like the idea of less manual work but cannot say what the company sells. These overlapping counts are not market estimates.

- **Observed within the illustration:** Wrong categories and late comprehension.
- **Inferred:** The abstract hero and delayed product account cause clarity failure.
- **Primary layer:** Clarity, despite some favorable value reactions. [S1]
- **Secondary issue:** Repeated use-case sections delay the explanation. [S5]

### Improved result

**Hero headline:** Payroll correction software for multi-state HR teams.

**Subheading:** Check payroll corrections against your rules and send approved changes to your existing payroll system, without managing another correction spreadsheet.

**CTA:** See a correction workflow.

**Page outline:** Hero, short correction workflow, supported integration details, one consolidated multi-state use-case section, implementation questions, demo CTA.

**Proof block:** Show a product walkthrough using fictional demonstration data. Add named payroll integrations only after verification. Omit a time-saving percentage because this exercise contains no measured result.

### What changed and why

Naming the category answers the first buyer question. The workflow explanation connects the capability to recognizable work. The spreadsheet comparison supplies a relevant current alternative, but remains a differentiation hypothesis until buyers explain why it matters. Consolidation removes repeated explanation without imposing a universal short-page rule. [S1][S3][S5]

### Retest and decision

Retest the same ICP definition and page scope with neutral paraphrase questions. Check whether prospects identify payroll correction software before being told the category, locate the explanation early, and understand what remains in their existing payroll system. Recommend launch only when the primary misclassification is resolved under the team's declared criterion; otherwise revise. The improved copy is proposed, not validated. [S4]

## Illustration 2: Replace feature sameness with a relevant, supported difference

### Brief and weak first draft

ReviewTrail sells audit evidence collection software to security leads at SaaS companies with 100 to 500 employees. Current buyer research in this invented brief identifies repeated evidence requests and version confusion before SOC 2 reviews as a priority. Buyers currently use shared folders and reminders. Assume verified exercise inputs: the product assigns recurring evidence requests, preserves submission history, and works alongside an existing compliance platform. It does not replace auditors or certify compliance.

**Hero headline:** The all-in-one compliance platform for everyone.

**Subheading:** Seamless automation, powerful integrations, and best-in-class insights help you achieve compliance faster.

**CTA:** Get compliant now.

### Illustrative evidence and diagnosis

A fictional target-buyer test finds that prospects understand it is compliance software but assume it replaces their current platform. They recognize the broad problem, then ask why they would migrate and what makes this different from tools they already own. Some interpret the CTA as a compliance guarantee.

- **Observed within the illustration:** Broad category comprehension, migration worries, and a mistaken guarantee.
- **Inferred:** The all-in-one claim creates a misleading comparison and unnecessary switching friction.
- **Primary issue to fix first:** Clarity about scope, followed by differentiation.
- **Proof gap:** The faster-compliance claim has no supporting evidence. [S1]

Do not declare differentiation the first failure just because respondents can name a broad category. Misunderstanding what the product replaces is still a material clarity failure.

### Improved result

**Hero headline:** Audit evidence collection for SaaS security teams.

**Subheading:** Schedule recurring evidence requests, track submissions, and keep their history in one place. ReviewTrail works alongside your compliance platform, so you can replace folder-and-reminder tracking without migrating your whole compliance program.

**Difference block:** Built for recurring evidence collection, rather than an all-in-one compliance replacement. See how a request moves from assignment to submission history.

**Proof block:** Product demonstration of request scheduling and history, plus verified compatibility documentation. No performance numbers or customer logos are added without evidence.

**Scope note:** Supports evidence collection; your auditor determines whether your evidence meets the review requirements.

**CTA:** See an evidence request workflow.

### What changed and why

Scope clarity removes the mistaken replacement assumption. Buyer context makes recurring requests the main use case, rather than adding generic benefits. The alternative is the actual folder-and-reminder process. The difference is a product specialization with a visible tradeoff, not a superlative. The scope note answers an observed doubt without promising certification. [S1][S2][S3]

### Retest and decision

Ask comparable buyers to describe what ReviewTrail does and does not replace, what workflow they would use instead, and why this approach merits consideration. If scope becomes clear but buyers see no sufficient benefit over their current tools, diagnose value before embellishing differentiation. Retain the focused product position only if it is both accurate and relevant; feedback may reveal a strategic offer gap that copy cannot fix. A preference test is appropriate only if the team has two or three supported, viable directions left to choose among. [S1][S3][S4]
