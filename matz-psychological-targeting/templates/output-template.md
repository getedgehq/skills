# Psychological Personalization Decision Brief

Copy the fields below into the deliverable and replace bracketed prompts. Bracketed prompts are intentional fill-in fields. For an audit, describe current behavior first, then the proposed correction. Use "unknown" for missing evidence rather than inventing it. Method rules are in [references/method.md](../references/method.md).

## 1. Decision and status

- **Project and decision owner:** [Name the intervention and accountable owner.]
- **Requested decision:** [Specify what this brief helps decide, such as whether to test a recommendation.]
- **Status:** [Choose proposed hypothesis, evaluated intervention, or audit finding.]
- **Disposition:** [Proceed, revise, generic fallback, or stop. Explain the deciding condition.]
- **Audience and context:** [Who receives the intervention, where, and when?]

Guidance: Do not present a completed brief as permission to deploy. Identify unresolved evidence or authorization separately. See M1 and M12.

## 2. Goals and interests

- **Recipient goal:** [Use a stated goal; label an assumed goal explicitly.]
- **Sponsor goal:** [Name the behavior or business outcome desired.]
- **Expected recipient benefit:** [State a concrete benefit and how it could be observed.]
- **Conflict or cost:** [Include unsuitable purchases, time, disclosure, pressure, or reduced choice.]

Guidance: Explain why influence serves the recipient rather than equating engagement with value. Matz describes both beneficial support and exploitation. [S1] [S2]

## 4. Influence hypothesis and strategy

- **Strategy:** [Message fit, product fit, or both; justify the choice.]

Guidance: Product and message fit are separate levers; usefulness depends on the offer and context. See M7 and M8. [S5]

## 6. Recipient explanation and controls

- **Explanation text:** [Plain wording describing why this option appears.]
- **Correction:** [How recipients change the preference or challenge an estimate.]
