# Sources

All eight URLs below were fetched successfully with curl on 2026-10-02. Interviews and public pages were read as accessible page text; [S6] was read as open full-text XML. [S7] and [S8] were read as arXiv abstract pages, not their full papers. No book text or paywalled material was used. The package paraphrases substantive content and uses no direct source quotations.

1. [S1] Josie Cox, *What Your Digital Footprint Says About You*, Columbia Magazine, Spring/Summer 2025. https://www.magazine.columbia.edu/article/digital-footprint-sandra-matz-mindmasters
   Contribution: Matz's explanations of digital traces, insight and influence, agency risks, echo chambers, possible exploration harms, and privacy by design.
2. [S2] *Sandra Matz: Mindmaster - Data & Behavior*, The Second City, interview excerpts, April 29, 2025. https://www.secondcity.com/sandra-matz-mindmaster-data-behavior
   Contribution: The two-stage insight and influence framing and the possibility of supporting recipients' self-chosen goals.
3. [S3] Sandra Matz, *Biography and Research Overview*, personal website, undated. https://sandramatz.com/
   Contribution: Background on her computational social science work and its stated aim of supporting better and more ethical decisions; not efficacy evidence.
4. [S4] Jason Hreno, *Q&A with Sandra Matz*, Rotman Management, January 1, 2025. https://rotmanutorontoca-lb01-production.terminalfour.net/news-events-and-ideas/rotman-magazine/back-issues/2025/winter-2025/sandra-matz/
   Contribution: Context-dependent personality expression, self-determination, limits of persuasion, exploitation warnings, and easier protective defaults.
5. [S5] Peter van der Bel, *Personality & marketing effectiveness. What makes CTR and conversion go through the roof?*, interview with Sandra Matz, Center for Applied Product Personality Research, March 6, 2017. https://cappr.org/sandra-matz-personality-marketing-effectiveness-makes-ctr-conversion-go-roof/
   Contribution: Conditional product versus message fit, trait selection, noisy clicks, and historically unresolved comparative-effectiveness questions.
6. [S6] Sandra C. Matz, Michal Kosinski, Gideon Nave, and David J. Stillwell, *Psychological targeting as an effective approach to digital mass persuasion*, PNAS, 2017, open full text via Europe PMC. https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5715760/fullTextXML
   Contribution: Three field experiments, audience-by-message fit tests, creative validation, separate click and conversion findings, proxy drift, and ethical limitations.
7. [S7] Heinrich Peters and Sandra Matz, *Large Language Models Can Infer Psychological Dispositions of Social Media Users*, arXiv:2309.08631, abstract page, revised June 5, 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08631
   Contribution: Abstract-level evidence of moderate inference accuracy and age- and gender-related heterogeneity in predictions from Facebook text.
8. [S8] Heinrich Peters, Moran Cerf, and Sandra C. Matz, *Large Language Models Can Infer Personality from Free-Form User Interactions*, arXiv:2405.13052, abstract page, May 19, 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13052
   Contribution: Abstract-level evidence that conversational design affects inference accuracy and that ordinary assistant interaction can reveal some personality information.
