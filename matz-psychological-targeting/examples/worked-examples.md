# Worked Examples

These scenarios, organizations, messages, and proposed evaluation plans are invented illustrations. No measured results or source endorsements are implied. Rule numbers refer to [references/method.md](../references/method.md); source IDs refer to [references/sources.md](../references/sources.md).

## Illustration 1: A library reading club

### Scenario and weak first draft

A library offers two free Saturday sessions for adults: quiet reading and facilitated discussion. Its registration form already allows people to select either format. The sponsor wants attendance; readers want an enjoyable way to spend an hour.

Weak draft:

> Use browsing histories to identify introverts and extroverts. Send introverts: "Escape the people who drain you. This club is your safe space." Send extroverts: "Everyone will be there. Don't miss your chance to belong." Success means more email clicks.

This draft invents profiles, introduces anxiety and social pressure, and ignores volunteered preferences. Its metric does not answer whether readers found a suitable experience.

### Improved decision brief

**Goals:** Recipient goal is an enjoyable reading session in a preferred format. Sponsor goal is attendance and sustained participation. Benefit is plausible but remains to be checked through reader feedback.

**Signal:** Use the participant's current format selection, collected for registration and this invitation. Do not infer Big Five scores or collect browsing histories. Describe the selection as a preference for this visit. Missing selection leads to the generic invitation.

**Strategy:** Choose the matching experience first, then highlight its relevant features. Keep both formats available to everyone.

**Generic invitation:**

> Join our free Saturday reading club, 10 to 11 a.m. Choose quiet reading or a facilitated book discussion. Both meet at the library, and you can change your choice before the session. See both formats and register.

**Quiet-reading invitation:**

> Enjoy an hour of uninterrupted reading at our free Saturday club, 10 to 11 a.m. Bring a book for the quiet-reading session, or choose the facilitated discussion instead. See both formats and register.

**Discussion invitation:**

> Share ideas about books at our free Saturday club, 10 to 11 a.m. Join the facilitated discussion, or choose the quiet-reading session instead. See both formats and register.

**Explanation and control:** "We highlighted the format you selected. Change your selection or receive the general invitation." Retain the preference only for this registration cycle unless the participant chooses otherwise.

**Evaluation:** Within each selected format, compare the highlighted invitation with the generic invitation using comparable delivery. Primary measure is attendance among invited participants; secondary measures are registrations, clicks, and optional post-session format satisfaction. Set the observation period to four sessions. With small numbers, report descriptive evidence and uncertainty rather than a definitive uplift.

**Disposition:** The brief is ready for review. Deployment still requires the library's authorized process. Use the generic route if preference use is unclear or people cannot switch formats.

### Rules that changed the result

- M1 and M11 replaced clicks with attendance and recipient satisfaction as relevant outcomes. Attention and action can diverge. [S6]
- M4 and M5 replaced incidental trace inference with a sufficient volunteered preference, implementing the agency concerns in the interviews. [S1] [S4]
- M7 distinguished selecting an experience from adapting its invitation. [S5]
- M8 and M9 preserved common facts, practical choice, and an explanation rather than treating profiles as identities. [S4] [S6]

## Illustration 2: An online course recommendation

### Scenario and weak first draft

A learning provider offers two equally priced introductory photography courses. One uses a stable weekly sequence and repeated exercises. The other uses varied weekly prompts and experimentation. Adult learners explicitly opt into recommendations and choose "structured practice," "varied challenges," or "show me both." Both courses cover the same foundational skills.

Weak draft:

> Infer openness from learners' social posts. Recommend the experimental course to high-openness people because they are more creative. Hide the structured course from them. Declare success if paid enrollments increase by 30 percent.

The draft equates a trait with competence, uses unrelated data, removes choice, and promises an unsupported result. Enrollment alone cannot establish that the learner received a suitable course.

### Improved decision brief

**Goals:** Recipient goal is a course they will enjoy and complete. Sponsor goal is enrollment with low cancellation and refund rates. Course suitability matters more than finding a persuasive label.

**Signal:** Use the opted-in learning-format selection. It is a preference, not a measurement of openness, intelligence, or artistic ability. Permit changes whenever recommendations are shown.

**Strategy:** Use product fit because the course formats differ meaningfully. Order recommendations by the selected format, show both, and apply light message tailoring. Never change skill prerequisites or price by profile.

**Generic recommendation:**

> Both introductory photography courses teach the same foundational skills at the same price. Choose repeated weekly exercises for a stable routine, or varied prompts for more experimentation. Compare schedules, sample lessons, and cancellation terms.

**Structured-practice recommendation:**

> You selected structured practice. The weekly-sequence course uses repeated exercises and a consistent routine. The varied-prompts course is also available. Compare both courses, schedules, sample lessons, and cancellation terms before choosing.

**Varied-challenges recommendation:**

> You selected varied challenges. The weekly-prompts course offers different experiments while teaching the same foundational skills. The weekly-sequence course is also available. Compare both courses, schedules, sample lessons, and cancellation terms before choosing.

**Controls:** Display the selected preference and links to change it or turn recommendations off. Avoid pulling in social posts. Do not assume that a preference remains accurate across future courses.

**Evaluation:** Compare tailored ordering and framing against the generic comparison within each preference group. The primary recipient outcome is course completion after eight weeks. Report enrollments, cancellations, refunds, and optional format-fit feedback separately. Predefine a pause if cancellations or complaints suggest that recommendations pressure learners into unsuitable courses. Any apparent difference could reflect ordering and framing together; this design does not isolate their individual effects.

**Disposition:** Testable hypothesis, with no forecasted percentage lift. If both formats perform similarly or samples are too small, retain the generic comparison and report the uncertainty.

### Rules that changed the result

- M5 rejected a universal trait-to-product rule and unnecessary personality inference. Matz treats relevant traits as context dependent. [S5]
- M7 prioritized actual product fit over a label-driven message. [S5]
- M8 kept substantive information consistent and made the intervention's scope explicit, following the logic of fit testing. [S6]
- M9 and M12 kept alternatives visible and created a harm-based stopping rule, operationalizing agency concerns. [S1] [S4]
- M10 and M11 removed an invented uplift and separated enrollment from completion and suitability. [S5] [S6]
