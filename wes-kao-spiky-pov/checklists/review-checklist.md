# Finished-output review

Internal review: run it silently on your draft and fix what fails. Never put this checklist, PASS/FAIL marks, a review note or a list of limitations in the deliverable; a gap that matters to the user becomes one short open question at the end (three at most). The gates are package adaptations of the public method, not a scoring system published by the expert.

## Pass/fail gates

- [ ] **Credibility:** The position is in the author's relevant domain and does not invent their experience or conviction. [S1]
- [ ] **Relevance:** The intended audience gets a useful interpretation of a problem they face. [S1]
- [ ] **Clarity:** The opening contains one identifiable assertion, with a scope the reasoning can support. [S1][S3]
- [ ] **Debatability:** A credible opposing view is recorded, with a reason a capable peer could hold it. [S1]
- [ ] **Purpose:** The edge serves better thinking or action instead of attention alone. [S1]
- [ ] **Learning:** The piece adds something beyond familiar advice, summary, or rhetorical questions. [S1][S9]
- [ ] **Defensibility:** Support is traceable, assumptions are visible, and no invented number, customer, quote, or outcome appears as fact. [S1][S13]
- [ ] **Mechanism:** The link between observation, interpretation, and recommendation is explained. [S3][S6][S13]
- [ ] **Conviction:** The author advocates an answer they can endorse, rather than leaving a list of options. [S1][S3]
- [ ] **Certainty:** Confidence reflects evidence, consequences, and authority; necessary uncertainty remains. [S5][S8]
- [ ] **Objection:** The main substantive challenge is addressed, or it has caused an appropriate scope revision. [S8]
- [ ] **Reader consequence:** The audience knows what to think or do differently, with concrete application where useful. [S3][S9]
- [ ] **Motivation:** If buy-in is missing, the piece explains why the reader should care before detailed logistics. [S14]
- [ ] **Questions:** Substantive questions receive the author's attempted answer; an invitation to debate follows a stance. [S4]
- [ ] **Language:** Desired actions are easy to identify, and qualifiers or emphasis words earn their place. [S10][S11]
- [ ] **Density:** The piece keeps necessary context and reasoning while cutting repetition and irrelevant backstory. [S7]
- [ ] **Strategy:** Each passage advances the intended change rather than venting or seeking the last word. [S15]
- [ ] **Format:** The output fits the requested channel without sacrificing a material boundary or overstating a result. [S5][S7]

## Audience-feedback record

Record TESTED or NOT TESTED separately from the editorial verdict. If tested, note the actual version, audience, setting, and reaction. Look for follow-up questions, recalled wording, and active curiosity. Polite approval is weak evidence. Engagement informs relevance; it does not independently verify factual correctness. [S12]

## Common failure modes and fixes

| Failure | What to change |
| --- | --- |
| Consensus slogan | Find a disputed priority, condition, or framing within the author's real experience. [S1] |
| Unsupported provocation | Add the reasoning and support, narrow the scope, or drop the claim. [S1][S13] |
| Observation without consequence | State the interpretation and advocate what changes because of it. [S3][S6] |
| Strawman opponent | Write the alternative's strongest rationale, then revise the claim around a real trade-off. [S1] |
| Borrowed perspective | Return to the author's actual decisions and beliefs; use others' examples only to understand form. [S1] |
| Outside the author's expertise | Move the claim into a credible domain or identify it as a question for investigation. [S1] |
| Invented personal evidence | Remove it, request the missing fact, or use a clearly labeled hypothetical explanation. [S1][S5] |
| Questions replace the take | Give the author's answer before inviting discussion. [S4] |
| Stacked hedges | Retain the modifier that represents real uncertainty and remove redundant cushioning. [S2][S5] |
| Fake certainty | Distinguish fact, experience, and hypothesis; qualify the condition that actually matters. [S5][S8] |
| Generic advice about importance | Show the specific action, script, example, or decision rule. [S9] |
| Missing causal step | Explain who does what, with which constraints and risks, to produce the expected result. [S13] |
| Caveats bury the position | Put the claim first, then the material boundary and main objection. [S2][S8] |
| Short but misleading | Restore essential context or narrow the claim so it fits accurately. [S7] |
| Instructions before motivation | Explain the reader's benefit before the how unless buy-in is already secure. [S14] |
| Venting instead of persuasion | Rewrite around the reader's desired future behavior and remove punishment or scorekeeping. [S15] |
| Claimed resonance without testing | Mark it untested and propose observation of real audience reactions. [S12] |

After revision, recheck the failed gates and any gate affected by the change. A stronger hook cannot compensate for weak evidence or an unsupported conclusion.
