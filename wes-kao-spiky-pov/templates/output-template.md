# Point-of-view piece skeleton

Fill with the author's own material. The finished output is the piece; keep candidate positions, the five-trait check and the opposing view in your head. The claim-evidence-objection logic follows Kao's public writing; the layout is a package convention.

1. **Opening claim** (one or two lines): the position, specific enough that a peer could disagree.
2. **Why** (two to four short paragraphs): the author's real observation or example from the input, and what it means.
3. **The objection** (one to three sentences): the strongest pushback and the honest answer, including any real exception.
4. **What to do instead** (one to three lines): a concrete action, script or rule for the reader.
5. **Close**: when the author wants discussion, one or two specific, answerable questions that invite readers' own experience or where they would draw the line; never a generic "thoughts?".
