# Point-of-view deliverable template

Use this for a post, essay argument, talk thesis, or positioning rationale. Adapt its visible shape to the requested channel. Working fields support the draft; return them only when requested or when an unresolved gap materially affects the output.

## Working brief

- **Author and credible domain:** [Who is speaking, and what relevant experience supports their authority?] Ground the claim in the author's material rather than a borrowed persona. [S1]
- **Audience and situation:** [Who needs this now, and what decision are they facing?] Identify what readers already believe so the piece can add something useful. [S1][S9]
- **Desired change:** [After reading, they should believe or do ____ because ____.] Choose a consequence beyond liking or sharing the piece. [S3]
- **Format and constraints:** [Channel, length, voice, required content.] Respect the requested format; retain material qualifications when a claim is consequential. [S5][S7]
- **Candidate positions:** [Three to five possibilities, or a wider inventory.] Explore before polishing; a larger set is useful for developing a body of positions. [S1][S2]
- **Selected assertion:** [In situation ____, I recommend/believe ____ because ____.] State an owned conclusion rather than an observation alone. [S3]
- **Selection reason:** [Why this candidate best serves the reader and is supportable.] This is a package selection convention, not a numerical Kao score.

## Reasoning record

| Field | Fill in | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation | [What happened or was noticed] | Keep the observation separate from its interpretation. [S3] |
| Support and provenance | [User document, experience, checked public fact, or missing evidence] | Identify exactly what supports the claim; method citations do not prove industry outcomes. [S1] |
| Interpretation | [What it means through the author's lens] | Explain the bridge the reader may not see. [S6] |
| Mechanism | [Why the proposed action could produce the outcome] | Expose assumptions, constraints, cost, and downside. [S13] |
| Strong opposing view | [A capable peer's alternative and its rationale] | Test a real trade-off; avoid a strawman. [S1] |
| Most obvious objection | [Likely challenge] | Choose the objection most relevant to this claim. [S8] |
| Response or boundary | [Answer, exception, or narrower scope] | Revise the assertion if the objection reveals a flaw. [S5][S8] |
| Confidence | [Fact, experience-based recommendation, or hypothesis] | Match the wording to evidence and consequences. [S5] |
| Specific application | [Example, exact script, or decision rule] | Help the reader act rather than only agree. [S9] |
| Missing evidence | [What remains unknown] | Keep a gap visible; never invent a result to fill it. |

## Finished piece

### Opening claim

[State the specific, debatable position in the first lines. Name the relevant situation or audience where needed.]

A useful opening lets the reader identify the judgment being advocated. It can be a recommendation or a contested frame. [S1]

### Evidence and interpretation

[Present the strongest support available. Explain what it means and why it changes the usual decision.]

Use the author's actual story, a checked fact, or an explicitly provisional mechanism. A fictional illustration must be labeled, and must not masquerade as experience. [S1][S5][S6]

### Objection and scope

[Acknowledge the substantive objection. Answer it, or name the condition where the recommendation changes.]

Use a short passage for a short piece and enough detail for a consequential argument. Do not surround the opening with every conceivable caveat. [S2][S8]

### Reader consequence and specific how

[Explain what the reader should believe or do differently. Include a concrete next action or decision rule.]

When readers are not yet persuaded, show why this serves their interests before presenting procedural detail. If the underlying benefit is already understood, move quickly to application. [S3][S9][S14]

### Optional invitation

[Invite discussion about a real assumption, trade-off, or boundary after giving the author's answer.]

Do not replace the position with an unanswered closing question. [S4]

## Final working notes

- **Review:** [Pass, revise, or provisional, plus the material issue.] Apply [the review checklist](../checklists/review-checklist.md).
- **Audience test:** [Not tested, or actual audience and version tested.] Observe active interest, confusion, and remembered language; do not invent reactions. [S12]
- **Next revision:** [Which assumption, phrasing, or application should change based on actual feedback?] Treat resonance as a signal about relevance, not proof of the claim.
