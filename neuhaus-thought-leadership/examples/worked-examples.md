# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations. Organizations, executives, situations, evidence, schedules, and targets are fictional. They are not INSIGHT clients or documented results. Sample first-person content assumes the invented executive has confirmed the underlying facts. In real work, obtain that confirmation before presenting such content as publication-ready.

## Illustration 1: Industrial CEO seeking relevant buyer conversations

### Brief and evidence

Mara Weiss leads the fictional Rheinwerk Systems, a 70-person manufacturer of retrofit monitoring equipment. The primary audience is operations directors at regional industrial plants. The business objective is conversations about identifying downtime causes, not general fame. In this illustration, Mara confirms that a pilot team found a maintenance reporting gap and that the client has permitted an anonymized process description. No quantified performance result is supplied.

### Weak first draft

> I am proud to lead an innovative company into the future. Digital transformation is changing everything. Our passionate team delivers world-class solutions. Follow me for leadership inspiration, and contact us to revolutionize your business!

The strategy behind this draft is to post daily motivational statements, copy a famous technology founder's style, and measure success through follower growth. It provides no reason for an operations director to remember Mara or trust the company's judgment.

### Improved positioning and profile

Positioning: Mara helps operations directors diagnose downtime before buying more monitoring equipment. Her contribution is practical judgment about the boundary between data collection and decision ownership. The company connection is retrofit monitoring that fits existing maintenance workflows.

Sample headline:

> CEO at Rheinwerk Systems | Practical retrofit monitoring for industrial teams | Diagnose the stoppage before buying another dashboard

Featured material: an approved, anonymized pilot explanation and a diagnostic worksheet. Next action: request the worksheet or discuss a relevant downtime problem. Do not describe the worksheet as proven to improve performance without evidence.

### Improved post

> In a recent retrofit pilot, the useful discovery was a handover gap.
>
> Operators were recording stoppages. Maintenance was receiving the records. But no one had clear responsibility for comparing repeated events across shifts.
>
> I initially wanted us to add more monitoring. The maintenance lead asked a better question: who will use the extra information, and when?
>
> We mapped the handover before deciding what else to measure.
>
> My lesson: before buying another dashboard, identify one recurring downtime question, the person responsible for answering it, and the meeting where the answer will change a decision.
>
> For operations directors considering a retrofit: where does your current information stop becoming useful?

### Execution and review

Illustrative first cycle: the coordinator prepares questions for a weekly 20-minute CEO interview. The writer drafts two posts per week from confirmed material. An engineer verifies technical claims, Mara reviews wording, and the team prepares a worksheet. These frequencies are example choices, not Neuhaus's required cadence.

Record the number of qualified operations-director conversations and their next steps. Baseline: unknown, to be established from authorized records. Treat reach and relevant comments as supporting signals. At monthly review, inspect which questions bring the right audience into dialogue. At quarterly review, decide whether the niche and distribution need changing. Paid distribution remains a proposal until audience response, measurement, budget, and authorization support it.

- A real learning moment supplied warmth without family disclosure: section 6, grounded in [S3] and [S5].

## Illustration 2: Employed CEO attracting specialist talent

### Brief and evidence

Jonas Keller is the fictional employed CEO of Nordlicht Cloud, a 420-person infrastructure software company. The priority is attracting senior site reliability engineers. Corporate communications wants the CEO's public voice to reinforce an honest employer promise. In this illustration, Jonas confirms that the company changed an incident review practice; its impact on retention is unknown. The recruitment team supplies the actual interview process and role requirements.

### Weak first draft

> We are the best employer in cloud technology. Our people are family, and burnout does not exist here. Join our winning culture. I will share my private family routine every week so everyone can get to know the real me.

The associated plan is to seek viral employer posts and celebrate likes. The draft contains unsupported absolute claims, a private disclosure Jonas does not want, and an employer promise no operational evidence supports.

### Improved positioning and boundaries

Positioning: Jonas explains how infrastructure leadership can make reliability work sustainable by addressing accountability, learning, and tradeoffs. The audience is experienced engineers evaluating a serious employer. The company link is an observable engineering practice and a candid hiring process.

Voice: technically literate, direct, and willing to acknowledge an imperfect decision. Personal content includes professional learning; family information is excluded. Corporate communications checks employer claims, and engineering verifies descriptions of operational practice. Sensitive incident information remains excluded.

### Improved post

> I used to open our incident review by asking who owned the failure.
>
> Our engineering leads challenged that question. It could push people to defend their part of the system before we understood how the system had failed.
>
> We changed the opening: what did the on-call engineer know at the time, and what options were available?
>
> Accountability still matters. The review now records an owner for each improvement after the team has examined the contributing conditions.
>
> I cannot claim this has solved workload or retention. I can explain the practice we are changing and why.
>
> If you are evaluating a reliability role, ask how your future employer turns incident learning into funded work. Our hiring team can walk candidates through our review process and the role's responsibilities.

### Execution, measurement, and edge case

Build a profile summary that states Jonas's actual scope and links to approved engineering material and current role pages. Prepare a small content batch about confirmed leadership decisions and team contributions. The recruitment lead owns applicant qualification and asks candidates how they found the role. Report relevant applications, progress to interview, and self-reported influence separately from engagement.

If engineers report that public claims conflict with daily practice, stop amplifying those claims and investigate with the responsible internal team. Do not manufacture employee testimonials. If Jonas later leaves, the company retains its own engineering explainers and recruitment assets, while personal brand continuity is assessed separately.

- A talent objective and clear stakeholder replaced general visibility: section 3, grounded in [S8].
- Competence plus warmth replaced celebrity imitation: section 6, grounded in the limited show notes of [S3].
- Persönlich versus privat preserved an authentic public voice without unwanted disclosure: section 6, grounded in [S5].
