# Performance Content campaign deliverable

Replace bracketed fields with user facts, explicit assumptions, or unresolved questions. Include only sections needed for the request, but identify dependencies of any isolated component. Cite [S#] when explaining the public method, using [sources](../references/sources.md). Label recommendations as proposed implementation choices. Do not label a proposal a campaign result.

## 1. Context and desired outcome

- Company and offer: [What the company sells and implements.]
- Scope: [Requested asset or complete funnel; planning or authorized execution.]
- Business outcome: [Accepted opportunities, demos, or another specific result.]
- Geography and language: [Markets and customer vocabulary.]
- Buying cycle and capacity: [Known sales timing and people available.]
- Budget and limits: [User budget, spending cap, and time boundary; use unknown if absent.]
- Inputs and assumptions: [Separate supplied evidence from inferred choices.]

Guidance: Do not expand the scope merely because the method includes additional channels. Define the outcome before selecting an ad format.

## 2. Buyer and Trigger-Symptom

- Primary buyer: [Role, firm attributes, buying responsibility.]
- Symptom: [Observable situation in buyer language.]
- Consequence: [What the problem costs or prevents, supported by evidence.]
- Existing evidence: [Interview, sales note, process, or other provided artifact.]
- Targeting proposal: [Relevant inclusion and exclusion criteria.]

Guidance: Explain why this buyer would use the asset and plausibly buy the offer. Distinguish an end user from an economic decision maker.

## 3. Expert position and proof

- Expert and review owner: [Real accountable person.]
- Hauptbotschaft: [Relevant challenged assumption plus a better decision rule.]
- Proof: [Verified example, process, observation, or user-supplied evidence.]
- Claim limits: [What the evidence does not establish.]

Guidance: Avoid unsupported superlatives and invented customer results. The expertise should be evident in the teaching.

## 8. Content Recycling

Guidance: Reuse thinking and evidence, not identical promotional text everywhere.

## 9. Experiment and decision rules

- Hypothesis and comparison: [What should change, and what remains comparable.]
- Budget cap and period: [Actual user limits or unresolved input.]
- Metrics: [Spend, lead counts, valid contacts, ICP fit, qualified requests, accepted opportunities.]
- Definitions and attribution window: [Comparable counting rules.]
- Review point: [Date or evidence condition.]
- Stop condition: [Spend limit, broken delivery, or other concrete condition.]
- Scale condition: [Sufficient evidence of quality and acceptable economics.]
- AMO diagnosis: [Audience, Messaging, Offer; credit AJ Wilcox through S9.]

Guidance: Include counts beside rates. CPL = spend / counted leads. Cost per qualified lead = spend / qualified subset. Show undefined for zero denominators. Distinguish projected, reported, and observed numbers.

## 10. Review and next decision

- Checklist outcome: [Pass or fail and unresolved items.]

Guidance: Apply [review-checklist.md](../checklists/review-checklist.md). Planning can be complete while launch dependencies remain; report that distinction honestly.
