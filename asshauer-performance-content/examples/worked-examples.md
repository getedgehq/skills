# Worked examples

These are invented illustrations, not Michael Asshauer's clients, campaign results, or proprietary templates. The page sequences, copy, budgets, and decision criteria demonstrate application guidance from [the method](../references/method.md). They do not promise performance.

## Illustration 1: Embedded software testing SaaS

### Brief

Invented company: ReleaseTrace. Offer: regression-test prioritization software for automotive suppliers. Buyer: embedded test managers and engineering leaders at German firms with 100 to 1,000 employees. Symptom: release reviews stall because the team cannot explain which changed requirements lack test evidence. Expert: the company's test engineering lead. Goal: qualified technical discovery conversations. Existing material: an internal release-readiness checklist, cleared for external use.

### Weak first draft

Ad: Discover the future of automotive software testing with our innovative platform. Download our exclusive whitepaper and book your demo today.

PDF: Page 1 is a logo. Pages 2 to 5 describe the company, awards, features, and customer logos. All practical material is gated. The form asks for ten fields. Every registrant becomes an SQL, and sales calls immediately. Success is measured by the cheapest download.

Why it fails: Neither the symptom nor the useful outcome is clear. The preview demonstrates little expertise. The contact request and sales follow-up ask for more commitment than the content has earned.

### Improved result

Hauptbotschaft: Release confidence comes from visible evidence gaps, not simply running more tests.

Asset: A 12-page release-evidence checklist with a worked traceability example. Assumption: the expert verifies every technical recommendation and can publish the example.

Ad copy: Your release review stops at the same question: which changed requirements still lack test evidence? This checklist shows how to expose the gaps before the meeting. Read the first five pages here. Register to get the full checklist and a worked evidence map.

| Page | Content that the reader can use |
| --- | --- |
| 1, free | Name the stalled-review symptom and the checklist's scope |
| 2, free | Show an invented change-to-requirement-to-test chain |
| 3, free | Teach the distinction between failed evidence and missing evidence |
| 4, free | Apply the distinction to one changed requirement |
| 5, free | Give three review questions and explain the remaining tools |
| 6 to 12, gated | Full checklist, blank evidence map, another example, limitations, and a restrained technical-discussion invitation |

Capture: Request name, work email, and company as proposed fields, subject to current platform support. Confirm appropriate geography and role through targeting and available records. Show what the form delivers and explain optional ongoing marketing clearly. Record campaign, variant, asset, delivery state, and permission state in the CRM. Verify actual preview behavior before launch.

Nurture, proposed rather than sourced cadence:

1. Immediately deliver the asset and point to its blank evidence map.
2. Two days later, explain one frequent classification error with an example.
3. Five days later, offer a short expert walkthrough of evidence gaps.
4. Nine days later, invite a technical discussion for teams with an active release problem.

Example second email: A failed test and an absent test are different gaps. For one changed requirement, record the expected evidence, the evidence available, and the missing link. Start with the worksheet's first row. If the classification is unclear, reply with an anonymized description and our test lead can help.

A requested discussion goes to sales. A download with no other intent remains a nurture contact. An explicit request can skip the planned sequence.

Experiment: Compare two hooks on the same document and audience. Illustrative total cap: EUR 600 over 14 days. Review delivery daily and compare valid ICP contacts and discovery requests at the end. Do not automatically split into five variants. If hypothetical results were EUR 600 for 12 leads, of which 8 fit the agreed criteria, CPL would be EUR 50 and cost per qualified lead EUR 75. These figures are invented, not a forecast. Two requests would still need sales acceptance before being counted as opportunities.

Reuse: Adapt the expert's explanation into one personal post and one email lesson. Use a Thought Leader Ad only if amplifying that eligible personal post serves the awareness goal and the user authorizes it. Do not assume the gated document uses that format.

### Rules that changed the result

R1 replaced a broad category with a recognizable symptom. R2 made the expert's position testable. R4 used real process knowledge. R5 provided usable preview value. R6 separated ad formats. R7 separated downloading from buying intent. R9 and R10 made the test interpretable without assuming cheap leads are sales-ready.

## Illustration 2: Procurement advisory for cloud projects

### Brief

Invented company: CloudScope Advisory. Offer: cloud migration procurement and scope reviews for Austrian and Swiss midmarket firms. Buyer: finance and procurement leaders reviewing cloud proposals. Symptom: two bids appear comparable until exclusions and assumptions change the cost. Expert: a procurement adviser with an anonymized comparison process. Goal: relevant scope-review conversations. Existing audience: a small email list and a few LinkedIn followers.

### Weak first draft

Expert post: We are proud to launch our premium cloud consultancy. Our experienced team delivers end-to-end solutions. Book now.

Document Ad: A five-page checklist gated after page five. No additional content remains. The campaign targets everyone in technology across Europe. The plan requires daily original videos on six channels, assumes remarketing starts immediately, and forecasts EUR 23.15 leads because Asshauer reported that figure.

Why it fails: The asset has no gated continuation, targeting misses the decision maker, production exceeds capacity, and the forecast substitutes another advertiser's early result for evidence.

### Improved result

Hauptbotschaft: Compare cloud proposals by their assumptions before comparing their headline prices.

Awareness post: Two cloud bids can quote the same total and describe different projects. Before ranking prices, compare the migration boundary, customer responsibilities, and the definition of completion. Put those assumptions in three columns. The cheapest-looking bid may simply leave the largest decisions with your team.

Use that eligible expert post as a proposed Thought Leader Ad to the relevant procurement and finance audience. Measure relevant consumption and audience fit. Do not attach an imaginary gated form to the format.

Lead magnet: A 10-page cloud bid comparison guide. Replace the invalid five-page asset with a real continuation:

| Page | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1, free | Why comparable totals can conceal different scopes |
| 2, free | Three assumption categories buyers should compare |
| 3, free | A fictional pair of proposal excerpts, clearly labelled |
| 4, free | A filled comparison row showing an excluded task |
| 5, free | Three questions to send both bidders and a preview of the worksheet |
| 6 to 10, gated | Blank comparison matrix, remaining sample rows, scope-review instructions, limitations, and an optional consultation invitation |

Document Ad copy: Before choosing the cheaper cloud bid, check what each supplier assumes your team will do. Preview five pages of our comparison guide. Register for the full matrix and examples you can use in your next proposal review.

Delivery and nurture: Send the requested guide, then three proposed lessons on hidden responsibilities, acceptance evidence, and questions for a supplier meeting. Each has a practical exercise. Invite a scope-review discussion when the contact identifies an active procurement decision. The adviser reviews claims; no compliance or savings guarantee is invented.

Reuse: One 20-minute adviser interview becomes the comparison guide, the awareness post, and three emails. Assign an editor and expert reviewer. The team maintains those assets rather than committing to daily production across unrelated channels.

Measurement: Define a valid lead as a reachable contact from a relevant firm and buying role; define readiness as an explicit request or verified active procurement need. With a limited budget, run one capture experiment first and keep personal post amplification optional. Retargeting remains a later option until audience eligibility is confirmed.

Decision example: If engagement is strong but form completion is weak, inspect the promised worksheet, visible continuation, and form clarity through AMO. If downloads are plentiful but firms are irrelevant, inspect targeting and the asset's buyer specificity. Do not respond to either problem by multiplying channels.

### Rules that changed the result

R1 narrowed the role and symptom. R3 linked paid distribution to a reachable audience. R5 fixed the empty gate. R6 distinguished credibility amplification from capture. R8 reduced the Content-Hamsterrad. R9 directed diagnosis. R10 removed borrowed CPL promises. R11 kept retargeting and production proportional to resources.
