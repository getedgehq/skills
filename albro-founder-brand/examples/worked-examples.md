# Worked Examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. All companies, conversations, evidence, and schedules are fictional. They demonstrate the method, not real customers, expert case studies, or expected results. Rule citations identify the method applied, not support for the invented facts.

## Illustration 1: Repair shop intake software

### Brief and raw material

Maya builds intake software for independent repair shops. Her buyer is the owner of a busy single-location shop. The commercial goal is qualified diagnostic conversations about missed bookings. In this scenario, five owner interviews describe calls going unanswered while advisors serve customers at the counter. Maya previously helped at a family shop. Assume she has permission to share a nonidentifying version of that experience.

### Weak first draft

> AI is transforming every industry. We are proud to launch the future of automotive service. Our revolutionary platform empowers shops to unlock growth. Follow me for more innovation insights!

The draft has no specific buyer problem, earned perspective, practical payoff, or personal connection. It could belong to almost any software company. A follower request does not establish a path to the intended commercial conversation.

### Improved founder brand plan

**Desired perception:** Maya understands front-desk pressure and prioritizes recovering bookings while preserving the advisor's customer relationship.

| Column | Story and theme | Distinctive view |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer priority | Busy counter service leaves some calls unanswered; measure missed-call recovery before selecting a tool. | Start with calls that would otherwise be lost. |
| Transformation | Shops can move from reactive phone handling to intake that preserves context for advisors. | Automation should protect the human handoff before expanding into more tasks. |
| Personal journey | Maya saw a family shop struggle to answer calls while serving people in person. | Respect the advisor's competing responsibilities instead of blaming staff. |

**Core narrative:** Repair shops should not have to choose between helping the customer at the counter and answering the next booking call. I saw that conflict at our family shop. I am building intake software around one priority: recover the calls you currently miss and give the advisor enough context to continue the conversation.

### Improved post

> Before automating your repair shop's front desk, count the calls nobody can answer.
>
> In five owner interviews, the same conflict came up: an advisor is helping someone at the counter when the phone rings again. The issue is competing demands, not a team that does not care.
>
> At our family shop, I watched that choice play out. It shaped what I am building now.
>
> For one week, track unanswered booking calls and whether anyone calls back. That baseline will tell you more about your first automation priority than a feature list.
>
> My view: recover lost booking conversations first, then decide what else to automate.

The count remains bounded to this fictional interview sample; it is not an industry statistic. The transformation claim is presented as Maya's recommendation rather than an inevitable market prediction.

### Content, distribution, and revenue hook

Create two ideas per column: a missed-call measurement guide and an advisor handoff checklist; an intake transition map and a discussion of automation boundaries; the family shop incident and a product decision it influenced. Publish three short pieces weekly in a trade forum only if owner participation and community rules support that cadence. This example's forum choice is an assumption to verify.

Participate in relevant operational discussions and answer substantive replies. If an owner asks for help measuring missed calls, draft this founder note:

> You mentioned missed calls during counter rushes. I can share the measurement sheet we discussed. Would a 15-minute conversation about your intake process be useful?

The owner requesting help is a stronger signal than a passive view. Check buyer fit and the actual problem before recording a qualified meeting. No message is sent as part of this illustration.

### Rules that made the difference

- The buyer's concrete priority replaced an abstract technology announcement. [S2][S3]
- Three connected story types provided a practical prescription, a market view, and relevant personal experience. [S2][S5]
- Founder voice and bounded observations replaced generic promotional language. [S3]
- Buyer presence determined distribution, and relevant engagement determined a short founder invitation. [S2][S3]

## Illustration 2: Enterprise security review software

### Brief and raw material

Jon builds software for security review teams at midmarket SaaS companies. His buyer is a security leader who must prevent reviews from delaying deals. In this scenario, Jon has observed repeated requests for supporting evidence and a clear approval owner during design-partner discussions. He can disclose the pattern but not customer names, documents, or exact results. He naturally speaks in concise technical explanations.

### Weak first draft

> Security questionnaires are dead. AI will replace security teams within a year. We saved customers millions. Comment SECURITY and I will DM you our secret enterprise playbook.

The draft invents certainty and results, insults the buyer's role, and uses a creator-style solicitation that Jon would not say aloud. It also assumes every commenter is a prospect.

### Improved founder brand plan

**Desired perception:** Jon helps security leaders make repeatable reviews faster without losing responsibility for evidence and approval.

| Column | Story and theme | Distinctive view |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer priority | Teams need reusable answers with traceable evidence and an approval owner. | Fast drafting is only one part of review readiness. |
| Transformation | Assisted drafting may shift the bottleneck toward verification and ownership. | Treat the shift as a hypothesis to test, not a promise to eliminate teams. |
| Personal journey | Jon's prototype initially optimized generation; design-partner feedback changed the design. | Admit the mistaken assumption and show what changed. |

**Core narrative:** I started by trying to make security answers faster to draft. Design-partner conversations changed my mind about the real bottleneck: teams also need to know where an answer came from and who can approve it. We are building around that handoff. My hypothesis is that better drafting will make evidence ownership more important, not less.

### Improved post

> A fast security answer can still be unready to send.
>
> In our design-partner discussions, two questions kept returning: what supports this answer, and who can approve it?
>
> My first prototype focused on drafting speed. That feedback made us put the evidence trail and approval owner beside the answer.
>
> Before adding another generation tool, take one completed review and check whether each answer has a current source and a named owner. Gaps there may explain delays that better prose will not fix.
>
> I expect assisted drafting to shift more attention toward verification. We are testing that view with teams now.

### Content, distribution, and revenue hook

Six starting ideas: a source-and-owner audit and a stale-answer checklist; an assisted-review workflow and its verification tradeoffs; the prototype mistake and the first design decision it changed. Test buyer presence on LinkedIn before selecting it as the primary channel. Jon can dictate rough notes and edit short text posts, targeting Monday, Wednesday, and Friday in the first week.

A security leader's question about approval workflow gets a substantive public reply. A direct request to discuss the product gets a founder invitation:

> You asked how we handle approval ownership. Would you be open to a 20-minute walkthrough using a sample review? I would value your feedback on the handoff.

A like from a student remains audience feedback. An account impression is a weak signal and does not justify claiming its security leader read the post. Track qualified workflow discussions and meetings, not the number of keyword comments.

### Rules that made the difference

- Evidence controls removed invented savings and labeled the forecast as a hypothesis, a package safeguard.
- A candid change in the founder's thinking supplied differentiation and authenticity. [S2][S3][S5]
- The larger shift remained tied to a useful customer prescription. [S2][S5]
- The follow-up path separated direct interest, public questions, and weak exposure. [S2]
