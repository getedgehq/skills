# Film language

## Story before effects

The unit is a visible action: a source becomes a card; a card enters a task; a result takes over. The product is a capability, not a crawler architecture lesson. Full-screen output beats should feel desirable, not like a slide about the output.

Good reference energy comes from short creator-made product films that go from a tiny input to a tangible output. Study their editing, rhythm and structure. Never reuse their footage or music, and never promise equivalent reach.

## Composition

Follow the locked brand kit (see brand-kit.md). In the bundled Edge example, white or pale sky is dominant, ink is primary text and cobalt is the brand signal. Large stable type, tight tracking, generous negative space. Keep backgrounds and objects quiet enough that the next action is immediately understandable. Use the exact logo or lockup as a vector, never a typed approximation.

Separate cinematic content inside a real output from your own brand wrapper. A dark artwork can be the payoff; that does not turn the brand into dark glossy SaaS creative.

## Motion

Use short arrivals, clean masks, card transfers and meaningful spatial changes. Use smoothstep/ease-out for purposeful movement and a restrained overshoot for a snap. Do not bounce every element, stack arbitrary transitions, or apply a slow zoom to every screen. Hold text while a viewer reads. The end card is locked for at least 1.5 seconds.

At 128 BPM, 30 seconds equals 64 beats. A useful starter grid is 0, 3.75, 7.5, 11.25, 15, 26.25, 30 seconds. It is a production choice, not a measurement of a source film.

## Sound

Prefer a cleared instrumental track. The bundled starter synthesizes an original electronic score with no third-party samples. Edit to its downbeats, with subtle selection, transfer and resolve effects. Master around -16 LUFS with a -1 dBTP ceiling and inspect for clipping. Keep silence at the end short but allow the final transient to decay.

The film must also communicate with sound off. Do not clone a person's voice or make the creator appear to say the copy.

## Copy

One thought per shot. A source quote remains exact and attributed. Avoid unsupported numerical claims, broad safety guarantees, "first try", "automatic" or "no prompting" unless the specific evidence supports them. Use normal capitalization in the film unless the user selects lowercase. No em dashes.
