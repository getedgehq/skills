# Story and shot system

Use this reference while writing `STORYBOARD.md`.

## Pick a story shape

Choose the smallest structure that carries the product's truth.

### Wish to capability

Open with a task the audience wants to perform, reveal the product as the
mechanism, show the work changing, then close on the new capability. Best for
products that turn a previously difficult task into a direct request.

### Friction to flow

Show the current fragmented workflow, compress it into the product's new
sequence, demonstrate the result, and state the operational consequence. Best
for workflow and infrastructure products.

### Artifact first

Open on the strongest finished output, reverse into how it was made, then prove
that the process is repeatable. Best when the output is more persuasive than a
feature description.

### Before and after

Hold the task constant, show the baseline, apply the product, and reveal the
changed result with the comparison boundary visible. Use only when the two
states are genuine and comparable.

## Give every shot one job

A shot should primarily do one of the following:

- create tension;
- name the wish;
- reveal identity;
- explain the mechanism;
- demonstrate the product;
- establish proof;
- close with an action.

If a shot has three jobs, split it or remove the least important claim.

## Time-coded development

Describe three phases for each shot:

1. **Entrance:** what creates orientation.
2. **Development:** what changes while the viewer is watching.
3. **Exit:** what hands attention to the next shot.

Tie important reveals to narration words or musical events. A useful shot does
not finish communicating in its first quarter and then wait for the edit.

## Product fidelity

- Use captured product states whenever the interface itself is the proof.
- Crop toward the action, not toward decorative chrome.
- Keep pointer motion purposeful and physically plausible.
- Do not show an interaction the real product cannot perform.
- Present popularity, benchmarks, and user quotes only with their source and
  observation date.

## Review questions

- Can a cold viewer explain the product after one silent viewing?
- Does the first five seconds create a question worth answering?
- Is the product visible before the film becomes abstract?
- Does each claim have a corresponding artifact or source?
- Would removing a shot make the argument weaker? If not, remove it.
