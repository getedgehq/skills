# Three-role production and a reusable UGC hook library

## Evidence boundaries

The documented core is separate creative specialists, explicit visual briefs, early attention, repeated weekly output, and performance-led iteration. Savannah's first-person agency pages establish her creative and analytical background and weekly production service. The public production recap reports more than 200 ads weekly across roughly 50 clients, supported by 40 creators and approximately 10 editors. Those are reported operational figures, not audited results or staffing ratios to impose on another team. [S1][S3][S4]

S5 and S7 are Motion's publicly available summaries of Savannah's talks with extracted examples and timestamps. S6, S8, S11, and S13 are publisher recaps or a promotional customer story. S2's caption confirms a metrics post exists but does not expose its attachment. Do not imply that an inaccessible attachment, full recording, or paid course was reviewed.

The tables, identifiers, capacity arithmetic, diagnostic branching, and acceptance gates below are **package implementation guidance**, not claimed names or exact rules invented by Savannah. The hook library is a curated paraphrased selection of public examples, not a reproduction of her entire catalog. No direct quotations are needed to execute it.

## 1. Keep three production jobs separate

The strategist analyzes prior ads, researches inspiration, writes scripts and shot lists, reviews footage, briefs editors, provides revision notes, and owns the creative through delivery. The creator receives that direction and performs it, delivering raw footage. The editor receives approved footage and direction, builds the sequence, adds overlays and effects, and exports the ad. Savannah warns against leaving strategy with creators or editors and requiring the strategist to perform and edit. [S1]

| Role | Required output | Handoff acceptance | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strategist | Concept, evidence, script, shot list, variant map, final review | Another specialist can execute without guessing the audience or promise | Own the strategy; do not become this ad's performer or editor |
| Creator | Raw takes, separate voiceover where needed, testimonials if genuine, B-roll | Usable coverage matches shot IDs and approved meaning | Do not select strategy or deliver only a finished edit |
| Editor | Directed cuts, captions, graphics, versioned exports | Each version maps to its assigned hook and assets | Request decisions when footage or claims conflict with the brief |

Distinct people are required to follow this package's central production rule. Multiple creators or editors may support one strategist; expertise can cross roles without combining their assignments on the same ad. A creator becoming a strategist on a later project is not a violation. If one person is available, complete the planning artifacts, state the staffing gap, and recommend contracting the missing specialist or reducing scope. Do not present solo production as faithful execution.

Tools are replaceable. Premiere, After Effects, or a board are implementation choices, not the source of the method. The 2021 blog permits accessible mobile editing, while the later production recap rejects creators doing their own edits. Resolve this by separating people and handoffs rather than banning a software brand. [S9][S1]

## 2. Research produces the creative direction

Review actual account creatives with results, then inspect organic platform formats and relevant external ads. Savannah's public teaching emphasizes repeatable formats over guessing what resonates and testing over assuming a universal winner. Her baseline formats include testimonials, lifestyle application, reasons to buy, and comparisons. [S9]

Implementation guidance: each inspiration entry needs a URL or asset link, date, source context, audience, problem, written opening, opening action, treatment, and a note explaining why it could transfer. Separate an observed winner in this account from a publisher-reported success elsewhere. An active competitor ad is inspiration, not proof of profitability. Adapt the mechanism with original scripts, owned footage, and verified claims.

The public production summary describes tracking past successful hooks by client and week and maintaining named transitions with example links. This turns research into usable briefs and a searchable learning record. [S5, 10:25 and 26:11]

## 3. Treat the opening as a coordinated attention unit

Specify four things together: first-frame composition, physical action, written or spoken hook, and the bridge into the product benefit. Make the opening understandable with sound off. Savannah's earlier blog stresses immediate attention, captions, concise messaging, and clarity over clever spectacle. [S10]

The later production recap describes a weekly structure of two overarching inspirations, two written hooks per inspiration, and two visual openings per hook for a smaller client package. Interpreted as a complete cross-product, this yields eight finished variants. The eight-count is package arithmetic, not proof that every client receives eight. [S5, 27:01]

The source attributes an observation to Savannah that Meta distinguishes visually different openings better than text-only changes. Apply it as a testing recommendation: change the scene, action, composition, or performance in the opening three to five seconds. Do not restate it as a guaranteed current platform deduplication rule. Typography-only versions can test copy, but should be labeled copy tests rather than broad visual diversification. [S5, 28:17]

For a controlled hook comparison, keep body, CTA, destination, and other relevant settings comparable. When exploring new personas or benefits, label the test exploratory because several variables can change. Fewer well-defined tests are preferable when budget cannot support eight useful observations.

## 4. Curated public hook library

Names below describe mechanisms in this package. Spoken lines are freshly written for the brand, not copied from source ads. Choose a mechanism only if it supports the product's real benefit and the buying stage.

| Mechanism | Written angle and opening visual | Best use and boundary | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Silent reaction review | Convey a benefit in captions while talent reacts expressively to the product | Visually demonstrable products; expressions must carry meaning without voiceover | [S6] |
| Ironic objection | Frame a desirable outcome as a playful inconvenience, then reveal the benefit | Clear irony; avoid an opening that viewers mistake for a genuine safety warning | [S6] |
| Rapid question response | Place a genuine customer question beside a short creator answer | Consideration or retargeting; use actual questions or label dramatized prompts | [S6] |
| Search frustration | Show repeated browsing followed by a useful product discovery | A real selection problem; connect the scrolling scene to the solution quickly | [S6] |
| Gift reveal | Begin with gift anticipation, unbox, and capture recipient reaction | Giftable products; preserve honest reactions and show the item clearly | [S6] |
| Delayed payoff | Open a specific unanswered question and resolve it later | Short story or slideshow; deliver the promised reveal rather than empty suspense | [S6] |
| Staged response stitch | Film an original prompt with one performer and a response with another | Objection or misconception; do not reuse unlicensed strangers' footage | [S6] |
| Dramatic proof | Demonstrate one benefit through a memorable, relevant action | Physical products with demonstrable capabilities; stay within supported use | [S6] |
| Routine inventory | Introduce useful items in a familiar bag, room, or daily routine | Lifestyle integration; make this product's purpose specific | [S12] |
| Before and after workflow | Contrast the old situation with the improved experience | Apps and services; show a credible process change without guaranteeing outcomes | [S7] |
| Self skit | Film talent as two characters with different concerns or viewpoints | Explain objections conversationally; label role-play and preserve approved claims | [S7] |
| Walk and talk | Begin with movement and a relevant spoken problem | Relatable service discovery; keep audio usable and the destination clear | [S7] |
| Whiteboard or paper flip | Reveal a short problem, comparison, or benefit using physical writing | Abstract services; plan legible text and purposeful reveals | [S7] |
| Search-intent opener | Visualize the customer's practical search, then show the solution | Apps or services with a concrete task; match the destination to that task | [S7] |
| Genuine success story | Lead with a supported customer outcome and explain the journey | Social proof; require permission and evidence, never invent a customer history | [S7] |
| Joy transformation | Pair a visible positive reaction with the product entering frame | Emotional payoff; avoid implying unsupported health or financial effects | [S13] |
| Playful exception | Introduce a lighthearted rule and a product exception with a surprising reveal | Everyday preference; avoid promoting dangerous excess | [S13] |
| Conversational message exchange | Use a short fictional text conversation leading into a product use case | Durable conversational format; do not fabricate real endorsements | [S13] |
| Delivery mini-skit | Start with a small buying decision, then arrival and the reason for purchase | Unboxing that moves promptly into value rather than a long setup | [S13] |
| Unexpected discovery | Reveal the product from an unexpected place with a curious reaction | Simple physical surprise; the reveal must connect to a relevant benefit | [S13] |

S6's 2024 examples and S13's newer recap demonstrate evolution rather than a fixed all-time list. Review cultural fit and format freshness before use. The production summary warns against trends so specific or fleeting that they expire before delivery. [S5, 46:30]

## 5. Turn the vision into filmable and editable instructions

Creator brief implementation: use one row per raw shot, with script line, action, framing, location, props, reference, take count, and filename. Request separate opening options and reusable B-roll. Explain the customer context so delivery feels natural, while keeping concept selection with the strategist.

The production summary describes exact scripts for new creators and more freedom in wording for trusted creators with product affinity. It also calls for reviewing footage before edits and disabling HDR to avoid post-production problems in her workflow. Treat camera configuration as something to verify for the actual pipeline, not a universal camera prohibition. [S5, 11:09, 12:45 and 37:40]

Editor brief implementation: provide a separate version table containing time range, voiceover, overlay, visual asset, transition, and intended attention focus. Link the opening to its matching raw takes. Request pickups when evidence or coverage is missing. An editor may suggest a better execution, but the strategist approves changes to audience, promise, and concept.

Maintain creator relationships and predictable handoffs. The public recap emphasizes organized, fairly treated creators and agreeing broad usage rights early to reduce tracking problems at scale. Record the actual permitted duration, channels, territories, and permissions. Never assume that agency practice grants rights to your assets. [S5, 06:17 and 40:42]

## 6. Operate a weekly conveyor belt

The reported cadence is filming Monday and Tuesday, editing Wednesday and Thursday, and sending four to twelve ads per client for Friday review. The agency homepage independently describes Monday filming and Friday delivery. Friday delivery means client review, not necessarily automatic publication. [S1][S4]

Implementation guidance: finalize research, scripts, product availability, and creator logistics before the filming window. Track brief ready, filming, footage review, editing, revision, strategist approved, and client review. Use a board that records owner, deadline, dependency, and version history. Communication messages can link to it without becoming the only task record.

Capacity is bounded by the weakest stage: usable footage, editing capacity, strategist review, client turnaround, and test budget. Count new concepts, visual variations, copy variations, and finished exports separately. Twenty-five complete eight-variant batches would equal 200 exports in an illustrative model; this is not a claim about Savannah's exact allocation. Shipping more creates learning opportunities only if the variants are testable and results are reviewed.

## 7. Diagnose performance and iterate deliberately

The accessible publisher metrics recap names four diagnostic signals: thumbstop for the opening, hold for continued attention, clickthrough for interest in taking action, and click-to-purchase for the journey after the click. It also describes comparing team predictions with actual results to expose intuition's limits. [S8] The original caption alone does not establish formulas. [S2]

Use the platform or report's actual definitions. Optional local definitions include three-second plays divided by impressions for thumbstop, outbound clicks divided by impressions for CTR, and attributed purchases divided by outbound clicks for click-to-purchase. These are declared reporting conventions, not attributed Savannah formulas. Hold rate requires an explicit viewing threshold and denominator; write both or retain the report's definition. Record attribution window, sample sizes, dates, placement, objective, spend, and business result.

| Observed pattern versus a comparable baseline | Next hypothesis | Controlled next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weak thumbstop | Opening misses relevance or attention | Change opening action or written hook while preserving body |
| Strong thumbstop, weak hold | Body loses the promise or clarity | Preserve hook and rebuild the body or payoff |
| Good viewing, weak CTR | Benefit, offer, or CTA is unconvincing | Test one of those elements with the opening preserved |
| Strong CTR, weak click-to-purchase | Promise and destination mismatch, or offer friction | Review destination, price, checkout, tracking, and audience before blaming the hook |
| Good business result, fatigue emerging | Winning mechanism may transfer | Try another suitable creator or opening without replacing everything |
| Low spend or inconsistent measurement | Evidence is inadequate | Report inconclusive and request a comparable evaluation |

These branches are hypotheses, not causal diagnoses. S11 gives concrete examples of keeping a strong hook while changing the body, and keeping a winning script while changing talent. [S11] Do not call high attention a winner if the business objective deteriorates. Agree budgets and evaluation conditions with the media buyer; this skill drafts a test plan and does not authorize spending or publishing.

## 8. Edge cases and warnings

- **No account data:** produce a research-led pilot with untested labels. Do not fabricate winners or pretend outside results establish this brand's baseline.
- **No physical product:** show the app interaction, service process, or credible outcome. Paper, writing, dialogue, and role-play can make an abstract benefit visible. [S7]
- **Old advice conflicts with new advice:** preserve the durable principle and identify context. The 2020 blog favors short ads, while later public material allows more audience-specific education. Duration is a hypothesis to test, not a universal maximum. [S10][S7]
- **Pacing guidance conflicts:** use enough movement to sustain interest and enough time to read. Do not translate the production summary's approximate three-second legibility advice into a mandatory duration for every shot. [S5, 19:56]
- **Regulated or sensitive claim:** use the client's approved evidence and appropriate review; switch mechanisms if surprise or irony obscures limitations. This is package quality guidance rather than a sourced specialist compliance method.
- **Authenticity without testimony:** actors can demonstrate or perform a labeled skit; they cannot truthfully claim invented personal results. Replace false testimony with observable use or permitted customer evidence.
- **Clever hook, unclear product:** preserve attention while making the benefit and solution obvious. Savannah's public blog warns against sacrificing clarity for entertainment. [S10]
- **Volume overwhelms QC:** shrink the batch until each handoff is usable and each test can answer a question. Report the capacity gap rather than claiming to achieve 200 weekly ads through cosmetic duplicates.
