# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations. Brands, people, capabilities, scripts, and numbers are fictional planning assumptions, not Savannah Sanchez clients or reported results. The scripts are original. No live ads were created or launched.

## Illustration 1: FoldCup commuter bottle

### Intake assumptions

FoldCup is a fictional commuter bottle whose client has supplied evidence that the lid resists leaks during ordinary bag transport and that it fits the filmed bag. Target: office commuters considering a replacement bottle. Platform: Meta Reels. Destination: the bottle's product page. No approved discount or customer testimonials exist. Test budget is unknown, so the batch is ready for production planning and the evaluation remains provisional.

### Weak first draft

Give one UGC creator the product and ask them to think of something viral, film it, edit it, and send five ads. All five begin with the same face-to-camera shot. Only the captions change.

Script: Hi everyone, welcome back. I am so excited to tell you about this amazing bottle. It changed my life. It is the best bottle ever. Use code SAVE20 and buy now.

Why it fails: the creator owns all three roles; the opening delays the benefit; five overlays are counted as five visual treatments; personal experience, comparative superiority, and discount are unsupported; neither footage coverage nor editing direction exists.

### Improved result

Owners: Maya, strategist; Leo, creator; Nia, editor. Maya writes the hypotheses and approves the cuts. Leo delivers raw takes. Nia edits from the approved version map.

Concept C1 uses dramatic proof: keep a commuter's bag dry with a properly closed bottle. Concept C2 uses routine inventory: demonstrate why this bottle belongs in a commute kit. These adapt public mechanisms described in [S6] and [S12], with separate roles from [S1].

| Concept | Written hook | Visual opening 1 | Visual opening 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1-H1 | My work bag is not a bottle test site. | Top-down: properly closed bottle enters bag beside a notebook | Close-up: creator checks the lid, then places bottle sideways in bag |
| C1-H2 | A closed lid should keep this page dry. | Same top-down setup with H2 delivery | Same lid-check setup with H2 delivery |
| C2-H1 | One less thing to worry about on the train. | Creator packing bottle into a commute bag | Creator lifting the bag and revealing the bottle pocket |
| C2-H2 | The bottle that earns its space in my commute kit. | Same packing action with H2 delivery | Same pocket reveal with H2 delivery |

IDs: C1-H1-V1, C1-H1-V2, C1-H2-V1, C1-H2-V2, C2-H1-V1, C2-H1-V2, C2-H2-V1, C2-H2-V2. Eight variants, two concepts, four written hooks, and four visual setups. Reusing a setup across written hooks creates a controlled copy comparison; V1 versus V2 provides the visual comparison. Export crops are counted separately.

C1 body, approximately 3 to 18 seconds: close the lid as instructed; show the bottle placed in the bag during an ordinary commute; reveal the dry notebook at the destination. Voiceover: Close the lid, pack your bag, and take it along. This one fits beside my notebook. Overlay identifies FoldCup and the demonstrated lid benefit. CTA: See the lid and dimensions on the product page.

C2 body, approximately 3 to 18 seconds: show the commute kit, bottle placement, and bottle removal at the desk. Voiceover: Here is what I pack for the trip to work. My notebook, headphones, and FoldCup. The bottle fits this pocket, and I close the lid before it goes in. Same factual CTA. The body stays fixed within C2's hook tests.

Creator handoff: record two clean takes of every assigned opening with both relevant lines; record separate lid, bag, notebook, desk, and product-name close-ups. Deliver S01 through S08 as raw files. Use daylight, clear voiceover, and the actual instructed closure. Do not stage an extreme spill over electronics or make broader waterproof guarantees.

Editor handoff: connect each ID to its matching opening, hold the concept body fixed, caption every substantive line, preserve enough time to understand the lid action, and show the brand during the benefit demonstration. Maya reviews raw coverage before Nia begins. Monday and Tuesday filming, Wednesday and Thursday edits, Friday client review are proposed dates, conditional on product arrival. [S4][S5]

Evaluation: compare hook variants within each concept under agreed settings. Define thumbstop and hold using the report's actual denominators, record outbound CTR and purchase conversion, and assess the purchase objective. No winner is declared before results exist. [S8]

Rules that changed the draft: separate owners, visual plus written hooks, demonstrable benefit, original adaptation, sound-off clarity, raw-footage gate, and a defined test question. Outcome: an executable eight-variant brief, not a promise of eight profitable ads.

## Illustration 2: DayMap planning app

### Intake assumptions

DayMap is a fictional app that collects tasks and lets users drag them into a daily schedule. The client has supplied a current interface capture confirming those capabilities. Audience: freelancers switching between separate task notes. Platform: TikTok. Objective: qualified trial signup. No verified time-saving claim or user success story is available. Two distinct written hooks and two visual setups fit the available production capacity.

### Weak first draft

Ask the editor to make an exciting app ad from an old screen recording. Add rapid effects over a tiny interface and a fictional testimonial: I save ten hours every week. Begin with five seconds of animated logo. End with Download now, although the destination is a browser trial form.

Why it fails: strategy is delegated to the editor; the first frame says little about the audience's problem; an unsupported testimonial replaces proof; the UI is unreadable; CTA and destination disagree.

### Improved result

Owners: Priya, strategist; Omar, creator; Tess, editor. Priya chooses a search-intent opener and before-and-after workflow drawn from the public app and service examples. [S7] Omar performs the problem scene and delivers raw takes. Tess combines approved performance with current UI footage.

| Variant | Written hook | First frame and action | Constant body |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1-H1-V1 | My tasks are in three places. My day is in none. | Top-down hands reveal three task notes | B1 |
| C1-H1-V2 | Same H1 | Creator glances between notes, then sets phone beside them | B1 |
| C1-H2-V1 | Which of these lists tells me what to do next? | Same top-down note reveal with H2 delivery | B1 |
| C1-H2-V2 | Same H2 | Same notes-and-phone action with H2 delivery | B1 |

Body B1, approximately 3 to 20 seconds: Omar opens DayMap, gathers three sample tasks, and drags them into visible time slots. Voiceover: I put these tasks in one list, then drag them into today's schedule. Overlay: One task list. A visible plan for today. Final CTA: Start a DayMap trial on the website. No invented hours saved, personal life transformation, or unavailable app-store action.

Creator handoff: film both openings with both lines, phone handling, reaction, and clean transition into the interface capture. The strategist supplies sample tasks and approved capabilities. If Omar has never used the app, use a demonstrator's script rather than pretending to be a longtime customer.

Editor handoff: enlarge only the relevant UI area, conceal example personal data, and synchronize the visible drag action with voiceover and captions. Avoid covering the task names with decorative text. The strategist checks that the exact app build supports the workflow. The batch contains four hook variants of one concept; it is not described as four new concepts.

### Invented diagnostic exercise

For illustration only, suppose a previous DayMap pilot logged 10,000 impressions, 3,600 three-second plays, 900 fifteen-second plays, 180 outbound clicks, and 9 attributed qualified trials. The team's declared hold convention is fifteen-second plays divided by three-second plays. This gives 36% thumbstop, 25% hold, 1.8% outbound CTR, and 5% click-to-qualified-trial. Qualified-trial conversion replaces purchase conversion because this is the actual objective. These conventions are local reporting choices, not Savannah's prescribed formulas.

Assume the comparable prior baseline had similar thumbstop but 40% hold under the same definition and video length. Priya's provisional hypothesis is that the opening attracts attention but the body loses clarity. Preserve the stronger observed opening and test a body with a larger, simpler UI demonstration. The public case study describes this preserve-hook, change-body iteration pattern. [S11] The aggregate counts alone do not establish a winner between the four variants; results must be available per variant, with comparable spend, audience, and attribution.

Rules that changed the draft: keep strategy with the strategist, visualize an abstract service, replace unverified outcomes with a demonstrable workflow, match CTA to destination, and select the next variable using evidence. Outcome: a four-variant production brief and a bounded diagnostic hypothesis.
