# Worked positioning examples

Both scenarios below are invented illustrations. Products, customer evidence, buyer reactions, and results are fictional assumptions used to demonstrate the workflow. They are not Dunford case studies or evidence about real markets. Method citations identify the rules being illustrated.

## Illustration 1: ShiftLedger, from broad fleet tool to delivery evidence

### Weak first draft

> ShiftLedger is the most innovative fleet management platform for every logistics company. Our powerful AI dashboard improves efficiency and delivers complete peace of mind.

The draft claims universal fit and category leadership without evidence. It invites comparison with fleet suites on dispatch, maintenance, and routing, even though those are not the offering's strengths. It never explains why a buyer should stop using their current workflow. [S2] [S3] [S5]

### Assumed evidence and application

In this illustration, three recent customer interviews and delivery records indicate that regional refrigerated-food distributors bought ShiftLedger to handle shipment-specific evidence requests. They previously collected logger files, driver notes, and emails manually. A demo verifies timestamped temperature records linked to the shipment and an exportable exception packet. None of these assumed facts establishes a regulatory certification or a quantified reduction in losses.

| Component | Revised decision | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alternatives | Manual logger-file collection, driver calls, and email assembly | These appeared in the assumed wins; large fleet suites did not |
| Different capabilities | Shipment-linked temperature history and automatically assembled exception records | Demonstrated against those manual workflows, not claimed unique across all software |
| Differentiated value | A coordinator can retrieve a delivery's relevant records together instead of reconstructing them across people and files | Customer accounts support the mechanism; no percentage savings claim |
| Best fit customers | Regional distributors with temperature-sensitive routes and recurring customer evidence requests | Observable workflow conditions, not every food distributor |
| Market frame | Cold-chain delivery evidence software | A provisional frame requiring comprehension testing |

Compare two frames. Fleet management software suggests broad operational control and routing capabilities that ShiftLedger lacks. Cold-chain delivery evidence software centers the record-retrieval job, but may lead buyers to expect certified compliance or sensor hardware. Retain the latter as a candidate and explicitly test those expectations. Reframing does not permit misleading category promises. [S4] [S5]

### Improved result

> ShiftLedger is cold-chain delivery evidence software for regional food distributors that frequently answer shipment questions. It links temperature events to each delivery and gathers the exception records in one packet, so coordinators can respond without collecting logger files and driver notes by hand.

This explanation comes after the canvas. It makes the present mechanism and target conditions clear without claiming to replace a complete fleet suite or guarantee compliance. [S4] [S8]

### Rules that made the difference

- Buyer alternatives replaced a generic industry competitor list. The real comparison became evidence assembly by hand. [S3]
- Specific capabilities were linked to a customer action, replacing generic efficiency language. [S2] [S4]
- Recurring evidence requests distinguished the strongest customer from a broad logistics persona. [S4]
- The inherited category was compared with another context rather than accepted automatically. [S4] [S5]

### What still needs testing

Ask target coordinators to explain the product back, describe their current evidence workflow, and identify what a delivery-evidence product must include. If they expect certification, clarify the scope and consider a more familiar category qualifier. If evidence requests are rare or current assembly is already adequate, narrow the segment or revise the value hypothesis. These are package validation questions applying the source's iteration principle. [S4]

## Illustration 2: RuleWeave, turning apparent complexity into customer value

### Weak first draft

> RuleWeave is a simple, all-in-one workflow platform for all businesses. It replaces every operations tool with effortless automation and will soon use AI to handle any process.

The product is not simple for beginners and does not replace every tool. The pitch hides its actual strength, promises future capability, and treats small teams and specialist operations groups as if they value the same things. [S4] [S8]

### Assumed evidence and application

Suppose four renewals and implementation notes show that specialist service operations teams use RuleWeave's versioned branching rules and replay facility to manage frequently changing exception paths. Product demonstrations establish that users can replay a stored case under a proposed rule version before publishing it. Actual alternatives in the assumed deals are scripts maintained by an analyst and a basic approval tool that supports only linear paths.

An initial feature review calls configurability a weakness because onboarding takes effort. Applying the method keeps the attribute in consideration until its value and audience are understood. Teams with stable, linear approval flows do not care enough to justify that effort; teams with frequent exception-rule changes may do so. [S4]

| Alternative | Relevant difference | Enabled value | Who cares most |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Analyst-maintained scripts | Operations owners can edit versioned rules and replay cases without modifying code | Proposed changes can be inspected by the people responsible for the workflow | Teams repeatedly waiting for analyst availability |
| Basic linear approval tool | Branching conditions and case replay | Teams can examine exception paths before deploying a rule change | Operations groups with frequent changes to branching exceptions |

The first chain does not prove all scripts lack versioning or testing. Its scope is the scripts and working arrangements documented in this invented situation. A sophisticated code workflow may erase that advantage. [S2]

### Frame comparison

General workflow automation emphasizes ease, breadth, and rapid setup, which are weak fits for this product. Configurable workflow software for exception-heavy service operations retains a recognizable category while highlighting the reason to accept configuration effort. Creating a new category called an operational intelligence fabric would require unnecessary education before buyers could evaluate it. [S2] [S6]

The narrower frame implies implementation help and knowledgeable support. The team should record those requirements alongside the messaging decision rather than promising effortless adoption. [S4] [S6]

### Improved result

> RuleWeave is configurable workflow software for service operations teams with frequently changing exception rules. Operations owners can version branching rules and replay past cases before publishing changes, helping them examine exception paths without relying on ad hoc script edits or flattening them into linear approvals.

The canvas identifies stable, linear workflows as poor fit. It marks faster rollout as an unmeasured hypothesis and excludes the planned AI capability from the present purchase rationale. [S4] [S8]

### Rules that made the difference

- An unusual attribute was preserved until the team found customers who valued it. Complexity was neither hidden nor assumed universally valuable. [S4]
- Separate comparisons kept each differentiation claim tied to a real alternative. [S2] [S3]
- A recognizable subcategory made the mechanism more visible without inventing an unfamiliar market. [S6]
- Current capabilities replaced future ambition in the buyer explanation. [S8]

### What would falsify the candidate

If target teams already control scripts with equivalent replay and governance, revisit the differentiation. If nontechnical owners still require analyst assistance for every rule edit, the first value chain fails. If exception changes are rare, these customers may not be best fit. If no distinct value remains after reviewing stronger implementations and actual wins, report an offer gap instead of writing a more ambitious claim. [S1] [S4]
