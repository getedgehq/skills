# Positioning: deliverable skeleton

Fill with the user's facts. One short paragraph or a few bullets per heading; the whole thing fits on a page. Leave out anything the input cannot support rather than inventing it. Do not add evidence registers, frame-comparison tables, validation plans or review sections: do that thinking silently. The component order follows Dunford's public method [S2] [S4]; the layout is a package convention.

## Positioning: [product], [date]

**Competitive alternatives.** What the best-fit buyer would do if this product did not exist (including the status quo or manual work), in their words.

**Differentiated capabilities.** The two to four things this product does today that those alternatives do not.

**Value.** What each capability lets the customer achieve, grouped into one to three themes. Concrete outcomes, no generic benefits.

**Best-fit customers.** Who cares most about that value, described by traits a seller can spot (workflow, trigger, team, data). One line on who is not a fit.

**Market category.** The frame that makes the value obvious, and one sentence on why this frame rather than the obvious alternative.

**Relevant trend (optional).** Only if a real, current shift makes the value more urgent for these customers.

**How we explain it to a buyer.** A short paragraph, plain language, that a salesperson or the homepage could use as is.

**Open questions (optional, max three).** Only those whose answer would change a choice above.
