# Obviously Awesome Positioning

Based on April Dunford's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by April Dunford. Sources: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md).

## Output contract

Read this first. It decides what the user receives.

- **The deliverable is the positioning itself, ready for the team to adopt:** one page with Dunford's components filled in for this product, followed by a short buyer-facing explanation. Not an evidence register, not a methodology walkthrough, not a workshop plan.
- **Length and format:** about one page (roughly 400 to 800 words). One short paragraph or a few bullets per component. A file is fine if the user works in files; then the chat reply is two or three lines pointing to it.
- **What the method changes:** the choices, not the volume. The alternatives are what buyers would really do instead (often the status quo, a spreadsheet, an agency, a suite they already own), not a list of every vendor. The differentiators are capabilities this product has today. The value is what those capabilities let the best-fit customer achieve. The best-fit customer is the segment that cares most, described by traits a seller can spot. The market category is chosen to make that value obvious.
- **Facts:** use only facts from the request and the files provided. Never invent customer quotes, interviews, win rates, metrics, competitor weaknesses, pricing or roadmap features. When the input does not settle a choice (for example which of two segments wins), make the call from what is there, say in one clause what it rests on, and move on. If a claim would need data the user has not given, leave it out rather than inventing a number.
- **Never output:** a recap of the five-component framework or how positioning works, evidence IDs or labels (E1, observed/hypothesis), frame-comparison matrices, validation plans, test questions, owner tables, implementation checklists, review results, source tags like [S2], or "provisional" disclaimers on every line. Think through the frames and the evidence silently; show the decision.
- **Open questions:** at most three, at the end, only where the answer would change the positioning.

## The method, as decisions

Detail and edge cases: [references/method.md](references/method.md).

1. **Forget the inherited category first.** How the team describes the product today is a candidate, not a constraint; it may be setting the wrong expectations. [S4]
2. **Competitive alternatives = what the buyer would do without you.** Include doing nothing or the manual workflow. Ignore theoretical rivals no buyer actually weighs. If two buying situations have different alternatives, they are two positions; pick the one that matters most now. [S2] [S3]
3. **Differentiated capabilities are features, delivery, model or combinations the alternatives lack today.** Not roadmap, not vision. Keep odd attributes on the table until you have checked whether they create value. [S4] [S8]
4. **Value is the payoff of those capabilities for the customer,** grouped into a few themes, each traceable to a capability. Generic benefits anyone could claim ("save time", "easy to use") do not count. [S2] [S4]
5. **Best-fit customers are the ones who care a lot about that value,** described by observable characteristics (workflow, data, trigger, team shape), not just industry and size. The segment must still be big enough to matter. [S4] [S6]
6. **Pick the market category last** so it triggers the right assumptions about competitors, features and price. Prefer an existing category or a subcategory; reframing or creating a category carries an education cost and is rarely the right first move. [S2] [S5] [S6]
7. **Positioning is not messaging or vision.** The buyer-facing explanation is a translation of the components, written plainly, about the product that exists today. [S5] [S7] [S8]
8. **If no real difference survives,** say so plainly in the deliverable and name the gap; do not dress up parity as differentiation. [S1]

## Shape that works

Use the skeleton in [templates/output-template.md](templates/output-template.md): competitive alternatives, differentiated capabilities, value, best-fit customers, market category (with the one-line reason), optionally the relevant trend, then a one-paragraph buyer explanation. Do not open with a fill-in-the-blank positioning statement: Dunford criticizes writing that sentence before the components are worked out [S2] [S4]. Worked illustrations in [examples/worked-examples.md](examples/worked-examples.md) show the reasoning, not the format to hand over.

## Before you hand it over (silent)

Check without writing it down: every capability exists today and is in the input; every value claim traces to a capability; nothing invented (quotes, numbers, competitor flaws); no section the user would delete before sharing it with the team; no longer than the job needs. Fuller gates: [checklists/review-checklist.md](checklists/review-checklist.md).
