# Hook storyboard template

Fill the fields below with production instructions, not abstract adjectives. Replace the placeholders. Omit optional sections that do not help the request. If the user requests a critique, add the original draft and its diagnosis before the revised storyboard.

## Brief

- **Audience:** <Specific viewer and relevant problem, interest, or shared knowledge.>
- **Topic and takeaway:** <What the video actually explains or demonstrates.>
- **Constraints:** <Platform, voice, footage, production capacity, and any user limits.>
- **Evidence:** <Support for the hook's claim; label hypothetical, simulated, or uncertain material.>
- **Assumptions or missing inputs:** <Only items that could change the opening.>

These fields are package guidance. They prevent a visually attractive hook from promising an unsupported result.

## Hook decision

- **Format:** <One primary archetype, optionally plus Magician. Explain why it fits the visual.> [S1]
- **A, expected reality:** <What the audience believes or normally does.> [S1][S3]
- **B, alternative:** <The supported difference shown or explained.> [S1][S3]
- **Contrast mode:** <Stated or implied, with a reason the baseline will be understood.> [S3]
- **Curiosity question:** <The specific answer the viewer wants next.>
- **Key visual:** <Exact shot, subject, crop, action, and asset required.> [S1]
- **Selection rationale:** <One or two sentences explaining visual fit, relevance, and credibility.>

## Timed opening and first payoff

Use as many rows as the delivery needs. The sample intervals are placeholders, not a requirement to finish all speech in five seconds. Aim for topic clarity by about two seconds and readable initial contrast in the first few seconds. [S1][S3]

| Time | Visual and motion | Overlay and placement | Spoken words | Audio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <0.0 to 2.0s> | <Visible subject and framing> | <Exact hook text; position and target> | <Context lean> | <Cue, level relative to speech, or none> |
| <2.0 to 4.0s> | <What changes, if anything> | <Hold or update; avoid competing messages> | <Expectation pivot if needed> | <Purposeful cue or none> |
| <4.0 to end of hook> | <Shot that confirms the alternative> | <Exact confirming text> | <Alternative or promise> | <Speech remains intelligible> |
| <Immediately after hook> | <First evidence, answer, or demonstration> | <Clarifying label if useful> | <Deliver the first value> | <Continue or remove cues> |

Overlay text identifies the topic or promise. Captions, if requested, transcribe speech and need their own placement. Audio may be absent. Do not create a competing sound cue merely to fill a column. The layered alignment and three spoken functions come from [S1][S2]; the table and production fields are package adaptations.

- **Estimated spoken duration:** <Total words, assumed pace, pauses, and resulting duration; replace with measured delivery when available.>
- **Sound-off read:** <One plain sentence describing what the visible opening communicates without speech.> [S1]
- **Sound-on confirmation:** <How speech reinforces what the viewer sees; identify any necessary explanatory bridge.> [S1]

## Word functions

This is a semantic audit based on the secondary account in [S5]. A phrase may fill several functions; identify where each appears rather than inserting redundant words.

| Function | Actual phrase or supporting element |
| --- | --- |
| Subject | <Who or what is involved> |
| Action | <What happens> |
| Objective or outcome | <What state changes or matters> |
| Contrast | <How expectation differs from the alternative> |
| Proof, optional | <Genuine evidence or omit> |
| Time, optional | <Supported duration or omit> |

## Payoff and handoff

- **First payoff:** <Exact answer or action, its timing, and the promise it fulfills.> [S2][S6]
- **Next beat, optional:** <What follows if needed to prove continuity with the body.>
- **Production requirements:** <Assets, demonstration setup, labels, or recording still needed.>

## Review status

- **Review basis:** <Storyboard-only, recorded playback, or both. Do not imply footage was watched if unavailable.>
- **Checks:** <Pass/fail for clarity, relevance, contrast, alignment, sound-off comprehension, timing, and payoff. See the checklist.>
- **Unresolved failures and repairs:** <Concrete blockers, or none.>

Use [the review checklist](../checklists/review-checklist.md). Method citations in a client-facing storyboard are optional unless requested; factual claims still need their own evidence. Skill citations explain this technique and do not prove the deliverable's subject-matter claims.
