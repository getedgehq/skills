# Hook review checklist

Mark each applicable item **PASS** or **FAIL** with brief evidence. For an intentionally absent layer, mark **PASS, absent by design** only when comprehension still works. An unavailable recording permits a storyboard review, not a claim that actual playback passed. These review conventions are package adaptations.

## Pass/fail checks

| Check | PASS criterion | If FAIL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intended viewer | A specific audience can recognize why the topic matters. [S3] | Replace creator-centered excitement with the viewer's interest or problem. |
| Topic speed | The visible opening or first sentence identifies the subject within roughly one to two seconds. [S3] | Remove preamble or reveal the concrete subject sooner. |
| One interpretation | The wording is plain and has a clear referent; jargon does not obscure the idea. [S3] | Simplify without changing the claim. |
| Key visual | The shot establishes or demonstrates the opening's subject. [S1] | Reframe, construct a suitable illustration, or specify missing footage. |
| Credible contrast | A and B differ meaningfully, and the difference is supported. [S1][S3] | Find a real expectation change or narrow an exaggerated claim. |
| Baseline understood | Stated contrast names A; implied contrast rests on obvious visual context or shared knowledge. [S3] | State the baseline explicitly. |
| Format fit | The named archetype expresses the contrast through the available visual. [S1] | Choose a better-fitting format rather than changing only its label. |
| Layer alignment | Shot, overlay, speech, and sound reinforce the same proposition. [S1] | Repair the mismatched layer or remove the competing cue. |
| Sound-off comprehension | Frame plus overlay make the topic and relevant difference understandable. [S1] | Add a useful label or choose a clearer shot. |
| Spoken structure | The short opening establishes context and reveals an alternative; a separate connector is omitted only when redundant. [S1][S2] | Replace filler with context or a real pivot. |
| Word functions | Subject, action, outcome, and contrast are identifiable; proof and time are useful and truthful when included. [S5] | Repair missing meaning; do not mechanically add all six slots. |
| Attention control | Motion guides the eye without preventing reading; sound leaves speech clear. [S1][S2] | Reduce competing movement or audio and emphasize the key subject. |
| Timing feasibility | Spoken words, pauses, and overlays fit the planned duration at a natural pace. | Extend the timeline or shorten the wording. |
| Immediate payoff | The next beat delivers evidence, an answer, or a usable action that fulfills the opening. [S2][S6] | Move value forward or revise the promise to match the body. |
| Evidence integrity | Numbers, results, credentials, future claims, and simulations have support or explicit qualification. | Remove invented certainty and label material conditions. |
| Review honesty | Recorded checks are distinguished from conceptual checks; production blockers are visible. | Correct the review status and list what still requires footage. |

Timing feasibility, evidence integrity, and review honesty are package safeguards. A clean writing checklist does not establish retention or predict views.

## Common failure modes and fixes

| Failure mode | Typical symptom | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decorative opening | Logo or unrelated footage accompanies a specific promise. | Start on the relevant object, interaction, or comparison. [S1] |
| Abstract category | A grand claim appears over imagery that cannot explain it. | Name and show the concrete phenomenon before its significance. [S1] |
| Spoken polish only | A punchy sentence sits over a weak first frame. | Revisit the key visual before another wording pass. [S1] |
| Vague suspense | The viewer hears excitement without a topic. | Put the subject in the first frame or sentence. [S3] |
| Wrong-audience trick | A spectacular entry creates curiosity unrelated to the body. | Connect the attention cue directly to the actual premise. [S6] |
| Empty reversal | The opening contradicts a belief, but the body never supports the alternative. | Supply the answer immediately or choose a supportable contrast. [S6] |
| First-person detour | A personal setup obscures the viewer's benefit. | Reframe around the viewer unless the personal experiment is the evidence. [S3][S4] |
| Six-slot overload | Credentials and durations crowd out the main idea. | Preserve core meaning and omit optional slots. [S5] |
| Visual overload | Several labels and movements compete for attention. | Keep one focal point and only clarifying motion. [S2] |
| Unreadable timeline | Four sentences are assigned to three seconds. | Read aloud, shorten, and allocate realistic intervals. |
| Inflated result | The draft invents speed, certainty, or a universal benefit. | Use the demonstrated result and disclose limits. |

The final two fixes are package guidance, not attributed expert rules.

## Review loop and completion

Diagnose the earliest comprehension failure first: topic, wording, relevance, then curiosity. Inspect visual fit before spending more time on speech. Recheck only the repaired item and any dependent layers, then run a complete final pass. [S1][S3]

A deliverable passes only when every applicable check passes at the stated review level. If an essential shot or payoff is unavailable, return a proposed storyboard with the requirement clearly marked. If the premise cannot support an aligned opening, recommend a narrower angle or explain why the current concept needs more evidence. Never hide unresolved failures behind a polished score.
