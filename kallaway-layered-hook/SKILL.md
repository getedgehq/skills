# Kallaway Layered Hook

Based on Kallaway's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Kallaway. Sources: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md).

## Output contract

Read this first. It decides what the user receives.

- **The deliverable is the script, ready to shoot:** for a full video, the timed script from the first frame to the end; for a hook-only request, the opening plus the first payoff beat. Each beat lists what is on screen (visual), the on-screen text, and the spoken line.
- **Length and format:** a compact table or timestamped lines. Spoken words fit the duration (roughly 2.5 words per second, so about 70 to 80 words for 30 seconds). You may add one alternate hook. If you save a file, the chat reply is two or three lines.
- **What the method changes:** the opening, not the length. The first frame shows something concrete about the topic; the on-screen text and the spoken line say the same one thing; the hook sets up a contrast between what the viewer assumes (A) and what is true or possible (B); the payoff starts right after the hook, with no throat-clearing. The rest of the script delivers on the hook's promise.
- **Facts:** use only facts, claims and footage from the request and the files provided. Never invent statistics, results, testimonials or footage the creator does not have; if a shot needs something specific, describe an achievable shot.
- **Never output:** briefs, hook-format menus, word-function audits, contrast analyses, sound-off/sound-on review results, rationale sections, source tags like [S2], or the expert's name. Do that thinking silently; the script shows the result.

## The method, as decisions

Each rule is sourced in [references/method.md](references/method.md), which also covers detail and edge cases.

1. **Visual first.** Most viewers decide with the sound off. Choose the most concrete, readable shot that shows the topic in the first second.
2. **Layers agree.** Visual, on-screen text and spoken words all point at one proposition; mismatched layers confuse and lose viewers.
3. **Clear topic, then contrast.** Make the subject obvious immediately, then flip an expectation: the common belief or baseline (A) versus the new reality (B). Prefer stating the contrast when the viewer's baseline is uncertain. Contrast must be credible, not clickbait.
4. **Two to four short spoken lines:** context, the redirect, the reveal. Plain words; subject, action and outcome are clear.
5. **Pay off fast.** The first demonstration or answer comes right after the hook; the hook's promise is kept.
6. **Diagnose existing hooks by cause:** delay, confusion, irrelevance or disinterest, and fix the cause, not the wording.

## Before you hand it over (silent)

Check without writing it down: muted, the first frame and text alone say what the video is about; text and speech match; the word count fits the duration; nothing invented; the creator could shoot from it as is. Fuller checks: [checklists/review-checklist.md](checklists/review-checklist.md).
