# Outline: UK Soft Drinks Industry Levy Extension - Rapid Evidence Assessment

**Document type:** Policy evidence assessment / rapid evidence review  
**Target audience:** Treasury officials and consultation respondents  
**Purpose:** Support consultation response arguing for SDIL extension to milk-based drinks and threshold reduction

**Scale tier:** Short piece (consultation annex)  
**Target word count:** 2,200 words (2,600 hard ceiling per brief)  
**Word basis:** Custom allocation for policy brief format  
**Minimum references:** 16 (brief requirement; currently have 30 verified)

**Main claim:** The UK Soft Drinks Industry Levy has demonstrably reduced sugar consumption and childhood obesity through reformulation incentives and purchase reductions. This evidence base supports extending the levy to milk-based beverages and reducing the lower sugar threshold, though direct evidence on milk-based drinks is absent and extension relies on transferability of observed mechanisms.

## Argument flow

**Key findings box** → Current state: SDIL has achieved substantial sugar reduction (Gap X: but no evidence exists for milk-based drinks specifically)  
      ↓  
**Background** → Problem: Sugar overconsumption drives obesity, dental disease (Gap Y: limited dental evidence)  
      ↓  
**Evidence on SDIL effectiveness** → Existing evidence: Purchasing down, reformulation widespread, obesity reduced (Gap Z: purchase-to-intake translation unvalidated but obesity outcomes imply real effects)  
      ↓  
**Mechanisms and transferability** → Mechanism: Tiered structure incentivizes reformulation; demonstrated across beverage types (Argument W: mechanism should transfer to milk drinks despite no direct evidence)  
      ↓  
**Equity and economics** → Equity: Health gains concentrated in deprived groups, offsetting regressivity concerns; Economic: Cost-effective with minimal industry disruption  
      ↓  
**Evidence gaps and limitations** → Acknowledged: No milk-drinks data, purchase-intake gap, limited dental evidence (Honest disclosure strengthens case rather than weakening it)  
      ↓  
**Conclusions and recommendations** → Extension supported by mechanism transferability; threshold reduction aligns with international evidence; pilot implementation recommended

### 3. Evidence on SDIL Effectiveness (900 words)

**Purpose:** Demonstrate SDIL achieved intended effects; establish empirical base for extension argument

**3.1 Purchasing behavior changes** (250 words)
- **Main finding:** Pell 2021 {cite_10.1136/bmj.n254} controlled ITS on Kantar panel
  - 155ml/household/week reduction high-levy drinks post-announcement
  - 136ml/household/week reduction mid-levy drinks
  - No significant substitution to low-levy drinks
  - Effects sustained at 1 year post-implementation
- **Supporting:** Rogers 2023 purchasing {cite_10.1136/jech-2023-220944} confirms reductions
- **International comparison:** Mexico 12% reduction {cite_10.1136/bmj.h6704}, US cities 9-21% {cite_10.2105/ajph.2016.303362} {cite_10.1001/jama.2019.4249}
- **Note purchase-intake limitation:** Data measure household purchases, not individual consumption; obesity outcomes below provide indirect validation

**3.2 Industry reformulation response** (250 words)
- **Main finding:** Scarborough 2020 {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1003025} controlled ITS
  - 44.5g sugar reduction per household per week post-announcement
  - 33.8g reduction post-implementation
  - Driven by reformulation (reducing sugar content), not product mix changes
  - Price increases modest; sizes/numbers stable
- **Supporting:** Bandy 2020 {cite_10.1186/s12916-019-1477-4} 
  - 29.5% total sugar sales reduction 2015-2018
  - 85% occurred before implementation (anticipatory reformulation)
- **Supporting:** White 2023 {cite_10.1136/bmj-2022-072372}
  - Widespread reformulation across major brands
  - Products reformulated below thresholds to avoid levy
  - Tiered structure created strong reformulation incentive
- **Mechanism insight:** Tiered levy more effective than flat tax for incentivizing reformulation

**3.3 Health outcomes** (250 words)
- **Primary finding:** Rogers 2023 {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1004160} ITS on NCMP
  - Year 6 obesity reduced by 1.6 percentage points (95% CI 1.1-2.1)
  - ~20% relative reduction vs counterfactual trajectory
  - Effects attributed to pre-implementation reformulation
  - Greater effects in most deprived areas
- **Dental outcomes:** Watt 2024 {cite_10.1136/bmj-2023-078051}
  - Reduction in hospital admissions for tooth decay in children <10
  - Effect size modest but statistically significant
  - First dental health evidence for SDIL
- **Modeled projections:** Briggs 2017 {cite_10.1016/S2468-2667(16)30037-8}
  - Estimated 144,000 fewer adults with obesity annually
  - Ex-ante modeling broadly validated by Rogers 2023 empirical outcomes

### 4. Equity and Economic Considerations (350 words)

**Purpose:** Address Treasury concerns on regressivity and economic impacts

**4.1 Equity impacts** (200 words)
- **Conventional regressivity concern:** Allcott 2019 {cite_10.1093/qje/qjz017}
  - SSB taxes regressive in narrow sense (lower-income households pay larger share of income)
  - But health benefits offset when welfare correctly measured
  - Optimal tax rate 1-2 cents/oz (higher than most implemented)
  
- **UK empirical equity evidence:** Saksena 2023 {cite_10.1136/jech-2023-220944}
  - Greater purchasing reductions in most deprived areas
  - Reformulation benefited all groups similarly
  - Health gains (obesity reduction) larger in deprived groups {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1004160}
  - SDIL appears to reduce rather than widen health inequalities
  
- **Conclusion:** Progressive health impacts offset regressive tax incidence

**4.2 Economic impacts and cost-effectiveness** (150 words)
- **Industry impacts:** Jones 2025 {cite_10.1016/j.lanepe.2024.101077}
  - Minimal negative economic impacts reported
  - Industry adapted through reformulation without major disruption
  - No evidence of significant job losses or market structure damage
  
- **Health economic value:** Cobiac 2025 {cite_10.1016/j.lanepe.2024.100993}
  - 21,700 QALYs gained over 10 years
  - £39 million NHS cost savings
  - Cost-effectiveness highly favorable
  - Benefits concentrated in younger cohorts

**Word target section 4:** 350 words

### 5. Transferability to Milk-Based Beverages (350 words)

**Purpose:** Build case for extension despite absence of direct evidence

**5.1 Evidence gap acknowledgment** (100 words)
- **Critical limitation:** Zero studies evaluate taxation of milk-based or dairy beverages specifically
- Searches returned technology/formulation studies on milk beverages {cite_10.3390/foods8120634} but no fiscal policy evaluations
- All SSB tax evidence addresses carbonated drinks, juices, sports/energy drinks
- Milk-based drinks have different nutritional profile (protein, calcium), consumption patterns, consumer perceptions

**5.2 Mechanism transferability argument** (150 words)
- **Reformulation mechanism demonstrated across multiple beverage types**
  - Soft drinks: extensive reformulation {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1003025}
  - Mechanism: tiered structure creates strong incentive to reformulate below thresholds {cite_10.1136/bmj-2022-072372}
  - Not beverage-specific; structure-driven
  
- **Technical feasibility**
  - Dairy industry has reformulation capacity (existing low-sugar milk drink variants)
  - Sugar reduction technologies available (though formulation constraints differ from soft drinks)
  - International dairy products demonstrate feasibility
  
- **Policy innovation acknowledged**
  - Extension to milk-based drinks would be novel policy territory
  - No jurisdiction precedent
  - Justifies pilot/phased approach

**5.3 Threshold reduction (4g vs 5g)** (100 words)
- **International evidence:** Multiple countries use thresholds at or below 5g/100ml
- **Reformulation incentive:** Teng 2019 meta-analysis {cite_10.1111/obr.12868} shows higher tax rates/stricter thresholds more effective
- **Alignment:** 4g threshold would align UK with stricter international standards
- **Mechanism:** Captures currently exempt products just below 5g; incentivizes further reformulation

**Word target section 5:** 350 words

### 6. Evidence Gaps and Limitations (200 words)

**Purpose:** Honest disclosure of evidence limitations (strengthens rather than weakens case)

**Acknowledged limitations:**

1. **No direct milk-based beverage evidence** (stated above; most critical gap)

2. **Purchase vs consumption data:**
   - All UK studies use household purchase data (Kantar) {cite_10.1136/bmj.n254} {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1003025}
   - Do not measure individual dietary intake
   - Obesity outcomes {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1004160} provide indirect validation that purchases translated to real consumption changes
   - Acknowledged limitation in all UK SDIL evaluations

3. **Limited dental health evidence:**
   - Only one study {cite_10.1136/bmj-2023-078051} on dental outcomes
   - Hospital admissions capture severe cases only
   - Broader dental caries impacts unknown
   - Faculty of Dental Surgery concern noted

4. **Long-term outcomes:**
   - Longest follow-up 7 years {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1004160}
   - Adult health outcomes require longer observation
   - Modeling {cite_10.1016/j.lanepe.2024.100993} suggests sustained benefits but empirical confirmation awaited

5. **Industry economic impacts:**
   - No detailed economic analysis of manufacturer revenues, employment {cite_10.1016/j.lanepe.2024.101077}
   - Industry claims of harm not rigorously assessed
   - Milk/dairy sector different from soft drinks manufacturers

**Transparency note:** These limitations do not undermine core findings that SDIL reduced sugar and obesity, but do indicate where evidence base could be strengthened and justify cautious/pilot approach to extension.

**Word target section 6:** 200 words

### 7. Conclusions and Recommendations (120 words)

**Purpose:** Clear statement of evidence-based position

**Conclusions:**
- SDIL effectiveness demonstrated across multiple outcomes (purchasing, reformulation, obesity)
- Reformulation mechanism is structure-driven and should transfer to milk-based beverages
- Equity evidence counters regressivity concerns; health gains concentrated in deprived groups
- Health economic case favorable (£39m savings, 21,700 QALYs)

**Recommendations:**
- **Support extension to milk-based drinks:** Justified by mechanism transferability despite absence of direct evidence
- **Support threshold reduction to 4g/100ml:** Aligns with international evidence; incentivizes further reformulation
- **Recommend pilot implementation:** Given evidence gap, pilot in one UK nation creates natural experiment for evaluation before full rollout
- **Commission economic modeling:** Milk-drinks-specific modeling needed urgently to refine projections

**Word target section 7:** 120 words
