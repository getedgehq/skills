# Source Literature: UK Soft Drinks Industry Levy Extension - Rapid Evidence Assessment

## Search Strategy

This search supports a rapid evidence assessment for Treasury consultation on extending the UK Soft Drinks Industry Levy to milk-based drinks and lowering the sugar threshold.

**Queries run:**
1. "UK soft drinks industry levy SDIL 2024" 
2. "milk-based beverages sugar tax levy"
3. "sugar sweetened beverage purchases consumption intake"
4. "dental caries tooth decay sugar-sweetened beverage tax"
5. "beverage reformulation sugar reduction industry"
6. "soft drinks levy childhood obesity United Kingdom"
7. "sugar tax effectiveness meta-analysis systematic review"
8. "Pell Mytton Scarborough Adams soft drinks" (author names)
9. "sugar-sweetened beverages milk substitutes policy"

Plus 12 sources from Marcus's starter bibliography.

**Total sources:** 30 unique records after deduplication  
**Search date:** 2026-09-16  
**APIs used:** Crossref, OpenAlex

## Source Quality

Sources ranked by: (1) direct relevance to UK SDIL and proposed extensions, (2) recency (2020+), (3) methodological strength (controlled studies, systematic reviews), (4) venue quality.

Priority given to:
- UK SDIL evaluation studies (Pell, Rogers, Scarborough, Bandy groups)
- Recent 2023-2026 UK studies including equity and health impact assessments
- International comparators (Mexico, US city taxes)
- Meta-analyses and systematic reviews
- Studies addressing identified gaps (milk-based beverages, purchase-to-intake, dental outcomes)

## Gaps Noticed

- **Milk-based beverages:** Very limited direct evidence on taxation of milk-based or dairy drinks. Found 10 sources on milk beverage technology/formulation, but none specifically on fiscal policy applied to sweetened milk drinks.
- **Dental outcomes:** Minimal evidence on SSB taxation and dental caries/tooth decay outcomes. This is a known evidence gap; most studies focus on obesity and purchasing.
- **Purchase-to-intake translation:** Some evidence exists but remains an acknowledged limitation in the field; household purchase data (Kantar) does not directly measure individual consumption or dietary intake.
- **Post-2023 UK evidence:** Good coverage with several 2024-2025 studies now available, including Watt et al. 2024 on dental outcomes and health economic modeling from Cobiac et al. 2025.
