# Conclusions and Recommendations

The UK SDIL demonstrates clear effectiveness in reducing sugar consumption and childhood obesity. Evidence from multiple independent studies shows substantial purchasing reductions {cite_10.1136/bmj.n254}, widespread industry reformulation {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1003025} {cite_10.1186/s12916-019-1477-4}, and measurable population health improvements {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1004160}. The reformulation mechanism—industry proactively redesigning products to avoid levy liability—proved more powerful than anticipated and is the primary driver of public health gains. Health benefits are concentrated in more deprived populations {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1004160}, and health economic value is favorable {cite_10.1371/journal.pmed.1004371}.

**Four recommendations:**

**First, support extension to milk-based beverages.** Despite absence of direct evidence on milk-based drinks, the tiered levy structure's reformulation incentives should transfer to this product category. The mechanism is structure-driven rather than beverage-specific. Extension is evidence-informed by soft drinks experience, though not directly evidence-proven for milk-based products.

**Second, support threshold reduction to 4g per 100ml.** This captures products reformulated to just below the current 5g threshold, aligns with international standards, and maintains reformulation pressure. Industry has demonstrated capacity to reformulate to precise threshold levels.

**Third, recommend pilot implementation for milk-based drinks extension.** Given the evidence gap, implement initially in one UK nation (Scotland or Wales precedent exists for policy pilots) to assess actual reformulation response and effectiveness before UK-wide rollout. This generates evaluation evidence while managing implementation risk.

**Fourth, commission milk-drinks-specific economic modeling urgently.** Modeling should incorporate dairy-specific reformulation constraints, price elasticity estimates, and baseline consumption patterns to refine impact projections and inform implementation design.
