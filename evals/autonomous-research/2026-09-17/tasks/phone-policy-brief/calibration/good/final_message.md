Three things from the notes did not survive checking, and I have changed them rather than print them:

1. **The 6.41% figure.** Beland and Murphy report 6.41% *of a standard deviation* in exam scores, not a 6.41 per cent rise in results, and the gain is concentrated in the lowest-achieving quintile with no detectable effect at the top. The Leader's line as it stands overstates the effect by an order of magnitude, so I would flag it before Tuesday.

2. **SMART Schools.** Goodyear et al. (2025) is cross-sectional and reports *no evidence of a difference* in mental wellbeing between restrictive and permissive schools (adjusted mean difference -0.48, 95% CI -2.05 to 1.06). It cannot lead the wellbeing section as evidence that policies improve wellbeing. Its genuinely useful finding is that restrictive schools cut in-school use without cutting total daily use.
