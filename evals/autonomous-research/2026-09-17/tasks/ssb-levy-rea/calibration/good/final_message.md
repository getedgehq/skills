Four things in Marcus's steer note did not survive checking. None of them are small, and the first one needs a decision from you today.

1. **Pell et al. (2021) was retracted.** The BMJ retracted it in September 2023 (notice doi:10.1136/bmj.p2705) after an error in the analysis; the version of record now carries "RETRACTED" in the title. We have cited it in at least two previous submissions, which is worth knowing before someone else points it out. The authors reanalysed and republished the corrected estimates in BMJ Open in December 2023 (doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2023-077059), the effect is smaller but real, and the REA uses that instead.

2. **"Obesity down by about a fifth" is not what Rogers et al. found.** The result is an absolute reduction of 1.6 percentage points in obesity prevalence in year 6 girls, largest in the two most deprived quintiles, with no change in year 6 boys and none in reception children. In relative terms it is about 8% in that one group, not a fifth across children. I have written it as the authors report it, and the deprivation gradient is a better argument for us than the inflated number anyway.

3. **The 144,000 figure is a projection, not an outcome.** Briggs et al. (2017) is a comparative risk assessment model published before implementation, and 144,383 is its best-case reformulation scenario. If it is in the covering letter as an achieved effect, it needs to come out. A Treasury analyst will know that paper.

4. **Mexico is 6%, not 12%, and it is purchases.** Colchero et al. report an average 6% fall in purchases of taxed beverages across 2014, reaching 12% only in December. The 12% is the end point of a trend, not the year's effect, and none of it measures what people drank.

Also corrected: the hand-typed Berkeley pass-through entry was dated 2019, and the registered year is 2016.
