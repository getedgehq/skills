# Corporate visibility deliverable template

Credit: An original implementation template based on the public method of Ilkay Özkisaoglu. It is not his proprietary template.

Use applicable fields for the user's requested post, newsletter, or visibility plan. Keep private evidence and strategic notes outside publication copy. Replace bracketed fields with supplied information; preserve unresolved inputs as explicit questions. Do not make employees adopt this template as identical public wording.

## 1. Deliverable brief

- **Requested output:** [Post / newsletter issue / visibility plan]. State the selected format and scope.
- **Author and role:** [Person, corporate role, relevant expertise]. Use verified credentials only.
- **Audience:** [Professional role, industry, situation]. Identify the people who can benefit.
- **Decision or problem:** [What the reader needs to understand or decide]. Avoid broad themes without an application.
- **Professional purpose:** [Why the author is contributing]. Connect personal authority with corporate relevance.
- **Primary outcome:** [Recognition / recruiting / buyer consideration / qualified conversations / other user goal]. Define what progress would mean.
- **Language and tone:** [Language, degree of technical depth, author's natural voice]. English is appropriate for this package, not mandatory for every client draft.

## 2. Expert positioning and evidence

- **Expert territory:** [Audience + issue + distinctive experience]. Keep the perspective attributable to this author.
- **Konsistenz check:** [Connection to values and recurring subject areas]. Explain why the topic belongs.
- **Core observation:** [One supplied experience, approved example, or sourced fact]. Identify whether it is real, hypothetical, or uncertain.
- **Evidence ledger:** [Claim | supporting source or owner | date | publication permission | limitations]. Add a row for each consequential factual assertion.
- **Author's interpretation:** [What the observation suggests]. Separate it from established evidence.
- **Missing inputs:** [Questions or assumptions]. Do not fill gaps with invented experiences.

## 3. Publication draft

**Title or opening:** [Name the professional problem or useful perspective]. Do not exaggerate the evidence to earn attention.

**Situation and evidence:** [Explain the relevant observation succinctly]. Include factual attribution where needed.

**Expert contribution:** [Show the decision, tradeoff, or qualification the author can explain]. Preserve useful technical substance.

**Reader action:** [A concrete next step or diagnostic question]. It should be feasible without purchasing the author's product.

**Boundary:** [Where the advice does not apply or what remains unknown]. Omit only if unnecessary for accuracy.

**Conversation invitation:** [A relevant question for the professional audience]. Avoid demands for likes or generic comments.

For a newsletter, also complete:

- **Recurring reader promise:** [Specific value readers will receive across issues].
- **Issue structure:** [Connected explanations or examples needed for this topic]. Add depth rather than repeating the opening.
- **Series connection:** [Previous or next topic, if actually planned]. Never invent a published edition.
- **Subscription invitation:** [Explain the recurring value briefly]. Treat subscriptions as a LinkedIn relationship, not possession of email addresses.

For a visibility plan, add one row per proposed item:

| Audience question | Expert owner | Evidence | Format | Planned date | Corporate connection | Response owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Question] | [Owner] | [Material] | [Format] | [Date] | [Relevance] | [Owner] |

## 4. Production and participation

- **Format rationale:** [Why this format serves the audience and fits capacity].
- **Kontinuität:** [Workable rhythm and review point]. Separate cadence from thematic alignment.
- **Conversation opportunities:** [Relevant existing discussions and what the expert can contribute]. Do not invent live posts or identities.
- **Response responsibility:** [Who will answer, clarify, and moderate]. Reserve enough capacity for the chosen rhythm.
- **Corporate and employee roles:** [Who contributes and what boundaries apply]. Preserve voluntary individual voices.
- **Optional Live preparation:** [Topic, guest, questions, rehearsal, reminders, recording plan]. Verify current platform access before execution.
- **Publication status:** [Draft / approved for a stated action / published if verifiable]. Do not imply that drafting sent or published anything.

## 5. Measurement and review notes

- **Baseline and window:** [Known starting data and review date]. Say when data is unavailable.
- **Visibility indicator:** [Available profile, post, or subscriber measure]. Explain its limits.
- **Audience-quality indicator:** [Relevant roles, organizations, questions, or invitations]. Use data the client can legitimately access.
- **Opportunity indicator:** [Qualified conversations or other goal-aligned evidence]. Do not claim causality without support.
- **Next decision:** [Continue / revise / pause production based on defined evidence]. Avoid automatic scaling from impressions alone.
- **Method notes:** [Relevant sections and S citations from the source register]. Keep these separate from sources proving the client's facts.
- **Review result:** [Checklist failures resolved, or remaining concrete input needed].
