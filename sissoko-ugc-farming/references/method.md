# UGC farming and clipping operating method

## Evidence convention

[S#] identifies a fetched source in [sources.md](sources.md). Published practice means the source explicitly describes the mechanism. Package recommendation means an implementation rule added here to make a usable operating brief; it is not attributed to Sissoko. Numerical campaign outcomes are agency-reported claims, not independently verified results. The agency management listing connects Sissoko to GATE STUDIOS, while the LinkedIn post supplies direct personal commentary. Institutional articles should retain institutional attribution. [S3][S9]

## 1. Distribution is the scarce resource

The central premise is that easier product creation intensifies competition for attention. Farming is a continuing distribution operation rather than a single influencer activation or occasional content shoot. A standing creator pool supplies native product content on dedicated accounts, and successful formats receive further development. The operational unit is a recurring production and learning cycle. [S1][S8]

Cheap reach matters only when it advances the product or commercial outcome. The related commerce playbook connects acquisition of attention, a measurable path into the shop, and reuse of assets to improve conversion. Its warning is that inexpensive impressions without a funnel remain unproductive impressions. [S4]

Package recommendation: state one primary business event and its acceptable cost before defining output quotas. Keep supporting reach metrics visible without letting them substitute for activation, purchase, or retained usage.

## 2. Decide whether a farm belongs in the current phase

The headline farming playbook describes 15 to 50 creators acting simultaneously on brand-specific accounts. Each creates 30 videos; distributing those videos to TikTok and Instagram yields 60 postings per creator. At 50 creators, this is 1,500 created videos and 3,000 platform postings in a month. Cross-posting changes posting count, not original video count. [S1]

The farming service page argues that fewer than 15 creators provide insufficient volume for its working model, while more than 50 add coordination costs that need multi-market scale to justify them. This is the agency's operating guidance, not a universal minimum for every experiment. [S8]

For AI and prosumer software, the separate growth playbook usually sequences UGC after a proven PLG motion. It describes onboarding 30 to 50 creators with an initial two to five videos each, discovering breakout formats, and expanding. Effects may take one to three months to emerge. These initial batches and the farming article's monthly production cadence describe different phases, so do not force them into one quota. [S5]

Package recommendation: use a bounded discovery cohort if product adoption or creative fit is unproven. Label a cohort below 15 as an experiment with weaker portfolio coverage. For high-intent B2B adoption, consider the source's emphasis on YouTube rather than choosing TikTok solely for cheap views. Pause volume expansion when the product funnel cannot turn qualified attention into the chosen outcome.

## 3. Creator selection and oversupply

GATE treats creator selection as its dominant creative lever. Its commerce and software playbooks illustrate sourcing 200+ prospects, contacting 100+, and obtaining 45+ actual offers for 15 slots. The purpose is selection and negotiating choice, not filling a roster with the first respondents. Its farming article also describes scouting around 1,000 creators as part of the hidden operational workload, without prescribing that as a fixed quota. [S1][S4][S5]

Market discovery precedes the majority of budget commitment. Compare actual responses, proposed economics, content quality, and audience relevance across plausible verticals and creator types. Cultural relevance and credible product fit are part of the offer; the commerce article recommends leading with the opportunity to represent a fitting brand rather than starting with price alone. [S4][S5]

Package recommendation: maintain a candidate table with category, language, sample format, evidence of platform craft, product understanding, real offer, capacity, and agreed terms. Evaluate ability to make useful native content rather than follower count alone, especially when posting begins on a new dedicated account. Keep backup candidates and replacement ownership visible. Never imply false scarcity or guaranteed selection.

Adjacent practice: the paid UGC article describes sourcing two to three times the needed creator volume and a 10% production buffer. These figures belong to that production service and can inform a proposed resilience plan, but are not published farming contract requirements. [S6]

## 4. Dedicated accounts and sustained operations

The farm distributes through accounts created specifically for the brand, rather than depending on each creator's personal audience. The sources describe niche warming and account trust as real execution requirements, but do not publish a precise warming script, number of days, device configuration, or posting interval. Do not invent those as insider instructions. [S1][S5]

The service page identifies continuous sourcing, replacements, account infrastructure, batch briefing, central review, and prompt payment as determinants of whether output holds. Ask who controls the accounts, who replaces departing creators, who reviews quality, and when payment happens. Creators operate their own postings; the agency explicitly distinguishes this from inauthentic automation. [S8]

Package recommendation: document ownership, operator, recovery access, account IDs, platform, locale, approved brand relationship disclosure, cadence, and status. Use ordinary creator activity and platform-permitted workflows. Do not turn burner-account terminology into fake personal endorsements, concealed sponsorship, automated engagement, account-ban evasion, or duplicate spam. The agency's assertion that farming is permitted is a marketing statement, not an authoritative platform policy assessment. Verify applicable current platform rules when actually launching.

Review can be lightweight enough to preserve creator voice while catching false claims, unusable footage, missing disclosure, broken CTAs, and uncleared material. Hold unhealthy accounts and resolve the cause instead of multiplying replacements to bypass enforcement. These are package operating controls, not additional published tactics.

## 5. Brief formats, proof, and creator voice

Sissoko's JD Sports post stresses entertainment-led formats and execution across five countries. His examples include successive two-option choices, guessing a song or price, and audience nominations of category favorites. The transferable principle is a repeatable participation mechanic with cultural relevance, not indiscriminate reuse of a sneaker format for unrelated software. Reported uplifts in that post belong to its campaign and should not be merged with other JD Sports figures on the agency site. [S3]

The agency builds briefs from funnel evidence: converting hooks, objections that block adoption, and patterns already working. Assets should fit their placement; the UGC service page emphasizes vertical production, immediate hooks, and different cuts for different surfaces. [S7][S8]

Adjacent paid UGC practices can enrich farming briefs when useful: three to five hook variants, modular bodies and CTAs, and separate product B-roll. The paid article proposes roughly 60% blueprint-based content and 40% original storytelling, adjusted to creator strengths. It emphasizes two committed modes: highly native presentation or conspicuously fast, actionable presentation. Its warning is against an indecisive middle that feels produced but lacks a compelling payoff. These are paid-creative defaults, not fixed farming quotas. [S6]

Package recommendation: each brief names a hypothesis, audience problem, hook, product demonstration, payoff, CTA, and allowed claims. Preserve the creator's own framing. Describe measurable product evidence instead of requiring them to pretend to be an independent satisfied customer. Blueprint reuse means adapting a structure, not copying another creator's footage, script, or identity.

## 6. Clipping is a distinct stream

The UGC service page defines clipping as repurposing existing footage, long-form material, and creator output into numerous short-form cuts distributed across accounts. It connects this stream with the claimed EUR 1.20 CPM farming economics and uses organic performance to discover formats for paid reuse. [S7]

Package recommendation: maintain a rights-cleared source inventory with parent asset ID, owner, allowed channels, territories, duration, and edit permissions. Choose moments that make sense independently, then vary the opening, framing, subtitles, product shot, payoff, and platform cut. Do not equate a resized export with a new creative hypothesis.

Track original videos, clipped derivatives, and platform postings separately. Keep parent IDs so reviewers can attribute a winner to its underlying material. Preserve truthful context in excerpts. Do not promise an undocumented clip length, clips-per-hour target, or clipping-specific creator payment formula.

## 7. Read, iterate, reuse

The farming service specifies a weekly read by hook, format, and creator. Winners receive more development instead of being archived. The UGC agency page similarly describes cutting losers, updating briefs from actual performance, and keeping raw footage moving rather than relying on occasional shoots. [S7][S8]

Related commerce practice reuses content across organic posts, paid amplification including Spark Ads, and shoppable product-page video. Storefronts and bio links create an attributable path from feed to shop. These are complementary reuse and funnel mechanisms, not a requirement that every app needs a retail storefront. [S4]

Package recommendation: compare assets over a consistent maturity window and within relevant platform and market cohorts. Record both total portfolio performance and dependence on individual breakout posts. A qualified winner advances the business event at an acceptable cost; high views alone justify creative learning, not necessarily more acquisition budget.

Develop a winner through a new hook, stronger demonstration, different objection, creator adaptation, or localized cut. If watch-through is strong but activation weak, inspect the CTA, landing destination, and product onboarding before ordering more videos. Paid reuse needs both creative evidence and explicit rights. Keep paid spend and attributed results separate from organic farm reporting.

## 8. Performance pricing and honest economics

The farming article presents performance-based UGC, and the farming service describes Layla as priced per user rather than per post. The commerce article discusses view-based CPM and click-based pricing; the broader farming article also mentions CPA-based creator marketing. These establish outcome orientation but do not disclose a universal farm payout schedule. [S1][S4][S8]

Package recommendation: specify the payable event, event verification, attribution rule, measurement window, rejected traffic, dispute handling, payout date, minimums if any, and budget ceiling. Do not treat creator compensation CPM, organic program CPM, and paid auction CPM as interchangeable.

Use these explicit formulas, which are package measurement conventions:

- Program CPM = defined program cost / recorded program views x 1,000.
- Clicks = views x view-to-click rate, where the denominator and click definition are stated.
- Activated users = clicks x click-to-signup rate x signup-to-activation rate.
- Cost per activated user = defined acquisition cost / verified activated users.
- Posting capacity = creators x original videos per creator x eligible platforms, before separately counting extra clipping outputs.

Include creator payouts, editing and clipping, management, account operations, measurement, and any guarantees in fully loaded cost. State exclusions such as product development or paid amplification. Aggregated platform views are not deduplicated people. Use observed events for settlement, not modeled clicks.

The farming article claims EUR 1 to 3 CPM economics and compares them with EUR 10 to 20 paid CPMs. Its own scenario table includes a EUR 5 conservative case and EUR 2 higher-scale cases. At 100M views, EUR 1 to 3 CPM implies EUR 100,000 to 300,000 in whichever cost scope is used. EUR 1.20 implies EUR 120,000. It does not imply organic distribution is costless. [S1][S2][S8]

The article's view-rate arithmetic labels modeled click counts as users without supplying a separate click-to-install or activation stage. Preserve this limitation instead of presenting the arithmetic as measured acquisition. Package forecasts must include those missing stages. [S1]

## 9. Evidence, warnings, and scale decisions

Agency-reported examples include Layla's 72M views, 12,000+ posts and 700K users in two months at EUR 0.12 per user; Lovable's 100M views in three months; and Rebind's 30M views in 45 days at EUR 2.50 per install. The broader 100M+ monthly claim and 12,000+ monthly assets relate to the operator's service output, not a guaranteed single-brand cohort. The dedicated farm example can exceed the simple 50-creator illustration because reported campaign systems need not share that configuration. Do not infer a roster from the headline alone. [S1][S7][S8]

Some sources use different windows or figures for the same client. Retain each metric's source, scope, and period; do not combine them into a reconstructed case history. External company growth and valuation examples in the farming article do not establish that UGC caused all their growth. This package does not reproduce those causal claims. [S1][S2][S8]

Published warnings concern auction saturation, unpredictable influencer economics, sourcing workload, account trust, cross-account tracking, inconsistent production, coordination overhead, delayed farm effects, and the failure to convert attention into revenue. Paid channels still have a role, particularly as amplification of proven creative; cheap farming is not grounds for abandoning every existing channel. [S1][S4][S5][S6][S8]

Package recommendation: define numerical scale, hold, and stop thresholds from the user's economics before launch. Expand a roster only after viable conversion evidence and demonstrated review, replacement, and payment capacity. If account health, rights, attribution, or payments fail, hold the affected stream while fixing the operation. For multiple countries, localize cultural references and measure each market separately. For products with unsupported promotional claims, narrow the brief to demonstrable benefits before increasing volume.

The public opportunity is time-dependent. Reassess current platform behavior and observed unit economics when executing; do not treat a published distribution advantage as permanent.
