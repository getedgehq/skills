# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations, not Patrick Wind case studies. Brands, data, copy, budgets and outcomes below are hypothetical. The improved results are planning deliverables, not measured campaign results. Apply the rules in [method.md](../references/method.md).

## Illustration 1: TrailNest daypack shop

### Brief and constraints

A fictional shop sells a EUR 90 daypack in Germany. Contribution before advertising is assumed to be EUR 36 per first order after product, fulfillment and expected refund costs. Its proposed acquisition target is EUR 24, below that contribution. It has reliable product, cart and purchase events and a product feed. Total test budget is EUR 1,400 over 14 days. No audience sizes or performance data are supplied; allocations depend on eligible pool size. The shop has permission to use an actual customer review, but no expiring promotion.

### Weak first draft

Create three campaigns named Cold, Warm and Hot. Spend one third of the budget on each. Show all audiences a carousel saying: Last chance, 20% off today. Buy now. Target interests for cold, everyone who visited within 30 days for warm, and everyone who liked an Instagram post for hot. Continue showing the same ad to purchasers. Scale any campaign showing the highest reported return.

Why it fails: the hot label is unsupported, the discount has no economic justification, the deadline is invented, the pools overlap, and identical ads do not answer different questions. A high reported retargeting return cannot establish business growth.

### Improved result

**Audience specification.** Proposed windows are local assumptions, subject to buying-delay data. Cold prospecting excludes active eligible visitors, engaged users and purchasers. Cold retargeting comprises light eligible engagement within 14 days, excluding meaningful product interest, cart activity and purchasers. Warm comprises product visitors within 14 days without a cart or purchase. Hot comprises cart or checkout activity within seven days without purchase. Purchasers exit first-acquisition delivery for a proposed 90-day window; replenishment or repeat offers require a separate rationale. If cold retargeting is too small, consolidate its delivery with warm and preserve both message briefs.

**Creative briefs with AIDA copy.**

| Audience | Original illustrative copy | Format, destination and job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold | Heading out after work? See how the TrailNest daypack organizes a short trip. Watch the compartment demonstration and find the size that fits your routine. Explore the pack. | Introductory demonstration to product overview; explain relevance without assuming familiarity |
| Warm | Wondering how the compartments work on a real walk? See the packing demonstration and the verified customer review beside it. Compare dimensions before choosing. View the details. | Demonstration plus substantiated review to details page; resolve practical uncertainty |
| Hot | Still comparing the TrailNest pack? Review the full price, delivery terms and return policy before deciding. Open the product page to continue your order. | Product reminder to the matching product or valid cart destination; remove ambiguity without manufactured urgency |

Actual review wording and feature claims must come from supplied evidence before launch. These invented ads do not claim a customer said anything specific.

**Retargeting sequence.** A hot event starts the proposed clock. Days 1 to 3 allow a dynamic product reminder; days 4 to 7 allow verified proof and practical details if no purchase has occurred. Later eligibility excludes the early bucket. A fresh cart event can reset the anchor according to verified platform behavior. Purchase exits both windows. After day 7, a still-active product visitor can re-enter the relevant warm rule until its retention window expires; otherwise retargeting stops. This is inspired by Wind's published sequence [S7], not an assertion that every user sees both ads.

**Test and delivery.** Provisionally reserve EUR 1,000 for new-prospect testing and up to EUR 400 for all retargeting combined. The operator checks pool size before activating that ceiling. Unused retargeting allowance may remain unspent or be reassigned after review. Test an introductory problem hook against a packing demonstration with the same destination and offer. Track orders, cost per first order, contribution after ads, product engagement and checkout completion. Review after 14 days and the agreed conversion-lag allowance; do not scale from one order. If evidence remains sparse, conclude inconclusive within the EUR 1,400 cap.

**Rules making the difference.** Journey-specific communication [S1][S3], AIDA [S1][S2] and changing messages over time [S7] drive the creative improvement. Strongest-intent assignment, purchaser exclusions, margin limits and bounded tests are package adaptations. They make the plan implementable and economically reviewable without misattributing them to Wind.

## Illustration 2: ClearLedger bookkeeping service

### Brief and constraints

A fictional service sells monthly bookkeeping to small businesses in the United Kingdom. The buying cycle is four to eight weeks. It has a pricing page, a service-fit checklist and booked consultation events. It lacks reliable website audiences and a product feed. Eligible first-party contact lists and social engagement are available, but the hot pool is currently empty. The proposed test cap is GBP 900 over 21 days. Gross margin and close rate are unknown, so no profitable cost-per-lead promise is possible.

### Weak first draft

Copy the ecommerce funnel. Run dynamic product ads to cart abandoners for three days, then an influencer ad. Treat all checklist downloads as hot. Advertise guaranteed tax savings and claim that consultation spaces expire tonight. Set an automatic rule to double spend whenever form cost is below GBP 10. Report every form as a customer.

Why it fails: the events and product feed do not exist, the cycle is wrong, the claims and urgency are unsupported, and cheap submissions may be poor leads. The source provides no exact automated scaling threshold.

### Improved result

**Audience specification.** Cold prospects have no known eligible relationship. Warm comprises eligible recent social engagement or checklist leads who have not requested a consultation. Propose a 45-day warm window based on the stated cycle, pending real delay data. Hot comprises explicit consultation requests or qualified sales enquiries; only activate once an eligible pool exists. Booked meetings move to appointment support, and customers exit acquisition. A completed consultation is tracked separately from a booked one. Record missing website tracking as a blind spot rather than inventing pricing-page retargeting.

**Creative briefs with AIDA copy.**

| Audience | Original illustrative copy | Destination and job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold | Sorting receipts after every workday? See how our bookkeeping process organizes the handoff. Use the service-fit checklist to decide whether it suits your business. Get the checklist. | Checklist page; explain the service and invite evaluation |
| Warm | What would switching bookkeeping involve? Review our onboarding steps, required records and service scope. If the process fits, request a consultation. | Onboarding explainer; address switching friction |
| Hot | Preparing for your bookkeeping consultation? Review the scope and bring your questions about the handoff. Choose a suitable time to discuss your requirements. | Consultation information and booking page; support a deliberate decision |

Verify the actual service process before using the copy. Avoid invented financial savings or testimonial claims.

**Retargeting handoff.** Proposed warm eligibility uses the most recent eligible interaction. Days 1 to 14 offer service-fit information; days 15 to 45 address onboarding and common objections, excluding the early bucket and explicit enquiries. Consultation requests leave nurture for the hot job if delivery is feasible. Booked meetings exit booking ads. After day 45 without renewed interest, paid nurture ends. The longer schedule is a local adaptation; [S7] supports varying messages, not these service-specific dates.

**Delivery and evaluation.** Allocate provisionally up to GBP 750 to cold acquisition and GBP 150 to a consolidated warm layer. Do not fund an empty hot pool. The operator can activate differentiated hot delivery later if volume supports it. Track submitted leads, qualified leads, booked and attended consultations, customers and known revenue with distinct event definitions. Compare an onboarding explainer against a service-scope FAQ among warm prospects. Keep the same offer and destination purpose. Judge lead quality first; sales conclusions remain provisional until the buying cycle matures.

**Next decisions.** At the GBP 900 or 21-day boundary, review lead quality and data completeness before further spend. Request actual margin and close-rate evidence before setting an economic scale threshold. The operator owns audience feasibility, the service owner verifies claims, and the analyst checks lead status at the planned review. No automatic doubling rule is proposed.

**Rules making the difference.** Stage-specific messages [S1][S3], attention to acquisition economics [S5] and message variation [S7] guide the plan. Longer windows, sparse-pool consolidation, qualification events and no invented automation are adaptations. They preserve the method's communication logic while fitting a service funnel.
