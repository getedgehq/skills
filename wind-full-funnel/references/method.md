## 1. Core logic and taxonomy

**Public method.** The recurring idea is matching messages to the customer's place in the journey. Baas & Baas reports prospecting alongside cold, warm and hot retargeting. Its recap recommends introducing the company to less familiar people and considering benefits or a discount nearer purchase. OneCoWork's earlier talk recap instead describes branding, prospecting and retargeting. Treat these as different public descriptions of the same journey logic, not identical platform configurations. [S1][S3]

**Implementation adaptation.** Temperature combines relationship and intent; recency is a separate axis. Do not classify everyone seen recently as hot. A person viewing a video yesterday may be less purchase-ready than someone requesting pricing last week. A cold lookalike prospect resembles a customer but has no established relationship with the advertiser.

| Logical audience | Observable starting definition | Message job | Illustrative creative | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cold prospecting | No known eligible brand interaction | Establish relevance and explain the offer | Problem-led demonstration or introductory story | Discover the product or suitable next step |
| Cold retargeting | Light interaction without strong intent | Continue the introduction | Clear explanation of use and benefit | Explore details |
| Warm | Meaningful engagement or research without a decisive intent event | Resolve uncertainty and establish confidence | Demonstration, verified customer experience, comparison or FAQ | Evaluate the offer |
| Hot | Cart, checkout, explicit enquiry or another defined intent event | Remove the remaining conversion barrier | Product reminder, shipping clarity, suitability or terms | Complete purchase or qualified enquiry |
| Converted | Confirmed purchase or completed acquisition goal | Exit acquisition; consider a separate lifecycle goal | Onboarding or relevant repeat offer | Use, renew or buy again where appropriate |

The event examples and converted-state rule are adaptations. Validate definitions against the business: a pricing visit may be warm for a complex service, while a booked consultation may be hot. Cold retargeting can share delivery with warm when there is no meaningful message difference or insufficient volume. Retain the distinct communication jobs in the brief even if delivery is consolidated.

## 2. Commercial goal and signal inventory

**Public method.** The public podcast description emphasizes controlling acquisition cost to sustain profitable ecommerce, and reports an early campaign loss. It mentions an unintuitive campaign process but does not reveal that process. Do not infer a mandatory launch order from that teaser. [S5]

**Implementation adaptation.** Before audiences, define:

- The conversion and its business meaning: paid order, qualified enquiry, attended consultation or another agreed event.
- Economics: net revenue, contribution margin before ad spend, refund exposure, service capacity and acceptable acquisition cost.
- Buying cycle and geographic constraints.
- Available creative, product feed, destination and observable events.
- Measurement uncertainties, including unmatched users and delayed conversions.

If contribution before ads is EUR 30 per order, EUR 30 acquisition cost is a break-even ceiling before any additional omitted costs, not a growth target. For leads, distinguish submitted forms from qualified leads and customers. Do not forecast lifetime value without evidence. Where essential inputs are absent, deliver a provisional plan and request the specific dependency.

Check that events describe real actions, customer records are usable, and measurement does not count the same outcome twice. Use eligible, appropriately collected data. This package supplies no legal assessment or claim about current platform capabilities.

## 3. Audience and recency rules

**Public method.** The German episode's public description links the consumer's funnel stage and time range to the message delivered. Preserve Full-Funnel Retargeting as the method label. [S4]

**Implementation adaptation.** Specify each segment as an implementable rule: inclusion event, retention window, stronger-intent exclusions, converter exclusion and fallback if undeliverable. Record the clock's anchor, such as latest eligible product visit. An operator must be able to explain what happens when a person engages, starts checkout, buys or ages out.

For a mutually exclusive plan, assign strongest active intent first: converted, hot, warm, cold retargeting, then cold prospecting. Overlapping time ranges require subtraction. For example, visitors eligible within seven days excluding visitors eligible within three days approximate a later recency bucket, subject to platform semantics. Verify what updates or resets eligibility. If a later interaction restarts the clock, document that behavior rather than promising a fixed individual ad order.

Platform eligibility does not guarantee exposure to every ad. A retargeting sequence means planned message eligibility, not a guaranteed watched sequence. If an account's automated audience expansion prevents strict boundaries, disclose that limitation and choose a structure the operator can actually verify. Recheck current tools before giving click-by-click instructions.

## 4. Creative and AIDA

**Public method.** AdsAccelerator advertises AIDA copy guidance. The agency recap explains attention, interest, desire and action, and reports question openings, emojis and time-related language as techniques. Those are testable techniques, not proof that a particular style wins universally. [S1][S2]

**Implementation adaptation.** Build one complete ad per material segment:

1. Attention: a relevant problem, visual demonstration or direct question.
2. Interest: explain the mechanism or use case without assuming prior knowledge.
3. Desire: connect a substantiated benefit to the customer's situation and remaining objection.
4. Action: one clear next step, with a destination that fulfills the promise.

Cold ads should explain why the offer matters. Warm ads should add useful information rather than replay the introduction. Hot ads should address the actual barrier; product intent alone does not imply the shopper needs a discount. Distinct copy must reflect distinct jobs, not three cosmetic rewrites of the same offer.

**Public method.** The event recap recommends consistent landing pages, real customer video proof and urgency in retargeting. Its case account also stresses building a lasting brand. [S3]

**Implementation adaptation.** Match ad, landing page and offer. Use only genuine reviews and permissioned testimonials. Scarcity must be real; if no deadline or stock constraint exists, use a concrete benefit or helpful reminder. Check margin before proposing an incentive. Historical claims about brain hemispheres are not adopted as scientific creative rules. Historical placement combinations are not assumed available today.

## 5. Retargeting sequences

**Public method.** In his published Omniconvert contribution, Wind recommends prospecting for likely converters and varying retargeting messages instead of repeating dull ads. His illustration starts with a dynamic product ad on days 1 to 3 and, if purchase has not happened, switches to testimonials, reviews or an influencer on days 4 to 7. It is an example, not a universal schedule or guaranteed lift. [S7]

**Implementation adaptation.** For every sequence, define trigger, anchor, message window, intended uncertainty, destination and exit. Exclude converters and stronger active intent where relevant. State what happens after the last window: stop, recycle after a defined interval, or enter a separately justified nurture segment.

Use product reminders only when product identification and the feed are reliable. If they are unavailable, a product-specific static creative may serve the communication job. Extend or shorten windows according to observed buying delay and audience volume. Days 8 onward are a local design choice; the fetched contribution gives no complete later schedule. Do not describe aging out as becoming colder automatically if there is fresh stronger intent.

## 6. Budget, testing and optimization

**Public method.** The agency recap stresses ongoing experiments across audiences and creative, even after an apparent winner, and adapting to platform changes. AdsAccelerator publicly presents optimization of each funnel step, automation and scaling as parts of its training. [S1][S2]

**Implementation adaptation.** Allocate budget by available audience, commercial limits and test purpose. Do not force equal thirds or a fixed cold/warm/hot split. Small retargeting pools may need less spend or consolidated delivery. Preserve a source of new prospects unless the assignment is explicitly a short-term reactivation campaign.

For each test write the hypothesis, changed variable, business outcome, supporting indicators, evaluation window and bounded spend. Avoid changing audience, offer and copy together when the aim is causal diagnosis. Do not call a single purchase decisive. Choose a data requirement proportionate to the decision and disclose sparse evidence.

Use diagnostics as hypotheses, not automatic verdicts:

| Observation | Investigation | Possible next decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention but little useful engagement | Hook-to-offer relevance and audience fit | Rewrite explanation or test another relevant audience |
| Clicks but few meaningful visits or actions | Page load, message consistency, click quality and tracking | Repair destination or measurement before more spend |
| Product interest but few checkouts | Price, suitability, trust, delivery and offer clarity | Test proof or address a verified objection |
| Checkout starts but few orders | Checkout friction, shipping surprise, payment failure | Repair checkout and test a targeted reminder |
| High retargeting return but weak total acquisition | Overlap, attribution, organic demand and new-customer supply | Evaluate whole-account outcomes before reallocating |
| Rising frequency with worsening outcomes | Pool saturation, repetitive messaging and insufficient supply | Refresh message, reduce spend or consolidate |

Record total spend, observed outcomes, cost per acquisition and revenue return with explicit attribution windows. Stage metrics describe behavior; they do not establish incrementality. Where feasible, use controlled experiments or holdouts before claiming retargeting caused additional sales. Compare lead quality and downstream sales as well as form cost.

Scale after measurement is credible and economic outcomes are acceptable over the agreed observation period. Increase exposure in bounded increments, review fulfillment capacity and revisit audience/message fit. Stop or revise when the agreed spend boundary is reached without sufficient supporting outcomes. No numerical increment is attributed to Wind.

Automation requires a metric, evaluation period, minimum data, threshold, action, spend cap and notification or review owner. Account for delayed outcomes. Start with review recommendations when information is incomplete. Do not manufacture the exact settings of the three rules advertised publicly. [S2]

## 7. Edge cases and warnings

**Warnings supported by public material:** repetitive retargeting loses relevance [S7]; acquisition cost must be controlled [S5]; an apparent audience winner does not end testing and platform learning [S1]. Avoid presenting these as verbatim quotations.

**Implementation adaptations:**

- New account: leave empty retargeting segments ready to activate after eligible signals accumulate; do not invent pools or divert the whole budget to them.
- Low volume: consolidate delivery while retaining differentiated creative; segmentation is a communication framework, not an obligation to create many ad sets.
- Long sales cycle: use enquiry intent, educational proof and qualified pipeline outcomes; do not apply a short ecommerce sequence mechanically.
- Missing tracking: use observable eligible signals and label blind spots. Do not infer checkout abandonment from social likes.
- Repeat buyers: exit first-acquisition messaging; run separately justified retention or replenishment communication.
- Unavailable proof or incentive: request evidence or propose a truthful demonstration; never invent a testimonial, deadline or discount.
- Sensitive or restricted offers: check applicable current advertising requirements before recommending audience use or copy.
- Historical source conflict: preserve the public difference and explain the chosen mapping; do not claim exclusive ownership of common funnel or AIDA concepts.

Deliver a traceable plan that connects audience evidence, next question, creative, handoff and measurement. An attractive diagram without these decisions is incomplete.
