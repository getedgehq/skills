# Strategic narrative storyboard template

Use this as a drafting scaffold, adapting the number of slides or sections to the requested deliverable. The five-part arrangement is a package convention. It organizes Raskin's change, stakes, desirable future, contextual capabilities, and proof; it does not prescribe an official five-slide format. [S1][S2]

Replace bracketed fields with supplied or verified material. Leave `[needs source]` where proof is absent. Keep uncertainty visible in the handoff rather than presenting a hypothesis as publication-ready fact.

## Brief

- **Company and offering:** [What the company actually provides.] Guidance: establish capability boundaries; this field is working context, not the recommended opener.
- **Audience and decision:** [Role, segment, situation, desired next action.] Guidance: choose a specific buyer before broadening the story.
- **Requested format:** [Deck, homepage, keynote, launch narrative, investor adaptation, review.] Guidance: respect the user's channel and length constraints.
- **Evidence supplied:** [Inputs with source locations and dates.] Guidance: identify facts separately from interpretations.
- **Story status:** [Hypothesis / evidence-backed draft / leadership-selected narrative.] Guidance: label the actual status, not an aspirational one.

## 1. Change in the buyer's world

- **Title:** [Old winning mindset] to [New winning mindset]. Guidance: name the rules of success, not messy versus streamlined product workflows. [S4]
- **On-slide text:** [One compact statement.] Guidance: aim for about twelve words when useful; this is a drafting target, not a method requirement.
- **Speaker note:** [Two to four sentences explaining the change and relevance.] Guidance: answer why it is happening without this seller and how it affects this buyer. [S1]
- **Evidence:** [Claim, source, date, scope, confidence.] Guidance: every material market claim needs its own support.
- **Discovery prompt:** [A neutral question about how the change affects this audience.] Guidance: invite experience rather than demand agreement.
- **Open gap:** [What could disprove or narrow the change?]

## 2. Stakes and old mindset

- **Title and on-slide text:** [Concrete contrast between adaptation and persistence.]
- **Positive future:** [What successful adaptation enables.]
- **Negative future:** [What persisting with the old mindset risks.]
- **Relevant example:** [Verified actor and outcome, or `[needs source]`.]
- **Enemy:** [The old assumption, not a vendor list.] [S5]
- **Speaker note and evidence:** [Explain the mechanism and limits.] Guidance: distinguish illustrative analogy from actual proof. [S2]

## 3. Buyer mission

- **Mission:** [Desired buyer goal state, optionally phrased as a question.]
- **On-slide text and speaker note:** [Compact rallying message plus explanation.]
- **Success means:** [Observable buyer condition beyond owning the product.]
- **Reasonable disagreement:** [Who may prefer another goal, and why.]
- **Strategic implication:** [One product or operating choice the mission would favor or reject.]
- **Promise boundary:** [What the company can credibly help achieve.] Guidance: choose the highest level that still defines a distinctive movement. [S7][S12]

## 4. Obstacles and gifts

Repeat this row only for capabilities necessary to the mission. [S1][S2]

| Obstacle to mission | Consequence | Capability or service | How it helps | Demo or evidence | Boundary or buyer effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Specific barrier] | [Why it matters] | [Actual help] | [Causal mechanism] | [Source or gap] | [Limit or prerequisite] |

- **Title and on-slide text:** [Compact summary of the essential mappings.]
- **Speaker note:** [Explain why the outcome is hard and how the help changes that.]
- **Unanswered obstacle:** [Gap requiring narrower promise, additional support, or research.]

## 5. Proof and next action

- **Proof type:** [Customer story / demonstration / internal use.]
- **Buyer context and achieved outcome:** [Who reached what part of the mission.]
- **Source and attribution:** [Location, date, permissions if relevant, exact scope.]
- **On-slide text and speaker note:** [Outcome plus why it matters.]
- **Limitations:** [What this evidence does not establish.]
- **Next action:** [A concrete continuation appropriate to the requested artifact.]

Prefer relevant customer results in their voice. Without them, identify demonstrations and internal results accurately. Credentials can support credibility here but cannot replace outcome evidence. [S1][S2]

## Adaptation and handoff

- **Shared change:** [What stays consistent across audiences.]
- **Audience-specific mission, obstacles, and proof:** [What changes and why.] For investors, address market, model, and team. [S3]
- **Leadership decision:** [Selected top message, or alternatives with strategic implications.] [S8]
- **Review result:** [Passes, failures, and effect of unresolved gaps.]
- **Evidence plan:** [Missing claim, needed evidence, proposed next step.]

Use framework citations [S#] in reasoning notes as needed. Link actual business evidence separately. Expand into the requested prose or presentation once the compact story is coherent. [S10]
