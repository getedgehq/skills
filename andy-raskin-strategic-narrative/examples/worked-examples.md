# Worked examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations written for this package, not Raskin's cases. All companies, buyer conditions, and product capabilities below are fictional. Market changes are illustrative hypotheses, not research findings. `[needs source]` marks proof that a real deliverable must obtain. There are no invented testimonials or reported performance statistics.

## Illustration 1: LedgerPilot, invoice operations for mid-market finance

### Weak first draft

> AI is transforming finance. Manual invoices waste time. LedgerPilot is the best automation platform, with extraction, approvals, and ERP integration. Join our impressive customers and save money.

This could describe many vendors. It asserts a problem, gives a generic change, claims superiority, and never defines what the buyer must now achieve. Its features have no larger purpose. [S2][S6][S11]

### Working inputs

Assume discovery with the fictional target segment suggests that finance leaders now face cash-allocation decisions between monthly closes. Assume the product can normalize incoming invoices, expose approval queues, and update a cash view from connected systems. These are scenario premises to verify in a real engagement.

### Improved storyboard

1. **Shift: Monthly reporting to daily steering.** On-slide: Finance decisions no longer wait for month end. Speaker note: The proposed shift is from measuring accuracy at the close to steering commitments during the month. Evidence needed: recurring decision requests and independent evidence that the pattern extends beyond one account. `[needs source]`
2. **Stakes: Act while choices remain.** On-slide: Early visibility preserves options; late visibility leaves committed costs. Speaker note: Show a verified peer making an allocation decision before commitments became irreversible. Explain the late-discovery consequence without claiming all monthly-reporting teams will fail. `[needs source]`
3. **Mission: Know today's committed cash.** On-slide: What would it take to see today's cash commitments? Speaker note: The buyer wants an actionable view, not an invoice platform. Some CFOs may find monthly reporting adequate; the mission intentionally focuses on a different priority.
4. **Obstacles and gifts: Make commitments visible.** On-slide: Mixed formats -> normalization; hidden approvals -> shared queues; delayed data -> updates. Speaker note: Explain how each capability contributes to visibility. State connector coverage and update latency. Do not claim a real-time view if the system cannot provide one.
5. **Proof: Show a decision enabled.** On-slide: Demonstrate a commitment becoming visible before an allocation decision. Speaker note: Without a customer outcome, use a labeled synthetic-data demonstration. It establishes capability, not verified external success. Seek a consented customer account of the relevant decision. `[needs source]`

### Rules that made the difference

The external decision cadence creates demand independently of the seller. The enemy is the month-end-only mindset, rather than rival invoice tools. [S4][S5] The mission is concrete enough to guide product choices: a decorative analytics feature matters less than complete commitment coverage. [S7][S12] Capabilities answer mission obstacles and proof is bounded. [S1][S2]

A remaining gap matters: if discovery finds no independent move toward interim cash decisions, reject this narrative hypothesis. Do not rescue it with a stronger adjective.

## Illustration 2: FieldReady, training for distributed service teams

### Weak first draft

> Training is broken. Our AI learning platform has personalized courses, mobile delivery, dashboards, and badges. We improve productivity for every industry. Our competitors use outdated technology.

This insults buyers, piles up features, and makes a universal claim. It also confuses competitors with the enemy. [S2][S5]

### Working inputs

Assume the fictional target buyers manage service teams whose equipment variants and procedures change during deployment. Assume supervisors previously relied on annual course completion and now need confidence that each technician can execute the current procedure. Assume FieldReady supports procedure-linked practice, observed assessments, and versioned readiness records.

### Improved storyboard

1. **Shift: Course completion to field readiness.** On-slide: Passing last year's course does not establish readiness for today's task. Speaker note: Explain the hypothesized buyer mindset change using observed procedure turnover in the target segment. Do not assert that annual courses are universally obsolete. `[needs source]`
2. **Stakes: Prepared for the current job.** On-slide: Prepared teams execute; outdated preparation creates rework and escalation. Speaker note: Seek a relevant verified example of readiness practices improving execution. Show the risk mechanism and avoid unsupported safety or productivity statistics. `[needs source]`
3. **Mission: Qualify every technician for the next job.** On-slide: Qualify every technician for the next job. Speaker note: The mission defines an operational goal. A buyer prioritizing certificates alone may disagree, revealing the difference in philosophy. Product ownership is not the success criterion.
4. **Obstacles and gifts: Connect practice to execution.** On-slide: Changing procedures -> versioned practice; uncertain competence -> observed assessment. Speaker note: Readiness records connect assessment to the procedure version. A supervisor still judges observed performance; the product does not guarantee competence through content consumption.
5. **Proof: Follow one readiness decision.** On-slide: Trace procedure, practice, assessment, and assignment. Speaker note: Demonstrate the chain and label simulated records. For customer proof, document a comparable team's operational outcome rather than merely showing completion rates. `[needs source]`

### Audience adaptation

For technicians, emphasize knowing what the next job requires. For operations leaders, emphasize confidence in assigning qualified staff. Keep the same shift and connect both goals instead of inventing unrelated brand stories. For investors, preserve the shift and add evidence about market size, business model, and team; leave unavailable figures as gaps. [S3]

### Rules that made the difference

The change explains why training features matter. The old mindset is treating completion as sufficient readiness, not a competitor's technology stack. [S4][S5] The mission balances a larger operational goal with observable specificity. [S7] Proof must follow the mission all the way to an execution decision, and concise slides force removal of unrelated badges and personalization claims. [S1][S10]
