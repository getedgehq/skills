# Worked examples

These are invented illustrations, not YOYABA client cases, Rath quotations, or forecasts. All organizations, numbers, budgets, thresholds, owners, and dates below are fictional. Rules refer to [the method](../references/method.md).

## Illustration 1: Sales-led software with apparently cheap leads

### Brief and available evidence

LedgerFlow sells invoice automation to mid-market finance teams. Average new contract ARR is EUR 18,000; the typical sales cycle is 90 days. Monthly paid spend is EUR 24,000. Marketing reports 240 form submissions, but only 12 became sales-accepted opportunities. Thirty-five percent of forms lack source data. Sales reports that routing can take four days and many prospects misunderstand the product's integration requirements. The CRM links neither campaign identifiers nor duplicate contacts consistently.

These inputs support a routing, fit, and measurement diagnosis. They do not prove that paid demand is intrinsically unprofitable.

### Weak first draft

Increase LinkedIn spend by 50 percent because CPL is EUR 100. Publish ten SEO articles each month and measure clicks. Add a dashboard to show performance. Set a target of doubling pipeline next month.

### Improved result

**Commercial diagnosis:** The limiting constraint is the conversion of recorded interest into qualified progress, compounded by unreliable attribution. Media-only cost per submission is EUR 100; that is neither CAC nor proof of quality. A next-month revenue promise conflicts with the stated purchase cycle.

**Metric contract:** An opportunity counts only after sales confirms ICP fit, integration feasibility, a relevant use case, an estimated contract amount, and an accountable buyer next step. Report new qualified pipeline as opportunity ARR, with duplicates excluded. Closed-won ARR remains a separate result.

**RevOps action:** Mira, the RevOps lead, audits a sample of 30 recent journeys by 16 October. She repairs campaign fields, deduplication, and assignment rules. Sales manager Leon agrees a one-business-day follow-up standard for this experiment and logs rejection reasons. This timing is a fictional team agreement, not a Rath rule.

**Integrated initiative:** Content lead Ava interviews sales and three customers, then creates an integration-readiness guide, a relevant use-case page, and an expert video explaining the finance team's operational problem. Paid lead Sam amplifies the video to the same ICP and routes interested buyers to the guide and a clear assessment offer. Sales receives the submitted readiness context alongside the inquiry.

**Bounded experiment:** Keep the existing EUR 24,000 monthly media ceiling. Reallocate EUR 4,000 from demonstrably weak campaigns after source validation. Review data readiness and routing first, buyer fit after four weeks, and commercial progression after a full sales-cycle window. Scale only if the source-specific mature cohorts support the team's acquisition economics. If fit remains poor, revise the message and audience. If the sample remains too small, preserve the spending ceiling and report the uncertainty.

**Measurement:** Keep technical and self-reported sources separate. Report submission-to-accepted-opportunity conversion and pipeline amount alongside spend. Do not add influenced pipeline to sourced pipeline. Compare cohorts under the same definitions and maturity windows.

### Rules that made the difference

- Method sections 1 and 3: Fix the binding constraint and make handoffs observable before adding traffic.
- Sections 2 and 6: Define commercial measures and retain attribution limitations.
- Sections 4 and 5: Reuse buyer insight across educational content, paid distribution, and sales follow-up.
- Section 7: Respect low certainty and sales-cycle lag; do not promise borrowed uplift.

The improved result coordinates all three capabilities while concentrating immediate effort on RevOps and buyer fit. It is a plan to test, not a claim of achieved improvement.

## Illustration 2: PLG software with misleading signup growth

### Brief and available evidence

RosterNest sells self-serve scheduling software at EUR 80 per month. Average monthly gross profit per retained new customer is EUR 64. The team sets a six-month CAC payback ceiling, implying a planning CAC ceiling of EUR 384, assuming that gross profit remains stable. This simplification excludes churn effects and needs cohort validation.

The established search program produces customers below that ceiling. A new social test spends EUR 6,000 and records 400 signups. Only 40 users activate and four become paying customers within the available observation window. Some cohorts remain immature. Brand search increases, but there is no controlled evidence showing what caused it. Campaign identifiers do not persist reliably between web signup and product usage.

### Weak first draft

Social delivers EUR 15 signups, so increase its budget immediately. Launch LinkedIn, YouTube, and Reddit together. Give Google all revenue credit because it wins last click. Ask the organic team to write general productivity articles.

### Improved result

**Commercial diagnosis:** Cheap signups conceal weak downstream progress. Observed media-only customer acquisition cost is EUR 1,500 for the test window, but immature cohorts and incomplete source persistence prevent a settled channel conclusion. Brand search growth is a supporting signal, not proof of incremental social revenue.

**RevOps and product action:** Noor, the operations owner, links web, product, and billing identities, preserves source fields, and separates mature from immature cohorts. Product lead Eli defines activation as publishing a usable staff schedule and inviting two colleagues within seven days. This invented definition reflects the product's value moment and must be tested against retention.

**Organic action:** Content owner Jo creates a practical first-schedule walkthrough, a shift-handover example, and an objection page explaining migration from spreadsheets. These assets address observed buyer needs and support onboarding as well as discovery. The team checks whether users from search and social encounter the same activation obstacle.

**Paid action:** Maintain the established search campaigns that meet the agreed economic target. Run one EUR 2,000 follow-up social test over four weeks using a customer demonstration and the revised onboarding path. Choose the platform where the target small-business managers can be reached economically; do not default to an expensive B2B platform merely because the product is business software.

**Decision rules:** Review activation before expanding channels. If activation remains weak across sources, prioritize product and onboarding. If it is healthy for search but weak for social, reassess audience, expectations, and creative. Stop this test at the cap if the evidence cannot support continued spending; do not treat extra time as an automatic budget increase. Assess mature paid conversion and gross-margin payback once cohorts have sufficient time.

**Measurement:** Report cost per activation, activation-to-paid conversion, retained customer gross profit, and blended acquisition efficiency. Pair recorded sources with self-reported discovery. Avoid concluding that last-click search generated all demand or that every brand-search increase came from the test.

### Rules that made the difference

- Section 2: Define denominators, cost scope, cohort maturity, and gross-margin economics.
- Section 6: Adapt to PLG and keep activation separate from signup volume.
- Sections 4 and 5: Connect useful education to creative and product expectations.
- Section 7: Preserve a working channel, limit diversification, and diagnose product friction before buying more traffic.

The improved result uses the three capabilities together and makes the next spending decision reviewable without inventing attribution certainty.
