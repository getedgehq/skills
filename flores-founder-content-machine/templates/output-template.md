# Content operating brief template

Use only the fields needed for the assignment. Keep supporting evidence and internal approval notes separate from the public draft. Bracketed fields are intentional fill-in prompts, not completed claims. The phase labels and diagnostic metrics are implementation aids described in [method.md](../references/method.md).

## 1. Assignment

- Executive and role: [Name and responsibility]. Identify who can legitimately own the perspective.
- Requested deliverable: [Interview brief / draft / program / handoff]. Preserve the user's scope.
- Audience and decision: [Specific reader facing a specific choice]. Explain what the piece should help them decide.
- Business objective: [Desired change]. State why this audience and decision matter.
- Success evidence: [What observable response would indicate progress]. Agree this before choosing topics; avoid promising revenue from posting.
- Channel and constraints: [Format, length, timing, disclosure limits]. Use the actual publishing context.

## 2. Constraint diagnosis

- Current symptom: [What is happening]. Point to a draft, interview note, or workflow observation.
- Main constraint: [Articulation / editorial flattening / execution / mixed]. Explain the evidence for the classification.
- Current phase and proposed phase: [A, B, C, or D]. Distinguish what already works from what still needs challenge.
- Evidence gaps: [Missing facts or context]. Mark unresolved items without filling them with assumptions.

## 3. Context and executive lanes

- Product premise: [What the company does and the position it consistently supports]. Use reader-understandable language.
- Buyer problem and alternative: [Current struggle and what the reader does today]. Include relevant technical or commercial nuance.
- Executive lane: [Decisions, expertise, and experience this person owns]. For co-founders, repeat this field for each person under the same product premise.
- Context sources: [Authorized documents, calls, expert notes]. State what was actually reviewed.

## 4. Interview agenda

- Candidate episode: [A decision, failure, or changed assumption]. Start with experience rather than an abstract theme.
- Opening question: [What happened and what was at stake?]. Avoid suggesting the conclusion.
- Follow-ups: [Initial belief, alternatives, chosen path, accepted cost, outcome]. Recover judgment.
- Outside challenge: [Reader misunderstanding, strongest objection, exception]. Test whether internal context is concealing the useful point.
- Fact and permission questions: [What needs verification or clearance?]. Resolve concrete issues.

## 5. Idea card

Repeat for each selected idea.

- Idea identifier: [Short label]. Link notes, draft, and review to the same record.
- Reader decision: [The choice this idea illuminates]. Keep one main decision per piece.
- Founder position: [A supported, bounded claim]. Attribute it only when the executive has supplied or endorsed it.
- Situation and previous assumption: [What the founder initially expected]. Establish the reasoning context.
- Alternatives and choice: [Available paths and selected path]. Include the rejected option fairly.
- Trade-off: [Cost or downside accepted]. Make the decision useful rather than celebratory.
- Evidence and source location: [Transcript timestamp, note paragraph, or document]. Distinguish observation from inference.
- Exception or uncertainty: [Where the position might not apply]. Keep meaningful limits in the draft.
- Missing support: [Open question or none]. Hold unsupported claims.

## 6. Draft and editorial rationale

[Insert public-facing draft here. Present the situation, the choice, why it mattered, and a reader implication in the executive's endorsed voice. Omit internal evidence labels from publishable copy.]

- Judgment preserved: [The sentence or passage that explains how the executive decides]. Compare it with the idea card.
- Change from weak draft: [What became specific]. Explain the material revision.
- Review objections and resolution: [Concrete issue, correction, or held claim]. Do not replace a defensible position with generic consensus language.
- Approval state: [Draft / awaiting verification / approved for intended use]. Never infer publication authorization from completion of this template.

## 7. Operating ownership

| Responsibility | Named owner | Input and output | Agreed timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Context preparation | [Owner] | [Context brief] | [Timing] |
| Interview and challenge | [Owner] | [Agenda to supported idea cards] | [Timing] |
| Drafting and production | [Owner] | [Cards to channel-ready draft] | [Timing] |
| Fact and representation review | [Owner] | [Verified draft and resolved issues] | [Timing] |
| Final approval and distribution | [Owner] | [Authorized content to chosen channel] | [Timing] |

Name actual owners or leave ownership explicitly unassigned. Choose cadence with the user rather than treating a fixed posting schedule as part of Flores's method.

## 8. Learning and handoff

- Reader response: [Comprehension, relevant questions, or business-relevant conversation]. Use available observations; reach alone does not establish useful thought leadership.
- Founder clarification workload: [Where the founder had to rescue meaning]. Diagnose articulation or training needs.
- Judgment lost during review: [Position removed and reason]. Identify recurring editorial flattening.
- Next task to transfer: [Specific responsibility]. Transfer production separately from articulation when useful.
- Readiness gate: [Observable evidence needed]. Include articulated perspective, consistent company positions, and external clarity.
- Challenge mechanism after transfer: [Who tests assumptions and when]. Specify independent critique proportionate to unsettled topics.
- Reversion trigger: [New narrative uncertainty or quality regression]. Reopen the relevant earlier phase when evidence supports it.
- Next action: [Owner, task, and agreed date]. State what resolves the most important remaining gap.
