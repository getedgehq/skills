# Method and operating rules

Credit: Adapted in original language from Jay Flores's public posts. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Jay Flores. Source keys refer to [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Core principles and attribution

### Articulation can be the bottleneck

A founder who constantly switches between product, hiring, and commercial decisions may carry the company narrative mostly in their head. Adding an internal producer does not automatically convert that context into something outsiders understand. Diagnose whether the founder needs help making their thinking explicit before treating the problem as insufficient writing capacity. [S1]

### Preserve knowledgeable distance

The useful outside collaborator learns the product, market, and failure modes while continuing to question assumptions that employees may share. Distance alone is insufficient: someone without context can misunderstand the executive. Familiarity alone is also insufficient: someone who only agrees can reproduce the internal bubble. Flores describes a continuing relationship of roughly two and a half years in which this balance remained useful as the company developed. That duration is descriptive, not a required engagement length. [S1]

### Protect judgment rather than decorate expertise

Executive experience often includes specific failures, consequential trade-offs, and actions the leader would avoid repeating. Editorial systems can suppress that material when they optimize mainly for consensus, shipping, reputational comfort, or engagement. The task is to make the executive's reasoning and boundaries explicit while retaining a defensible position. It is not to add artificial controversy or make unsupported claims sound confident. [S2]

### Start with goals and learn the nuance

Flores's older ideation account combines structured onboarding with active dialogue involving the founder, technical experts, and sometimes sales and marketing. Success criteria guide strategy. Sophisticated products and multiple verticals require enough understanding to explain benefits and buyer problems accurately. A questionnaire begins the work; it does not replace follow-up conversations. [S3]

### Share the product direction, distinguish the people

For two co-founders, Flores describes one shared product strategy plus separate strategies reflecting each leader's expertise and views. A CEO's commercial perspective and a CTO's technical perspective may illuminate different choices. Consistency about the product does not require identical voices or duplicated opinions. [S4]

### Document responsibilities before transferring them

Flores describes faster onboarding where editing and copywriting roles were documented, then the need to explain a broader creative director role encompassing strategy, ideation, recording, coaching, assignment, and brand representation. Use that separation to make responsibilities visible. This source concerns staffing at an agency; applying it to a client-side handoff is a package adaptation. [S5]

## 2. Diagnose before choosing an operating model

Use the following tests as implementation aids, not published Flores terminology:

| Observed symptom | Likely constraint | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder explains the idea clearly only after repeated probing | Articulation | Keep an outside interviewer involved and capture reasoning |
| Interview contains specific decisions but approved draft does not | Editorial flattening | Trace the removed position and resolve the review objection |
| Clear drafts exist but queue stalls in editing or scheduling | Execution | Document and transfer the production task |
| Readers need insider vocabulary to understand the post | Context bubble | Test unfamiliar-reader comprehension and rewrite the explanation |
| Two executives produce interchangeable posts | Voice collapse | Define shared product facts and separate expertise lanes |

A company's funding stage, headcount, or marketing hire is not a sufficient readiness test. The Series B narrative in S1 specifically shows that growth alone does not eliminate the benefit of outside challenge. [S1]

## 3. Onboarding and audience context

Collect the minimum context needed for the assignment:

- Which reader is being addressed, what they are deciding, and what they already understand.
- Which product or business problem matters to that reader, including current alternatives.
- What the company wants content to accomplish and how it will recognize progress.
- Which executive has relevant direct experience and which expert can verify technical detail.
- What recent decisions, failures, objections, or market changes are worth examining.
- Which topics or details require permission before disclosure.

The goal and expert-input requirements derive from S3. The intake fields are practical additions. Do not force a full company questionnaire for a narrow draft review. For multiple verticals, select one reader and use case per piece unless the comparison itself is the insight. Describe technical mechanisms sufficiently to make the benefit believable. [S3]

For multiple executives, record a shared product premise and an individual experience lane for each. Do not invent disagreement for differentiation. If the leaders disagree substantively, clarify the different conditions or reconcile the factual conflict before presenting a company position. [S4]

## 4. Outside interview protocol

The sequence below operationalizes the challenge-and-articulation principles in S1 and the judgment-preservation principle in S2. Flores does not publish this exact question list in the fetched posts.

1. Locate a concrete episode: What decision did you face? What was at stake?
2. Recover the initial model: What did you believe before that happened?
3. Identify alternatives: What other path was available? Why did it look attractive?
4. Surface judgment: What made you choose this path? What trade-off did you accept?
5. Test evidence: What happened, and how do you know? What remains uncertain?
6. Find a boundary: Under what conditions would you recommend the opposite choice?
7. Translate for a reader: What would a smart person outside the company misunderstand?
8. Pressure-test the conclusion: What is the strongest reasonable objection?
9. Confirm the learning: What do you now do differently, and why?

Pause on abstractions such as transformation, innovation, customer centricity, and operational excellence. Ask what changed in a real decision. Do not substitute a stronger slogan for unfinished thinking. Do not supply the answer in the question. Constructive challenge asks for reasons and exceptions; performative disagreement demands a provocative sound bite. [S1][S2]

Maintain an idea card containing the reader, decision, original assumption, alternatives, choice, evidence, current position, exception, and source location. If the material is not available, produce questions and a draft outline with explicit gaps. Never present an invented interview as an actual founder statement.

## 5. Editorial decision rules

A useful draft lets a reader understand what the founder believes, why, and when that belief applies. Prefer one specific position with context over several broad leadership lessons. Include enough of the rejected alternative to make the reasoning useful. These writing tests are package applications of S2, not an attributed copywriting formula.

Apply three passes:

- **Substance:** Is the position supported by the founder's actual account? Does the draft preserve the trade-off and limit?
- **External clarity:** Can someone outside the company follow the situation without undocumented assumptions? [S1]
- **Representation:** Does the executive endorse the reasoning, and do technical or commercial details accurately represent the business? [S3][S5]

If a reviewer wants to remove a sharp sentence, ask which concrete issue it creates: factual error, confidential disclosure, personal attack, unsupported generalization, or simply discomfort with a position. Correct the real issue while preserving a supportable judgment. If that cannot be done, hold the claim or choose another angle. Flores's criticism of risk avoidance does not establish permission to reveal confidential material or bypass required approvals. [S2]

Do not invent metrics, named customer outcomes, motives, or founder history. Keep claim support in working notes and remove research annotations from the public draft where appropriate. Paraphrasing preserves meaning; it must not convert a conditional belief into a universal law.

## 6. Readiness-based path to internal ownership

Flores supports a later transition when the founder's perspective is articulated, the company knows its positions, and content reinforces established clarity. He warns against removing outside challenge while thinking is still forming. He does not specify the following four phases or their labels. They are this package's operating design, drawing on S1 and the documentation lesson in S5.

| Phase | Ownership | Evidence needed to advance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Discover the perspective | Founder supplies experience; outside interviewer challenges and helps articulate; production is assigned to available owners | Supported idea cards and recurring positions that the founder can explain clearly |
| B. Transfer production | Internal owner handles documented editing, formatting, coordination, and distribution; outside interviewer continues extraction and challenge | Internal drafts preserve reasoning, evidence, and outsider comprehension |
| C. Co-own articulation | Internal interviewer leads; outside collaborator reviews assumptions and difficult pieces | Company positions are stable and the internal interviewer can probe without merely seeking agreement |
| D. Internal ownership with a challenge mechanism | Internal owner runs the program; company decides how to retain independent critique | Content reinforces clarity, readers understand it, and specific judgment survives review |

Agree a review window with the user rather than imposing a calendar. At each gate, compare interview notes with final drafts, ask the executive what required clarification, and collect reader comprehension feedback where available. Track founder clarification workload and reasons positions were removed. These are diagnostic additions, not Flores's published KPIs.

Keep a hybrid arrangement if production is mature but perspective formation still needs outside challenge. Do not treat outsourcing as a permanent requirement. If the internal team has strong interview capability and stable positions, progression is reasonable. If a pivot or new market makes the narrative unsettled, revisit the earlier articulation work for that topic without undoing competent internal production. [S1]

## 7. Edge cases and warnings

- **Founder is unavailable:** Prepare a narrow question brief, use existing authorized notes, and mark gaps. The agent cannot simulate the founder's judgment as evidence.
- **Founder wants viral content:** Reconnect topic selection to the agreed goal. Reach can be reported, but S2 warns against optimizing away judgment for engagement.
- **Outside interviewer knows little:** Improve context first. Outsider status is not evidence of interview quality. [S1][S3]
- **Long engagement becomes an echo chamber:** Invite a fresh challenge to the assumptions and reader language. Independence is a function of behavior, not a contract label. This is an inference from S1.
- **Every draft requires founder rescue:** Reopen articulation and role training before expanding volume. More producers may multiply clarification work. [S1][S5]
- **Sensitive experience contains the best lesson:** Remove identifying facts only if the remaining account still supports the point, or choose a different episode. State genuine uncertainty instead of replacing it with certainty.
- **Internal team feels attacked:** Frame the issue as shared context and incentives. S1 explicitly recognizes capable internal teams; the method does not depend on disparaging them.
- **Co-founders have overlapping expertise:** Distinguish the decisions they own and the examples they can substantiate. Avoid artificial personality differences or forced opposing takes. [S4]

The repeatable loop is context, challenge, articulation, drafting, evidence review, and ownership learning. Its success is a clear, useful executive perspective that survives production, with a defensible plan for who maintains that quality next.
