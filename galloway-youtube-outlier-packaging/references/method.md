# Method and decision rules

## Attribution and interpretation

This reference paraphrases the public materials indexed in [sources.md](sources.md). Source citations support Galloway's recommendations, not a guarantee of results. The four selection gates, evidence ledger, normalized comparison procedure, and deliverable structure are this package's operational synthesis. They make the public method executable without implying that Galloway published this exact workflow.

The organizing principle is that an idea determines a video's potential, while packaging and execution determine how much of that potential is realized. Spend meaningful effort before filming. His later lessons recommend allocating at least a quarter of working time to ideas, titles, thumbnails, and intros, rather than concentrating almost everything on production. [S3]

## 1. Establish the operating model

Gather the channel's niche, recent uploads, comparable view counts, team capacity, creator strengths, filming access, budget, and deadline. Identify whether the task is a channel strategy, an idea slate, a single upload, or a post-publication diagnosis. Do not infer analytics that the user has not supplied.

Galloway's strategy image presents three operating choices: quality-focused production at about one or two uploads monthly; volume-focused production at two or more weekly; and a hybrid weekly long form model. Choose one for the planning period and explain the capacity tradeoff. These are alternative approaches, not stages every creator must pass through. [S1]

His category growth article gives a more specific prescription: weekly long form, five annual skips, videos around 15 to 40 minutes, and at least three Shorts weekly. It also explicitly allows a skip when a video is not good enough. Treat this as the prescription for that particular growth playbook, not a universal requirement or a reason to extend a video artificially. [S5]

Start in one or two recognizable sub-niches and widen as formats become established. Adjacent uploads should plausibly serve overlapping viewers. This is a useful test of channel coherence, not a ban on experiments. [S5]

**Decision rule:** If the schedule makes strong ideas or packaging impossible, reduce output or simplify execution. Galloway warns against prioritizing the posting schedule over what is being published. [S2][S5]

## 2. Map advantage and viewers

Identify observable advantages in four areas: knowledge, skill, personality, and access. Examples include a repeatable technical demonstration, uncommon professional experience, a recognizable comic point of view, or permission to enter a location others cannot film. Record what the audience will actually see or learn. These categories come from the strategy image; requiring proof points is a package convention. [S1]

Describe three viewer groups:

| Group | Relationship to the channel | Planning question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core | Watches most uploads | What depth, recurring interest, or expertise will reward them? |
| Casual | Watches selected uploads | What recognizable subject or practical stake draws them back? |
| New | Has not watched the channel | What understandable consequence or novelty makes this worth trying? |

A strong growth idea offers all three a reason to click and enjoy the video. Specialist substance can be framed through a widely understandable problem. Galloway discusses a land management video whose desert framing opens the subject to outsiders. Do not erase the specialist content while broadening the entry point. [S1][S6]

**Explicit warning:** Focusing entirely on insiders restricts reach; focusing entirely on newcomers can lose the audience the channel already serves. [S1][S6]

**Edge case:** A narrowly requested tutorial can intentionally prioritize core viewers. Label it as a service upload rather than pretending it is a broad growth candidate. This is an operational accommodation, not Galloway's named category.

## 3. Build an evidence ledger and idea pool

For a full strategy pass, generate at least 100 ideas and repeat the exercise monthly. Keep all three sourcing lanes alive. The strategy image defines internal outliers as roughly the highest-performing tenth of the channel's videos, external outliers as videos around three times their channel's average, and innovation as original ideas. [S1]

| Lane | Evidence to record | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal outlier | Upload, views, cohort, ranking | Something already resonated with this audience. |
| External outlier | Source URL, channel, views, baseline, ratio | A transferable premise or format may have demand. |
| Innovation | Novelty hypothesis and audience rationale | A fresh idea can be viable without precedent. |

Study successful videos deliberately: isolate premise, format, stakes, visual proof, and the unresolved question. Galloway recommends about an hour daily in the earlier materials and at least five weekly hours in his later lessons. Save candidates in a playlist and review them regularly. His later lessons also suggest ten new ideas daily. Choose a sustainable routine rather than treating these overlapping recommendations as cumulative quotas. [S1][S2][S3]

### Operational comparison procedure

The sources supply rough thresholds, not a complete statistical protocol. Use the following package conventions to avoid misleading comparisons:

1. Record the observation date and whether views are lifetime totals or measured at a common age after upload.
2. Define the comparison cohort before claiming an outlier: similar format, length category, period, and channel context.
3. State the baseline calculation and sample size. Prefer age-matched observations when available; disclose differences when they are not.
4. Calculate external ratio as observed views divided by that channel's stated average. Do not quietly substitute subscriber count or a different channel's baseline.
5. If the mean is distorted by a huge prior hit, show that limitation and optionally a median alongside it. Label the median as a sensitivity check, not the source's average threshold.
6. With no baseline, call the video an interesting comparable, not a verified outlier. With a zero baseline, do not calculate a ratio.

An external outlier is a research signal, not proof that the same topic will work on this channel. Adapt the underlying mechanism to the creator's advantage. Looking outside the niche for formats is explicitly encouraged. A lane for original concepts prevents the pool becoming only remakes. [S1][S2]

**Edge cases:** New channels have no internal evidence; use external comparisons and innovation with uncertainty labels. Very small samples make top-decile rankings fragile. Shorts and long form should have separate comparison cohorts. These are package safeguards, not extra numerical rules attributed to Galloway.

## 4. Select before production

Use four pass, revise, or reject gates. This gate structure combines Galloway's public idea traits with the strategy's creator advantage requirement. [S1][S4][S6]

- **Demand or novelty:** There is relevant outlier evidence or a specific, defensible novelty hypothesis.
- **Viewer fit:** Core, casual, and new viewers can understand why they would care, or the narrower service role is stated.
- **Creator advantage:** The team can deliver a distinctive version within access and budget constraints.
- **Package clarity:** A short title and simple thumbnail can communicate the appeal truthfully.

Write the premise in one sentence: subject, surprising tension, and question or outcome to be resolved. Ask relevant peers what interests or confuses them. Galloway treats genuine excitement from people in the niche as a positive sign; this package uses reactions diagnostically rather than as a statistically representative vote. [S6]

**Explicit warning:** An interesting topic that cannot be conveyed in the title and thumbnail is not yet a strong YouTube idea. Revise the framing before production. [S6]

Reserve occasional ambitious videos for ideas with broad appeal inside the niche and a credible reason for greater filming and execution effort. Galloway argues these can raise the channel's future view floor, even if their direct return is limited. He also warns against making every upload an oversized swing. Set the financial ceiling from the user's constraints; this is not authorization to overspend. [S4]

## 5. Develop the combined package

Write at least ten genuinely different title candidates. Explore different stakes, questions, comparisons, and novelty angles. His earlier lessons recommend ten or more titles and three or more thumbnails; his later lessons call for five or more thumbnail ideas and making at least three. [S2][S3]

A concept is a proposed visual argument. A variant is an actual visual execution. Five font changes are not five concepts. If the deliverable is a planning brief, prepare three detailed briefs and mark visual production as pending. Do not describe unrendered concepts as completed thumbnails.

Evaluate each pair through the three stages described in the viral video article:

1. Something catches attention during scrolling.
2. The pair creates a clear unresolved question.
3. The viewer sees worthwhile value and believes the video can deliver it. [S4]

The title and image should work together. The image can show stakes or evidence while the title establishes the question. Complementarity is this package's practical interpretation of assessing the pair as one promise; it is not a rule that titles and thumbnails may never overlap.

Keep about one to three focal elements. Galloway emphasizes processing speed and describes a glance test: show a small thumbnail very briefly between blank slides and check whether the central idea registers. Count what competes for attention, not every literal object or pixel. Visual simplicity should support meaning rather than merely produce attractive empty space. [S6]

### Truth and visual treatment

The interview allows some stylized emphasis and illustrative embellishment, provided it does not mislead. This package uses a conservative production rule: distinguish illustration from documentary proof, and never imply an experiment result, person, access, or measurement the video does not contain. An arrow or contrast adjustment can aid comprehension; fabricated evidence changes the promise. The operational boundary is grounded in his emphasis on satisfaction and truthful delivery, rather than a claim that he forbids every illustrative manipulation. [S4][S6]

## 6. Deliver the promise

Plan visual or verbal confirmation of the premise within roughly the first five seconds. This confirms that the click was worthwhile; it need not disclose the final answer or collapse the whole story into the intro. Show the experiment, unusual object, access, or consequence that the package led viewers to expect. Then progress toward the promised resolution. [S4]

Write down required footage before filming. If the key visual cannot be obtained, revise the package or change the shoot. Check captions, audio, continuity, and story structure: Galloway gives a negative example where strong packaging attracted initial views but the video failed to deliver and contained sloppy details. [S4]

Viewer satisfaction is central in his playbook, but he also acknowledges that there is no simple directly visible satisfaction metric. Treat retention and audience feedback as imperfect clues. Do not invent a satisfaction score or assert that click metrics prove viewers were satisfied. [S4]

**Edge case:** If the footage already exists, package only what it supports. If an experiment has an unknown outcome, use a question or process promise until the evidence supports an outcome claim.

## 7. Learn and repeat

Compare views against the channel's normal performance at the stated observation window, inspect retention drop points, and examine the audience reached. Galloway prioritizes views and retention curves in his later lessons and also names audience demographics in his earlier ones. He explicitly warns against discussing click-through rate and average view duration without context. [S1][S2][S3]

Diagnose separately: an unclear package, a weak premise, poor opening confirmation, and disappointing execution require different changes. That diagnostic split is this package's synthesis. Avoid changing an effective package merely to satisfy a generic metric target. Changes should address an observed problem and include a written hypothesis.

The category playbook treats around twice the channel's average views as enough signal to try a related episode quickly. If the idea works twice consecutively, develop it as a format. Preserve the repeatable mechanism while changing the subject, stakes, or episode question. Keep testing other ideas alongside winners. These are practical signals, not statistically guaranteed predictors. [S2][S5]

**Explicit warnings:** Do not abandon a successful format solely because repetition worries the creator. Do not let endless optimization prevent publication. Galloway recommends time constraints for packaging decisions and repeated attempts because outcomes are uncertain. [S2][S4][S5][S6]

## Scope boundary for broader advice

The lesson lists also caution against overhiring, copying another creator's spending model, joining an MCN, and compromising trust for short-term monetization. Those are his public business views, not mandatory financial or contractual instructions in this packaging skill. Use them only when they explain a relevant production constraint; do not expand a packaging task into business restructuring. [S2][S3]
