# Worked examples

All scenarios, people, dialogue, and drafts below are invented illustrations. They are not memories belonging to Matthew Dicks or the user. The sample drafts demonstrate structure; their invented details must never be imported into a user's true account.

## Illustration 1: The extra pencil

### Available scenario

A teacher has asked students to bring pencils. Lena arrives with an almost empty bag. The teacher assumes she has ignored the request and reaches for the classroom's spare box. Lena takes out two pencils and hands one to a classmate who has none. The teacher notices that they judged her before seeing what she had planned. The teacher puts the spare box back.

### Weak first draft

> I teach in a busy school with twenty-eight students. Lena is in my class. We have been working on responsibility, and yesterday I sent a supply reminder. This morning I checked the cupboard, took attendance, answered a parent email, and got ready for our lesson. Lena came in with two pencils and gave one to another student. It was a lovely moment. Children can teach us so much, and we should always remember to be kind.

The broad moral conceals the teacher's own mistaken assumption. Background delays the scene, and the morning's tasks do not clarify the change. [S3][S5]

### Capture entry

| Date | Moment cue |
| --- | --- |
| 2026-09-14 | Classroom door: reached for spare pencils, assuming Lena forgot. Her second pencil was for a classmate. Put the box back; I had judged before looking. |

This is a retrieval cue, not a requirement to draft a story that night. [S2][S4]

### Change and structure

- **Before:** I take Lena's nearly empty bag as evidence of carelessness.
- **Moment:** I see her hand the second pencil to a classmate.
- **After:** I realize I have mistaken quiet preparation for neglect.
- **Audience question:** Has Lena brought what she needs, or will I need to step in?
- **Opening:** My hand is already on the spare-pencil box.

### Improved result

> My hand is on the spare-pencil box when Lena comes through the classroom door. Her bag looks almost empty. After yesterday's reminder, I am already preparing to solve the problem I assume she has brought me.
>
> She takes out a pencil. Then another.
>
> I start to put the box down on her desk, but she passes the second pencil to the student beside her, whose desk is bare.
>
> I had seen an empty bag and decided what kind of morning she was having. She had arrived prepared for two people.
>
> I put the unopened box back on the shelf.

### Rules that made the difference

The opening shows the teacher's prior state through a real action in the scenario. The draft keeps only the reminder, bag, pencils, and spare box because they clarify the turn. The ending stays with a modest revised judgment rather than claiming the teacher's whole life changed. The final action is usable because the scenario explicitly includes it. [S3][S4][S5]

In a real task, ask whether the teacher actually made this assumption and put the box back. If either detail is absent, remove it or leave a question. A structural improvement does not authorize inventing the narrator's thoughts or gestures. See the safeguards in [method.md](../references/method.md).

## Illustration 2: The rejected dashboard

### Available scenario

An analyst spends a week building a detailed dashboard to prove they can solve problems independently. During a review, a colleague says the team only needs to know which orders are late. The analyst initially feels dismissed. Watching the colleague return repeatedly to the late-orders list, the analyst realizes the elaborate display serves their wish to look capable more than the team's immediate need. They close the other tabs and work on the list together.

### Weak first draft

> Last month we had a project at work. I used several tools and built a dashboard with eight tabs. There were some issues with the data, but I fixed them. Then we had a meeting and my colleague suggested simplifying it. I listened, and we made it better. Teamwork is essential, and great professionals always embrace feedback.

This reads like a project report. It replaces the narrator's embarrassment and recognition with a polished claim about professional excellence. The audience cannot identify the instant of change. [S3][S5]

### Capture entry

| Date | Moment cue |
| --- | --- |
| 2026-09-18 | Dashboard review: colleague kept returning to late-orders list. I realized the other tabs were proof of effort for me, not useful to them. Closed tabs and worked on list together. |

### Change and structure

- **Before:** Showing how much I built feels like proof that I am useful.
- **Moment:** I notice the colleague keeps ignoring the display and returning to the same list.
- **After:** I see that usefulness depends on their problem, not the visibility of my effort.
- **Audience question:** Will the dashboard I worked on all week actually help the team?
- **Opening:** I open the eight tabs in the review meeting, expecting appreciation.

### Improved result

> I open all eight dashboard tabs in the review meeting. I have spent a week on them, and I want my colleague to see that I can handle this without help.
>
> I start with the overview. She asks which orders are late. I show her the late-orders list, then move on to a chart. She asks to go back to the list.
>
> I feel as if she is refusing to look at my work. But as she checks the orders, I notice what she is actually doing: using the one thing that answers her question.
>
> The other tabs are answering mine. Do I look capable?
>
> I close them. We stay on the late-orders list and work through it together.

### Rules that made the difference

The narrator's actual hope supplies stakes without fabricated danger. The final recognition contrasts with the opening desire to demonstrate independence. The story includes enough project context to understand the meeting but drops tool names and implementation history. It ends after a supported action shows the new priority. [S3][S5]

The draft uses indirect speech because the scenario supplies meaning rather than exact wording. For a job interview, preserve the user's requested tone and add a brief verified result only if the question requires one. Do not invent business impact to strengthen the ending.

## Illustration 3: A quiet day that remains a memory

### Weak first draft

> Nothing happened today except watering the balcony plants. Watching the water soak in taught me that patience is the foundation of all growth, and I became a more grounded person.

### Available facts and improved capture

The owner only remembers watering plants after work and noticing a new leaf. No changed belief or emotion has been supplied.

| Date | Moment cue |
| --- | --- |
| 2026-09-19 | Watered the balcony plants after work; noticed a new leaf on the basil. |

**Candidate status:** Memory cue. No supported internal change yet.

The improvement removes the invented revelation and preserves the ordinary moment. Capture can succeed even when story development is premature. If drafting is requested, ask what the new leaf meant to the owner rather than suggesting a lesson they must adopt. [S2][S3][S4]
