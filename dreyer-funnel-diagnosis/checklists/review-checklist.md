# Review checklist

Mark each item PASS, FAIL, or N/A with a reason. A deliverable passes only when every applicable item passes. A failed item may remain visible in a provisional research deliverable, but must not be hidden behind confident copy or treated as publication approval.

## Attribution and evidence

- [ ] The work distinguishes Dreyer's sourced public principles from this package's operational extensions.
- [ ] Every public-method claim resolves to an actual fetched source in references/sources.md.
- [ ] The 600+ figure is not presented as independently verified, and the agent does not claim access to the underlying scripts.
- [ ] Client observations have source, date, segment, and limitations; inferred motivations are labeled.
- [ ] The work does not attribute other authors' posts or comments to Dreyer.
- [ ] Examples and hypothetical numbers are visibly labeled illustrations, not client outcomes.

## Diagnosis

- [ ] The business objective has a defined metric and denominator, or an explicit evidence gap.
- [ ] Funnel counts are comparable or their cohort and attribution differences are explained.
- [ ] Tracking and technical friction have been considered before assigning a psychological cause.
- [ ] The selected Engpass connects an observation to a testable hesitation hypothesis.
- [ ] At least one plausible competing explanation and a falsifying observation are recorded.
- [ ] Priority reflects business consequence and evidence strength rather than only the largest percentage drop.
- [ ] Real affordability, offer fit, targeting, and sales handling are considered when relevant.

## Psychology and strategy

- [ ] The brief addresses a defined segment and distinguishes buyer roles where needed.
- [ ] Trigger, desired progress, prior attempts, risk, objection, and language have evidence or uncertainty labels.
- [ ] The angle addresses the diagnosed problem and names a coherent belief transition.
- [ ] The proposed difference is relevant and supported, rather than decorative novelty.
- [ ] The tone fits buyer evidence rather than copying Dreyer's personal social-post style.
- [ ] The promise stays coherent across the selected assets.

## Copy and proof

- [ ] The opening gives the intended buyer a recognizable reason to continue.
- [ ] Features connect to useful consequences without unsupported result claims.
- [ ] Every number, testimonial, superiority claim, guarantee, and deadline has appropriate support.
- [ ] Demonstrations are distinguished from real customer results.
- [ ] Material terms and the next action are clear.
- [ ] Missing proof is a visible placeholder or the claim has been narrowed.
- [ ] Production placeholders are resolved before publication readiness is claimed.
- [ ] Requested assets are complete; additional asset modules are included only where useful to the assignment.

## Testing and handoff

- [ ] The test states its hypothesis, control, treatment, allocation, outcome, and quality guardrail.
- [ ] Multiple simultaneous changes are labeled a bundle test with limited attribution.
- [ ] The observation requirement follows the baseline and useful effect; no arbitrary universal threshold is invented.
- [ ] Adoption, rejection, inconclusive, and early-stop conditions are defined before results.
- [ ] Sales-cycle delays and relevant confounds are considered.
- [ ] The next action has an owner and a review point.
- [ ] Proposed drafts, measured findings, and publication status are clearly distinguished.

## Common failures and fixes

| Failure | Why it fails | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rewrite everything before diagnosis | Creates many changes without an explanatory hypothesis. | Select a probable constraint and a bounded intervention. |
| Call every price objection a value problem | Ignores budgets, procurement, and alternatives. | Check the objection's actual reason and offer conditions. |
| Use demographic stereotypes as psychology | Substitutes assumptions for decision evidence. | Collect trigger, objection, prior-attempt, and language evidence. |
| Personalize only with a first name | Does not establish contextual relevance. | Reflect the buyer's situation and concern where supported. |
| Promise effortless or guaranteed outcomes | Strong language outruns proof and delivery. | Narrow the promise and state applicable conditions. |
| Optimize clicks while losing qualified buyers | Rewards a proxy at the expense of the business. | Judge qualified demand and downstream economics. |
| Declare the lowest conversion rate the bottleneck | Treats unlike transitions as equivalent. | Compare context, opportunity, economics, and confidence. |
| Copy a competitor's hook or a Porsche execution | Borrows expression without establishing relevance. | Develop an original angle from this audience and offer. |
| Treat a small interview sample as representative | Overstates qualitative evidence. | Label directionality and test the segment hypothesis. |
| Use one benchmark for every funnel | Ignores channel, audience, and offer differences. | Use an appropriate baseline or mark it unknown. |
| Attribute a bundle win to one line | Confuses total treatment effect with component effect. | Report the bundle result and isolate components later if useful. |
| Claim Dreyer's proprietary protocol | Public evidence does not disclose such a protocol. | Preserve the public-principle and operational-extension boundary. |
