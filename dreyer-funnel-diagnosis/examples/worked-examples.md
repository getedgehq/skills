# Worked examples

These are invented illustrations. Businesses, evidence, figures, offers, and copy below are fictional. They are not Dreyer client cases, empirical results, or quotations from his material. Public principles P1 through P6 and operational extensions refer to references/method.md.

## Illustration 1: B2B invoice automation landing page

### Brief and invented evidence

A software service targets finance managers at small agencies. The requested deliverable is a landing-page opening and objection section. The fictional offer includes a sample workflow preview before a purchase decision.

In a hypothetical four-week report, 1,000 loaded visits produce 12 inquiries, or 1.2%. Of those, eight meet the company's qualification definition. Tracking and form checks show no obvious failure. Six fictional prospect interviews mention disrupted approvals and uncertainty about exception handling. This small interview set is directional, not representative.

### Weak first draft

> Transform your business with our innovative AI automation platform. We offer integrations, workflow optimization, and next-generation solutions. Book a demo now and unlock limitless efficiency.

The draft lists capabilities, gives no recognizable finance situation, and leaves the operational risk unanswered. Its unlimited claim has no support.

### Diagnosis and strategy

Leading hypothesis: prospects do not trust the transition from their current approval process to an automated one. Alternative: visitors lack purchase authority or budget. Confidence: medium-low, because the interviews are limited and the traffic's role mix is unknown.

Angle: review the actual workflow before committing. Desired belief transition: from automation may hide errors to I can inspect how approvals and exceptions work. The promise is control over the buying decision, not a guaranteed reduction in labor.

### Improved result

**Headline:** See how invoice approvals would work in your agency before you commit.

**Supporting copy:** Bring one typical invoice workflow to a preview. We will walk through who approves each step, how exceptions are flagged, and what your team would still handle.

**Risk section:** Before choosing a rollout, review the approval map and identify any steps that need human review. If the workflow does not fit your process, the preview will make that clear.

**CTA:** Request a workflow preview.

**Proof block for production:** [Insert an approved example of the approval map. Identify what is a demonstration and what reflects a live customer implementation.]

The fictional business must confirm these preview terms before publication. The proof placeholder is not publishable copy.

### Rules that changed the draft

P2 replaces supplier jargon with a finance manager's operational concern. P3 connects features to a concrete decision benefit. P4 keeps the angle attached to actual research in the invented scenario. The Engpass extension localizes the intervention to perceived implementation risk rather than assuming the headline needs more excitement.

### Measurement and next decision

Compare the existing opening and revised opening concurrently, holding traffic and the rest of the page stable where feasible. Primary measure: qualified inquiries divided by loaded visits. Guardrail: qualification rate among inquiries, plus any change in sales acceptance. The owner sets the meaningful effect and required observations before launch using the baseline and available traffic.

If interest increases but qualification declines, reject the apparent win. If finance-role visitors understand the preview yet still do not inquire, investigate authority, budget, or offer fit. No improvement is claimed: these are proposed drafts and test conditions.

## Illustration 2: Creative strategy for a reporting-service ad and VSL

### Brief and invented evidence

A reporting consultancy sells a paid pilot to owners of boutique consulting firms. The fictional pilot reconciles one month's project hours and invoices, then reviews possible discrepancies with the owner. It does not guarantee recovering revenue.

A hypothetical campaign generates 500 loaded visits, 50 pilot inquiries, and five inquiries that meet the eligibility criteria. A fictional lost-lead review finds that many visitors expected a free dashboard tool. Five fictional owner interviews instead emphasize uncertainty about whether project hours reach the invoice.

### Weak first draft

**Ad:** Get a beautiful dashboard in minutes. Our expert team revolutionizes your reporting. Click to see the future of data.

**VSL opening:** Welcome! We are passionate reporting experts with a proven methodology. Today we will show you our powerful dashboards, advanced analytics, and unmatched service quality. Stay until the end for an amazing offer.

The ad attracts software seekers. The VSL repeats provider-centered claims and delays explaining what buyers are considering.

### Diagnosis and strategy

Leading hypothesis: the ad promises a different category and decision than the actual offer. The high inquiry count hides a qualification problem. Alternative: the eligibility criteria are too restrictive. Confidence: medium, conditional on reliable lead classification.

Angle: reconcile delivery and billing for one month before choosing broader reporting work. Segment: owners who already track project hours and issue invoices. Motive: confidence in revenue visibility, without invented claims about personal status or guaranteed financial gain.

### Improved result

**Ad script, approximately 25 seconds:**

| Visual | Spoken copy |
| --- | --- |
| Owner compares a project-hours sheet and invoice export. | You delivered the work. Did every billable hour reach the invoice? |
| Show a clearly labeled illustrative reconciliation, without customer data. | Our paid reporting pilot compares one month of project hours with your invoices and flags entries to review. |
| Display eligibility and next action. | Already tracking hours and invoicing clients? See the pilot scope and decide whether it fits your firm. |

**Landing-page bridge:** Reconcile one month's project hours and invoices. Review flagged discrepancies with a reporting specialist before deciding on broader reporting work.

**VSL opening and mechanism segment:**

> If your firm records project hours and sends invoices, you have two records that should tell a consistent story. Our paid pilot compares those records for one month. We flag differences and review them with you, because a difference may be a valid exception rather than missed billing. You receive a reconciliation and a review of the entries that need explanation. Then you can decide whether broader reporting work would be useful.

**VSL proof and close:** [Show an approved illustrative reconciliation and explain its limitations.] The pilot covers one month and requires your hours and invoice records. [Insert confirmed price, turnaround, data handling terms, and exclusions.] View the scope and request a fit check.

### Rules that changed the draft

P1 makes the opening relevant to a live business question. P3 presents a buyer outcome instead of a dashboard feature list. P5 differentiates through a bounded reconciliation service. P6 encourages examining the motive, while the brief rejects unsupported assumptions about status. The asset-continuity extension aligns ad, page, and VSL around the same paid offer.

### Measurement and next decision

This is a bundle test because the ad, page bridge, and VSL change together. Compare qualified pilot inquiries per loaded visit and cost per qualified inquiry; track accepted pilots as the delayed business outcome. A lower click-through rate can still accompany better qualified demand.

Predefine eligibility, allocation, minimum useful observations, and adoption conditions. If fewer inquiries arrive but more are qualified, assess acquisition economics before deciding. If qualification remains poor, review targeting and eligibility. Do not attribute any bundle result to the ad hook alone. No test has been run in this illustration.
