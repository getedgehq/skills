# Method and operational interpretation

## Provenance and scope

This is an English synthesis of public principles, followed by an original agent workflow that makes those principles actionable. It is not a transcription of Dreyer's private process. Citations [S1] through [S6] resolve in sources.md. Public posts establish audience relevance, language fit, buyer motives, value explanation, research, and differentiation. They do not establish a complete funnel analytics procedure.

The user's brief describes data-driven, psychology-based funnels, creative strategy, finding the real bottleneck, and experience across 600+ ad scripts, landing pages, and VSLs. The fetched sources do not independently establish that volume or the exact process. Treat it as supplied positioning, not an audited corpus or causal evidence. The agent must not claim to have analyzed those assets.

Sections headed Operational extension are this package's synthesis. Their tables, test rules, and asset sequences are useful implementation choices, not Dreyer-branded formulas. Zielgruppe means target group, Zielgruppenverständnis means understanding that group, Tonalität means tone, and Engpass means bottleneck. These are descriptive German terms, not verified proprietary names.

## Documented public principles

### P1. Relevance earns attention

Dreyer challenges the explanation that people simply stopped reading marketing text. His alternative is that readers are selective and poorly targeted writing loses their interest. [S1]

Application: lead with a recognizable situation relevant to the intended decision. Assess relevance before assuming that shortening everything will solve the problem.

### P2. Use the audience's language

He identifies inappropriate tone, inaccurate descriptions of pain, and words the audience would not use as reasons website communication fails. [S2]

Application: choose vocabulary from buyer evidence. Technical terminology may fit specialist buyers; forced simplicity or artificial slang can both weaken fit.

### P3. Explain value through outcomes

His B2B price discussion emphasizes making the value of a service understandable through the results buyers want, rather than listing the activities performed. [S3]

Application: connect a capability to a practical consequence. Treat unclear value as one explanation for a price objection, while checking affordability, procurement, and competing priorities.

### P4. Research makes personalization credible

In his comment on another author's post, Dreyer links effective personalized copy to accurate target-group research and warns that weak research can leave readers unable to recognize themselves in it. [S4]

Application: personalization should reflect the buyer's circumstances and decision, not merely insert a name into generic copy.

### P5. Communicate a meaningful difference

His comment on website positioning favors standing out and questions reluctance to communicate differently from competitors. [S5]

Application: find a relevant distinction in the offer, mechanism, evidence, or experience. Novel wording alone is not evidence of a superior offer.

### P6. Motives inform creative expression

His Porsche commentary illustrates recognition, status, and aspiration as motives and praises concise, audience-fluent creative treatment. [S6]

Application: investigate functional, emotional, and social reasons for buying. Do not generalize the example's motives to every audience or reuse its advertising execution.

## Operational extension: intake and evidence

Define the offer, eligible buyer, next action, channel, delivery constraints, price terms, and allowable proof. Identify the business outcome before choosing a copy metric. A lead-generation business may need qualified opportunities or profitable acquired customers; a sale may need contribution after acquisition costs and returns.

Use three evidence labels:

- Observed: directly supported by a provided report, buyer statement, or asset, with source and date.
- Inferred: a proposed explanation connecting observations to behavior.
- Unknown: information needed to discriminate between explanations.

Keep public-method citations separate from client evidence IDs, such as C1 for a report or C2 for interview notes. A public post cannot verify a client's customer motivation.

Map impressions or reach, relevant engagement, clicks, loaded page visits, offer engagement, next-action completion, qualified leads, sales, and retained value as applicable. Record numerator and denominator rather than combining incomparable rates. For example, leads divided by loaded visits differs from leads divided by ad clicks. Note duplicate events, consent gaps, device differences, attribution window, and sales-cycle lag.

Compare like audiences and periods. A decline after a targeting change may reflect different traffic rather than weaker copy. The lowest numerical conversion rate is not automatically the most valuable bottleneck. Some transitions naturally have low rates. Prioritize a plausible constraint using baseline context, evidence quality, affected volume, economics, and intervention cost.

## Operational extension: psychological audience analysis

Build a brief for one decision-making segment at a time. Capture:

| Field | Evidence to seek | Consequence for creative strategy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | What happened before the buyer looked for help? | Establish the opening situation. |
| Desired progress | What practical outcome matters? | Define the central promise. |
| Emotional or social motive | What would the change mean personally or socially? | Choose a relevant framing hypothesis. |
| Prior attempts | What has already been tried? | Explain a meaningful alternative. |
| Objection | What stopped the last decision? | Decide what explanation or proof is needed. |
| Risk | What could go wrong after purchase? | Clarify process, limits, and terms. |
| Language | How does this segment describe the issue? | Set tone and vocabulary. |
| Decision context | Who uses, approves, pays, or blocks? | Address different roles without conflating them. |

Use interviews, sales conversations, reviews, support issues, or research supplied or authorized by the user. Record whether each observation comes from customers, prospects, employees, or competitor customers. A vivid anecdote may generate an angle; it does not establish prevalence. Preserve contradictory findings and seek the segment difference that explains them.

Translate behavior into a cautious hypothesis: the buyer may need confidence that implementation will not disrupt work. Avoid asserting hidden motives as psychological facts. Psychology here is a model of the decision to test, not mind-reading or clinical diagnosis.

## Operational extension: locating the Engpass

For each candidate, record the affected stage, observed loss, buyer hesitation hypothesis, competing explanation, proposed intervention, confidence, and falsifying observation.

| Pattern | Candidate hesitation | Alternative explanation | Useful next check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Relevant reach, little engagement | Opening lacks personal relevance | Wrong audience or poor delivery | Segment performance and buyer reaction to the opening. |
| Clicks, few loaded visits | Cannot infer hesitation yet | Slow page or tracking failure | Compare clicks with server or page-load evidence. |
| Loaded visits, little offer engagement | Promise feels unfamiliar or unclear | Ad attracts a different need | Check arrival expectations against the page opening. |
| Offer engagement, few inquiries | Value, trust, effort, or risk remains unresolved | Broken form or genuine budget limit | Inspect the form and collect abandonment objections. |
| Many inquiries, few qualified buyers | Promise attracts unsuitable people | Qualification definition changed | Compare ad promises with eligibility and lead reasons. |
| Qualified leads, few sales | Business case or approval risk unresolved | Follow-up delays or delivery mismatch | Review sales objections, timing, and lost-deal reasons. |
| Purchases, refunds or churn | Expectations exceed delivery | Product or service problem | Inspect expectation-setting and delivery evidence. |

These are diagnostic candidates, not benchmarks. Do not invent universal CTR, conversion, or watch-time thresholds. When measurement is unavailable, deliver a provisional audit and the smallest useful evidence request. When technical failure dominates, recommend that repair before presenting new copy as the fix.

Select one leading hypothesis and the strongest plausible alternative. Describe what result would make you switch. For a bounded copy request, complete the requested draft and note the diagnosis limitation without turning the task into an unsolicited rebuild.

## Operational extension: creative strategy and drafting

Write an angle brief with segment, trigger, central motivation, supported outcome, mechanism, proof, primary objection, tone, and next action. Explain the belief transition: what the buyer currently doubts and what they must understand to act.

Choose a distinct angle only when it changes the buyer's interpretation. A fear-led opening, an outcome-led opening, and a mechanism-led opening may test different explanations. Three synonyms for the same headline do not test three strategies.

Preserve message continuity. The ad introduces a relevant promise and attracts the intended buyer. The landing page confirms that promise and supports the decision. A VSL can develop the explanation where spoken demonstration or sequencing helps. Do not add a VSL solely because the package supports one.

Suggested drafting structures, all original extensions:

- Ad script: recognizable situation, relevant outcome, explanation or demonstration, credible proof, next action. Pair narration with visual directions so the key claim remains understandable.
- Landing page: arrival promise, audience fit, mechanism, evidence, material objection, conditions, next action. Move sections according to the diagnosed hesitation rather than following a fixed long-form formula.
- VSL: buyer situation, problem explanation, possible alternative, mechanism demonstration, evidence, objection resolution, offer terms, next action. Estimate length from spoken word count and delivery pace; edit for relevance instead of requiring an arbitrary duration.

Quantify only supported results, with scope and limitations. Where proof is missing, use a clear production placeholder or a narrower claim. Explain why a feature matters to this segment. Let tone follow the buyer and offer, rather than imitating Dreyer's social-post voice. Avoid borrowed campaign hooks, false urgency, fabricated authority, and absolute psychological claims.

## Operational extension: test, learn, and decide

State the causal hypothesis before launch: for this segment, this message change should reduce this hesitation, improving this stage and preserving this business-quality measure.

Choose the primary outcome and denominator, quality guardrail, comparison unit, allocation, test period, and decision owner. Fix the main strategic variable where practical. If several assets change together, call it a bundle test and do not attribute the result to one headline.

Use a concurrent randomized comparison where feasible. If only a sequential comparison is possible, record changes in spend, audience, seasonality, pricing, and sales handling. Plan adequate observations for the baseline and meaningful effect; do not invent a universal sample size. If traffic is insufficient, use qualitative comprehension checks to refine the hypothesis while withholding a causal uplift claim.

Define adoption, rejection, inconclusive, and early-stop conditions before results. Wait for relevant sales-cycle outcomes. A click increase with declining lead quality may fail the business objective. Preserve useful negative findings: they can weaken the psychological explanation and redirect work toward targeting, offer, or operations.

## Warnings and edge cases

Documented warnings: relevance and audience understanding cannot be replaced by assumptions about unwilling readers [S1]; language mismatch can block communication [S2]; service descriptions may obscure value [S3]; weak research undermines personalization [S4]; sameness can weaken differentiation [S5].

Package safeguards: price objections can be real affordability constraints; emotional motives coexist with practical and organizational requirements; unusual creative must still be relevant; polished copy cannot repair a broken offer or tracking system. These safeguards are the package's interpretation, not additional quotations or claims about Dreyer's private teaching.

For cold buyers, establish context before unfamiliar mechanisms. For knowledgeable buyers, avoid explanations they do not need. For multiple buyer roles, tailor evidence and consequences to each role. For unsupported promises, narrow the claim or request proof. For conflicting data, document the conflict and the observation needed to resolve it. For new funnels, describe the bottleneck as a predicted risk until performance evidence exists.
