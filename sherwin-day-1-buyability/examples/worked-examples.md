# Worked examples

Credit: Illustrations applying Marcus Sherwin's public method and the research cited in [the method](../references/method.md). All companies, budgets, research findings, assets, and targets below are invented. They are planning examples, not client case studies or observed results. Any numerical threshold is an illustrative operating choice unless explicitly attributed.

## Illustration 1: A capable cloud platform that rarely gets considered

### Situation and weak first draft

Northstar Data is an invented European cloud data platform selling to mid-market organisations. In this scenario, a small exploratory interview set suggests that buyers who evaluate it like its implementation model, but it seldom comes to mind before vendor research. These interviews establish a hypothesis, not a market-wide estimate.

Weak draft:

> Hire the largest LinkedIn technology creator we can afford. Publish one post about our fast connector library, boost it to Heads of Data, and send every ad viewer a demo sequence. Use 500 likes as proof that Buyability improved. Offer the creator commission on every deal.

The draft selects reach before audience fit, misses guardians, confuses engagement with purchase confidence, assumes viewers can be identified, and supplies no implementation or financial evidence.

### Improved result

**Diagnosis:** Provisionally low initial consideration with a credible delivery story once evaluated. Prioritise early visibility through real implementation expertise while checking buyer confidence with a broader role sample. Apply the low-presence, strong-offering rule. [S3][S5]

**Commercial frame:** A proposed ninety-day learning programme for a defined set of 120 accounts with an anticipated migration need. Illustrative budget cap: EUR 15,000 including creator fees, content support, distribution, and research. The owner must allocate the cap before activation; no revenue promise follows from it.

| Buyer | Concern and proof | Voice and content | Next useful destination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Data leader | Will migration improve team throughput? Use a permissioned workflow demonstration | Independent data practitioner with verified segment fit: walk through a migration trade-off | Migration planning guide |
| IT/security | What breaks at cutover? Use an approved architecture and rollback demonstration | Internal solutions architect: explain failure modes and recovery | Architecture review request |
| Finance | What is the full cost? Use a model including labour, parallel running, and support | Finance SME: explain base and downside cases | Editable cost model |
| Procurement | Can the vendor support the rollout? Use verified support terms and approved references | Deployment operator: explain onboarding responsibilities | Vendor evidence pack |

**Content and distribution:** Begin with three to five useful organic posts across willing voices. Select two for amplification based on relevant buyer response, following Sherwin's published post-selection preference. Use role-specific account targeting where feasible and a follow-on proof campaign. Preserve the practitioner's story and disclose paid relationships. Obtain a fixed-flight usage agreement and platform permission before spending. [S1][S6][S7][S8]

**Handoff:** The assumed stack supplies account-level ad reporting and identified website form submissions, not named ad viewers. Create an account review when credible activity spans multiple role audiences; send a contact task only when an identified request or another valid contact signal exists. Sherwin's three-role, thirty-day trigger remains an optional calibration point, not a claim that these data identify three individuals. [S1]

Illustrative seller message:

> Many data teams find that parallel-running costs change their migration business case. We have a cost worksheet covering that trade-off. Would it help with your planning?

The message makes no assertion that the recipient viewed an ad.

**Measurement:** Record the exploratory buyer baseline, role coverage, qualified proof visits, explicit requests, and later consideration evidence. Compare opportunity progression with a similar account cohort if feasible. Report influenced opportunities separately from sourced ones. Do not claim a specific win-rate lift from the pilot.

### Rules that made the difference

- Diagnose consideration separately from confidence before selecting the channel. [S3]
- Map Finance, IT, and Procurement to their own evidence needs. [S1]
- Pick practitioner credibility and audience fit before follower count. [S5][S6]
- Learn organically, amplify selectively, and carry the conversation onward. [S7]
- Separate usage rights, permission, and compensation. [S8]
- Apply the package's observability rule before designing CRM automation.

## Illustration 2: A familiar HCM vendor that loses on confidence

### Situation and weak first draft

PeopleHarbor is an invented HCM provider already appearing on many initial shortlists in its target segment. Recent loss interviews in this illustration suggest that Finance worries about unpredictable implementation cost and IT distrusts its migration schedule. The company has a real service weakness: handover ownership is unclear.

Weak draft:

> Our brand is known, so run a famous HR creator's endorsement every week. Say migration is effortless and ROI is guaranteed. Target HR directors, put all responses into a lead score called Bain LTB, and have sales contact accounts after three likes. Increase budget if demos do not improve.

The draft adds awareness to a confidence problem, exaggerates delivery, omits Finance and IT, and invents an official metric.

### Improved result

**Diagnosis:** Strong initial consideration but insufficient delivery confidence. The first decision is to repair the handover and cost evidence, rather than increase reach. Product, services, and marketing must share ownership of the decisive weaknesses. [S3]

**Readiness dependency:** The services lead defines a named implementation owner, escalation path, and milestone responsibilities. Finance validates cost ranges and exclusions. Content launch waits for those particular claims to become supportable; this is a delivery dependency, not a general approval ritual.

**Proof programme:**

| Priority | Improved content | Evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Implementation confidence | Implementation SME explains migration milestones, dependencies, and rollback choices | Approved sample plan; no claim that every deployment fits one schedule |
| Value against total cost | Finance SME explains the first-year cost range and sensitivity to data cleanup | Transparent assumptions and exclusions; no guaranteed ROI |
| Internal defensibility | Willing customer practitioner describes the evaluation process and trade-offs | Permissioned reference; no scripted universal endorsement |
| Ease of working together | Services operator explains ownership and escalation | Published service commitments consistent with actual operations |

Retain the useful HR creator if their genuine experience fits the brief. Add voices relevant to Finance and IT instead of asking one HR personality to answer every concern. Maintain creator freedom within factual boundaries. [S1][S6][S9]

**Activation:** Publish evidence-led posts over the consideration period. Amplify relevant performers to the mapped committee, with a follow-on implementation resource and an optional technical workshop. Repeat the proof throughout active evaluations; for accounts whose evaluation has begun, describe this as confidence support rather than retroactive Day 1 placement. [S3][S7][S9]

**Sales play:** A workshop request triggers a named account owner's response. With only aggregate campaign signals, create an account review rather than personal engagement claims. A live opportunity can proceed on an explicit buyer request even if it has not crossed the illustrative multi-role engagement threshold.

**Learning:** Run an adapted buyer study separating unaided consideration from perceptions of cost, implementation, safety, needs, and working relationship against named alternatives. Keep it labelled a custom diagnostic. Review whether technical objections and implementation-related stalls decline for the defined cohort. Require sufficient observation time before attributing win-rate changes to the programme. [S3][S4]

**Operating decision:** If the handover remains unclear, repair delivery before amplifying its promise. If delivery improves but buyers still misunderstand it, revise proof and distribution. If buyers understand and trust it but losses persist, investigate other decision themes rather than assuming creators need more spend.

### Rules that made the difference

- High consideration with low confidence calls for better proof and delivery. [S3]
- The five factors reveal why a familiar brand can still be a risky choice. [S3][S4]
- Internal expertise and third-party validation perform different credibility jobs. [S1][S6]
- Ongoing consideration content matters alongside initial exposure. [S9]
- A custom activity score is not Bain's LTB instrument.
- Explicit requests and verified signals govern the package's sales handoff.
