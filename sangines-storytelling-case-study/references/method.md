# Method and operating guidance

Credit: Based on Marvin Sanginés's public method and notus's public accounts. Independently paraphrased; not affiliated with or endorsed by Marvin Sanginés or notus.

## Scope and evidence

Storytelling Case Study is the named post format. Content Call, Content Engine, Content Archetype, and Setup Sprint are terms used in the public material. The fetched substantive sources are in English, so no German method name requires translation. Preserve these names where useful without implying access to proprietary training. [S1][S3][S7][S8]

The public method comprises four narrative moves: an emotional opening, the client's initial situation, the transformation process, and the resulting situation. It operates inside a people-led system that extracts a person's expertise through conversation, turns it into drafts, incorporates feedback, and connects attention with commercial opportunities. [S1][S2][S3]

Sanginés says this format accounts for about 60% of notus inbound leads. His separate account attributes about 60% of revenue to his personal LinkedIn profile. These are first-party claims with different denominators. The format is not a promise that any user will produce either percentage. [S1][S2]

**Implementation distinction:** The evidence ledger, missing-data rules, approval fields, anonymization guidance, and checklist in this package are editorial safeguards added to make the public method usable by an agent. They are not claimed as Sanginés's proprietary instructions. Sections explicitly labelled adaptation follow this distinction.

## System foundation

Start with the intended customer, the founder's experience, the business offer, and the reader's next step. Sanginés's workshop recap puts audience definition before profile optimization and content production. The profile should explain identity, offer, previous outcomes, and where visitors can go next. These foundations make a relevant story easier to understand and act on. [S5]

notus describes an initial strategy and profile setup, a recurring content engine, and growth amplification. Its founder describes a strategist managing content production, an appointment setter handling the inbox, and a partnerships lead conducting discovery calls. The person supplies lived knowledge; the surrounding operation handles the repeatable work. [S2][S7]

Founder dependence is a real concern in [S2]. His response is to build an operation around the founder, maintain independent fulfillment, and support other team members' personal brands. Do not interpret this as proof that all concentration risk disappears. For an agent, an appropriate adaptation is to document interview prompts, voice guidance, ownership, review routines, and evidence so continuity does not depend on one writer's memory.

## Story selection and evidence

Choose a transformation that resembles a problem the ideal customer recognizes. Relevance strengthens the opening; impressive but unrelated outcomes are weaker commercial evidence. The narrative should reveal the client's goals, barriers, actions, and resulting changes. [S1]

Build an internal evidence ledger before writing. This is an implementation adaptation:

- Record the client's permitted identity, relevant role, starting situation, objective, and actual intervention.
- For every metric, record baseline, final value, unit, measurement window, definition, and the supplied evidence location.
- Separate the founder's interpretation from an independently recorded observation.
- Record turning points, competing explanations, other contributors, and what is still ongoing.
- Mark client permission and founder review status without inventing either.

Prefer one strong, comparable result over an assortment of unrelated vanity metrics. If a count concerns qualified demos, specify what qualified means when known. Do not relabel impressions as buyers, leads as revenue, or correlation as proof of sole causation. This evidentiary discipline is an adaptation informed by the commercial emphasis in [S5] and source inconsistencies recorded in references/sources.md.

If outcomes are unavailable, ask for evidence or create an interview brief. A process story or progress update can be useful, but label it accordingly instead of presenting a completed case study. If anonymization is necessary, preserve permitted context and omit identifying combinations. Never fabricate a name to create the appearance of a named real client.

## Interview engine

### Cadence and agenda

For this package, use Sanginés's personal weekly podcast-style interview as the default. He describes calls with one strategist listener that were not published as podcasts, but became LinkedIn posts. The strategist drafts from what he says, sends the draft back, and revises from feedback. [S3]

Cadence varies across notus engagements. [S4] describes one-hour client calls usually every two weeks, and [S8] documents fortnightly calls. [S9] uses weekly calls. [S10] uses existing material, monthly calls, and asynchronous batch feedback. The weekly default is appropriate to the requested system, not an immutable requirement for every client.

Sanginés's agenda revisits recent work, discusses questions relevant to the ideal customer, reviews comments on earlier posts, and considers successful posts from comparable creators. The purpose is to surface fresh experience and reflect on what has happened, not imitate someone else's wording. [S3]

### Preparation and depth

The public preparation process brainstorms three ideas for each of four pillars, selects one idea per pillar, sketches a potential post, and sends three or four questions per selected idea before the call. These are planning heuristics for obtaining substantive input, not a requirement to publish twelve posts after every conversation. [S4]

The four pillars are tactical advice, aspirational client stories, insightful industry perspectives, and personal founder experiences. Storytelling Case Studies belong in the aspirational pillar. The remaining pillars contribute expertise, judgment, and personality around proof. [S4][S5]

Useful case-study questions, supplied here as an adaptation:

1. Who was the client, and what was at stake for them?
2. What had they tried, and what still prevented progress?
3. What did you do first, and why that sequence?
4. What happened that made you change the plan?
5. Which result can we substantiate, over what period?
6. What changed in the client's daily work or opportunities?
7. What continues now, and what may we publicly disclose?

During the call, keep the eventual post outline in mind, probe beyond surface answers, ask difficult follow-ups, take notes, and reflect meaning back. The founder should share authentic and specific insights, including concrete stories. Sanginés explicitly criticizes generic AI content and shallow input. The agent's role is to extract and shape genuine material, not supply invented experience. [S4]

Recording and transcription support extraction. Riverside is a tool mentioned in the public process, not a required dependency of this skill. Clear audio matters; good video is useful if later repurposing is requested. A public podcast is optional, not a prerequisite for the interview engine. [S3][S4]

## Four-part post

### 1. Emotional opening

Open with a substantial client problem, a meaningful outcome, or an unexpected client fact. Make the target reader care about what happened. A supported before-to-now contrast can make the change tangible. [S1]

Adaptation: draft a few opening options and select the one that best joins reader relevance with evidence. Emotion can come from frustration, relief, surprise, or aspiration. Do not manufacture distress, imply a guaranteed result, or conceal a qualification that changes the meaning of the opening.

### 2. Client's initial situation

Establish the client's identity or relevant context, their goal, and the barrier. Sanginés recommends contrasting an existing strength with a continuing struggle. Put the most familiar problem first and the more distinctive complication later. [S1]

For example, expertise may already exist while a reliable way to reach buyers does not. This is more informative than calling someone an ambitious entrepreneur without describing the bottleneck. Keep the client as the person undergoing change.

### 3. Transformation process

Use meaningful stage names expressed as nouns, and connect them with actual actions and discoveries. The process should read as a sequence of changes, including a real adjustment where one occurred. A brief indication of progress may create anticipation before the full outcome. [S1]

Adaptation: distinguish what was done from why it mattered. A stage labelled Evidence Review needs an action such as comparing objections in sales notes, followed by the resulting decision. Labels alone do not demonstrate expertise. Do not invent an iteration because a narrative seems too linear without one.

The Vicktoria case gives a concrete system example: interview and strategy work, profile reconstruction, recurring calls, and later retention through a podcast and newsletter. Those stages are examples for that case, not obligatory services in every Storytelling Case Study. [S8]

### 4. Resulting situation

Combine concrete metrics with qualitative changes and continuing benefits. Sanginés limits broad category-leadership outcomes to one and recommends closing with the continuing relationship. [S1]

Adaptation: use no category-leadership claim if none is supported. Distinguish completed results from current work. If the engagement ended, end with a true lasting benefit or lesson rather than inventing an ongoing relationship. If a numerical outcome is unavailable but a qualitative change is documented, use it explicitly and explain the missing measure in the internal record.

An optional next step can follow the closing when requested and suitable to the business goal. A CTA is not a fifth required narrative stage and is not a mandatory rule in the four-step source.

## Review and voice

The founder-to-strategist feedback loop is central: extract the founder's words, shape a post, obtain feedback, and revise. Preserve characteristic vocabulary and level of certainty without mechanically reproducing a transcript. [S3]

For expert domains, the review can include a specialist before final founder sign-off. The Vanessa case shows scientific editorial review alongside batch production. This is a useful adaptation when subject accuracy requires additional expertise, not a universal approval chain. [S10]

Do not copy source examples into a new client's story. Do not make the founder sound like Sanginés unless the founder actually speaks that way. Keep internal evidence notes and method citations separate from the audience-facing post unless the user requests a sourced public deliverable.

## Distribution and learning

Sanginés's broader playbook combines a relevant network, consistent publishing across four pillars, conversations based on audience signals, amplification of proven posts, and iteration. Its recap recommends at least three posts per week, assessing promising posts for paid promotion after three months, and evaluating the broader strategy over at least six months. These describe his public playbook rather than universal platform laws or prerequisites for producing one draft. [S5]

A successful Storytelling Case Study was subsequently used as a Thought Leadership Ad targeted at notus's ideal audience. Treat that as a distribution option after organic evidence, not proof that every case study should receive ad spend. [S1]

Pay attention to relevant readers, substantive comments, and qualified conversations or bookings alongside reach. Silent buyers may learn over time before contacting the business, and the website can extend that education through proof and process explanations. A single buyer story supports this possibility but does not establish a universal buyer-journey percentage. [S5][S6]

Adaptation: report observed signals separately from attribution assumptions. Recommend an owner for content review, measurement, and commercial follow-up. Drafting does not itself authorize posts, messages, connection requests, or paid campaigns.

## Warnings and edge cases

| Situation | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Generic or superficial input | Ask for actions, examples, and the underlying experience; the public interview guidance warns against generic AI output. [S4] |
| Case story dominates the entire calendar | Keep aspirational proof alongside the other three pillars. [S4][S5] |
| High engagement from irrelevant readers | Revisit audience and topic fit instead of assuming pipeline impact. [S5] |
| Founder has a rich content archive | Use original interviews, podcasts, or articles as input, with current review, rather than forcing a new call for every post. [S10] |
| Conflicting public metrics | Keep each metric tied to its source and period, or omit it pending clarification; do not average incompatible snapshots. Adaptation. |
| Unsupported victory or stalled project | Request evidence or publish an accurately labelled progress story. Adaptation. |
| Client cannot be named | Use permitted anonymized context, remove identifying details, and disclose anonymization. Adaptation. |
| Finished relationship | Close on an enduring benefit instead of suggesting continued collaboration. Adaptation. |
| Several contributors caused the change | Explain the actual contribution and avoid exclusive credit. Adaptation. |
| Paid materials are advertised | Use only accessible public descriptions; this package does not claim to reproduce the private playbook. Adaptation. |
