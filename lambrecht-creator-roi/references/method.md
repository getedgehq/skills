# Performance creator marketing for e-commerce

## Evidence and terminology

Read [sources.md](sources.md) for [S1] through [S8]. Source-backed guidance is cited below. Sections labelled implementation guidance are this package's operational additions, not claimed quotations or proprietary Lambrecht rules. No exact expert formula was available from the supplied video's public description. BrandFormance is a public framework name connecting brand effects and measurable performance. TKP means Tausenderkontaktpreis, the price per thousand contacts; Neukunden means new customers; Zielgruppen-Penetration describes penetration of an audience; Staffelrabatte means tiered discounts. [S2] [S4]

The central discipline is to buy audience access and credible storytelling with a testable business hypothesis, then use campaign evidence to decide the next investment. Followings, fame, and polished pitches cannot establish profitable acquisition. Neither perfect attribution nor immediate profitability is a credible universal expectation. [S1] [S3] [S6]

## 1. Start with the acquisition problem

State the intended customer, product, market, time horizon, and measurable outcome before creator outreach. Review store and analytics evidence, including new-customer share and prior channel performance. Lambrecht's public case uses existing customer demographics and historical comparable-audience data to select YouTube and Twitch rather than defaulting to Instagram. The case is self-reported, so its results are not a portable forecast. [S3] [S7]

Implementation guidance: Record acceptable acquisition cost, contribution per order, maximum loss, stock constraints, and cash payback horizon. Derive thresholds from the brand's economics. If only gross revenue is available, provide a revenue ratio and mark profit ROI as unavailable. A request for profitable growth cannot be resolved by impressions alone.

## 2. Create a ledger that supports decisions

Capture estimated and actual views, post-placement audience insights, sessions, conversion rate, CAC, and new-customer ROAS. Monthly and campaign-level reporting answer different questions. Persistent YouTube conversions should not disappear when the publication month ends. [S4]

Implementation guidance: Use one placement ID for creator, platform, live date, format, integration length, fee, rights, samples, management allocation, tracking links, actual reach, views, sessions, order IDs, new-customer flag, net revenue, and observation date. Keep unknown values unknown, not zero. Track data provenance and whether measurements are estimated, reported by the creator, or verified in first-party data.

Maintain two views: calendar-month spending and revenue for cash management; cumulative placement cohorts for campaign evaluation. Define an allocation policy for bundled fees and shared work. Never change that policy midway to make a creator look better.

## 3. Instrument, then interpret

Use creator-specific links with UTM parameters, dedicated landing pages where useful, codes when appropriate, and store/analytics data. Links and codes provide complementary visibility; multi-platform journeys limit last-click attribution. Recent public notes emphasize code leakage and UTM tracking. [S1] [S3] [S4] [S8]

Implementation guidance:

- Identify platform, campaign, creator, and placement in a documented naming scheme. Test the destination and preservation of parameters before publication.
- Deduplicate code and UTM orders by order ID. A purchase may appear in both views but enters the combined total once.
- Separate first orders from returning-customer orders. Separate orders from customers when a customer buys twice.
- Report attributable outcomes as model-dependent observations. Brand-search uplift, direct traffic, and assisted conversions are supporting signals, not extra revenue to add on top of the same orders.
- Investigate code redemptions before publication, coupon-site exposure, and changes in returning-customer share. Show raw and adjusted views with an exclusion rationale.
- Keep organic creator delivery and paid amplification distinguishable. Include their respective costs and disclose shared touchpoints.

A broken link or missing customer status makes a campaign inconclusive. Fix the measurement or narrow the conclusion. Do not promise complete attribution merely because UTMs exist.

## 4. Select the audience and integration

Compare audience demographics, geography, engagement quality, content style, product relevance, and past brand integrations. Examine competitor history and the quality of comments, not only their count. Actual active audiences can outperform much larger nominal followings. Platforms differ in explanatory depth, longevity, community interaction, and creative context. [S3] [S8]

Implementation guidance: Request recent comparable placement evidence and a range of likely delivery. Compare TKP only with its denominator stated: impressions, views, or unique reach. Total followers are not delivered contacts. Do not mix a view-based YouTube TKP with an impression-based Instagram TKP without explaining the difference. Historical analogues should match market, format, product price, and customer type where possible. Scouting tools can surface candidates; they cannot certify profitability. [S4]

## 5. Forecast defensibly

The Upway article recommends conservative and optimistic scenarios, planning internally against the conservative one, and learning across campaigns. It foregrounds CAC, session costs, conversion, and authenticity. It uses revenue-to-cost ratios under an ROI label, while the tracking article distinguishes ROI from ROAS. Preserve the decision logic while explicitly resolving those metric definitions. [S1] [S8]

Implementation guidance: Let B be all-in campaign cost, M be media/creator spend under a disclosed convention, V be views, q be sessions per view, c be orders per session, n be new-customer share, a be net average order revenue, and g be contribution margin rate before acquisition costs.

- Sessions = V x q.
- Orders = sessions x c.
- New customers = orders x n, assuming one first order per new customer.
- CAC = B / new customers.
- Cost per session = B / sessions.
- Session CVR = orders / sessions.
- View-to-order rate = orders / views. This differs from session CVR.
- Revenue = orders x a.
- Revenue-to-total-cost ratio = revenue / B.
- ROAS = attributed revenue / M. State exactly what M includes.
- New-customer ROAS = attributed first-order revenue / M.
- Contribution before acquisition = revenue x g.
- Contribution ROI = (contribution before acquisition - B) / B.

Use net revenue after discounts and expected refunds, with consistent tax treatment. Contribution margin already includes the specified variable product and fulfillment costs; subtract acquisition cost once. For observed CAC, use distinct new customers directly rather than estimating them from order share. If new customers or sessions are zero, the corresponding cost metric is undefined or unbounded, not zero. No result here proves incrementality. Distinguish measured campaign contribution from incremental contribution established through suitable controls.

If reliable view-to-order evidence exists, a simpler forecast can use it. Do not multiply views by a session conversion rate. Do not import a generic 3:1 or 6.5:1 claim as the brand's target. Use validated cohort contribution for CLV scenarios, and separate speculative lifetime value from near-term cash return.

## 6. Keep the brief strict on substance and flexible on delivery

Require accurate product identification, core benefits, and a clear CTA; allow the creator to express them in their own style. The Upway narrative credits authentic explanation and unconventional creator storytelling, and warns against restrictive guidelines or copying international strategies without adaptation. Creative fit is part of performance, not decoration. [S1] [S3]

Implementation guidance: Give the creator the customer problem, product evidence, mandatory facts, destination, and prohibited unsupported claims. Confirm stock and landing-page message match. Specify paid-use permissions and additional production or distribution costs only if relevant. Do not assume organic purchase metrics also describe Partnership Ad performance.

## 7. Use BrandFormance and mature cohorts

Track business outcomes alongside signals of audience response. Public podcast notes describe sessions, brand-search uplift, brand fit, engagement, UTM traffic, and TKP/community ratio alongside CAC, new-customer ROAS, CVR, and CLV. The exact weighting of these signals is not established in the fetched descriptions. [S4]

Implementation guidance: Organize the evidence into awareness/delivery, trust/engagement, and conversion/economics. Do not invent a weighted BrandFormance score. Record the baseline, observed change, data confidence, and plausible confounders for each supporting signal. Strong soft KPIs can justify investigation or a capped retest, not an unsupported declaration of profit.

Lambrecht's more recent post favors evaluating creators over 60 to 90 days and describes repeat use of proven performers. Older tracking guidance recommends at least 30 days. For this package, use the recent longer window as a starting point where the customer journey warrants it; label earlier results provisional. Continue watching delivery problems and budget exposure during that window. [S5] [S8]

## 8. Diagnose before allocating

The public approach is iterative: test, measure, learn, remove weak approaches, and expand successful ones. Seven-day CAC-only evaluation can discard useful creators or scale misleading results. [S1] [S3] [S6]

The following table is implementation guidance, not a published Lambrecht scoring system.

| Evidence | Decision | Next evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missing links, customer status, or usable delivery data | Repair or declare inconclusive | Verified ledger and tracking |
| Mature cohort, acceptable CAC and contribution, good fit | Rebook or scale in stages | Marginal results from next placement |
| Strong traffic or trust signals, immature conversions | Observe; consider a capped retest if affordable | Mature cohort and attribution review |
| Strong traffic, weak conversion | Diagnose destination, offer, stock, and audience intent | A controlled repair with one main change |
| Weak qualified delivery and poor fit | Stop or change creator | New audience hypothesis |
| Attractive code ROAS, leaked codes or high existing-customer share | Recalculate before any expansion | Deduplicated clean Neukunden outcomes |
| Later placements deteriorate | Reduce frequency, revise terms, or stop | Marginal acquisition and audience evidence |

Every action needs a spend cap, owner, review date, and stopping condition. Preserve historical cohorts even if future spending stops. A slow conversion cycle is not a reason to tolerate broken execution or unlimited losses.

## 9. Challenge long-term bookings without abandoning relationships

Public material warns that audience penetration, declining creator reach, and inflexible booking structures can erode profitability despite advertised discounts. It discusses exit clauses and Staffelrabatte. The fetched show notes do not disclose exact contractual wording or the numerical TKP example, so neither is reproduced here. [S4]

Repeated use of proven creators can build trust; cross-platform expansion and paid reuse can extend their value. This supports sustained relationships with performance reviews rather than automatic acceptance of a prepaid package. [S5]

Implementation guidance:

1. Forecast every later placement with its own views, sessions, new-customer share, and marginal contribution. Model decay and overlap instead of repeating first-placement results.
2. Compare a staged option, a fixed package, and a downside case on consistent cost and time bases. Include unused commitment, exclusivity, rights, and cancellation charges where applicable.
3. Calculate marginal CAC for later placements separately from blended CAC. Blending can conceal unprofitable repetitions.
4. If considering Staffelrabatte, require later prices to remain economical under credible lower delivery. A percentage fee reduction can be smaller than a percentage decline in qualified reach.
5. Ask for review gates, observable delivery criteria, performance-triggered exits, notice periods, payment timing, and treatment of unused placements. These are proposed fields for negotiation, not claimed Lambrecht contract language.
6. Choose commitment only when its downside remains affordable and the evidence supports it. Waiting for conversion maturity does not require buying every future placement upfront.

Do not assume saturation always wins over trust building or that repetition always improves conversion. Measure both on later cohorts. Reach decline, audience renewal, creative variation, and product relevance can change the result.

## 10. Reduce discount dependence in phases

The public descriptions present three phases: build hard-KPI evidence while codes are used; relate that evidence to soft-KPI benchmarks; gradually reduce code dependence with UTM measurement maintained. Removing discounts overnight risks performance disruption. [S2] [S4]

Implementation guidance: Establish clean acquisition and contribution baselines first. Then compare traffic, trust signals, and purchase outcomes across sufficiently comparable placements. Reduce discount depth or availability in a bounded test, retaining tracking links and monitoring conversion, net margin, and CAC. Changing discount incentives changes buyer behavior, so old code-based conversion assumptions may no longer apply. If results deteriorate beyond the declared tolerance, pause or revise the test. Soft-KPI associations are forecasts, not substitute observed sales.

## 11. Edge cases and warnings

- **High-ticket products:** Long consideration windows and session economics matter; do not assume the Upway case transfers to another category. [S1]
- **Evergreen YouTube:** Keep cumulative cohort reporting after the booking month. Revenue arriving now may relate to earlier placements. [S4]
- **Multiple platforms:** Separate costs and deduplicate outcomes. Cross-platform reach totals are not automatically unique people. Implementation guidance.
- **Strong soft KPIs, mature losses:** Explain possible future value as a hypothesis, then cap the experiment. Do not relabel losses as success. Implementation guidance informed by [S4].
- **Low new-customer share:** Gross revenue may mask limited acquisition value; report Neukunden separately. [S4]
- **No comparable history:** Use explicit ranges and a bounded learning budget. Never fill the sheet with borrowed success-case results. Implementation guidance informed by [S1] [S7].
- **Operational confounders:** Stockouts, broken checkout, promotions, and overlapping media can explain changes. Mark affected cohorts rather than blaming or crediting the creator automatically. Implementation guidance.
- **Tools and agency claims:** Prefer inspectable campaign evidence and company-specific forecasts over prestige, tool sophistication, or promotional statistics. [S1] [S4]

Finish with a recommendation whose confidence matches the evidence, an economical next step, and the specific observation that would change the decision.
