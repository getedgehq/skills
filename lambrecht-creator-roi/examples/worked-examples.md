# Worked examples

All brands, creators, fees, performance figures, thresholds, and terms below are invented illustrations. They are not Lambrecht or Ad Specialist client results. The calculations use the implementation conventions in [method.md](../references/method.md).

## Illustration 1: A famous creator versus a matched audience

### Situation

An invented D2C homeware shop sells a EUR 100 net basket after discounts and refunds. Contribution margin before acquisition is 40%. Its acquisition target is CAC no greater than EUR 25 and nonnegative first-order contribution ROI. It has EUR 5,000 available for a test. All forecasts use an observation window of 90 days; actual early results remain provisional.

### Weak first draft

Choose Creator A because they have one million followers and promise 200,000 views for EUR 5,000. The shop converts 3% of visitors, so expect 6,000 orders from those views. Revenue will be EUR 600,000, giving ROI of 120:1. Sign a six-month agreement to secure a discount.

Problems: Followers do not establish audience fit; the forecast applies session CVR to views; revenue is called profit ROI; no new-customer share is specified; the all-in cost exceeds the fee; no evidence supports the six-month commitment.

### Improved result

Review audience data and prior comparable placements. Creator B has a smaller following but a better customer match and offers an authentic product demonstration. Both forecasts include management and sample costs. Media spend M means creator fee only in this illustration.

| Field | A conservative | B conservative | B upside |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Creator fee M | EUR 5,000 | EUR 2,800 | EUR 2,800 |
| Additional campaign costs | EUR 1,000 | EUR 700 | EUR 700 |
| All-in B | EUR 6,000 | EUR 3,500 | EUR 3,500 |
| Views V | 200,000 | 100,000 | 120,000 |
| Sessions per view q | 0.5% | 2% | 2.5% |
| Sessions | 1,000 | 2,000 | 3,000 |
| Orders per session c | 3% | 10% | 10% |
| Orders | 30 | 200 | 300 |
| New-customer share n | 60% | 80% | 80% |
| New customers | 18 | 160 | 240 |
| Net revenue | EUR 3,000 | EUR 20,000 | EUR 30,000 |
| CAC, all-in | EUR 333.33 | EUR 21.88 | EUR 14.58 |
| ROAS, revenue/M | 0.60 | 7.14 | 10.71 |
| Revenue/all-in cost | 0.50 | 5.71 | 8.57 |
| Contribution before acquisition | EUR 1,200 | EUR 8,000 | EUR 12,000 |
| Contribution ROI | -80% | 128.57% | 242.86% |

B conservative: 100,000 x 0.02 = 2,000 sessions; 2,000 x 0.10 = 200 orders; 200 x 0.80 = 160 new customers. EUR 3,500 / 160 = EUR 21.875 CAC. Contribution ROI = (EUR 20,000 x 0.40 - EUR 3,500) / EUR 3,500 = 128.57%.

These invented conversion assumptions would need supporting category and placement evidence in real work. Creator B's creator-fee TKP is EUR 28 per thousand views, higher than A's EUR 25, yet its forecast acquisition economics are stronger. Cheap views alone do not identify the better purchase.

Recommendation: Run B as a EUR 3,500 capped pilot, reserving EUR 1,500 without committing it. Marketing owns verified links and the ledger before launch. Review delivery after seven days, cohort evidence after 30 days, and mature economics at day 90. Pause new spend for broken tracking; do not reject the creator solely on immature CAC. Rebooking requires observed mature acquisition evidence, not this forecast. The revenue and profit calculations are attributed forecast values, not proof of incremental sales.

Rules that changed the decision: audience and brand fit, section 4; denominator discipline and full costs, section 5; creative freedom, section 6; mature observation, section 7; staged commitments, section 9. These operational calculations implement principles from [S1], [S3], and [S8].

## Illustration 2: A discount that hides expensive later placements

### Situation

An invented accessories brand has completed one creator placement. All-in cost was EUR 3,000, with 100,000 views and 120 distinct new customers, each generating one EUR 100 net order. Contribution per first order is EUR 40. The mature cohort therefore shows CAC EUR 25 and contribution ROI (EUR 4,800 - EUR 3,000) / EUR 3,000 = 60%.

The creator offers four future placements at EUR 2,400 each, a 20% all-in discount, with EUR 9,600 committed and no exit. The shop's maximum acceptable CAC remains EUR 30 and its maximum affordable committed downside is EUR 3,000.

### Weak first draft

The creator has already proved profitable. Buy the four-placement package because the lower fee means CAC falls to EUR 20. Forecast 480 new customers by repeating the first result four times. A recurring partnership will necessarily improve trust and conversion.

Problems: It assumes unchanged reach and acquisition for every placement, ignores audience penetration, and confuses a useful relationship with an economical fixed commitment.

### Improved result

Forecast later placement cohorts separately. The downside model assumes falling delivery and new-customer yield. Each placement's all-in cost remains EUR 2,400. TKP below uses all-in cost per thousand views consistently.

| Future placement | Views | New customers | CAC | All-in view TKP | Contribution less campaign cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 90,000 | 100 | EUR 24 | EUR 26.67 | EUR 1,600 |
| 2 | 75,000 | 75 | EUR 32 | EUR 32 | EUR 600 |
| 3 | 60,000 | 50 | EUR 48 | EUR 40 | -EUR 400 |
| 4 | 45,000 | 30 | EUR 80 | EUR 53.33 | -EUR 1,200 |
| Total | 270,000 | 255 | EUR 37.65 | EUR 35.56 | EUR 600 |

Package contribution ROI is EUR 600 / EUR 9,600 = 6.25%, but CAC breaches the EUR 30 acquisition target. It also creates commitment exposure beyond the EUR 3,000 limit. The first booking's EUR 30 all-in TKP becomes EUR 53.33 on the last placement despite the discount.

Recommendation: Reject these fixed package terms. Propose one EUR 2,400 placement with future options conditional on mature cohort results. Do not imply the creator must accept an optional single placement at the package price. If the standalone quote stays EUR 3,000, the same 100 forecast new customers imply CAC EUR 30, so the stage can still meet the acquisition ceiling. If neither price nor flexibility works, evaluate another creator.

Marketing owns the next placement review at day 90. Procurement records an exit proposal tied to agreed delivery evidence, notice, and unused fees, subject to negotiation. Rebooking depends on actual audience renewal, marginal CAC, and contribution, not an automatic saturation assumption. Repetition may strengthen trust; later evidence could support expansion.

Rules that changed the decision: mutable audience performance and cohort data, sections 2 and 7; marginal economics and Staffelrabatte, section 9; evidence-matched action, section 8. The contract fields and numerical scenario are this package's implementation guidance. Public support is the booking-risk discussion in [S4] and the sustained-partnership distinction in [S5].
