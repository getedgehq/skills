# Brand UGC deliverable template

Fill the bracketed fields. Guidance beneath each field explains what a complete answer contains. Delete guidance in the client-ready version. Cite public method rationale with [S#] and supplied product evidence with its actual origin. Label assumptions. No section should imply that invented examples are measured results.

## 1. Assignment

- Brand and product: [Name and specific offer]
- Audience and market: [People, situation, language, geography]
- Platform and placements: [Requested channels and placements]
- Intended action: [Purchase, qualified demo, download, or defined awareness outcome]
- Current control and symptom: [Existing ad or explicit lack of one]
- Resources: [Creators, locations, production time, reusable footage, budget]
- Missing inputs: [Unknown facts that limit the brief]

Guidance: Preserve the user's assignment. Distinguish a creative-production budget from an advertising-test budget. Do not silently replace a demo goal with downloads or a purchase goal with views.

## 2. Diagnosis and message foundation

- Generic pattern to replace: [What makes the current ad interchangeable]
- Evidence of the problem: [Creative observation, analytics, customer input, or hypothesis]
- Audience friction: [One concrete situation]
- Relevant capability: [Verified feature that addresses it]
- Proof: [Demonstration, approved customer evidence, or specific missing evidence]
- Awareness assumption: [What viewers already understand]
- Claims boundary: [What the team can and cannot substantiate]

Guidance: Link the friction to capability and proof before writing hooks. Audience beliefs are not product facts. List source and approval status for any claim that materially drives persuasion.

## 3. Format system

- Working series name: [A useful brand-connected title]
- Recurring premise: [One sentence describing what every episode does]
- Format choice and rationale: [Why this form fits the problem and resources]
- Stable recognition elements: [Role, setting, ritual, visual cue, or verbal framing]
- Brand connection: [Why those elements belong to this brand]
- Variable elements: [Questions, objections, products, demonstrations, openings]
- Recognition check: [What connects episodes without relying solely on an end card]
- Rejected alternative: [Optional, only if it explains a meaningful tradeoff]

Guidance: A color or watermark alone is insufficient if the series premise remains generic. Keep enough room for natural delivery and useful variation. A staged podcast is optional.

## 4. Creator and production brief

- Creator role and selection criteria: [Required performance and product competence]
- Actual relationship to the brand: [Presenter, employee, expert, or genuine customer]
- Natural-delivery freedom: [What wording or interactions may vary]
- Fixed facts and format elements: [What must remain accurate and recognizable]
- Location, props, and demonstration assets: [Feasible shoot requirements]
- Rights and distribution dependencies: [Intended accounts, placements, edits, duration]
- Transparency requirements: [Accurate presentation of staged or sponsored material]
- Minimum viable shoot: [The simplest production that preserves the premise]

Guidance: List unresolved permissions as dependencies. A brief is not permission to book talent, publish, or spend money. Do not portray actors as satisfied customers without an actual substantiated history.

## 5. Episode scripts

Create enough executions to demonstrate repeatability within the user's scope. A useful default is two complete episodes and one additional episode idea; this is a package recommendation, not a published method requirement.

### Episode [Number]: [Audience question]

- Message hypothesis: [Why this question should matter]
- Planned duration: [Estimate justified by the content and placement]

| Explanation | [Capability connected to friction] | [Mechanism] | [Supporting cue] | [Verified feature source] |
| Proof | [Specific supported statement] | [Observable demonstration] | [Necessary qualification] | [Evidence and status] |

- Alternative opening: [One changed variable]

Guidance: Timing is a working estimate, not a universal runtime. Do not put invented social proof in the proof column. When a screen demonstration is planned, name the actual capability and use appropriate demo data.

## 6. Test and decision plan

- Hypothesis: [Format change and expected business effect]
- Control: [Comparable current format]
- First variable changed: [Usually format, with other changes disclosed]
- Conditions held comparable: [Offer, audience, objective, destination, attribution window]
- Primary outcome and formula: [For example, spend divided by qualified inquiries]
- Qualification definition: [Who counts, who evaluates, and when]
- Diagnostic metrics and definitions: [Attention threshold, outbound clicks, watch depth]
- Business guardrail: [Margin, lead quality, or other relevant constraint]
- Spend cap and time window: [Client-approved limits or pending values]
- Minimum evidence to decide: [Baseline-informed sample and uncertainty treatment]
- Continue, revise, stop, or scale rule: [Action tied to evidence]
- Confounders and limitations: [Changes that prevent clean causal attribution]

Guidance: Leave missing budget values pending. Do not transplant case-study numbers as thresholds. A tiny sample produces a directional observation, not proof. Scale only within separately authorized limits.

## 7. Continuation and handoff

- Next audience questions: [New episodes with the same premise]
- Refresh logic: [Which variables change for which observed problems]
- Paid/organic learning link: [How relevant observations can inform a comparable test]
- Reusable assets: [What can be repurposed without distorting meaning]
- Owner and next action: [Who can resolve production or measurement dependencies]
- Evidence note: [Core method sources, supporting later guidance, and package adaptations]
- Review outcome: [Pass, revisions required, or provisional with named dependencies]

Guidance: Report what is ready and what remains uncertain. Reference the checklist. Distinguish favorable platform signals from demonstrated business impact and measured brand recognition.
