# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations of this package's operational rules. The brands, creators, scripts, budgets, and thresholds are fictional. They are not adslab client work, statements by Levitan, or measured results. Rule identifiers refer to references/method.md.

## Illustration 1: Dockline laptop dock

### Assignment and inputs

Dockline sells a USB-C laptop dock to hybrid workers. The supplied hypothetical product facts are three supported peripheral connections and a one-cable connection to a compatible laptop. The brand voice is practical and slightly playful. Budget permits one creator and a desk shoot. The goal is purchases, with an existing generic creator ad as the control.

Unknowns: exact compatibility list, permitted performance claims, price, and unit economics. The script cannot claim compatibility with all laptops or imply that every dock supports every monitor configuration.

### Weak first draft

Creator holds a box in a generic bedroom: "I found this amazing dock and it changed my workday. It's super convenient. You need one too. Shop now."

Problem: The praise is vague, the setting is interchangeable, and nothing demonstrates the capability. Adding a logo would identify the advertiser without creating a repeatable creative premise.

### Improved result

**Series premise:** Dockline Desk Rescue. The same creator acts as a practical desk troubleshooter. A cobalt work mat, an overhead desk view, and the recurring ritual of reconnecting a desk become the stable cues. New episodes address specific setup frustrations.

**Audience problem:** Reconnecting several peripherals when switching between home and office.

**Proposed episode:** A one-cable arrival routine, approximately 22 seconds as a production illustration.

| Beat | Spoken line | Image and text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | "Three desk connections. One laptop arrival." | Overhead view of three disconnected peripherals on the recurring mat; series title remains readable |
| Friction | "This is my setup every time I get back." | Creator reconnects peripherals individually; no exaggerated timing claim |
| Mechanism | "These stay in the Dockline dock. This cable connects it to my compatible laptop." | Continuous shot connects the cable and shows the verified setup working |
| Proof boundary | "Check your laptop and display setup before choosing a dock." | Compatibility page cue; actual supported devices must be verified before filming |
| Action | "See whether Dockline fits your desk." | Product and relevant compatibility destination |

**Creator brief:** Speak conversationally, explain the actual connection, and show the setup in a continuous take. Keep the role and demonstration ritual. Avoid universal compatibility and unsupported speed claims.

**Next episodes:** Switching from a shared desk, arranging a small desk, and explaining the verified port layout. These are new situations within the same series, not copies with different hooks.

**Test:** Compare the new format with the current control on the same product offer and destination. Fictional first-pass limit: EUR 600 over seven days, subject to authorization and a check against the account's actual conversion costs. Record purchases, CPA, outbound clicks, and initial attention with a defined view threshold. Compare the two CPA estimates only if each arm reaches the pre-agreed minimum of ten purchases; otherwise report the test as directional or inconclusive. A target of 15% lower CPA is an invented planning threshold, not a reliable statistical significance rule. Stop at the spend cap even if the sample remains small. Review contribution information before scaling.

### Rules that made the difference

- R1 exposed the interchangeable praise.
- R2 connected the worker's friction to a verified capability.
- R3 created recognizable cues tied to the brand's desk problem.
- R4 selected a demonstration instead of an unnecessary podcast set.
- R5 and R6 gave the creator a believable role and repeatable shoot structure.
- R7 prevented high viewing numbers from being mistaken for purchase success.

**Counterfactual repair:** If attention improves but purchases do not, check compatibility objections, price, and destination continuity before changing the mat or presenter.

## Illustration 2: Shortlist recruitment software

### Assignment and inputs

Shortlist provides collaborative candidate review for small hiring teams. Its supplied hypothetical capabilities are shared notes and a visible review status. It wants qualified demo inquiries from HR managers. It has two available presenters and a quiet meeting room. The previous creative attracts cheap, poorly qualified form submissions.

Unknowns: integrations, time savings, customer references, and any comparative performance claims. None may be invented. The fictional presenters are brand representatives, not independent industry experts or customers.

### Weak first draft

Presenter in front of a microphone: "Our software revolutionizes recruitment. Everyone is switching. Hiring managers, download our guide now."

Problem: A microphone supplies the appearance of a podcast without a recurring idea or evidence. Popularity is unsupported, and a guide download is being confused with the requested demo objective.

### Improved result

**Series premise:** Shortlist Hiring Room. A staged branded conversation tackles one handoff question per episode. The recurring host asks a skeptical operational question; a product guide answers with a visible workflow. A branded review board and the same conversational roles connect the episodes.

**Audience problem:** Interview feedback is distributed across messages, so colleagues cannot see whether a candidate has been reviewed.

**Transparency:** Describe the series as a brand-produced demonstration conversation. Identify the presenters' actual roles. Do not present it as a clip from independent journalism or an unsolicited customer interview.

**Proposed episode:** Approximately 30 seconds, adjusted to placement and clarity after an initial recording.

| Beat | Spoken line | Image and text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | Host: "Who has actually reviewed this candidate?" | Host points to a fictional scattered-message example; recurring series cue |
| Friction | Guide: "When notes live in separate chats, the next reviewer has to ask again." | Illustrative messages labeled as a demonstration |
| Mechanism | Host: "What can the team see here?" Guide: "The notes and each review status, together." | Screen recording of a working product demonstration with dummy data |
| Boundary | Guide: "This shows the handoff. It doesn't make the hiring decision for you." | Exact verified interface; no invented automation |
| Action | Host: "Manage hiring reviews? Book a walkthrough of this workflow." | Clear demo CTA with qualification prompt |

**Creator brief:** Keep the question natural and the response specific. A staged discussion is acceptable; invented results, credentials, and endorsements are not. Use dummy candidate records.

**Production plan:** Batch three handoff questions in the same room. Keep host roles and board; vary the objection and screen demonstration. Record a second opening about duplicated feedback without changing the body.

**Test:** First fix the mismatch between the intended demo outcome and the download CTA. Establish a new generic-format control using the same demo offer and qualification flow. Then compare it with Hiring Room. This separates the offer correction from the format comparison. Use cost per qualified demo inquiry and the proportion accepted by sales as primary business evidence, with attendance tracked later. Set spend cap and minimum qualified outcomes from the client's baseline; missing values remain dependencies. Raw lead cost alone cannot select the winner.

### Rules that made the difference

- R1 recognized a lead-quality problem rather than assuming an attention problem.
- R2 used a concrete team friction and observable features.
- R3 and R4 made the podcast option a recurring brand explanation format.
- R5 preserved truthful presenter roles.
- R6 generated reusable episodes with different objections.
- R7 isolated the CTA repair before testing format and measured qualified inquiries.
- R8 supplied a continuation plan based on where the conversion path fails.

**Counterfactual repair:** If form volume drops but qualified demo inquiries rise within the cost limit, report the improved qualification instead of declaring failure from volume alone.
