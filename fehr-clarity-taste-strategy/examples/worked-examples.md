# Worked examples

Credit: Independent illustrations applying the public principles of Sophia Marie Fehr and this package's adaptations. Source IDs refer to [the source register](../references/sources.md).

All organizations, people, evidence, figures, commitments, and results below are invented for illustration. These are not FEHR engagements. Improved results mean improved strategy briefs, not measured business outcomes.

## Illustration 1: A family hotel wants a higher room rate

### Invented inputs

House Linden has 28 rooms in an Alpine village. Recent guest notes repeatedly praise family hosts, simple regional breakfasts, and restful evenings. Existing guests are mostly walking couples. The owners want to attract short-stay urban guests seeking recovery. The building has comfortable rooms but uneven corridor acoustics. The founder handles most guest questions personally. The co-owner attending the meeting can approve an operating pilot but needs both owners' agreement for capital expenditure.

### Weak first draft

House Linden will become an iconic premium lifestyle destination for discerning travelers. We will introduce a fashionable identity, add a signature scent, use cinematic social content, and lift room rates by 25 percent. The strategy meeting will approve the whole rebrand after reviewing a 60-slide moodboard. Our authentic hospitality and elevated experiences will appeal to everyone who values quality.

### Diagnosis

The draft substitutes premium styling for a defined benefit. It confuses existing and desired guests, implies authority the attendee lacks, and leaves operational gaps untouched. Its sentence fits almost any hotel. The proposed rate increase has no evidence of willingness to pay. Clarity, mandate, audience choice, and offer execution all fail. [S1][S2][S3]

### Improved result

**Recommendation and status:** Pilot a calm weekend offer for urban couples seeking a short restorative break. The co-owner can approve the pilot today. Defer capital spending and any permanent rate change until both owners review readiness and guest evidence.

**Three diagnostic answers:**

1. Decision authority: The co-owner approves the pilot schedule and staff routines within the existing budget; both owners decide capital works.
2. Internal clarity: A newcomer restatement has not been tested. Proposed sentence: House Linden gives city couples a quiet Alpine weekend through attentive family hosting, regional breakfasts, and an unhurried stay.
3. Customer horizon: This pilot serves desired short-break guests. Current walking couples remain welcome, with breakfast timing and trail advice retained; the offer avoids loud evening events that would conflict with both groups.

**Core and proof:** Family hosting and regional food are lived strengths in the invented guest notes. Restfulness is promising but uneven acoustics limit the claim. Communicate a calm rhythm while addressing noise; do not promise perfect silence.

**Creative direction:** Show the host preparing breakfast, the actual walking route, and the pace of a real morning. Use practical arrival guidance and clear descriptions of the stay. Avoid nightclub-like launch events and generic luxury imagery. Taste should express the offer's specific character. [S1][S6]

**Delivery rules:** Assign a backup host on every shift. Record guest preferences in a shared handover. Place quiet-room requests explicitly in the booking flow. Map corridor noise and test low-cost improvements before deciding on construction. A restful offer draws on the quiet-hospitality observation but is chosen because these invented guests value it. [S5]

**Actions and review:** The co-owner launches a four-week pilot after staff briefing. The operations lead checks weekly whether promised room arrangements and breakfast routines were delivered. At the end, the owners review guest restfulness comments, unmet expectations, and demand at the pilot price before approving investment or rate changes. These measures and timescales are package adaptations.

**Excluded:** Event tourism as a primary growth audience, a broad luxury claim, and a permanent rate increase unsupported by guest evidence.

### Rules that made the difference

- Decision mandate converted a fictitious rebrand approval into a feasible pilot. [S2]
- The employee-sentence test exposed unresolved positioning. [S2]
- Current versus desired customer choice made the transition explicit. [S2]
- Core before expression connected creative choices to lived hospitality. [S3]
- Identity certainty and quiet as an offer changed both imagery and operating behavior. [S6][S5]

## Illustration 2: A grocery brand wants more distinctive creative

### Invented inputs

Pantry Signal runs one neighborhood shop and a local delivery service. Its existing customers are time-poor households buying reliable dinner ingredients. The founder wants to attract affluent wellness shoppers after seeing fashionable grocery stores online. Staff already test easy meal combinations, but the shop has no verified nutrition-screening process. The marketing lead can choose creative direction within the existing audience; the founder controls product range and positioning changes.

### Weak first draft

Make Pantry Signal the city's leading wellness lifestyle destination. Use monochrome packaging, fashionable collaborators, and premium organic language. Launch a curated range with influencer photography so affluent customers recognize us. A beautiful store will build trust, and our existing customers will appreciate the upgrade. The marketing team should announce the new position next week.

### Diagnosis

The draft imports another category's appearance without examining the existing offer. It assumes the desired customer will arrive, overlooks current customers' needs, implies nutrition expertise, and gives marketing authority over a product decision. It also equates price and visual polish with trust. Fehr's grocery analysis helps identify the difference between a purposeful curated offer and status-driven presentation. [S4]

### Improved result

**Recommendation and status:** Keep the dinner-use audience for the immediate creative refresh. Recommend a separate discovery project for the founder's wellness ambition. The marketing lead can approve this scoped refresh; no product or positioning expansion is approved.

**Three diagnostic answers:**

1. Decision authority: Marketing controls presentation, while the founder decides range standards and strategic audience shifts.
2. Internal clarity: Working sentence: Pantry Signal helps busy neighborhood households cook a dependable dinner with staff-tested ingredient combinations and clear substitutions. Staff can assess it at the next briefing; a newcomer test remains pending.
3. Customer horizon: The refresh serves existing dinner customers. Desired wellness shoppers are a future hypothesis requiring needs research and credible product standards.

**Core and proof:** Staff-tested combinations and substitutions are supported by the invented operating practice. Nutrition authority is unsupported. The brand's curation benefit is reducing dinner decisions, not certifying health outcomes.

**Creative direction:** Photograph real meal combinations in the shop's own setting. Explain why the ingredients work together, show substitutions, and use readable preparation cards. Avoid a fashionable collaborator whose main contribution would be a borrowed status signal. Select partners only if they strengthen dinner usefulness and the brand's specific point of view. [S4][S7]

**Delivery rules:** Every featured dinner bundle gets a staff preparation check, a complete ingredient list, and an affordable substitution. Merchandising and delivery pages use the same meal logic. Keep stock alternatives visible so the promise survives a missing ingredient.

**Actions and review:** Marketing drafts the new product-page and window brief within ten working days. The shop manager verifies the featured meal instructions before publication. Review whether customers understand the dinner benefit and whether featured bundles can be fulfilled. The founder separately decides whether to fund wellness discovery. Metrics and ownership are adaptations, not Fehr's stated protocol.

**Excluded:** Medical or nutritional claims, presumed affluent demand, and an immediate price rise based on visual redesign.

### Rules that made the difference

- Audience choice protected a useful current proposition while allowing a future hypothesis. [S2]
- Curation became a specific customer benefit with observable standards. [S4]
- Taste became deliberate image and partner selection instead of copied styling. [S1][S7]
- Execution requirements made the position repeatable across store and delivery. [S1][S3]
- Separating observations from aspirations prevented an unsupported health promise; this is a package safeguard.
