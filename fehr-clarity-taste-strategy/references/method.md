# Method and operational guidance

## Evidence and scope

Fehr names her values **Clarity. Taste. Strategy.** Clarity establishes relevance and priority, taste creates distinction, and strategy sustains success and consistency over time. She applies them across product development, strategic work, and creative execution. This is the source-backed center of the skill. [S1]

The public material is a set of principles and observations, not a published proprietary manual. Treat the method below as a careful operational synthesis. Labels indicate the strength and origin of guidance:

- **Public principle:** Explicitly stated or closely paraphrased from Fehr's own public material.
- **Profile account:** Described by Hospis, a third party, rather than asserted directly by Fehr.
- **Package adaptation:** A practical instruction, deliverable field, or decision rule constructed for agent use. Citations identify its inspiration, not proof that Fehr uses that exact tool.

Keep these distinctions when explaining the skill to a user. The one-sentence format and three diagnostics come from the published meeting slides; evidence tables, option comparisons, action ownership, and review thresholds are package adaptations. Do not claim access to her private consulting practice. [S1][S2][S3]

## Intake and evidence

**Package adaptation:** Begin with the specific decision. Examples include choosing a hospitality position, repairing a creative brief, deciding which audience a channel serves, or assessing whether an offer supports a proposed price. Gather the current offer, customer evidence, existing communications, operational constraints, and who owns the decision. Keep the requested scope: a visual review need not become an unsolicited rebrand.

Record each important claim as an observation, interpretation, or hypothesis. A founder's ambition belongs under hypotheses until the offer can substantiate it. A competitor's visual style is evidence of a category code, not evidence of what customers need. Customer reviews can suggest a strength, but their representativeness remains a separate question. This evidence discipline operationalizes clarity without pretending that the sources prescribe a research protocol. [S1][S3]

When evidence is incomplete, make a provisional recommendation with the missing test named. Do not generate fictional interviews, conversion rates, customer segments, or employee responses. Avoid a long intake questionnaire when only one missing fact would change the decision.

## Three meeting questions

**Public principle:** The supplied recurring installment contains three questions in attached images. The formulations here are paraphrases, in image order. Its caption indicates another installment; it does not establish these as an unchanging canonical trio across all posts. [S2]

### 1. Who has the mandate to decide today?

The first slide examines whether anyone present is authorized to make the decision. Fehr identifies an absent decision mandate as a reason strategy conversations fail to produce movement. Clarifying the authority changes how participants engage. [S2]

**Package adaptation:** Write the decision, named approver, remit, and today's possible status: approved, recommended pending approval, or exploratory. A senior attendee is not automatically the authorized approver. If nobody has the mandate, continue gathering options and draft the recommendation; route the unresolved choice to the right person. Do not label silence or consensus as authorization.

### 2. Can a newcomer summarize the position in one sentence?

The second slide asks whether a new employee could explain what the organization stands for in one sentence. Fehr frames failure as a possible positioning defect rather than merely a communication problem. [S2]

**Package adaptation:** Draft a sentence in ordinary language and test whether it communicates a recognizable audience, useful promise, and credible distinction. If an actual newcomer is available, ask for a restatement without showing a slide deck. If no test was conducted, say so. A new hire unfamiliar with basic context may also need onboarding; diagnose that possibility before treating one failed restatement as decisive evidence. Rewriting a slogan alone does not repair an unresolved position.

### 3. Does this decision serve existing customers or desired future customers?

The third slide separates the customers the organization already has from those it wants. Fehr observes that these may be different people, so choosing which to optimize for changes strategic choices. [S2]

**Package adaptation:** Describe each group through needs, behavior, buying context, and evidence. Name the chosen group for this decision. If growth depends on reaching a different group, specify the product, channel, price, and service changes needed, plus what happens to current customers. Do not turn an aspiration for affluent customers into an assumption that a more expensive visual identity will attract them.

**Combined output:** Record three answers and their consequences. An absent mandate changes decision status; an unclear sentence changes the positioning work; an audience mismatch changes the offer and transition plan. Asking questions without acting on their answers fails the purpose of the diagnostic. [S2]

## Core before expression

**Profile account:** Hospis describes a sequence of uncovering the brand core with the owner family, deriving positioning from that core, and carrying it into product and creative delivery. It emphasizes recognizing what already exists in lived practice, including elements that have not yet been articulated. [S3]

**Package adaptation:** Look for repeatable evidence: what guests return for, which customer problem the team solves well, what owners protect under pressure, and which behaviors survive across shifts. Convert the evidence into a small set of core commitments. For each commitment, record a lived example, relevance to the selected audience, and a limit or contradiction.

Do not automatically preserve every inherited practice. A family ritual may be valuable, irrelevant, or actively frustrating to the chosen guest. Retain what supports the promise. If the desired position exceeds present delivery, separate the authentic current core from the future proposition and name the investment gap. The sources support clarity and execution, not an obligation to freeze a brand in its past. [S1][S3]

## Position and exclusions

**Public principle:** Fehr's Happier Grocery analysis connects trust and curation to a space that expresses its intended audience. She highlights a clear sense of who belongs to that audience and who does not. Her meeting question similarly demands a choice between existing and desired customers. [S4][S2]

**Package adaptation:** Use this sentence structure when helpful: For [specific audience and situation], [brand] offers [meaningful benefit], supported by [credible distinctive behavior or offer]. It is a drafting aid, not Fehr's published formula. Then specify the tradeoff: an audience not prioritized, a service not offered, or a category convention declined.

Test the distinction by substituting a competitor's name. If the sentence still fits everyone, locate a stronger proof or narrow the audience and context. Avoid strings of virtues such as premium, authentic, sustainable, and innovative without evidence. A position should explain what someone can expect and guide decisions about the offer.

The Hospis profile extends positioning into reasons for price, employer identity, website purpose, channels, and spatial behavior. Use these applications when relevant, while preserving their third-party attribution. [S3]

## Taste as selection

**Public principle:** Fehr connects taste with differentiation. Her Zara Home commentary treats photography, art direction, and partner selection as sustained creative choices, while keeping her reservations about the wider business visible. [S1][S7]

**Package adaptation:** Define taste through selection criteria rather than the agent's preference. For every creative recommendation, identify the position it expresses, the audience response it aims to support, the concrete execution choice, and the risk of category sameness. An image instruction such as showing a host explaining an ingredient is more actionable than asking for elevated authenticity.

Compare two plausible creative directions. Explain why one makes the chosen promise easier to recognize and experience. Useful criteria include specificity to the organization, coherence across touchpoints, meaningful audience fit, and repeatability with available resources. These criteria are interpretive tools, not a source-backed scoring system.

Do not copy the visual codes of a brand Fehr discusses and call that taste. Happier Grocery is an observed example of audience alignment, not a universal recommendation for wellness styling. Grand hotels illustrate identity certainty, not a directive to adopt heritage interiors. [S4][S6]

## Strategy as continuity

**Public principle:** Fehr defines strategy through sustained success and consistency. Her grand-hotel commentary locates value in a confident identity rather than generic spaces optimized primarily for images. Her quiet-hospitality commentary treats reduced stimulation as a substantive part of an offer. [S1][S6][S5]

**Profile account:** Hospis describes brand realization in the offer and experience, including leadership clarity, space, employer proposition, and channel purpose. This supports a broad execution lens beyond a logo refresh. [S3]

**Package adaptation:** Translate the promise into observable requirements across relevant touchpoints. For a quiet retreat, consider room acoustics, arrival rhythm, scheduling, staff behavior, and booking expectations. For a curated shop, consider selection standards, staff explanations, pricing transparency, and range maintenance. Match the complexity to the assignment.

For each essential behavior, record an owner, operating rule, evidence of readiness, and a review measure. Ask whether it still happens when the founder is away. A founder's taste can originate a brand, but repeatable delivery needs a team that understands the choices. This absence test is an adaptation inspired by consistency and leadership, not a named Fehr exercise. [S1][S3]

Do not apply hushpitality indiscriminately. A lively restaurant may need social energy; a quiet promise should follow actual audience needs. Treat subtracting noise or options as one strategic possibility, not an aesthetic default. [S5]

## Decision rules and edge cases

The following rules are **package adaptations** grounded in the public principles:

| Situation | Decision rule | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attractive work, unclear promise | Resolve the position before finalizing creative execution; keep exploratory work provisional. | [S1][S2][S3] |
| No authorized decision maker | Deliver a recommendation with an explicit approval dependency. | [S2] |
| Current and desired audiences differ | Choose which guides this decision and describe a credible transition. | [S2] |
| Generic but polished concept | Replace borrowed cues with specific offer or behavior evidence. | [S4][S6] |
| Price rises without meaningful change | Identify the benefit and delivery improvement that could support the price; do not claim willingness to pay without evidence. | [S3][S4] |
| A compelling creative reference has troubling practices | Separate the useful creative observation from any endorsement; assess implications when material to this brand's promise. | [S7][S4] |
| A channel looks good but its purpose is unclear | Name the desired action and connect expression to that action; aesthetics alone do not establish effectiveness. | [S3] |
| Stakeholders disagree about taste | Return to audience, promise, and explicit criteria; preserve viable alternatives and name the unresolved tradeoff. | [S1][S2] |
| A new brand lacks a lived history | Use founder commitments and prototype behavior as hypotheses, then validate the promise in delivery. | [S1][S3] |

For a multi-audience organization, choose the audience relevant to the decision or specify a hierarchy. Do not require one customer type for every business line. For a short deadline, prioritize authority, position, audience, and the largest delivery gap. For a constrained creative assignment, identify strategic dependencies without expanding the work uninvited.

## Warnings and attribution limits

Fehr's meeting slides warn about mandate gaps, unresolved positioning hidden as a communication issue, and optimizing for an unchosen customer. Her hospitality commentary questions generic, image-oriented experiences; her curation analysis raises the tension between meaningful improvement and status signaling. Her taste commentary acknowledges that creative quality and concerns about company behavior can coexist. [S2][S6][S4][S7]

This package additionally warns against unsupported promises, invented research, mandatory moodboard aesthetics, and treating a brief as proof of results. These are authoring safeguards, not quotations or claimed Fehr doctrines.

Hotel König von Ungarn credits show Fehr's project role alongside other contributors. The Elizabeth Arden showcase documents a team campaign with Fehr credited as account director. Neither source demonstrates her private process or attributes sole creative authorship to her. Use them to understand practice context and collaborative credit, not as evidence that this operational workflow produced those projects. [S8][S9]

## Handoff

Use [the output template](../templates/output-template.md) to capture a recommendation, the three diagnostics, positioning, creative choices, and delivery actions. Review it with [the checklist](../checklists/review-checklist.md). Success means a reader can identify the choice, its rationale, its exclusions, and the next decision or action. It does not mean a promised revenue increase or that every stakeholder agrees.
