# Application rubric (YC, a16z Speedrun)

Every rule carries its public source. Score each answer 0-10. The weakest critical rule caps the score; it is not an average. Quote the exact words that cost points.

## A. Clarity
- **A1 Plain English, no jargon.** "Plain English is best because Y Combinator uses plain English to figure out whether or not you know your stuff." (Garry Tan, "Garry Tan's tips for applying to YC", YC Library). Flag buzzwords and insider terms: platform, infrastructure layer, leverage, synergy, hyper-, unbundling, AI-powered, next-generation, seamless, revolutionary.
- **A2 Friend test.** "Does the writing sound like something you would say to a friend? If not, rewrite." (Tan). Not corporate, not a press release.
- **A3 Bottom line first.** Conclusion first, proof right after (Tan). "Tell me what's important in the first three sentences, because if that's not good, we're out." (Fareed Mosavat, a16z Speedrun).
- **A4 Simple statements that add up.** "Very simple, understandable statements put side by side ... when you zoom out, you realize that is a really big idea." (Tan)
- **A5 Brevity.** "Don't write a novel. More words is not better." (Dalton Caldwell, "How to Apply and Succeed at YC", 2024). "Edit mercilessly until there is not a single word you could remove." (Tan, 2014). "Brevity is clarity." (a16z Speedrun)

## B. Substance
- **B1 Show, don't tell.** What is built, who uses it, who pays (Tan). Best news front and centre, don't bury the lead (Sam Shank, a16z Speedrun).
- **B2 The reader's questions.** After reading, the reviewer knows: what it is, whether it is built, whether there are users, and what evidence says people want it (Caldwell).
- **B3 Path to big.** "In the next five to ten years, is there some real way for you to get to a hundred million dollars a year in revenue?" (Tan). For a moonshot: "In a world where X happens, we're gonna need Y." (a16z Speedrun)
- **B4 Earned insight.** "What's the secret you've learned?" Non-obvious, not a tar pit (Fareed Mosavat). State the difference: "we're different from XYZ because of this" (Kenan Saleh, a16z Speedrun).
- **B5 Why this team.** Signals that the founders are exceptional and close to the problem (a16z Speedrun partners).
- **B6 Honesty (critical).** Nothing reads as live when it isn't. No inflated or undefined metrics. Misrepresentation is disqualifying (Caldwell). Things not built yet are in future tense ("we will", "next"). Every number must be one the founder can defend in the interview, with its definition.

## C. The one-line description (the 50-character field)
- **C1 Name and what it does, no problem setup.** "Make it sound dumber than you think it should." "You don't need to sound cool. You need to be clear." (Michael Seibel, "How to Pitch Your Company", YC Library)
- **C2 Walk the user path if it is hard to say.** "We build a website with a box in it. You can type any question into that box..." beats "We organize the world's information." (Seibel)
- **C3 Email test.** A smart friend can explain it back without questions. No acronyms, no "platform". (Seibel)
- **C4 Concrete nouns.** What you make, the problem, the customer. If 10 teams built from the line, they would build the same thing (Kevin Hale, "How to Pitch Your Startup", YC Startup School).
- **C5 What, not why or how.** No padding to look bigger. Multi-stage companies describe stage one (Hale).
- **C6 "X for Y"** only if X is a household name, Y clearly wants X, and Y is a large market (Hale).
- **C7 Length.** YC's field is 50 characters. Count them. About 10 words for Speedrun's one-liner.
Reference lines (Hale): Airbnb "the first online marketplace that lets travelers book rooms with locals, instead of hotels"; Dropbox "Synchronizes files across your or your team's computers".

## D. Speedrun bad vs great (Troy Kirwin, a16z Speedrun, with Philipp Wehn, public LinkedIn teardown of a rejected and an accepted application, 2026-10)
- **D1 One-liner:** what, for whom, which domain, about 10 words. Rejected: "a pioneering process automation platform that applies intelligent reasoning..." ("I have no idea what that means"). Accepted: "We build industrial agents for operations in Heavy Industries."
- **D2 Traction specific enough to verify:** named buyers, amounts, a defined metric, or one concrete before/after case. "This level of specificity gives confidence that there's meat on the bone."
- **D3 Founder-market fit as first-hand insight,** not awards or résumé lines.
- **D4 Play to win:** no volunteered weakness the question did not ask for, no hedges written to please investors.
- **D5 Progress velocity** for reapplicants: what shipped, customers closed, hires made since the last application (Jonathan Lai, a16z Speedrun).

## E. The 20-second rules (feedback from experienced YC founders reviewing applications)
- **E1 About 20 seconds per answer,** roughly 60-80 words. Strongest fact first. The first answer gets the most attention.
- **E2 Real active metrics lead:** daily and weekly actives, retention, revenue. Cumulative signups only as support. "100 DAU" beats "3,000 signups". Define each metric.
- **E3 Two-sentence pitch:** sentence 1 is what you make and for whom; sentence 2 is why it matters, shown with traction.
- **E4 Honest learnings over a pitch.** Pivots are fine. Show what you tried, what you learned, how fast you moved. Execution over vision.
