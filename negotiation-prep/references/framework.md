# Negotiation Analysis Framework

## BATNA/ZOPA Structure

### BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement)
For each party, identify:
- Walk-away option: what happens if there is no deal?
- Walk-away cost: financial, relational, time, legal
- Walk-away probability: how likely is each side to actually walk?

Your BATNA is for internal use only. It never appears in a message.

### ZOPA (Zone of Possible Agreement)
- Reservation price: each side's worst acceptable deal
- Aspiration price: each side's ideal deal
- ZOPA = the overlap between the reservation prices
- If there is no overlap, look for trades that create value: issues the two sides value differently (timing, volume, scope, payment terms, risk allocation)

Mark every estimate of the other side's numbers as an estimate, with the evidence behind it.

## Message Scoring Rubric

Score each draft 1-10 on these dimensions.

### 1. Information Leakage (critical)
- Does it reveal your BATNA or walk-away point?
- Does it reveal details the other side can use against you (your budget, your margin, why a particular structure benefits you)?
- Does it reveal timeline pressure?
- Does it show which points you care about most vs. least?
- RED FLAG: sentences like "this saves me...", "for me this means...", "I can afford..."

### 2. Anchoring
- Does it set or reinforce your anchor, or accept theirs?
- Does framing a concession ("I'm coming down from X") confirm that X was the baseline?
- Does the number read as a starting position or a final offer?
- Moving from YOUR anchor = strong. Moving toward THEIR anchor = weak.

### 3. Tone Calibration
- Matches the channel (chat = concise, email = structured, call = flexible)
- Firm without being aggressive
- Acknowledges their concerns without validating weak arguments
- No emotional loading that invites an emotional reply
- No accusations, sarcasm or passive aggression

### 4. Logical Consistency
- No internal contradictions
- Nothing that undermines a position taken elsewhere in the same negotiation
- Every argument supports the overall position
- Example trap: stressing how far you have already come down from an early number while also arguing that a different, lower figure was the agreed baseline

### 5. Leverage Preservation
- Does not reveal cards unnecessarily
- Keeps future concessions available
- Keeps optionality on issues not yet discussed
- What is not said matters as much as what is said

### 6. Trap Detection
Common traps to flag:
- "Protection until [event]" where [event] is exactly when protection matters
- "Standard" or "market practice" claims without evidence
- Sweeteners that distract from the real issue (a bigger headline number with weaker terms underneath)
- Time pressure ("we need to close this today")
- Fait accompli ("legal has already approved this version")
- Emotional reframing ("this is about the partnership, not about us")
- Scope creep (new demands added while old ones are discussed)
- False equivalence ("you get X, so Y is fair" when X and Y are unrelated)

## Concession Ledger Template

For each round, record:
```
Round N: [date, channel]
GAVE: [what you conceded]
GOT: [what you received]
NET: [your assessment of the trade]
LEVERAGE CHANGE: [stronger / weaker / neutral]
RED LINES HIT: [any non-negotiables threatened]
```

Running totals:
- Total given vs. total received
- Pattern: are you giving more per round? (concession fatigue)
- Are red lines holding or eroding?

## Counterparty Analysis Template

### Position vs. Interest
- POSITION: what they say they want
- INTEREST: why they want it (the underlying need)
- When positions conflict but interests align, look for a creative structure

### Contradiction Detection
- Compare statements across rounds
- Compare stated principles with actual behavior
- Compare what is said in different channels
- Time-based shifts: what changed between rounds?

### Power Assessment
- Who needs this deal more?
- Who has more alternatives?
- Who has more information?
- Who controls the timeline?
- Who controls the process (meetings, drafts, approvals)?

## Pre-Send Checklist

Before any message goes out:

1. INFORMATION: Am I revealing anything the other side can use against me?
2. ANCHORING: Am I reinforcing my frame or theirs?
3. ROOM: Do I have space to move if they push back?
4. TONE: Would I be comfortable if a neutral third party read this aloud?
5. OMISSIONS: Is anything conspicuously absent that I want to stay absent?
6. CONTRADICTIONS: Does anything here contradict my position in other channels?
7. INVITATION: Am I inviting a response I don't want?
8. TIMING: Is now the right moment? (my emotional state, their deadline, information still pending)
9. FACTS: Is every number, date and claim in this message verified? If not, remove it or mark it as an assumption.
