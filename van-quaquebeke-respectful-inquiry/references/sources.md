# Sources

All seven URLs below were fetched successfully with curl on 2026-10-02. Only public page text, public podcast notes, an interview transcript, a report, an author's post, and university-hosted abstracts were used. No paywalled article, book text, linked book preview, or attached magazine interview was used. Source numbers apply throughout this package.

- **[S1] Respectful Inquiry: A Motivational Account of Leading Through Asking Questions and Listening**, Niels Van Quaquebeke and Will Felps, 2018, public abstract on Van Quaquebeke's KLU faculty page. URL: https://www.klu.org/faculty-research/faculty/resident-faculty/niels-van-quaquebeke . Contribution: Defines the combined behavior, explains the three motivational needs, and identifies contextual barriers and interaction history as moderators.
- **[S2] Leaders and Asking Questions: The Surprising Discovery of What Is Needed to Find Solutions and Build Relationships with Niels Van Quaquebeke**, listening ALCHEMY, public podcast notes. URL: https://listeningalchemy.com/listen-in/leaders-and-asking-questions-the-surprising-discovery-of-what-is-needed-to-find-solutions-and-build-relationships-with-niels-van-quaquebeke/ . Contribution: Provides Van Quaquebeke's account of intention, listening as space for self-exploration, premature fixing, and balancing inquiry with a leader's position.
- **[S3] Why leaders need to ask more questions, respectfully**, UNSW BusinessThink, 15 March 2017, interview with method coauthor Will Felps. URL: https://www.businessthink.unsw.edu.au/articles/Why-leaders-need-to-ask-more-questions%2C-respectfully- . Contribution: Explains perspective-taking barriers, stress and the urge for closure, advocacy alongside inquiry, and inviting someone's own assessment to support ownership.
- **[S4] Interview zu Respectful Inquiry**, Niels Van Quaquebeke, public LinkedIn post. URL: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/quaquebeke_interview-zu-respectful-inquiry-activity-7406985651461595137-VY2r . Contribution: Reinforces that questioning needs sustained listening; only the author's visible post, not comments or the attached interview, supports this package.
- **[S5] Sich zu benehmen ist erst mal nett**, Deutschlandfunk Kultur, 18 October 2012, public interview transcript with Niels Van Quaquebeke. URL: https://www.deutschlandfunkkultur.de/sich-zu-benehmen-ist-erst-mal-nett-100.html . Contribution: Distinguishes person-specific respect from courtesy rituals, obedience, and assumptions that others share one's norms.
- **[S6] Respekt kann man nicht verlangen**, Deutschlandfunk, public report containing Niels Van Quaquebeke's statements. URL: https://www.deutschlandfunk.de/respekt-kann-man-nicht-verlangen-100.html . Contribution: Distinguishes equal-worth recognition from performance-based admiration and warns against symbolic consultation and dismissing unresolved disagreement.
- **[S7] Exploring Respectful Inquiry in Leader-Team Interactions: The Differential Effect of Leader Question Asking and Listening on Team Interaction Dynamics in Decision Making**, Hemshorn de Sanchez, Rieg, Lehmann-Willenbrock, and Van Quaquebeke, public abstract on KLU's publications page. URL: https://www.klu.org/faculty-research/faculty/resident-faculty/niels-van-quaquebeke/publications . Contribution: Reports a laboratory experiment with 22 four-person teams in which asking without listening had the lowest decision performance among four conditions.

## Interpretation limits

[S1] is a theoretical account accessible here through its abstract. [S7] is an empirical study accessible here through its abstract; the package does not infer effect sizes or universal field outcomes. [S2] and [S4] are public summaries rather than complete interview transcripts. [S5] and [S6] concern respect more broadly and supply context, not a separate Respectful Inquiry protocol. [S3] represents Felps's explanation as coauthor. Operational instructions and invented dialogue are adaptations identified in the method and examples.
