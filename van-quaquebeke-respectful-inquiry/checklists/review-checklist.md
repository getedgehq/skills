# Respectful Inquiry review checklist

Use this checklist to review a conversation plan, revised script, or actual exchange record. It is a practical package review tool, not a validated instrument. Evaluate behavior and evidence, not how warm the language sounds. The method requires open questions together with attentive listening. [S1]

Mark each applicable item **PASS** or **FAIL**. Mark **N/A** only when the criterion genuinely does not apply, and state why. In an actual record, missing evidence fails the completeness check; do not conclude that a person behaved badly merely because the record is incomplete. Preparation passes when it creates a credible listening opportunity, not when it invents successful answers.

## Pass/fail criteria

| Check | PASS when | FAIL when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mode is honest | Preparation, script, or actual record is clearly identified. | Hypothetical dialogue is presented as consultation that happened. |
| Issue is concrete | The concern and relevant facts are identifiable; assumptions are tentative. | Character judgments substitute for the situation. |
| Boundaries are real | Fixed requirements, open choices, and authority are explicit where relevant. | The person is asked to choose an already settled outcome. |
| Intention permits learning | The plan or exchange allows an unexpected answer to affect understanding. | Questions are designed only to obtain agreement or stage a rebuttal. |
| Opening is open | The respondent can challenge the diagnosis or introduce another explanation. | The preferred cause or remedy is embedded in the invitation. |
| Attention is feasible | A plan allows time to listen, or a live exchange waits and engages with the response. | The agent immediately answers its own question or continues a monologue. |
| Reflection is faithful | Summaries use specific content and allow correction. | The listener invents feelings, exaggerates claims, or ignores correction. |
| Follow-up is responsive | Planned branches are conditional; actual follow-ups use the answer. | A fixed question list overrides what the person says. |
| Recognition is sustained | The person retains standing when challenging or disagreeing. | Respect is made conditional on deference or performance. |
| Motivational design is sound | Real latitude, relevant knowledge, and personal attention are visible. | Claimed autonomy is cosmetic or the person's competence is dismissed. |
| Advocacy is useful | Necessary information and decisions are stated without erasing the contribution. | Repeated questions replace needed guidance, or a pep talk replaces listening. |
| Contribution is traceable | Its effect or treatment is explained, including reasons for declining options. | Input disappears after a ceremonial invitation. |
| Closure is accurate | Proposals, commitments, owners, uncertainty, and dissent are distinguished. | Acceptance or consensus is inferred from silence or invented. |
| Claims are bounded | Outcomes remain tentative; evidence and adaptations are distinguished. | The script guarantees motivation or generalizes a small study to all settings. |

The substantive criteria draw on openness and motivational rationale [S1], intention and attention [S2], ownership and advocacy [S3], sustained listening [S4], person-specific respect [S5], and treatment of contributions [S6]. Asking without listening merits particular scrutiny given the limited laboratory finding. [S7]

## Most common failure modes and fixes

| Failure mode | Typical symptom | Concrete repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advice disguised as a question | An invitation already contains the proposed solution. | Ask first about the person's assessment; add the recommendation after hearing it. |
| Premature diagnosis | The script assumes poor motivation or incompetence. | State the observable issue and ask for the person's account. |
| Asking without listening | The next turn ignores the answer or supplies a predicted one. | Wait in live chat; in a plan, add an explicit listening interval and conditional follow-up. |
| Token question before a speech | One opening is followed by reassurance or a motivational lecture. | Reflect the response and explore its specific meaning before advocating. |
| Overloaded question list | Many prompts leave no room to develop any answer. | Choose one useful opening and retain other prompts as conditional options. |
| Courtesy treated as proof | Friendly phrasing masks dismissal or imposed rituals. | Engage the actual contribution and adapt to the person's stated preferences. |
| False choice | A settled decision is framed as a vote. | Announce the fixed boundary and identify genuine implementation choices. |
| Consultation disappears | No account of what happened to the suggestion. | Record its effect, pending status, or reason for declining it, and give a follow-through point. |
| Endless coaching | The person needs facts but receives only more questions. | Supply the necessary information, then explore the remaining judgment. |
| Manufactured agreement | A leader's decision becomes an alleged joint commitment. | Preserve dissent and identify who actually accepted or decided each action. |
| Unsupported success claim | Agreement or an elegant script is called proof of effectiveness. | Report observable contributions and decisions; keep motivation and results unclaimed. |

## Review outcome

- **Pass:** All applicable criteria pass. Deliver the artifact with its mode clearly identified.
- **Revise:** Repair wording, branches, boundaries, or closure that fail before delivering a preparation artifact.
- **Incomplete evidence:** For a completed exchange, identify the missing answer, correction, or commitment. Do not fabricate a repair retroactively. If appropriate, propose a continuation to obtain it.

Prioritize genuine openness, actual attention, and visible treatment of input. A high question count, polite tone, or agreeable participant cannot compensate for failure on these dimensions. [S1] [S2] [S6]
