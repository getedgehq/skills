# Method reference

Credit: Based on Simon Kaiser's public method and Klein aber's published practice. Independently researched, paraphrased, and adapted into an agent workflow. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Simon Kaiser.

## Evidence and attribution

The central method is to operate a brand channel as a creator: serve an audience with recognizable, recurring content, participate in its culture, and earn time and return visits. The direct OMR interview and Kaiser's own UPLOAD essay carry the strongest personal attribution. Agency pages add team practice; client and conference accounts add context. [S2][S10]

MEEDIA's public introduction confirms the long-form topic but does not expose the five reasons. The five-part rationale below is this package's synthesis, not Kaiser's verified five-item list. Podcast claims here come only from accessible show notes. Never invent a quotation, timestamp, official framework name, or private process. [S1][S3]

The operational choices below, including readiness gates, pilot scope, diagnostics, and measurement definitions, are implementation guidance derived from the public principles. They are labeled adaptation where attribution could otherwise be ambiguous.

## Channel readiness

A channel is an ongoing audience proposition, not simply a place to deposit brand films. Kaiser argues that uploading content and attaching advertising money misses the social and creative behavior that supports organic growth. The agency emphasizes continued improvement and scalable production. [S2][S7]

**Operational adaptation:** Establish an owner for the channel, a feasible production rhythm, host or cast availability, access to analytics, and capacity to respond to viewers. Clarify whether the objective is awareness, consideration, affinity, useful information, or action. Write down the business outcome separately from the channel metrics.

Proceed with a recurring pilot when the audience promise and resources are plausible. If the brand only wants a launch film or a burst of reach, recommend the appropriate limited deliverable rather than promising a long-term community. If there is no analytics history, identify the first batch as baseline collection. There is no sourced universal minimum budget, publishing frequency, or episode count.

## Audience and creator culture

Start inside the audience's viewing world. Observe the creators and conversations viewers already follow, their expectations of tone, and what they value enough to discuss. The point is to contribute to that world, including collaborations and relevant responses to its topics. Community develops around shared interests and values; it cannot be inferred from a demographic label alone. [S2][S10]

**Operational adaptation:** Build a short evidence map: observed viewing interest, supporting material, recurring viewer tension, brand permission to participate, and unresolved hypothesis. Sample a manageable set of relevant channels and comments; document the limits of the sample. Never present comments as representative market research.

A useful channel promise follows this structure: for a specific audience, this recurring show delivers a specific experience through a recognizable perspective, with the brand contributing a credible resource or role. Different audience needs can justify separate accounts, as the podcast show notes suggest. Splitting channels is a choice to evaluate against capacity, not a default rule. [S3]

Creator selection should reflect fit, personality, and audience expectations. The client's launch release stresses compatibility with the brand, including opinionated creators rather than neutral corporate spokespeople. Guest appearances should add narrative or subject value, not just borrowed follower counts. [S8]

## Five-part long-form rationale

These five reasons are an independent, source-backed synthesis. They explain why long-form can outperform short-form for a branded relationship objective, subject to content relevance and audience behavior.

1. **Depth and complete value.** More time can accommodate an explanation, story, or demonstration that would lose its payoff in a short clip. Earn that time through relevance; length alone adds no value. [S1][S10]
2. **More meaningful time with the brand.** Voluntary viewing creates more opportunity for brand understanding than a fleeting exposure. Track consumed minutes and engagement, not merely how many people encountered the content. [S2][S10]
3. **Stronger relationship and participation.** Personal continuity and shared interests can produce familiarity and discussion. The agency's analysis reports relatively greater commenting on longer videos, but does not establish that every long video creates community. [S9][S10]
4. **Serial continuation and return visits.** An episode can lead to another and to a regular viewing habit. Familiar people and ongoing stories make the library part of the experience. Design each episode so newcomers can join. [S2][S6]
5. **Potentially more efficient attention.** The agency reports lower costs per consumed minute for longer content and describes amplifying successful episodes. Evaluate actual production and media cost against earned attention; this is a testable advantage, not a guaranteed saving. [S9][S2]

Shorts remain useful for discovery and frequent contact. The current agency positioning explicitly combines those roles with series for engagement and community. Kaiser's essay also supports a bridge from short discovery to deeper viewing. A bridge must be relevant and measured; it is not proof that Shorts viewers will convert to series viewers. [S4][S10]

Kaiser's 2024 essay reports stronger average performance for videos in a 10 to 30 minute range in its referenced analysis. That observation is not a mandated runtime. Select duration from the experience and validate retention. Do not turn a historical observation or the MEEDIA discussion of very long US videos into a universal length rule. [S10][S1]

## Format and brand role

Think in formats before individual uploads: recurring people, a recognizable promise, and a repeatable source of new episodes. The agency offers fiction, challenges, tutorials, and reporting; the method does not require every brand to imitate the WG sitcom. [S3][S4]

In the Kleinanzeigen case, shared-flat situations create plausible occasions for buying and selling used goods. Later stories address the tension between sustainable consumption and limited budgets. The product's relevance is carried by decisions and consequences, allowing the audience to enjoy the show. [S2][S6]

**Operational adaptation:** Specify the episode engine as a repeatable question or conflict, for example whether a room can be transformed within a fixed budget. Define the cast's roles, stakes, tone, setting, and what changes each episode. Identify where the brand helps the story progress. Remove promotional speeches that interrupt the viewer's chosen experience.

A subtle role should still be intelligible. If the show could belong to any unrelated advertiser, make the brand's contribution more specific. If removal of the product destroys a lecture but leaves the story unchanged, rebuild the integration around an audience problem. Disclose commercial relationships appropriately; natural integration does not require concealing sponsorship. This last rule is package guidance, not an attributed Kaiser statement.

Tutorials can satisfy a clear search need without creating repeat viewing. If the objective is community, add continuity and a reason to watch another related episode. Do not dismiss useful help content when purchase guidance is the actual objective. The agency explicitly provides such formats. [S4]

## Episode and packaging

Kaiser's interview emphasizes early attention, newcomer accessibility, thumbnail click-through, and analysis of abandonment. A serial show must offer an immediate entry point even when the cast has a history. [S2]

**Operational adaptation:** Write the audience promise before the script. Align title, thumbnail, and opening around the same situation or question. Open on the stakes or value, introduce only the context needed, develop the action, and deliver the promised result. Offer a relevant next episode with enough context to make it attractive.

A continuing storyline may encourage return visits, but withholding the whole promised payoff undermines the current episode. Make the immediate arc satisfying and leave a fresh question for the next. Remove generic company greetings, logo sequences, and recaps that consume time without advancing the promise. Kaiser's essay warns against wasting audience time with unnecessary introductory material. [S10]

## Distribution

Use organic performance as evidence of audience fit. Paid distribution can accelerate discovery of that experience rather than compensate for an unwatchable show. The 2020 interview describes using whole episodes as advertising and gaining further episode viewing. The later agency case combines creative adaptation, VOD, Shorts, and paid media. Both belong in the method. [S2][S5]

**Operational adaptation:** Assign separate jobs to long-form episodes, Shorts, collaborations, and paid activation. For each, record the audience, content promise, destination, cost, and metric. Judge the quality of acquired viewing as well as reach. A successful paid introduction should lead to a relevant series experience; an isolated view may still serve awareness but is not evidence of fandom.

Do not reproduce old campaign types or targeting configurations as current platform instructions. Recheck current tools when implementation is requested. The case's 35% and 75% media figures have unclear denominators and sum above 100%; they are not a usable budget allocation. [S5]

## Measurement and iteration

Kaiser prioritizes Time with Brand over surface-level reporting. The agency's Lean Video Produktion and Learn- & Grow-Kreislauf connect scalable work with repeated measurement, analysis, optimization, application, and fresh learning. Proprietary tools are part of the agency's operations, not prerequisites for this package. [S2][S7]

**Operational adaptation:** Define Time with Brand as minutes watched within an explicit channel or episode scope and reporting window. It is a proxy for exposure to branded content, not a measure of how long a viewer consciously thought about the product. Where the business question requires it, pair viewing data with properly designed brand or action measurement.

Use this measurement hierarchy:

| Question | Evidence to request | Interpretation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did people choose the video? | Impressions, click-through, traffic source | Compare similar surfaces and audiences. |
| Did it hold attention? | Average duration, retention curve, watched minutes | Completion percentages alone disadvantage longer videos. |
| Did viewers return or continue? | Returning viewers, available next-video or playlist evidence | Do not invent unavailable cross-session attribution. |
| Was the audience relevant? | Available audience data and qualified feedback | Demographics alone do not prove interest or intent. |
| Did the brand benefit? | Brand research or attributable action data | Watch time alone does not establish incremental sales. |
| Was attention economical? | Defined production and media cost per watched minute | Keep cost scope and organic/paid separation consistent. |

For cost per consumed minute, divide the stated attributable cost by valid watched minutes for the same scope and period. Label whether cost includes media only or production plus media. Avoid counting the same viewing twice across exports.

Review each production batch. Weak click-through with healthy retention suggests packaging to investigate. Strong clicks followed by early exits suggest an opening or promise mismatch. Strong single-video viewing with weak return behavior suggests the format lacks a continuing reason to watch. These are diagnostic hypotheses, not guaranteed causes. Compare within relevant audience and traffic cohorts before acting.

Agree pilot goals and review timing with the brief. Change one major hypothesis at a time where feasible, then document the observed result. Stop or resize the pilot when the team cannot sustain it or repeated evidence fails the agreed outcome. No public source supplies universal pass thresholds.

## Kleinanzeigen case: chronology and limits

- **Launch, 2017:** The client describes original entertainment and advice formats with creators. It credits TLGG for conception alongside United Creators and Klein aber. Do not attribute the entire launch exclusively to Kaiser. [S8]
- **Reported in 2020:** OMR describes the WG's creator-led serial approach and reports 365,000 subscribers and 122 million total views, 61% organic. These are historical reported figures, not current channel measurements. [S2]
- **2022 case:** The agency reports 292 million watched minutes, approximately 75% organic, and around 800,000 returning monthly viewers. It links affordability and sustainability to new characters and continuing stories. Its commissioned research is cited by the page but was not independently accessed. [S6]
- **2025 case page, season beginning August 2023:** The agency reports more than 294,000 added subscribers, 122% more target-audience views, 31% higher retention, and 14% lower CPM after creative and media changes. The full measurement interval and comparison bases are not specified. These results describe an integrated approach, not a controlled long-form-versus-Shorts experiment. [S5]

Transfer the mechanism: an audience tension, a relevant brand role, consistent people, satisfying episodes, and iterative learning. Do not transfer claimed reach, dates, audience percentages, or economics as expected results for another brand.

## Warnings and edge cases

Avoid campaign thinking masquerading as a channel, vanity metrics as the only success criteria, excessive brand polish that overrides audience fit, and buying distribution without a worthwhile viewing experience. Each conflicts with the public emphasis on creator behavior, meaningful time, or appropriate production quality. [S2][S4][S10]

For a small budget, reduce cast, location, or format complexity before sacrificing a feasible learning cycle. This is an adaptation of scalable production, not a requirement to use a studio or weekly release. [S7]

For technical or sensitive subjects, use a qualified host and accurate claims within the brand's established review process. Interesting explanations can support depth, but entertainment is not permission to fabricate facts. For B2B, identify a professional audience interest and recurring problem; the method does not establish that every niche needs a sitcom.

For an existing fandom, preserve the audience promise when adding characters, Shorts, or paid activation. The case explicitly describes refreshing relevance without disrupting loyal fans. For a newcomer, start with accessible stakes rather than years of lore. [S5][S6][S2]

For immediate revenue needs, state that this method does not supply guaranteed conversion forecasts. For Shorts-only briefs, use the audience and format principles where useful but do not replace the user's assignment with a long-form channel. Recommend long-form only when its additional depth or relationship serves the stated goal.
