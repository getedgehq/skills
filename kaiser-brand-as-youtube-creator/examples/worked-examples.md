# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations. Brand names, briefs, schedules, budgets, and targets are fictional. They demonstrate the package's operational adaptation, not work by Simon Kaiser or Klein aber. There are no actual performance results.

## Illustration 1: A secondhand marketplace becomes a recurring creator

### Invented brief

ReNest wants young adults furnishing their first homes to consider secondhand purchases. It can fund a six-episode pilot with two recurring creators, one location, and an editor. Its challenge is to make reuse feel achievable on a limited budget. Existing viewing and brand research have not been supplied.

### Weak first draft

Publish a 30-minute film explaining ReNest's categories and sustainability mission. Start with a brand animation, a founder introduction, and a tour of the app. Invite a celebrity to read a promotional script. Cut random moments into daily Shorts, buy views for the full film, and declare success after reaching 100,000 views.

### Improved result

**Channel promise:** ReNest Room Rescue follows two resourceful roommates turning awkward rooms into useful spaces with secondhand finds and clear budget constraints.

**Repeatable engine:** Each episode starts with a real room problem and a fixed fictional budget. The hosts find items, negotiate practical tradeoffs, collect them, repair or adapt them, and reveal the result. Their different tastes create recurring tension. ReNest enables sourcing, while entertainment comes from decisions and setbacks.

**Pilot episode:** Title: Can We Make This Tiny Room Work for EUR 180? Thumbnail: the cramped before state, a conspicuous rescued desk, and the budget. Opening: one host cannot open the door because the bed blocks it; the other announces the budget and deadline. The episode proceeds through layout choices, sourcing, a failed pickup, an improvised fix, and a reveal with total spend. Initial runtime hypothesis: 12 to 16 minutes, subject to editing and retention evidence.

**Newcomer access and continuation:** Briefly establish who the roommates are through their disagreement. Resolve the room problem in this episode. Offer the related next installment about making storage from the leftover materials, rather than ending before the reveal.

**Community and discovery:** Ask viewers which remaining room problem should come next, with a producer reviewing comments twice weekly. Invite a compatible repair creator because the task needs their expertise. A Short resolves one small practical question and points interested viewers to the room episode. Treat that transition as a hypothesis to measure.

**Pilot decision:** Publish six episodes over eight weeks if production allows. At the midpoint, inspect clicks, early exits, watched minutes, and audience responses. After the final release window, review return behavior and costs. Set numerical gates from the available baseline before paid expansion. A low-click, well-retained episode gets packaging work; an episode that loses viewers during the app tour gets that tour removed or rebuilt around the action.

### Rules that made the difference

The improvement replaces product exposition with an audience tension and a repeatable format. It gives the brand a role in the action and preserves newcomer access. It treats runtime as earned time, measures Time with Brand and returning viewers, and gives paid and Shorts distribution defined jobs. See method sections on format, packaging, distribution, and measurement. The underlying principles are supported by [S2], [S4], [S6], and [S10] in references/sources.md.

## Illustration 2: A specialist B2B tool builds a useful series

### Invented brief

FlowDesk sells scheduling software for independent repair workshops. The audience is workshop owners who struggle with urgent jobs disrupting booked work. One credible host and a real workshop are available monthly. The business wants consideration among qualified owners, not broad entertainment reach.

### Weak first draft

Upload every webinar, each 55 minutes long, to one channel. Mix investor news, recruitment films, customer tutorials, and comedy clips. Open every video with a five-minute company overview. Optimize exclusively for subscribers and promise the channel will reproduce Kleinanzeigen's growth.

### Improved result

**Channel promise:** The Friday Workshop Reset shows one workshop owner solving a familiar operating problem, with actual tradeoffs visible rather than a slideshow of software features.

**Recurring format:** A qualified operator hosts a monthly field episode. Each visit follows one problem, an attempted solution, the tradeoff, and the outcome. Shorter task-specific help videos remain available for people needing immediate instructions. Separate recruitment content when it serves a substantially different audience and the team can maintain that destination.

**Pilot episode:** Title: The Emergency Job That Broke Our Friday Schedule. Thumbnail: a full job board beside the unexpected repair, with the host's visible dilemma. Opening: two promised pickups and an urgent job collide. The host maps choices, checks capacity, uses the scheduling tool where relevant, and explains the impact on customers and staff. Initial length hypothesis: 14 to 20 minutes. Customer data is fictionalized or used with appropriate permission; no private records appear in this illustration.

**Brand role:** FlowDesk makes a scheduling tradeoff visible. The host can acknowledge a constraint the tool cannot solve. Product relevance is specific, while the complete episode remains useful to a viewer who is evaluating alternatives.

**Return reason:** The next episode investigates repeat jobs that quietly consume margin. Viewers nominate situations from their own workshops. The host answers a manageable selection of substantive questions, creating a source of future episode topics.

**Distribution and pilot:** Run three monthly episodes, a fictional practical choice dictated by capacity. Use relevant workshop communities and one compatible collaborator. Add a Short about a single scheduling decision only if it interests the same audience. Reserve paid amplification for an episode that attracts relevant owners and sustains their viewing; report those viewers separately.

**Measurement and decision:** Track minutes watched, retention by segment, qualified comments, available return behavior, and clearly attributable demo requests. These are different signals, not interchangeable conversions. A small audience with relevant professional questions may outperform mass views for this brief. Agree cost limits and review dates before launch; do not import case growth percentages as targets.

### Rules that made the difference

The revision applies audience specificity and recurring format thinking to a professional niche. It retains Help content where useful without expecting every search visitor to become a fan. It removes irrelevant introductions, distinguishes business results from viewing proxies, and uses a production rhythm the team can sustain. See audience, readiness, episode, and measurement sections in references/method.md; the public foundations are [S3], [S4], [S7], and [S10].

## How to use these illustrations

Borrow the reasoning, not the invented constraints. Neither six episodes nor three monthly releases is a universal pilot requirement. Do not reuse the fictional titles, host setup, or budget as evidence about a real audience. When rewriting a weak draft, state which decision changes, why it changes, and which observation would verify the improvement.
