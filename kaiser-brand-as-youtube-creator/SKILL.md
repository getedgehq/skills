# Brands as YouTube creators

Based on Simon Kaiser's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Simon Kaiser.

## When to use

Use this skill when a brand needs a YouTube channel proposition, a repeatable show, a long-form pilot, or a review of a channel that publishes videos without building a returning audience. It produces a strategy and production brief that connects audience interests, creator behavior, commercial relevance, and measurement. It also helps explain when Shorts complement a longer series and when a channel investment is premature.

Treat the brand as a participant in an existing viewing culture. A successful channel gives people a reason to choose its videos and return for another episode. Long-form is a means to deepen that relationship, not a requirement to stretch every topic. Preserve the user's objective, budget, audience, and brand constraints; mark missing information as an assumption rather than inventing research.

Read the evidence boundaries in [references/method.md](references/method.md) before attributing advice. The MEEDIA article's exact five reasons are unavailable in the public introduction. This package supplies a clearly identified five-part synthesis from other fetched public material. Keep that distinction in any client-facing explanation. Practical pilot gates, diagnostic rules, and template fields are this package's operational adaptation, not a proprietary Kaiser framework.

## Procedure

1. **Frame the assignment.** Use the brief fields in [templates/output-template.md](templates/output-template.md). Record the business outcome, intended audience, existing channel, production capacity, and requested deliverable. Identify unknowns and the evidence needed to resolve them. Start with a recommendation that can be assessed against these constraints.

2. **Check channel readiness.** Apply the readiness rules in [references/method.md](references/method.md#channel-readiness). Determine whether the organization can support recurring production, a recognizable host or cast, audience responses, and analytics review. If resources only support a single campaign, recommend a bounded experiment and state its limits.

3. **Investigate the audience's viewing world.** Follow [references/method.md](references/method.md#audience-and-creator-culture). Identify relevant creators, recurring topics, audience language, interests, and unmet promises. Use supplied research or accessible public material. Separate observations from hypotheses, and explain how the brand can contribute credibly.

4. **Choose the role of long-form.** Use [references/method.md](references/method.md#five-part-long-form-rationale). Select the applicable mechanisms: depth, meaningful time, relationship, serial continuation, or efficient attention. Explain what additional runtime earns for this audience. Retain Shorts for a defined supporting purpose rather than assuming long-form wins every objective.

5. **Write the channel promise.** Complete the audience and proposition fields in [templates/output-template.md](templates/output-template.md). Describe whom the channel serves, what viewers receive, why this brand belongs, and why they would return. Choose one coherent audience promise before adding unrelated content categories.

6. **Design a repeatable format.** Use [references/method.md](references/method.md#format-and-brand-role) and the contrasts in [examples/worked-examples.md](examples/worked-examples.md). Specify host continuity, episode engine, viewer stakes, recurring structure, and natural product relevance. Choose fiction, challenges, tutorials, or reporting according to audience demand and feasible production.

7. **Make an episode accessible and compelling.** Apply [references/method.md](references/method.md#episode-and-packaging). Draft a title, thumbnail concept, opening, episode beats, payoff, and next-view invitation. A newcomer must understand the immediate situation without watching the archive. Remove introductions that delay the promised value.

8. **Build the community loop.** Follow [references/method.md](references/method.md#audience-and-creator-culture). Plan relevant collaborations, responses to comments, and a way for audience questions to influence future episodes. Assign an owner and realistic response capacity. Preserve the show's identity while incorporating useful feedback.

9. **Connect organic, paid, and Shorts.** Use [references/method.md](references/method.md#distribution). Explain each component's job and its audience match. Specify what organic evidence would justify amplification, where new viewers should go next, and how paid performance will be reported separately. Do not promise that short-video reach automatically creates long-form fans.

10. **Define the pilot and learning cycle.** Complete measurement and production fields in [templates/output-template.md](templates/output-template.md), using [references/method.md](references/method.md#measurement-and-iteration). Set a feasible batch, review date, agreed targets, and change or stop conditions. Diagnose packaging, retention, and return behavior separately before committing additional budget.

11. **Review and deliver.** Run [checklists/review-checklist.md](checklists/review-checklist.md). Resolve failed checks, qualify case statistics, and link source-backed claims through [references/sources.md](references/sources.md). Deliver the filled strategy, example episode, pilot decisions, and remaining assumptions in the user's requested format.
