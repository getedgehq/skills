# Worked examples

These are invented illustrations of the package's adaptation of Brendan Kane's public method. Brands, briefs, scripts, and candidate hypotheses are fictional. Product capabilities described below are assumed within each illustration and would require confirmation before real use. No performance result has been observed. Source identifiers refer to [the source register](../references/sources.md).

## Illustration 1: A bicycle repair service's short video

### Brief

Audience: City commuters who hear bicycle brake rubbing but do not know what to inspect.
Channel: A short vertical social video, planned for approximately 25 seconds.
Value: Help viewers identify the source of a familiar noise without making a repair claim.
Action: Visit a page describing what a workshop brake inspection includes.
Evidence assumed for this illustration: Footage of a workshop demonstration bicycle showing a brake pad touching the rim. No claim that all noises have this cause.

### Weak first draft

Opening: "Welcome to CityCycle. We have expert mechanics and offer comprehensive bicycle servicing."
Story: List repair services and years of experience.
CTA: "Book now for the best service."

Why it is weak: The opening describes the seller, postpones the audience's recognizable problem, and provides no immediate reason to watch. The story does not resolve a particular question. The superiority claim lacks support.

### Candidate directions

| Candidate | Opening execution | Hypothesis | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Close-up of spinning wheel with audible scraping; "Hear that scrape? Watch this pad." | A recognizable sound and visible cause earn attention | Audio-off viewers need visible text and a clear close-up |
| B | Wheel visibly slows; "This brake pad is touching the rim." | A direct demonstration creates immediate comprehension | May be less curious than the question-based opening |
| C | Show the contact point; "One wheel, one noisy contact point." | Specific visible context distinguishes the opening from service ads | The line may be too abstract without the image |

### Improved result: Candidate A

- **0 to 3 seconds:** Show the spinning wheel and brake pad touching the rim. Spoken line: "Hear that scrape? Watch this pad." On-screen text: "Brake rubbing?" No logo pre-roll.
- **3 to 8 seconds:** Point to the contact point. "On this bike, the pad touches the rim even when the lever is released."
- **8 to 16 seconds:** Show the mechanism close-up. "The noise tells us where to inspect. It doesn't tell us everything about the brake's condition."
- **16 to 21 seconds:** Show a mechanic inspecting the assembly. "An inspection checks the cause and the brake's condition before deciding what work is needed."
- **21 to 25 seconds:** "See what our brake inspection includes." Show the destination label and workshop identity.

Promise map: The viewer expects to see the scraping source; the visible contact point supplies it. The story then explains the value and limits of inspection.

Rules making the difference: Immediate attention precedes the service pitch [S2] [S4]; concrete context replaces a generic capability statement [S1]; the story fulfills the opening and preserves credibility [S2] [S4]. The timestamps and claim qualification are this package's execution aids.

### Planned test, not a result

Compare A and B with the same demonstration, story, CTA, audience, placement, and landing page. Hypothesis: The familiar sound with a direct visual pointer produces more opening attention without reducing relevant inspection-page visits. Track consistently defined opening view rate, retention, and inspection inquiries. Use the user's approved time and exposure limits; declare inconclusive if exposure is inadequate. Do not claim that either candidate wins before observing evidence.

## Illustration 2: A reporting software team's email

### Brief

Audience: Operations leads who reconcile a recurring report across two spreadsheets.
Channel: Email to an already authorized audience; this illustration drafts copy only.
Value: A public demo shows importing two files and flagging unmatched rows before export.
Action: Watch the 90-second demonstration.
Evidence assumed for this illustration: The demonstration exists and shows this exact workflow. No measured time savings or customer outcomes exist.

### Weak first draft

Subject: "Introducing our innovative reporting platform"
Preview: "Discover powerful features for your business."
Body: "We are excited to announce a next-generation solution with advanced automation, robust integrations, and a user-friendly interface. Book a sales call today."

Why it is weak: Familiar promotional wording asks for attention without a recognizable situation. Feature claims do not create a story. A sales call is a large next step for someone who has not seen the workflow.

### Candidate directions

| Candidate | Subject | Hypothesis | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | "Which rows disagree between your two reports?" | An immediate reconciliation question feels relevant | Assumes the audience handles two reports |
| B | "See unmatched rows before exporting your report" | A concrete useful outcome attracts qualified interest | More functional and potentially less distinctive |
| C | "Two files, one check before export" | A compact workflow contrast makes the message legible | Needs preview text to clarify the check |

### Improved result: Candidate A

Subject: "Which rows disagree between your two reports?"
Preview: "A 90-second demo shows the mismatch check before export."

Body:

"When two spreadsheets disagree, the final report needs a check before it goes out.

Our demo starts with two sample files. Import them, select the matching field, and the tool flags unmatched rows for review before export. You can see what is flagged and what still needs a human decision.

Watch the 90-second demo to see whether this workflow fits your reporting process."

CTA label: "Watch the mismatch-check demo"
Destination: The promised demonstration, with a clear description of supported file types and limits.

Promise map: The subject raises a question about disagreeing rows. The preview and body state that the demo shows how to find unmatched rows. The CTA offers that demonstration rather than substituting a sales meeting.

Rules making the difference: Audience context replaces the seller's excitement [S2]; specific variants replace generic positioning [S1]; credible substance sustains attention [S4]; business intent remains connected to the useful next step [S9]. The email field structure is a package adaptation.

### Planned test, not a result

Compare subjects A and B with identical preview text, body, destination, and comparable randomized audience groups when supported. Hypothesis: A earns more relevant demo visits without reducing completion or increasing unsubscribes. Judge the business outcome using demo completions and qualified follow-up actions; treat opens as supporting, noisy evidence. If audience fit is uncertain, revise the brief rather than expanding the claim. No sending is authorized by this worked example.
