# Hook Point deliverable template

Fill the bracketed fields and remove guidance text from a client-facing deliverable when appropriate. Use source citations for claims about the method. Separate supplied facts, assumptions, proposed creative, and observed results. This schema is an implementation aid, not Kane's proprietary template.

## 1. Assignment and outcome

- **Asset and channel:** [What is being created and where the opening appears.] State format, placement, approximate length, and technical constraints when known.
- **Audience and encounter:** [Who sees it, what they are doing, and what they recognize.] Distinguish known audience evidence from assumptions.
- **Audience desire or problem:** [Immediate reason to care.] Use concrete language the audience understands.
- **Value delivered:** [Useful lesson, demonstration, discovery, or offer.] Name what the story can actually provide.
- **Intended action:** [One primary action and destination.] Specify what success means beyond attention.
- **Evidence available:** [Files, demonstrations, verified claims, supplied facts.] Mark missing proof and claims that cannot yet be used.
- **Constraints and authorization:** [Brand, timing, scope, budget if supplied, and allowed actions.] Distinguish drafting from permission to publish or spend.
- **Baseline:** [Current creative and performance with period and definitions, or unknown.] Do not invent a baseline.

## 2. Attention research

| Reference and URL | Relevant context | Strong or weak evidence | Opening mechanism | Story execution | Transferable hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Reference] | [Audience, channel, date] | [Observed evidence or unknown] | [Why it may earn attention] | [How value and tension develop] | [What to test without copying] |

Guidance: Include contrasting executions where available. Separate public metrics from inferred causes. If research is unavailable, label the candidates unvalidated rather than fabricating reference evidence.

Guidance: Choose a manageable set suited to the brief. Distinct mechanisms teach more than a collection of punctuation changes. Check claims before ranking candidates.

## 4. Selected execution

- **Selected candidate:** [ID and editorial rationale.] Call it a proposed selection until tested.
- **Opening experience:** [First exposed words, first frame/action, sound, and readable text as applicable.] For email, include subject, preview, and body opening. For a pitch, include the first spoken statement and continuation.
- **Story beats:** [Situation, complication or discovery, development, payoff.] Use timing or a line sequence suited to the format.
- **Attention renewal:** [Actual new information, stakes, contrast, or twist where needed.] Do not add empty teasing for its own sake.
- **Credibility:** [What supports each material claim and what limits apply.] Place qualifications where they affect interpretation.
- **CTA:** [Exact wording, action, destination, and audience benefit.] Continue the promise on the destination.
- **Final script or copy:** [Complete usable artifact.] Supply the actual text, not merely advice to write it.

## 5. Promise map

- **The opening leads the audience to expect:** [One sentence.]
- **The story fulfills that expectation by:** [Specific beat, demonstration, or explanation.]
- **The next action extends that value through:** [Destination or interaction.]
- **Unresolved proof or fit gaps:** [Gap, revision needed, or none.] Avoid hiding a proof gap behind confidence.

## 6. Test plan

- **Hypothesis:** [Changing X should affect Y because Z.]
- **Variants and changed element:** [IDs and precise difference.]
- **Comparable conditions:** [Audience, story, offer, placement, destination, timing, spend if authorized.] State unavoidable differences for organic distribution.
- **Primary success measure:** [Business outcome with numerator, denominator, and measurement window.]
- **Opening attention measure:** [Available event and definition, or unavailable.]
- **Story retention measure:** [Watch duration, curve, completion, or format-appropriate proxy.]
- **Exposure and stopping boundary:** [User-approved time, spend, or observation limit; early stop conditions.] If absent, propose a boundary without executing.
- **Decision logic:** [What supports the hypothesis, what weakens it, and when evidence is inconclusive.]
- **Next revision by failure stage:** [Opening, story, trust, audience, offer, CTA, or destination.]

## 7. Review and handoff

- **Checklist outcome:** [Pass, conditional pass with named gaps, or fail.] Use [the review checklist](../checklists/review-checklist.md).
- **Observed evidence:** [Actual results or no live test conducted.]
- **Method sources used:** [Relevant S identifiers linked to the source register.]
- **Next authorized action:** [Draft revision, review, or approved test.] Do not imply that generating a plan authorizes external execution.
