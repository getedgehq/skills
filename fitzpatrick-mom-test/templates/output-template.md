# Interview guide skeleton

Fill with the user's audience and job. The finished output is the guide itself; keep the learning objectives, participant choice and evidence logic in your head. Question principles follow Fitzpatrick's public interviews; the layout is a package convention.

**What these calls should find out:** [one line: the two or three things that would change the founder's plan]

**1. How they do it today**
- [Walk me through how you handle X today / the last time you did X.]
  - [follow-up: who else is involved? what tools?]

**2. The last time it went wrong**
- [Tell me about the last time X broke / took too long. What happened?]
  - [follow-up: what did that cost you? what did you do next?]

**3. What they have tried**
- [What have you tried to fix it? Why that? What happened?]
- [What are you paying for this today, in money, time or people?]

**4. Their specific assumptions** (one block per belief, feature or price point in the notes; prototype and price only after sections 1 to 3)
- [Questions that could break the assumption, anchored in past behavior.]
- [Price: what they spend now, who decides; then the actual price stated neutrally, "what would make that worth it?"]

**5. Wrap-up (and, if there is an offer, the commitment ask)**
- [Who else should I talk to about this?]
- [If selling: a concrete next step, e.g. a session with the person who owns the budget, a paid pilot.]

*After each call, write down (optional):* [the five or six facts that settle the founder's decisions]

*Avoid (optional, one line):* "Would you use...?", "How much would you pay...?", "Do you like the idea?"

## Reviewing supplied questions or notes

Return the rewritten questions in the same order, or the conclusions from the notes (what people actually did, spent and committed to, versus compliments and hypotheticals), then a few lines on what changed.
