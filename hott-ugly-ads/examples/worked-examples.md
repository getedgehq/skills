# Worked examples

Both examples are invented illustrations. Brands, research observations, scripts, and offers are fictional scenario inputs, not evidence of real campaigns or performance. In actual use, replace them with verified product facts and observed audience references.

## Illustration 1: Premium meal-planning app

### Brief and assumed research

A fictional app, Lunchline, wants Instagram Reels concepts for office workers who abandon planned lunches by midweek. Its hypothetical offer is a free sample lunch plan. The desired action is to request that sample. Assume the brief supplies genuine functionality for choosing lunch ideas and building a grocery list.

For illustration, suppose research finds casual meal-prep recordings and candid desk-lunch stories. Their transferable qualities are ordinary kitchens, short conversational captions, and an opening that names a familiar frustration. These are invented observations; an actual brief needs links and dates. The choice follows Hott's research-before-creation guidance. [S7]

### Weak first draft

**Visual:** Animated logo, polished phone mockup, then a sequence of interface screens.

**Copy:** "Meet Lunchline. Premium planning. Smart lists. Personalized meals. Elevate your lunch routine."

**Action:** "Download now."

**Problem:** The viewer must care about the app before the opening becomes relevant. The feature list does not express the recognizable experience. Premium production is being chosen from the product's price rather than the viewing context. [S3][S4][S8]

### Improved result

**Premise:** The distance between an ambitious Sunday plan and another Wednesday delivery order.

| Beat | Production instruction | Draft words |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Phone shot of a desk lunch beside a crossed-out weekly menu. Keep personal information out of frame. | "My Sunday lunch plan did not survive Wednesday." |
| Situation | Speaker shows the overly ambitious meal list in an ordinary kitchen. | "I planned five recipes. I needed two lunches I could actually repeat." |
| Product contribution | Brief, legible view of the app selecting two lunches and building their list. | "Start with two options and one grocery list." |
| Benefit | Show a real prepared lunch and its ingredients, with a clear view of food quality. | "A plan that fits the week you're having." |
| Action | Simple end caption and spoken invitation. | "Get the free sample lunch plan." |

The first-person lines are draft dramatization for an actor. Use them as a genuine customer account only if the speaker actually had that experience. No money-saving or health outcome is claimed.

**Alternate opening:** Open on an unused bag of ingredients: "I bought groceries for a version of me with more free time." This tests waste and unrealistic planning as the audience moment. It needs a corresponding shot and story adjustment, not just another caption on the desk footage.

### Rules that changed the draft

- Person before interface: the product now explains a familiar planning mismatch. [S4]
- Native delivery: the setting and speech follow the assumed feed evidence. [S2][S7]
- Premium fit: a clear food shot supports quality without requiring a studio introduction. [S1][S3]
- Commercial clarity: the demonstrated capability and sample-plan action remain explicit. [S1]

### Review and proposed learning

A cold reviewer should identify the midweek lunch problem and the app's planning role. If they only remember a funny confession, strengthen the product bridge. Compare with the current best creative under documented equivalent conditions, using sample requests and downstream acquisition where available. Inspect shares as an additional diagnostic. No result or uplift is predicted. [S5][S6]

## Illustration 2: Premium leather work bag

### Brief and assumed research

A fictional brand, Alder Carry, sells a premium leather work bag to train commuters who move between offices. The desired action is to view its dimensions and interior layout. Assume the brief verifies a laptop compartment and interior organization; it supplies no lifetime durability evidence.

For illustration, suppose audience research finds commuter packing videos and bag-content walkthroughs. The useful grammar is a close phone camera, ordinary indoor light, visible objects, and practical explanations. This is a scenario assumption requiring validation in a real assignment. [S7]

### Weak first draft

**Visual:** Cinematic rotating bag, dramatic lighting, gold headline, logo reveal.

**Copy:** "Luxury redefined. An icon for the modern professional. Buy the last bag you'll ever need."

**Action:** "Shop luxury."

**Problem:** The opening gives the commuter no recognizable situation. The lifetime promise is unsupported. The production primarily expresses brand taste; it does not establish feed relevance. [S1][S3][S8]

### Improved result

**Premise:** Finding a work item while standing in a crowded train aisle.

| Beat | Production instruction | Draft words |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Close phone recording of the speaker reaching into a bag while holding a notebook. Film safely in a stationary setting. | "Ever need one thing from your bag when there's nowhere to put it down?" |
| Situation | Show the particular items the commuter needs, without exaggerated chaos. | "Laptop, notebook, keys. I want to find each without unpacking everything." |
| Product contribution | Demonstrate the actual compartment layout with the same objects. | "Here's where each one goes in the Alder Carry." |
| Quality cue | Brief close view of the real leather and stitching. | "A closer look at the finish." |
| Action | Show the bag open and leave the layout readable. | "Check the dimensions and interior before choosing yours." |

The speaker is a product demonstrator unless genuine customer experience is established. Keep the demonstration literal: do not fake effortless access if the real bag requires extra steps.

**Alternate angle:** A desk-to-meeting packing check: "What comes with me when my office changes twice today?" The human situation shifts from in-transit access to moving work locations. The same verified features can support a different premise.

### Rules that changed the draft

- Immediate relevance: the opening expresses a particular commuter situation. [S8]
- Product as answer: organization appears through actual use rather than an adjective list. [S4]
- Luxury adaptation: quality remains visible within a practical, natural recording. [S1][S3]
- Credibility: speaker role and claims do not rely on fabricated customer testimony. [S2]

### Review and proposed learning

Pass only if the opening is legible at phone size, the layout demonstration is accurate, and the call to action matches the destination. For a conversion campaign, compare acquisition outcomes with the existing winner and inspect sharing where useful. For a separate awareness placement, reconsider production and evaluation rather than assuming this feed execution is the only valid style. [S3][S5][S6]
