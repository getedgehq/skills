# The Ugly Ads method

Based on Barry Hott's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Barry Hott.

## Attribution and application

Hott's public method prioritizes an audience's recognition, trust, and response over conventional advertising polish. Roughness is useful when it helps an ad fit the content people consume. It is not an independent performance mechanism or a guarantee of success. An ad still needs relevant attention, a meaningful message, and an action. [S1]

The principles attributed below are grounded in the fetched sources. Brief fields, review gates, suggested comparison tables, and diagnostic procedures are this package's operational synthesis. They are not a proprietary framework claimed by Hott. Examples elsewhere in the package are invented illustrations, not reported campaigns.

## Start with the person

### Cold-viewer assumptions

Begin as though the viewer does not know the brand, has little time, and will only attend if the content clearly concerns them. Brand familiarity cannot be a hidden prerequisite for understanding the opening. Hott presents these as ideation assumptions, not a claim that every actual viewer is unfamiliar. [S8]

Write the audience as a person in a situation: an office worker deciding what to eat at lunchtime, for example, rather than simply a demographic label. Identify the moment that connects their experience to the product's value. Ask what happened, what they wanted, and what made the situation frustrating or rewarding.

### Translate features into experience

Hott cautions against starting app ads with screenshots, features, or interface tours. Those choices reflect the company's perspective before the viewer has a reason to care. Start with a recognizable human experience and make the product's role understandable within it. [S4]

Use this decision sequence:

1. Name the feature or product capability.
2. Describe what a person encounters before using it.
3. Describe the specific change the capability enables.
4. Open with the recognizable situation or change.
5. Introduce enough product detail to explain the connection.

This sequence is an application aid, not a requirement to conceal the product. A product can appear immediately when its use or context is itself recognizable and relevant. The error is asking strangers to admire it without a reason. [S2][S4]

Reject an opening that requires an internal explanation. Replace a generic promise with a situation the intended viewer could identify on sight or from a short line of natural speech.

## Research the feed

Hott recommends spending several focused hours studying the audience's organic consumption, saving examples, and taking notes before making ads. His post suggests two to three hours; treat that as a practical research recommendation, not a universal sufficiency threshold. [S7]

For each example, record:

- Link, platform, observation date, and why it is relevant to this audience.
- Topic, recognizable situation, and first visible or audible beat.
- Speaker or creator role, language, setting, camera distance, and lighting.
- Editing rhythm, caption behavior, and how the post communicates its point.
- What is observed, what is inferred, and what could transfer to the proposed ad.

Study organic content rather than relying solely on competitor advertisements or an internal mood board. A format's popularity elsewhere does not establish that this audience consumes it. Avoid treating your own recommendation feed as automatically representative; use audience-relevant creators, topics, and available customer evidence to explain the connection. This sampling caution is package guidance derived from the research principle. [S7]

When direct research is unavailable, state the limitation. Produce a provisional concept and a concrete research request. Never describe guessed references as observed behavior. Refresh old references when the feed's language has changed; Hott explicitly notes that native-looking content can become stale. [S2]

## Choose style and authenticity

### Native style depends on context

Hott treats relevance as having several dimensions rather than a yes-or-no property. Subject matter, delivery, visual treatment, and audience expectations can reinforce or undermine one another. Authenticity likewise depends on factors such as voice, delivery, speaker provenance, and aesthetics. A rough camera alone does not make a story believable. [S2]

Select a vessel that carries the human premise. A handwritten note might express an actual list or confession. A phone video might capture a use moment. A founder account can combine genuine experience with relevant authority. Do not choose a vessel merely because it is inexpensive or recognizably associated with ugly ads. [S1][S2][S4]

Remove unnecessary logo introductions, decorative transitions, studio conventions, or rigid scripting when they obstruct recognition. Keep information that helps comprehension or credibility. The method does not require bad audio, illegible captions, damaged product presentation, or purposeless visual noise. [S1][S4]

### Premium products and polished placements

Hott explicitly includes luxury and expensive products in the case for more human social creative. He also supports polished production in suitable contexts such as television or YouTube, particularly for brands operating at substantial scale. These are placement decisions, not a universal prohibition on beauty. [S1][S3]

For a premium offer in a social feed, preserve an honest view of material quality, finish, or service experience while testing a more natural surrounding story. For a placement whose consumption context differs, reassess style rather than recycling the same asset. Do not infer that every YouTube placement has identical expectations. [S3]

Awareness campaigns still compete for relevant attention. Changing the account objective does not change how the viewer experiences an irrelevant execution. Align evaluation with the stated business objective while retaining audience and placement fit. [S3]

### AI and genuine experience

Hott advises matching the audience's consumption: AI content can fit audiences that engage with it; human content can fit audiences seeking human experiences. AI can assist production and storytelling, but research must supply its context. Do not turn this into a categorical requirement to use or avoid AI. [S2][S7]

When using synthetic people, distinguish a spokesperson or dramatization from a genuine customer's account. Hott's talk cautions about fabricated testimonials and trust. This package therefore requires a truthful speaker description and supported claims. It does not provide a legal compliance assessment. [S2]

The talk includes invented charts and numerical claims that Hott explicitly identifies as made up. Exclude them from evidence, persuasive copy, and predicted uplift. Tool capabilities and examples in that 2025 recording are historical, not a current technical specification. [S2]

## Build content that sells

Use a simple causal arc: recognizable situation, meaningful product contribution, understandable benefit, and a relevant next action. This is a production scaffold derived from Hott's attention-message-action requirement and person-first emphasis. It is not a fixed-length formula. [S1][S4]

A concept card should state what appears first, what the viewer recognizes, how the product helps, and why the speaker or demonstration is credible. Include enough shot and caption detail to produce the asset. Avoid unsupported savings, invented before-and-after results, or testimonial language supplied by someone who has not had the experience.

An alternate concept should express a different human reason to care. An alternate opening can test another entry into the same story. State which type of change you are proposing so the comparison teaches something. Hott encourages faster creation and learning instead of endless perfection before launch. [S2]

Use the following choices when a draft stalls:

| Draft symptom | Decision |
|---|---|
| Attractive product with no recognizable situation | Reframe around a person's use moment. |
| Relatable opening with no product connection | Add a specific demonstration or explanation of its contribution. |
| Native format with unnatural dialogue | Rewrite in the speaker's language and retain truthful details. |
| Humorous premise with irrelevant engagement | Reconnect the joke to the audience's problem and the offer. |
| Rough execution that obscures the offer | Fix readability or sound without adding needless production gloss. |

## Review before production approval

Review the candidate at phone size in its intended context, beside suitable organic references. The mock-feed and cold-review checks are package procedures for applying placement fit and immediate relevance. A design canvas alone gives insufficient evidence about feed behavior. [S2][S3][S8]

Ask a reviewer unfamiliar with the brand what is happening, who it concerns, and what the product changes. Record their response before explaining the brief. Revise confusion that concerns the premise or product role. Do not replace comprehension evidence with a vote on attractiveness.

Executive and marketer preferences can supply constraints, but Hott explicitly warns against using their taste as a substitute for what the audience consumes. Resolve aesthetic disagreements with audience references and the campaign's goal. Do not discard legitimate production constraints; propose another native treatment. [S3][S7]

## Test and learn

### Compare within a meaningful environment

Hott recommends comparing new creative with the strongest existing ads under matching targeting, attribution, and account settings. His interview describes a controlled environment with concept-level ad sets and attention to cold audiences. Existing customers or recent visitors can change how apparent performance should be interpreted. [S6]

Record the benchmark, audience exclusions, placement, objective, attribution, offer, landing destination, delivery period, and budget approach. Give concepts a comparable opportunity to deliver. Separate ad sets are one described approach, not a claim that this setup is always available or experimentally definitive. Platform allocation, timing, or other differences can limit causal conclusions.

Low delivery is not conclusive proof of poor creative. Hott warns that overly restrictive bidding can prevent useful exploration. Investigate delivery conditions before deciding what the execution taught you. Where a controlled comparison is infeasible, describe it as observational and state the limitation. [S6]

### Evidence and interpretation

The evidence needed depends on product price, budget, normal conversion volume, and tolerance for learning costs. Hott mentions roughly twenty useful signals, ideally purchases, in his own practice. Do not promote that example into a universal stopping rule. Likewise, he rejects generic click or impression benchmarks as sufficient context. [S6]

Before testing, define business outcome, financial tolerance, maximum spend or duration, evidence goal, and who makes the decision. These stopping conditions are package guidance. Distinguish an ad that has failed its business criterion from one that lacks enough evidence to judge.

Hott highlights shares and cost per share because shared distribution may produce downstream activity that ad-level reporting does not fully capture. Treat that as a reason to investigate an apparent mismatch between delivery and reported acquisition results. Do not claim every shared conversion is invisible or that shares establish incremental sales. [S5]

Read outcomes alongside shares, comments where useful, and broader available business evidence. Cost per share is spend divided by reported shares; if shares are zero, report it as unavailable rather than zero. If poor reported acquisition coincides with heavy sharing, form an attribution hypothesis and keep any additional investigation within the agreed budget. If both are poor, diagnose relevance, message, format, offer, or delivery rather than defending roughness.

For an awareness objective, use evidence suited to awareness when available. Hott reports favorable brand-lift experience for platform-relevant content, but this does not forecast the user's result. [S3]

### Next iteration

State a specific hypothesis: a clearer opening, another human moment, better product linkage, different delivery, or better placement fit. Preserve a working premise while changing one meaningful variable when learning is the aim. Larger concept changes may be warranted, but acknowledge that their results cannot isolate the effect of one small edit. Without authorized live testing, deliver a proposed plan and label performance expectations as hypotheses. [S2][S6]

## Explicit warnings and boundaries

Hott explicitly cautions against:

- Making something ugly solely for ugliness, or forgetting that it must communicate and prompt action. [S1]
- Opening from the brand's internal perspective instead of a recognizable person's experience. [S4]
- Assuming a polished asset belongs in every placement or that awareness objectives excuse irrelevance. [S3]
- Designing primarily to please executives or other marketers. [S3][S7]
- Handing ideation to AI before learning what the audience consumes. [S7]
- Treating authenticity as an aesthetic switch or manufacturing customer testimony. [S2]
- Judging only last-click outcomes while ignoring substantial shared distribution. [S5]
- Using restrictive delivery or generic benchmarks without account context. [S6]

The package adds these operational boundaries: do not invent research, claims, or test results; do not promise an uplift; and do not infer authorization to publish or spend from a request for creative. These boundaries protect the usefulness and honesty of the deliverable without extending Hott's advice into a claimed universal system.
