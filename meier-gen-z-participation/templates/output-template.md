# Participation Decision Brief Template

Fill this template for one decision. Remove guidance from the finished brief, retain uncertainty labels, and omit conditional sections only with a short explanation. This is a package application tool, not Meier's proprietary deliverable. Its employer/customer distinction and participation focus follow the public approach. [S3] [S4]

## 1. Decision and status

**Decision statement:** To improve [outcome] for [segment], [decision owner] will consider changing [lever] because [observed friction].

Guidance: Name something the owner can change, not an ambition such as becoming more Gen Z friendly. Keep recruitment and customer decisions in separate briefs.

**Status:** [Discovery plan / provisional recommendation / participant-informed pilot proposal / reviewed pilot decision]

Guidance: Select the status supported by available evidence. Participant-informed does not mean statistically representative or causally proven.

**Constraints:** [Scope, budget, capacity, fixed policies, deadline, and authorized actions]

Guidance: Distinguish a genuine constraint from an assumption that could be tested. A recommendation does not authorize publishing, contacting people, or changing an external system.

## 2. Segment and participation

**Affected segment:** [Life stage, geography, role or need, relevant access conditions, age scope if useful, and exclusions]

Guidance: Describe why this group matters to the decision. Do not claim it represents every young person.

**Participants and gaps:** [Who contributed, recruitment route, contrasting experiences included, missing perspectives]

Guidance: Include source dates and selection limits. If no one participated, state that plainly and add a recruitment plan.

**Influence:** [What participants helped interpret or shape, what changed, what remained fixed, who makes the final decision]

Guidance: Show influence beyond being consulted or featured. This implements the participation principle. [S2] [S3]

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
