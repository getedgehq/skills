# Worked Examples

Both cases below are invented illustrations. Organizations, participant findings, numbers, dates, targets, and outcomes are fictional. The citations explain method choices, not evidence that Meier worked on these cases or that the proposed results occurred. Each improved result is a decision brief excerpt using [the template](../templates/output-template.md).

## Illustration 1: A regional bank's graduate recruitment

### Weak first draft

Gen Z dislikes traditional banks and cares more about purpose than salary. Launch humorous TikTok videos promising rapid career growth and flexible working. Have the youngest employee teach executives how to post. Aim for 100,000 views and expect applications to rise.

### Apply the method

Define the local decision before selecting a platform. In this illustration, eight recent applicants, four declined candidates, and six junior staff discuss their actual choices. Their accounts suggest that an unexplained wait after interviews and unclear first-year responsibility deter some candidates. Two participants value the bank's stability more than early autonomy, so the team avoids a single ambition-based persona. These small, selected groups inform a pilot rather than a population claim.

Inspect all three Love-Job dimensions. The supplied role has a clear, competitive salary range, but the existing preview omits it. Internal appreciation is less credible: the first-year development plan has no concrete project and feedback timing varies by manager. External appreciation is mixed: some participants view banking as stable; others cannot explain the role's customer benefit. The dimensions come from the public model, while these findings are invented. [S7]

Junior staff and a hiring manager revise the role preview and define a supported first project. Candidates review whether the description answers their uncertainties. A voluntary mentoring pair helps a senior manager understand the hiring journey while the junior partner learns how project decisions are made. The pairing supports this decision; it is not a mandatory social media lesson. [S5] [S8]

### Improved result

**Decision and segment:** The graduate hiring lead will decide whether to adopt a clearer role preview and predictable interview updates to increase qualified applications from final-year students seeking local customer-advisory roles.

**Evidence and limits:** The 18 invented participant accounts identify process uncertainty and unclear early contribution. They do not establish what all students want. Candidate portal records provide a fictional baseline of 40 qualified applications per 1,000 completed role-page visits in the previous comparable campaign. The definition of qualified remains fixed during the pilot.

**Recommended change:** Publish the actual salary range and a first-year project example. Commit to a status update within five working days after an interview, without promising a final decision by that date. Give new hires a bounded customer-information improvement project with a supervisor, approval boundaries, and fortnightly feedback. Show the existing flexibility rules accurately.

**Participation:** Two junior staff co-create the preview, candidate reviewers check clarity, and the hiring lead explains which revisions were accepted. One interested junior employee and one senior manager volunteer for a reciprocal learning pilot, with personal matching and goals in both directions.

**Communication:** Discovery participants report using the university careers portal and the bank's Instagram account. Test a concrete role demonstration linked to the revised page on those channels. The employer-branding manager owns ongoing updates and responses; platform choice remains segment-specific.

**Test:** During November 2026, compare the revised page with the current page using a split of eligible traffic where feasible. Target at least 50 qualified applications per 1,000 completed role-page visits, with no drop in the qualification rate among applications. Ask interviewees whether updates and responsibility claims matched their experience. Review on 2026-12-07. If traffic is too low for a credible comparison, continue collection and report uncertainty rather than declaring a win.

**Ownership and decision:** The hiring lead owns process updates by 2026-10-23; the advisory team manager confirms project capacity by 2026-10-26; the employer-branding manager prepares the reviewed preview by 2026-10-30. Scale only if the outcome target is met and promised updates can be sustained. Revise if interest rises but qualified applications do not; stop the unsupported project promise if supervisory capacity is unavailable.

### Rules that made the difference

- Direct participation replaced the universal dislike-of-banks claim and exposed a changeable process problem. [S2] [S3]
- The Love-Job diagnosis retained money and actual treatment alongside meaning and reputation. [S7]
- Early responsibility became a supported task rather than an unbounded promotion promise. [S5]
- A business outcome replaced views as the main decision measure; the numerical target is an illustrative pilot choice. [S9]

## Illustration 2: A transit app with low repeat use

### Weak first draft

Gen Z wants sustainability and entertainment. Rebrand the ticket app with slang, hire a viral creator, and shift the whole budget to TikTok. Run a launch contest and judge success by engagement. Ask the intern to approve the campaign on behalf of young riders.

### Apply the method

Separate adoption from communication. In this illustration, six student commuters, four apprentices, and four people who abandoned checkout review their recent journeys. The invented evidence suggests that some riders cannot tell which ticket covers a mixed bus-and-train trip until late in checkout. Nonstudents challenge the assumption that a student discount solves the problem. Two frequent users find the existing route guidance sufficient, pointing to a segment difference rather than a universal flaw.

The product team and affected riders prototype clearer fare coverage before commissioning promotion. This applies the distinction between product or brand analysis and creative execution. It does not apply the employer Love-Job model to consumers. [S4]

### Improved result

**Decision and segment:** The product lead will decide whether to introduce earlier fare-coverage guidance for local 18-to-25-year-old riders booking mixed-mode commuting trips, to improve purchase completion and appropriate repeat use.

**Evidence and limits:** Fourteen invented accounts identify coverage uncertainty; existing analytics show an illustrative 52% checkout completion rate for eligible trips. Interview selection may overrepresent frustrated riders. Price sensitivity remains a hypothesis requiring separate discovery.

**Recommended change:** Add a plain-language coverage summary before payment and a route-specific example. Let riders compare two prototypes, explain what they think each ticket permits, and correct the team's interpretation. Publish coverage claims only after the fare-operations lead verifies them.

**Participation:** Student commuters, apprentices, and checkout abandoners help review the prototype. The product designer records changes arising from each group and returns a brief explanation of accepted and rejected suggestions. The intern contributes as an employee with relevant experience, not as the sole representative of a cohort.

**Communication:** Once verified, create a short mixed-mode booking demonstration. Use the in-app route-selection screen for existing users and the vocational college newsletter for outreach because these are the journey touchpoints found in this illustration. Assess social distribution separately if new evidence supports it. The product marketing lead owns review and responses.

**Test:** Compare the new summary with the existing checkout for eligible trips for four weeks in November 2026. Target completion of at least 60%, track successful repeat purchase within 30 days as a secondary outcome, and require no increase in fare-related refund rate. Ask a small user sample to explain coverage in their own words. If completion improves but comprehension or refunds worsen, revise before scale. A small pilot is directional evidence, not proof of long-term retention.

**Ownership and review:** The fare-operations lead verifies ticket wording by 2026-10-23; the designer completes rider-reviewed prototypes by 2026-10-30; the analytics lead checks instrumentation before launch. The product lead reviews completion and comprehension on 2026-12-07 and the repeat-use window on 2027-01-08. Stop if the summary cannot be verified. Scale only after the completion target and comprehension check pass without a worsening refund rate.

### Rules that made the difference

- Contrasting affected people replaced symbolic approval by one young employee. [S3] [S6]
- Product experience became the first intervention, with communications explaining the verified change. [S4]
- Channel selection followed the local journey, avoiding a universal platform or budget prescription. [S4] [S9]
- Participant feedback and behavior jointly informed the test; the targets and decision rules are package tools. [S6] [S9]
