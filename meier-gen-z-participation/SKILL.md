# Meier Gen Z Participation

Based on Yaël Meier's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Yaël Meier.

## When to use

Use this skill to turn a youth-facing organizational problem into an evidence-led decision brief: improving applications or retention, changing a product or customer experience, reviewing employer branding, or building collaboration across generations. The deliverable connects what affected young people experience to a change the organization can make, an accountable owner, and a test.

Begin with the relevant segment and decision, rather than a universal Gen Z persona. Age alone does not establish needs, skills, purchasing power, or channel preference. This package applies public interviews, posts, and a reported talk; it does not reproduce the jointly edited book or claim access to ZEAM's proprietary instruments. See [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) for the source boundary. [S1]

The participation principle is central: young people help interpret evidence and shape choices before the final recommendation. If participant access is missing, deliver a provisional brief with a recruitment plan and explicit uncertainties. Do not invent interviews or present illustrative findings as research. Source-backed advice, actual participant evidence, and the agent's operational recommendations must remain distinguishable. [S2] [S3]

## Procedure

1. **Define the decision.** Read the scope rules in [references/method.md](references/method.md#1-scope-and-decision). Write the outcome, affected group, observed friction, changeable lever, and decision owner in one sentence. Choose employer, customer, or intergenerational work; keep separate decisions separate even when one organization owns them. [S4]

2. **Define the segment and evidence limits.** Use the evidence rules in [references/method.md](references/method.md#2-evidence-and-participation). Describe life stage, context, relevant needs, and exclusions. Classify supplied claims as observations, participant accounts, hypotheses, or public-method guidance. Treat executive family anecdotes as hypotheses. [S2]

3. **Bring affected people into discovery.** Follow [references/method.md](references/method.md#2-evidence-and-participation). Include contrasting experiences such as declined candidates or abandoned purchases. Ask about recent decisions and obstacles. Specify how participants can influence the work and what the organization cannot change. [S3] [S6]

4. **Interpret findings together.** Use the evidence table in [templates/output-template.md](templates/output-template.md). Record context, a theme, a counterexample, uncertainty, and the decision implication. Give participants an opportunity to correct interpretations. If evidence is absent or conflicting, narrow the recommendation or plan further discovery.

5. **Diagnose the underlying offer.** Apply the relevant branch in [references/method.md](references/method.md#3-diagnose-the-offer). For employers inspect money, internal appreciation, and external appreciation; for customers inspect benefit, experience, and reputation. Connect each gap to observed friction before proposing communications. [S4] [S7]

6. **Make participation operational.** Follow [references/method.md](references/method.md#4-design-participation-and-mutual-mentoring). Define a real contribution, bounded responsibility, support, feedback, and decision rights. If mentoring fits the problem, make it voluntary, reciprocal, personally matched, structured, and visibly supported by senior leaders. [S5] [S8]

7. **Develop and compare changes.** Consult [examples/worked-examples.md](examples/worked-examples.md) for the reasoning pattern. Invite young contributors to shape feasible options. Compare the current approach with changes to the offer, process, or participation. Explain tradeoffs and why the selected option addresses the evidence. Examples are illustrations, not benchmarks.

8. **Choose channels and content when needed.** Apply [references/method.md](references/method.md#5-communication-and-channel-decisions). Use the segment's actual attention and decision journey. Let connected contributors shape content, show concrete benefits, and ensure every promise is deliverable. Specify ownership of a continuing presence rather than assuming one campaign establishes relevance. [S4] [S6]

9. **Design a bounded test.** Follow [references/method.md](references/method.md#6-test-decide-and-transfer-ownership). Define a comparison, outcome metric, baseline, review date, and scale, revise, or stop rule. Include behavioral evidence and participant feedback; revise the generation hypothesis when findings contradict it. [S6] [S9]

10. **Deliver and review the brief.** Fill [templates/output-template.md](templates/output-template.md), then apply [checklists/review-checklist.md](checklists/review-checklist.md). Assign owners and dates, label unresolved evidence gaps, and cite Meier-specific assertions. Hand back a decision the organization can act on, with a clear next review and no invented certainty.
