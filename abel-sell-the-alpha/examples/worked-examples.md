# Worked examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. Companies, buyer statements, commitments, dates, savings, and prices below are fictional. They demonstrate the method, not evidence of customer results or prescribed pricing. Rule citations refer to the method's public foundations.

## Illustration 1: Supplier disruption signals for a grocery chain

### Scenario and weak first draft

A fictional startup, SignalHarbor, has a working supplier-risk product and wants to sell to Northfield Grocers, a national chain. Its founder writes:

> Our AI dashboard is faster and easier than your current supplier software. It includes alerts, integrations, reports, and advanced analytics. We can demo all the features tomorrow and give you unlimited access for $10,000 a year. Once you see the value, we can raise the price.

This draft invents comparative superiority, supplies no account-specific advantage, offers too much before discovery, and leaves later expansion indefensible. It also treats a software tour as the first learning activity. [S2] [S3]

### Improved result: Account sales brief

**Motion and entry point:** Enterprise selling to the executive responsible for seasonal availability. Begin with one seasonal produce category and the analysts handling its replenishment. Treat the executive's interest and the implementation team's capacity as unverified.

**Alpha hypothesis:** Northfield currently responds when supplier deliveries change. If upstream signals reliably precede those changes, the merchandising leader could reserve alternatives before shortages become visible through routine delivery reporting. SignalHarbor's signal feed and analyst workflow are the proposed mechanism. Earlier warning and commercial usefulness remain to be established. [S2]

**Outreach draft, four sentences:**

> Your seasonal availability team may have less time to act once a supplier misses a delivery. We are testing whether upstream production signals can reveal disruption before it appears in delivery reporting. That could give merchandising a window to reserve alternatives, if the signals are actionable in your categories. Would a short conversation about your last seasonal shortage help test that premise?

This message states a hypothesis rather than pretending to have researched Northfield's internal performance. It gives a reason to talk without listing the entire product. [S6]

**Discovery agenda:** Ask about the most recent disruption, attempted workarounds, measured lost sales or replacement costs, the accountable owner, and the practical lead time needed to reserve alternatives. Confirm the current systems before deciding which product functions to demonstrate. [S3]

**Illustrative discovery update:** Suppose the buyer reports recurring last-minute purchases and assigns a category analyst to quantify them. Record those as scenario evidence, while predictive lead time stays unknown. Arrange the next session with the merchandising executive and analyst to review historical events. Advance because the problem has an owner and a concrete next action; do not forecast a signed contract yet. [S3] [S6]

**Engagement choice:** Propose a product evaluation using a bounded set of historical disruptions and a limited live category. This evaluates an existing mechanism rather than inviting the buyer to direct the product roadmap. Agree how false positives, usable warning time, and analyst workload will be reviewed. The specific evaluation measures are illustrative package choices.

**Commercial frame:** Discuss a proposed $90,000 initial annual deployment for the defined category and user group, conditional on evaluation and a joint value case. This invented price sits within Abel's discussed enterprise range but needs justification. Explain that expansion adds categories and users with corresponding scope and value; an evaluation fee, if agreed, has separate terms. [S2]

**Procurement and next action:** Confirm data rights, IT review timing, financial approval, and agreement requirements with procurement and counsel. Build savings from verifiable replacement-spend reduction separately from speculative sales protection. The founder owns arranging the evaluation-design session within seven days; the buyer's willingness and calendar must still be confirmed. [S8]

### Rules that changed the result

The revision moves from product attributes to a capability the executive could defend, limits discovery to a specific need, makes the demonstration relevant, and explains expansion before a cheap contract creates the wrong anchor. The procurement case distinguishes testable financial evidence from strategic upside. [S2] [S3] [S8]

## Illustration 2: A design partnership that is pulling the company off course

### Scenario and weak first draft

A fictional founder at LedgerSpring is building an AI-assisted invoice discrepancy workflow for manufacturers. Two manufacturers have expressed interest, while an insurer offers feedback in exchange for custom claims software. The founder proposes:

> We have product-market fit because three design partners love the idea. We will build whatever each wants for free and call it a proof of concept. The insurer's brand will help us raise money. I will hire a VP of Sales now, and procurement can handle contracts after we deliver.

This confuses early enthusiasm with commercial validation, mixes customer profiles, mislabels an unfinished-product engagement, delegates learning prematurely, and exposes the company to unpaid delivery. [S5] [S7] [S8]

### Improved result: Account sales brief

**Motion and hypothesis:** Enterprise learning remains founder-led. Focus on manufacturing finance teams with repeated invoice discrepancies, rather than combining manufacturing and insurance requirements. The manufacturing hypothesis is still unproven: earlier identification might let controllers close periods with fewer unresolved exceptions. [S3]

**Alpha hypothesis:** Controllers currently investigate discrepancies after invoices accumulate. LedgerSpring could give them an earlier, traceable exception queue for the affected supplier group, improving their ability to resolve issues before close. The benefit depends on data quality and usable explanations; neither has been demonstrated. [S2]

**Outreach draft, three sentences:**

> Invoice errors may become harder to resolve when they first surface during close. We are exploring whether earlier exception review gives manufacturing controllers more time to resolve the cases that need human judgment. Would you share how your team handled its last set of recurring discrepancies?

**Discovery and decision:** Request actual examples, previous attempts, costs tracked, the accountable controller, and access to the people doing reconciliation. If one manufacturer only offers praise while the other supplies examples and agrees to a working session, advance the latter and clarify the former's timing. Treat no response as unresolved rather than assumed agreement. [S3] [S6]

**Partner scope:** Offer a paid, eight-week design partnership to the responsive manufacturer. Learn from one supplier group, review exception explanations weekly, and agree which product changes generalize to the manufacturing profile. Disclose that live automation is unfinished and excluded. These time and scope choices are invented for the illustration, not Abel's prescribed defaults.

**Commercial terms:** A proposed $18,000 development engagement buys bounded learning and implementation support, not unlimited annual software. Discuss a possible later deployment around $100,000 annually only if agreed capabilities, adoption, and value justify it. The later price is an assumption for negotiation, not committed revenue. Explain the transition before signature. [S2]

**Roadmap response:** Decline the insurer's claims build because it tests a different customer and problem. Revisit that market only through a separate strategic decision, not as an automatic concession for a logo. Early partners can improve the vision without owning it. [S5]

**Procurement and ownership:** Obtain agreement and payment approval before substantial delivery. Have counsel review data handling, obligations, and legal terms. Confirm procurement's timeline and include the controller in the first discussion. The founder owns sending the scoped draft within three working days, without assuming the buyer accepts it. [S8]

**Handoff assessment:** Postpone the VP recommendation. First capture the validated entry point and show that a non-founder can generate and close comparable opportunities under founder management. Keep founder responsibility for product-market learning explicit. [S7]

### Rules that changed the result

The revision separates product development from product evaluation, protects a consistent market, distinguishes an early learning fee from recurring software value, and substitutes observable buyer action for partner counts. It also replaces premature leadership hiring with a repeatability test. [S5] [S7]
