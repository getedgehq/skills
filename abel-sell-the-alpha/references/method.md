# Method

Based on Jen Abel's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Jen Abel.

## How to read this reference

This is a practical synthesis of the public teachings listed in [sources.md](sources.md). Numbered sections expand the main loop. Decision records, status labels, evaluation criteria, and template fields are package conventions unless attributed explicitly. Citations support the underlying teaching, not a claim that Abel prescribes this exact worksheet.

The central distinction is between learning what the buyer needs and communicating what the buyer could become able to do. Discovery tests a specific need against reality. Executive selling makes the future advantage compelling. A vision without evidence becomes an unsupported promise; evidence without an opportunity narrative can leave an executive with little reason to champion a startup. [S2] [S3]

## 1. Choose the motion

Abel distinguishes a marketing-led small business motion from relationship-intensive enterprise selling. Her challenge to the mid-market category is a warning against ambiguous operating choices, not a requirement to deny that medium-sized companies exist. Determine how the account actually buys: who commits budget, how much adaptation is needed, what approvals apply, and whether the economics sustain the selling effort. [S2]

Do not carry small business pricing and assumptions into an enterprise opportunity simply because the company already has down-market revenue. Abel treats moving into enterprise as renewed learning about a different buyer and value proposition. A large company buying a few seats is especially easy to misframe: the first contract creates a price reference for later expansion. [S2]

**Decision rule:** Write the motion and the evidence behind it before proposing price or sales talent. If the task is genuinely low-touch and low-value, use the relevant discovery insights without forcing an enterprise commercial model. Abel acknowledges that lower-value contracts can work with a sufficiently large market and strong win rate; evaluate the economics rather than declaring every small contract a failure. [S2]

## 2. Select the entry point

Constrain discovery to one customer profile, one role, and one unmet need. For an already-built product, enumerate plausible use cases privately, then test a narrow commercial entry point. The founder supplies a vision and learns how it fits reality; the market does not supply a coherent vision through unbounded interviews. [S3]

Include category leaders where the offer could help them preserve their position. Abel argues that leaders can be motivated experimenters and influential references. This is a rationale for considering them, not proof that any particular brand is ready or that procurement will be quick. Research an account-specific reason to act. [S2]

Map the executive who could sponsor the advantage and the people responsible for using, implementing, and approving it. A friendly user is not automatically a committed champion. If progress repeatedly dies in procurement, investigate seniority and sponsorship before treating paperwork as the root cause. [S2]

**Decision rule:** Prefer an ambitious account with a plausible contained entry point over a famous logo with no relevant use case. Record the fact or hypothesis that makes the account suitable. Founder authority remains essential while new market feedback may require changing the offer. [S2] [S3]

## 3. Articulate alpha

Alpha means the buyer's distinctive advantage: access to information, data, expertise, or a way of working that opens a capability unavailable through their current approach. Describe present capability, future capability, and the mechanism connecting them. The product matters as the mechanism; the executive opportunity gives the purchase meaning. [S1] [S2]

Abel warns that unsupported superiority claims can make a buyer defend an incumbent. A fresh, relevant perspective creates curiosity and helps the champion spend internal credibility. Avoid generic claims of speed or low cost as the entire proposition. [S4]

**Decision rule:** An alpha statement must answer what this buyer could do differently and why the mechanism makes it plausible. When an incumbent already addresses the stated problem, acknowledge that function and explore the additional opportunity instead of arguing. If the mechanism cannot support the advantage, narrow the claim or return to discovery. [S2]

**Edge case:** The advantage may be a hypothesis before discovery. Label it accordingly. Do not imply exclusive data access, guaranteed outcomes, or competitive superiority without evidence. This evidence discipline operationalizes the honesty emphasized in the public episode notes. [S6]

## 4. Earn the conversation

Abel's public episode notes recommend three or four sentences: relevance to the role, an unexpected observation, and an invitation to explore. The message earns attention rather than explaining every capability. A useful observation should concern the recipient's work, not merely announce that a product exists. [S6]

Her later interview favors manual research and less crowded paths to relevant buyers over relying entirely on shared prospect databases. Interpret this as a preference for account understanding and differentiated access, not a blanket prohibition on research tools. Choose email, LinkedIn, phone, or a suitable introduction based on context. [S1] [S2] [S6]

**Decision rule:** If research supports only a hypothesis, phrase the insight as a question or possibility. Never convert uncertainty into a fabricated account fact. Prepare drafts within the user's requested scope; the package itself grants no authority to send outreach.

## 5. Test priority and demonstrate selectively

Investigate three signals Abel names: previous attempts to solve the issue, current measurement of its consequences, and a clear owner. Use them as evidence to investigate, not a mandatory three-item gate. A claimed priority with no attempts, tracking, owner, or investment needs deeper questioning. Compliments alone reveal little. [S3]

Ask focused questions tied to the hypothesis:

- What happened the last time this issue occurred, and what did you try?
- What consequences are tracked, by whom, and how are they reviewed?
- Who is accountable, and what resources or commitments already exist?
- Why would changing this now matter, and what would make you decline?

These are synthesized question forms. Abel explicitly warns against broad questions about whatever worries the buyer or hypothetical wish lists: answers vary too much to test a coherent thesis. Probe why an idea sounds interesting and allow rejection to redirect the hypothesis. [S3]

Use the first call for learning rather than a long product tour. For a later demo, replay the buyer's main concerns, verify whether they changed, invite the buyer to select a starting point, and otherwise show only one or two relevant capabilities. Abel warns that showing everything can make the buyer feel they are paying for unused functionality. [S3] [S6]

**Decision rule:** Seek a specific next meeting while together. Refusal or vague deferral is a warning; clarify fit and timing directly. Advance on substantive evidence and mutual action, reframe a contradicted hypothesis, nurture a genuine timing constraint, or stop an unsuitable pursuit. These four status labels are this package's convention. [S2] [S6]

## 6. Choose and bound the engagement

A design partnership involves learning and building with uncertainty. A proof of concept evaluates an existing product. Choose the engagement according to product maturity and the actual uncertainty. [S5]

Partners should contribute informed feedback, accept experimentation, and share a consistent customer profile and problem. Explain unfinished capabilities, seek early payment and a contract, and preserve the founder's control of product direction. A partner helps refine the offer; a large payment should not authorize an unrelated roadmap. [S5]

**Decision rule:** Record what will be learned or evaluated, each party's contribution, boundaries, and what would justify a regular commercial agreement. These fields operationalize Abel's distinction; they are not her published contract form. Reject unrelated customization when it prevents serving the intended market. Partner participation is an early signal, while conversion into sustained paying use supplies stronger validation. [S5]

## 7. Co-author the commercial frame

Abel discusses initial enterprise contracts around $75,000 to $150,000 and warns that a $10,000 annual entry can undermine later expansion. That range is contextual guidance from her 2025 interview, not a universal price floor or current market benchmark. Match the proposal to scope, credible value, delivery effort, and growth expectations. A high number cannot repair a weak opportunity. [S2]

Contain the initial scope rather than selling unlimited access at the first price. Discuss intended later access, value, and price early. A lower initial amount can be appropriate for a short engagement with an explicit, defensible ramp; unexplained increases after an artificially cheap annual contract are harder to justify. [S2]

Co-author with the champion. Explore scope, term, expertise, and implementation support as well as price. Abel treats enterprise agreements as creative relationship work. Repeated pressure to discount can indicate weak belief in the value; investigate that belief before conceding. [S1] [S2]

A service entry can suit an enterprise that understands purchasing expertise but has not learned how to procure the technology. Bound the service work and state the transition to product use. Do not let customization indefinitely replace the scalable destination. [S2]

**Decision rule:** Every proposal should identify who gets access, what is delivered, what remains uncertain, how payment relates to scope, and how expansion would be justified. Model hard savings separately from time savings and strategic upside. Do not portray a hypothesis as an approved financial case. [S2] [S8]

## 8. Help the enterprise buy

Educate procurement about the actual offer and why the business wants it. Procurement coordinates shared functions including Finance, IT, and Legal. Keep the champion involved, especially early, and jointly manage outstanding requests. Disappearing sponsorship is a warning that the deal may lack support. [S8]

Ask for IT review timing before forecasting a close. Where permissible, a contained strategy or design service may proceed while product review continues, but it is not a device for evading security or vendor requirements. Confirm the applicable requirements with the buyer. [S8]

Build the financial case with the buyer. Abel notes that canceled outside contracts and reduced third-party spending are easier for Finance to assess than broad efficiency claims. Procurement approval governs payment even when a business leader owns budget; avoid substantial delivery before the agreement is signed. [S8]

Her AI procurement article reports willingness to consider shorter commitments, concerns about vendor revenue concentration, and questions about usage, outcomes, and model ownership. Treat these as issues to ask about. Do not assume every enterprise uses a six-month term, a concentration threshold, or the same disclosure requirements. The source is explicitly dated and contextual. [S8]

Abel specifically warns against navigating nuanced enterprise contracts alone or using ChatGPT as a substitute for a lawyer. Prepare a business brief and issue list; send contract interpretation and negotiation of legal language to qualified counsel. [S8]

**Decision rule:** An unsolicited RFP with little collaboration, rushed evaluation, and low patience may mean the startup exists only to complete a vendor comparison. Clarify sponsorship and influence before investing heavily. Participation in every RFP is not forbidden, and lack of co-authorship alone does not prove bad faith. [S8]

## 9. Transfer judgment before scaling

Abel's founder-managed stage demonstrates that non-founders can generate and close opportunities with comparable success under founder management. More customer logos do not demonstrate that learning has transferred. Sales may become more complex beyond early adopters as contracts grow and conversion changes. [S7]

Her article gives rough revenue stages for businesses selling above $30,000 annual contract value: founder-led near $1 million, founder-managed near $1 million to $3 million, then leadership hired to scale. Use these as contextual mental models; evidence of repeatability and retention matters more than crossing a revenue line. [S7]

Hire for business understanding, executive credibility, vision communication, and creative deal construction. A small business seller or large-company title does not automatically provide those capabilities. The founder remains responsible for market vision and new-market learning even after adding leadership. [S2] [S7]

**Decision rule:** If every meaningful opportunity still depends on the founder creating and closing it, prescribe managed transfer of judgment before scaling headcount. Record what a seller can do independently and what still requires founder intervention. [S7]
