# Worked examples

These are invented illustrations. The people, companies, capabilities, and conversations are fictional. They demonstrate application of the public method, not Braun's own scripts, actual customer evidence, or predicted results. Method citations apply to the analysis, not to the invented business claims.

## Illustration 1: Software account review, cold call

### Scenario and brief

AccessCheck is an invented service that compares software account lists with employee departure records. Priya's public role description indicates that she oversees offboarding. Her tools and completeness of coverage are unknown. The possible exposure is a team-managed account remaining active after the central checklist is complete. No loss amount is established.

### Weak first draft

Caller: Hi Priya, Alex from AccessCheck. We help companies save money and improve security with AI. Would you like to eliminate wasted subscriptions? I would love to show you a demo. Can you do Tuesday?

### Diagnosis

The benefit question makes disagreement awkward, assumes waste, and jumps to a meeting before examining the process. Generic benefits obscure the concrete exposure. The demo request centers the seller's wish. [S2][S4][S6]

### Improved call

Caller: Hi Priya, Alex from AccessCheck. I know this call is unexpected. Is there time for one quick question?

Priya: Sure.

Caller: Your role description suggests you oversee offboarding. Have I got that right?

Priya: Yes.

Caller: A possible gap is a team-managed account staying active after the central checklist is complete. How does your team check for accounts outside that checklist?

[Pause.]

Priya: We cover central tools, but individual team subscriptions are harder to see.

[Wait two beats.]

Caller: Sounds like the central tools are covered, while team subscriptions are less visible.

[Let Priya elaborate.]

Priya: Right. What does AccessCheck do?

Caller: It compares account lists with departure records so your team can review anything still active. I promised to be brief. Would a short conversation later this week to look at that process be useful?

### Alternate response branches

- Adequate process: Priya says every tool is reconciled. Respond: That sounds covered. Thanks for explaining it.
- Gap without interest: Priya identifies a gap but says it is low priority. Respond: Understood. Thanks for the context. Do not treat the gap as a meeting commitment.
- Refusal: Priya says she does not want to discuss it. Respond: Understood. Thanks for your time. Stop.
- Wrong owner: Priya corrects the assumption. Respond: Thanks for correcting me. If she remains willing, ask who handles the process; otherwise close.

### Rules that changed the result

The improved question names a verifiable mechanism without asserting that it occurs. Priya can explain a complete process comfortably. Permission and ownership checks establish relevance; the pause and grounded label let her describe the situation. The capability explanation answers her question, and the optional follow-up preserves brevity. [S1][S3][S5][S9][S10]

The question would also work without the gap sentence if that framing proved unnecessary. Choosing the shorter version is consistent with Braun's emphasis on curiosity rather than a fixed script. [S7]

## Illustration 2: Failed payment recovery, cold email

### Scenario and brief

RecoverLoop is an invented service that helps subscription teams review payments that remain unsuccessful after automatic retries. Mateo works in membership operations, but responsibility for payment recovery is unverified. The seller knows neither the recovery process nor its performance. The possible exposure is a failed payment receiving no follow-up after retries end.

### Weak first draft

Subject: You're losing thousands every month

Hi Mateo,

Our revolutionary platform guarantees more revenue. If I could recover the money your retry system misses, would you be interested? We work with companies just like yours. Book a demo here and send me your payment failure rate.

### Diagnosis

The draft invents a loss and a guarantee, implies unsupported customer familiarity, and asks for several actions. Its benefit question collects agreement rather than revealing the workflow. [S2][S7][S8]

### Improved email

Subject: After payment retries end

Hi Mateo,

I'm Lena from RecoverLoop. I understand you work in membership operations, though I may have the wrong person for payment recovery.

When a payment still fails after automatic retries, how does your team decide whether it needs another follow-up?

Thanks,
Lena

### Why this version stops at a question

The question examines what happens after a defined event; it does not claim that Mateo's team ignores failed payments. He can describe a complete process, explain that retries are not used, or correct ownership. The email provides one reply point instead of embedding the call's listening instructions or pushing a meeting. This is the package's email adaptation of Braun's neutral-question principle. [S2][S3]

### Illustrative reply and follow-up

Mateo: We usually leave it to automated retries. What are you offering?

Lena: Thanks for explaining. RecoverLoop helps teams review the payments that remain unsuccessful after retries and decide what deserves follow-up. Would it be useful to compare that with your current approach in a short conversation?

This explanation answers the prospect's question and keeps the next step optional. The reply alone does not prove a costly gap. [S3][S5]

### Alternate response branches

- Adequate process: Mateo explains that every remaining failure is reviewed. Reply: Sounds like you already have that covered. Thanks for clarifying.
- Refusal: Mateo asks not to be contacted. End the exchange; do not send a diagnostic follow-up.
- Wrong owner: Mateo says finance handles it. Thank him. Ask for ownership clarification only if continued conversation is welcome.
- Uncertain process: Mateo is unsure. Ask whether clarifying the workflow would be useful; do not translate uncertainty into a claim of lost revenue.

### Rules that changed the result

The revised subject removes the unverified loss. The body removes the leading promise and false familiarity, distinguishes role context from ownership, and asks about one concrete process. The response notes accept satisfaction and refusal rather than routing every answer to a demo. [S2][S3][S7][S8]
