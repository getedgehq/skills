# Finished-output review

Mark each applicable item PASS or FAIL. Use N/A only for a channel-specific item and explain why it does not apply. A failed item requires a revision or an explicit unresolved limitation before delivery. This review format is the package's synthesis, not a checklist published by Braun.

## Pass/fail criteria

- [ ] **Intent:** The draft learns whether a relevant problem exists. It does not assume the prospect needs the offering or make a meeting the destination of every branch. [S3][S8]
- [ ] **Job relevance:** The job is concrete and ownership is verified or explicitly uncertain. A title alone is not treated as proof. [S1][S5][S11]
- [ ] **Evidence:** Prospect claims, customer familiarity, numbers, and capabilities have support. Hypotheses remain hypotheses. Braun's example amounts are not imported as prospect evidence. This is the package's evidence rule. [S4][S7][S8]
- [ ] **Specific exposure:** The possible blind spot names a mechanism and credible consequence. Generic savings or efficiency language does not substitute for it. A process-first opening is acceptable when no exposure is established. [S2][S3][S4]
- [ ] **Non-accusatory frame:** The message leaves room for the situation not to apply. Peer stories are factual and do not imply that the prospect is incompetent. [S6]
- [ ] **Neutral question:** A prospect with an effective current approach can answer comfortably. The question does not bundle an obvious benefit with agreement to a pitch. [S2][S3]
- [ ] **Useful menu:** If present, choices are plausible, concise, and correctable. They help the person answer without making one answer the only acceptable choice. [S5][S12]
- [ ] **Opening:** The call has a brief identity and one coherent opening, with permission or ownership checks where needed. Familiarity is truthful. [S1][S5][S7]
- [ ] **Answer space:** A call pause or clear email reply point follows the main question. The draft does not immediately answer its own question with a pitch. [S1][S2]
- [ ] **Listening:** Call notes allow a brief pause, grounded reflections, and elaboration. They do not prescribe an emotion or a predetermined admission. [S9][S11]
- [ ] **Conversational delivery:** Spoken stretches are brief and calm. About fifteen seconds of uninterrupted speech triggers an edit, not faster delivery. [S12]
- [ ] **Adequate-process branch:** The response accepts a working solution and allows a respectful close. [S8][S12]
- [ ] **Refusal branch:** An explicit refusal ends the exchange. Optional clarification is restricted to continued willingness. [S8][S12]
- [ ] **Interested branch:** Capability explanation relates to the prospect's answer. The next step is optional and honors the promised brevity. [S1][S5][S10]
- [ ] **Channel fit:** Email contains a readable message and reply point, not call stage directions. Call notes support interaction rather than a recital. This is the package's channel adaptation. [S2][S9]
- [ ] **Deliverable completeness:** The brief, draft, question, branches, neutrality rationale, and evidence limitations are included. See [../templates/output-template.md](../templates/output-template.md).

## Common failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Why it fails | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benefit disguised as a question | Agreement exposes the intended sales destination. | Ask how the job is currently handled or verified. [S2] |
| A problem asserted as fact | The prospect must defend their process before explaining it. | Mark the exposure as possible, or ask about the process first. [S3] |
| Generic improvement language | The offer sounds equivalent to an existing solution. | Identify the actual gap in visibility, coverage, or verification. [S4][S12] |
| Invented loss or peer story | Specificity becomes a misleading claim. | Remove unsupported amounts and familiarity; use a qualitative hypothesis. Package evidence rule, informed by [S7][S8]. |
| Several qualification questions | The opening feels like an interrogation. | Ask only the necessary ownership question and use a grounded observation elsewhere. [S11] |
| Menu that ranks the prospect | The supposedly neutral question favors the seller's option. | Offer realistic approaches and room for another answer, or remove the menu. [S3][S5][S12] |
| Long product monologue | The prospect has no meaningful turn. | Shorten the explanation and return to the process question or answer. [S12] |
| Reflection that assumes distress | It smuggles the desired problem into listening. | Reflect only what was said and keep interpretations tentative. [S9] |
| Gap automatically triggers a demo | A problem is confused with willingness to engage. | Clarify interest and offer a next step only when useful to them. [S3][S10] |
| Refusal gets a rebuttal | The seller's agenda overrides the prospect's choice. | Acknowledge and end. Do not add a last-chance question. [S8] |
| Product question is evaded | Detachment is mistaken for withholding information. | Explain the relevant capability briefly and truthfully. [S5] |
| Unexpected call keeps expanding | Initial interest is consumed by a prolonged call. | Honor the brief opening and offer a later conversation. [S10] |

## Final review note

Record: [PASS or REVISE], [failed criteria], [repairs made], and [remaining assumptions]. Rehearse call delivery between conversations rather than using prospects as a practice audience. [S12]
