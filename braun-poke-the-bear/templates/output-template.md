# Deliverable skeletons

Fill with the user's facts. The finished output is the message itself; keep the analysis (job, current approach, blind spot, consequence, neutrality test) in your head. The single-ask format is this package's adaptation; Braun's underlying guidance rejects leading benefit questions [S2].

## Cold email

**Subject:** [short, about their process, does not imply an incident you have not verified]

[Name],

[One line of truthful context: who you are or why them, only if it helps the question make sense.]

[One or two sentences: a specific blind spot in how this kind of team usually does the job today, framed as a possibility, with its plausible consequence.]

[One or two plain sentences: what the product does about it, the strongest proof from the input with its limits, and what it takes to start (effort, cost if given).]

[The one ask: the user's goal as an easy yes/no ("Worth trying with your team?"). If the user named no goal, a neutral question about how they handle it now.]

[Sign-off]


## Cold-call talk track

1. Identity in one line, then a permission or direct opener.
2. The observation: a situation similar teams run into, leaving room for it not to apply.
3. The neutral process question. Pause.
4. If they describe a gap: reflect it, ask one clarifying question, then offer a later conversation. If it works fine: thank them and close. If no: stop.
