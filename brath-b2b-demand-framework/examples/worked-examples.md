# Worked examples

These are invented illustrations, not Kraftvertising clients, reported campaign results or promises. All businesses, budgets, figures and review criteria below are hypothetical. Source-backed rules are cited; experiment designs are package guidance.

## Illustration 1: scarce searches, valuable existing demand

### Brief and evidence

LedgerBridge sells freight invoice auditing software to mid-sized logistics companies in the UK. Its product is sales-led, with assumed annual contract value of GBP 24,000 and a four-month sales cycle. Sales interviews indicate that some buyers know the category. A keyword tool estimates 15 to 30 relevant monthly searches in the supported market. Actual search-term and conversion data are unavailable. The client permits a proposed media allocation of GBP 3,000 per month but has not authorized launch.

### Weak first draft

Search volume is too low. Allocate all GBP 3,000 to LinkedIn lead forms targeting logistics managers. Choose the creative with the lowest cost per lead. Put SEO on hold until search volume reaches 1,000 per month.

### Improved result

**Diagnosis:** Provisional combined branch. The interviews support category awareness, but the search estimates still need validation. Scarce demand may be commercially useful; there is no justified 1,000-search threshold.

**Capture:** Propose up to GBP 600 per month for a narrowly scoped Google Search test of freight audit category terms in the UK. Inspect actual queries and buyer fit. Separate branded and tangential traffic. If relevant volume cannot absorb the cap, do not broaden indiscriminately to spend it.

**Recognition:** Propose up to GBP 1,800 per month for LinkedIn communication to the selected company segment and its operational and financial stakeholders. Explain how recurring invoice errors affect costs, show an invented product workflow using clearly labelled demo data, and carry the same proposition onto the landing page. Hold GBP 600 for adjustments after evidence review. These allocations are illustrative choices, not a framework ratio.

**SEO:** Prepare clear product and category pages while collecting paid-search evidence. Prioritize expensive organic work around useful searches rather than tool volume alone.

**Measurement:** Confirm that demo requests reach the CRM. Record relevant accounts, accepted meetings and opportunity progression. Use target-company reach, repeat exposure and sales recognition feedback to diagnose social performance. Review traffic quality after six weeks, with the marketing lead responsible; assess downstream results later in line with the four-month sales cycle. A six-week review cannot prove lifetime acquisition cost.

**Decision:** Continue relevant presence within the cap if traffic fits and economics remain plausible. Change targeting or pages if intended buyers misunderstand the offer. Stop irrelevant query groups. Do not scale from a single lead or kill all capture because a small sample has no wins yet.

### Rules that changed the decision

- Low volume and absent demand are different situations. [S1]
- Search captures current intent while paid social can build future familiarity. [S1][S2]
- Paid-search behavior helps prioritize SEO without delaying all foundations. [S4]
- Buying committees and attention matter beyond individual form fills. [S3]
- Commercial feedback matters more than nominal conversion counts. [S2][S6]

## Illustration 2: buyers know the problem, not the category

### Brief and evidence

ShiftSignal offers an emerging product that forecasts staffing gaps across hospital support services. It targets operations directors and workforce-planning teams in Germany. Its positioning is still changing. Five invented discovery interviews describe spreadsheet reconciliation as painful, but none of those interviewees knows a software category for this workflow. A keyword tool reports no volume for the startup's invented category label. Related staffing queries exist, though their commercial relevance is unknown. The company is willing to consider a EUR 5,000 learning budget.

### Weak first draft

Publish 40 SEO articles for the invented category and bid broadly on staffing keywords. Google will create demand because everyone searches there. Call pricing-page visits conversions and declare success when traffic rises.

### Improved result

**Diagnosis:** Provisional education-first branch for the interviewed segment. The interviews suggest a solution-awareness gap. The keyword-tool result alone does not prove that every possible query has no demand. Research ordinary buyer phrasing before ruling out capture entirely.

**Education:** Propose a bounded paid-social test aimed at the relevant operations and planning stakeholders. Assess whether LinkedIn's professional targeting can reach them usefully. Compare two explanations: the cost of reconciling staffing data manually, and the consequence of spotting gaps too late. Show the current workflow, the alternative and a clear product walkthrough. Define the intended learning outcome: buyers understand how the product addresses their existing problem.

**Capture and SEO:** Maintain a clear product page and explore actual category or solution searches if research uncovers them. Keep related problem queries in a separate small experiment. Do not commit to 40 articles while terminology and product positioning remain unstable.

**Budget and learning:** Propose a six-week test capped at EUR 5,000, with a named marketing owner. Reserve EUR 500 within that cap for query research or a narrow search test only if useful terms are found. Review delivery to the intended roles, message comprehension from interviews, walkthrough engagement and accepted sales conversations. The cap and duration are illustrative controls, not evidence that the test will be conclusive.

**Measurement:** Treat pricing visits as diagnostics. Verify actual demo submissions, CRM entry and sales acceptance. Check whether early engagement predicts later interest; do not optimize toward a proxy that stops predicting meaningful outcomes.

**Decision:** Continue the clearer explanation if intended buyers demonstrate understanding and commercially relevant conversations emerge. Change the message when the audience fits but cannot explain the value. Investigate product usefulness if informed buyers consistently reject the proposition. Increase category capture only when relevant query evidence supports it.

### Rules that changed the decision

- Paid social offers room to teach buyers before they know what category to search. [S1]
- Related problem searches are not automatically high-intent category demand. [S1]
- Uncertain product-market fit makes a large primary SEO commitment risky. [S1]
- Accurate professional targeting still needs creative that earns attention. [S3]
- Earlier conversion signals require checks against real downstream outcomes. [S6]

## Transfer test

If LedgerBridge had no category-aware buyers, its first allocation would need more education. If ShiftSignal uncovered a small valuable set of solution searches, it could add immediate capture without abandoning education. Route from evidence about each buyer segment, not the company's age or the platform's reputation.
