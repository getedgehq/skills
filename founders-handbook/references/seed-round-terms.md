# Seed round terms: pre vs post-money, SAFE vs priced, side letters, investor updates

Source: 1984 Ventures Founders Handbook, section "2. Raising Your Seed". Each part below
names its chapter and URL. This file is a derived summary: credit 1984 Ventures and the
named author, link the chapter, and do not present it as original. The handbook's
disclaimer applies: general education, not legal advice. Clause wording belongs to YC
or the drafting counsel; send the founder to the chapter and to YC's documents for
exact text instead of drafting a clause from memory.

## Pre-money vs post-money SAFE conversion

Chapter: https://1984.vc/docs/founders-handbook/pre-money-vs-post-money-conversion
(by Ramy Adeeb)

- Pre-money agreement: the investor's ownership depends on the investment, the
  pre-money valuation, and the size of the round and of every other round until
  conversion. Investors dilute each other as well as the founders.
- Post-money agreement: ownership = investment / post-money valuation, regardless of
  other rounds. Later SAFE holders dilute the founders but not earlier SAFE holders.
- With a single SAFE there is effectively no difference ($2M on $8M pre = $2M on $10M
  post = 20%).
- Chapter example with two $2M SAFEs on the same terms:
  - Pre-money basis ($8M pre each): effectively $4M on $8M pre / $12M post. Investors
    end at 16.66% each, founders at 33.33% each (66.66% together).
  - Post-money basis ($10M post each): each investor gets 20%, founders 30% each (60%
    together).
- Conclusion in the chapter: post-money gives clarity; pre-money is kinder to founders
  across multiple rounds; but pre-money SAFEs have virtually disappeared, and the chapter
  puts post-money SAFEs at over 95% of Silicon Valley financing.

## SAFE vs priced round

Chapter: https://1984.vc/docs/founders-handbook/safe-vs-priced-round

1984's stated position: it prefers SAFEs but thinks the instrument matters less than
the right investor and limited overall dilution. Some investors still need a priced
round because their mandate requires a board seat.

| Dimension | SAFE | Priced round |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Process | Simpler; no negotiation of founder vesting or option pool; no round, signatures collected one by one | Minimum and maximum round size; more legal work |
| Control | Investors are not shareholders: founders can sell the company, issue options, or sign other financings without investor consent | Investors are shareholders with consent rights |
| Cost | Free (YC documents, Clerky); use counsel for side letters | Chapter cites $5K to $25K at seed, often fixed-price for a first equity financing |
| Dilution across rounds | Post-money: two $2M SAFEs at $10M cap leave founders at 30% each | Two $2M priced rounds at $8M pre leave founders at 33.33% each |
| Option pool | Usually not mentioned | Usually required; can outweigh the SAFE difference |
| Founder departure | Freed shares accrue to remaining shareholders, not SAFE holders | Freed shares accrue to all shareholders including investors |

The chapter lists the factors founders cannot predict that decide the real dilution
difference: number of SAFE rounds, pool size demanded at seed vs Series A and grants in
between, founder departures, and a possible M&A. Its conclusion: "six of one, half a
dozen of the other"; what matters is the right investors and not selling too much early.

## SAFE side letters

Chapter: https://1984.vc/docs/founders-handbook/safe-side-letters (by Ramy Adeeb)

Common asks and the chapter's guidance:

- **Pro-rata rights**: normal for $500K+ seed checks. Use YC's standard pro-rata letter,
  limit it to the next financing only (not perpetual), and use the standard formula
  (shares from converting the investor's SAFEs / Company Capitalization), not a hard
  ownership target.
- **MFN**: extends better terms given to later SAFE or note holders. Prefer YC's
  standard MFN, which puts the notice mechanism in writing and gives the investor 10
  days to elect. If you may apply to an accelerator, carve out SAFEs issued to it. Even
  without an MFN, reprice earlier SAFEs if you later raise at a lower cap.
- **Major Investor rights**: usually granted to the largest 1 to 2 investors; the
  downside is more pro-rata holders at the Series B, often renegotiable.
- **Board observer seat**: an acceptable give if the founder is comfortable with the
  involvement it signals.
- **QSBS matters**: founder and investor interests align; a C-corp with gross assets
  under the threshold at issuance, in a qualified business. Some buy-backs can
  disqualify the company (see the QSBS chapter for current thresholds).
- **Information and management rights**: do not sign up for reporting you cannot
  deliver (monthly financials, audited statements without an auditor).
- **Terms to avoid**: super pro-rata, advisory shares on the side, liquidation
  preferences above 1x, guaranteed returns.
- Be wary of a modified SAFE: non-standard SAFEs invite legal back-and-forth, changes to
  Company Capitalization can hurt founders, and later investors will ask for the same
  modified form.

## Monthly investor updates

Chapter: https://1984.vc/docs/founders-handbook/investor-updates

Purpose: turn every investor into an accurate advocate and get help early. Send one
update a month to everyone on the cap table, not only the lead. Plain email is fine;
keep the structure identical every month so the trend is readable.

Structure, in this order:

1. **TL;DR**: one or two sentences, biggest win and biggest problem.
2. **Key metrics**: revenue or usage growth, burn, cash on hand, runway in months,
   always the same set with month-over-month percentages.
3. **Asks**: right after metrics, specific (a named company to be introduced to, a job
   blurb to forward). "Any help appreciated" gets nothing.
4. **Highlights**: three to five bullets.
5. **Lowlights**: one to three bullets on what broke and the plan. The chapter says this
   section builds more credibility than the highlights.

Optional tools named in the chapter: Visible, Cabal, Carta, Pulley, Papermark, DocSend.
