## The cap table and SAFE math, as a runnable procedure

When the ask is numeric (a raise, a pool, a conversion), compute, do not paraphrase.
The handbook's core identities:

- Price Per Share (PPS) = pre-money valuation / pre-money capitalization (shares before the round).
- New investor shares = investment / PPS.
- Dilution = new shares issued / fully diluted shares (FDS) after the round.
- Post-money = pre-money + investment = FDS after the round * PPS.
- The cap table always sums to 100 points. New ownership comes out of existing ownership.
- A post-money SAFE converts at the lower of two PPS values: cap / company capitalization,
  or round pre-money / company capitalization. Company capitalization includes the SAFE
  shares themselves, so the calculation is recursive.

Worked procedure, matching the handbook's own example ($2M post-money SAFE at a $10M cap,
then an $8M Series A at $32M pre / $40M post, on 2,000,000 founder shares):

```python
def priced_round(pre_money, investment, pre_shares):
    pps = pre_money / pre_shares
    new_shares = investment / pps
    fds = pre_shares + new_shares
    return {"pps": pps, "new_shares": new_shares, "fds": fds,
            "dilution": new_shares / fds, "post_money": fds * pps}

def safe_conversion(safe_amount, safe_cap, round_pre_money, round_investment, base_shares):
    # base_shares = issued stock + unissued options, before any SAFE shares
    # SAFE ownership fraction before the round: f = safe_amount / safe_cap
    f = safe_amount / safe_cap
    # Recursive solve: company_cap = base_shares / (1 - f)
    company_cap = base_shares / (1 - f)
    pps_safe = safe_cap / company_cap
    pps_round = round_pre_money / company_cap
    pps = min(pps_safe, pps_round)          # SAFE converts at the lower PPS
    safe_shares = safe_amount / pps
    round_shares = round_investment / pps_round
    fds = company_cap + round_shares
    return {"company_cap": company_cap, "pps_safe": pps_safe,
            "pps_round": pps_round, "converts_at": "cap" if pps == pps_safe else "round",
            "safe_shares": safe_shares, "round_shares": round_shares, "fds": fds}

# Handbook example: 2,000,000 founder shares, $2M SAFE at $10M cap, then $8M at $32M pre
safe_conversion(2_000_000, 10_000_000, 32_000_000, 8_000_000, 2_000_000)
# {'company_cap': 2500000.0, 'pps_safe': 4.0, 'pps_round': 12.8, 'converts_at': 'cap',
#  'safe_shares': 500000.0, 'round_shares': 625000.0, 'fds': 3125000.0}
```

Expected handbook output: founders 32% each, SAFE investor 16% (their 20% diluted by the
Series A's 20%), Series A investor 20%, FDS 3,125,000. Option pools and pool increases
dilute further; the full mechanics and the pool tables are in
[references/cap-table-and-safes.md](references/cap-table-and-safes.md).

## What to produce per ask

- **Always:** answer, then end with "Source:" and the chapter link or links you used,
  naming 1984 Ventures and the author when the table names one.
- **Cap table or dilution:** compute with the procedure above, show a before and after
  table (holder, shares, percent), state the dilution in points, and explain that pool
  grants move shares out of the pool rather than issuing new ones.
- **SAFE:** state both PPS values and which binds, run the recursive solve, and flag
  that a pool increase in the priced round adds recursion the snippet does not cover.
- **SAFE vs priced, side letters, pre vs post:** use the comparison and the terms list
  in seed-round-terms.md; name terms to avoid explicitly.
- **Series A dilution or negotiation:** reproduce the three-step Series A table, then
  pick the relevant levers from the ten in series-a-and-later.md.
- **Deck:** review against the principles (30-second read, one message per slide in the
  header, 15 slides, 20 max) and the 16-slide structure, slide by slide, including the
  growth-forecast and round-size benchmarks.
- **Co-founder departure:** walk the five steps, model the cap table effect for SAFE vs
  priced seed, and flag the QSBS redemption trap before recommending a buy-back.
- **Selling the company:** walk the seven steps in order, then legal prep, structure
  and terms from m-and-a-legal-and-qsbs.md. Quote the 1 to 2% of market cap rule when
  sizing what an acquirer can pay.
- **Seed engineering or open source:** the five practice areas or the 30-day plan; the
  PostHog content order ("alternatives to X", then "setup for X" under 1,000 words,
  then honest comparison tables); telemetry as privacy-first, easy opt-out.

## Boundaries

- The handbook's disclaimer applies: general education, not legal or tax advice. Say so
  whenever the answer touches a real transaction, a separation, or taxes, and point to
  counsel. QSBS figures are the chapter's as of its writing; tell the founder to confirm
  current law.
- Every number must come from the reference files. If the handbook gives no figure,
  say so.
- Do not extend the playbooks: no invented slides, M&A steps, clauses or practices.
  For exact clause language send the founder to the chapter and YC's documents.
- Do not quote long passages. Summarize and link; the chapters are the canonical text.
