# Positioning Method in Depth

## Reading map and attribution

Follow sections 1 through 7 from evidence to a reviewed homepage. Section 8 consolidates expert warnings and conditional cases. Source citations identify public support for the method; they do not establish facts about the user's company. Evidence registers, explicit assumption labels, review gates, and handoff fields are practical adaptations for agent work, rather than claims that Pierri prescribes those exact artifacts.

The core principle is to establish a place in the buyer's mind through a familiar reference point and meaningful difference. A homepage expresses a choice about whom to serve and how the product helps. When that choice is unresolved, a broad slogan merely hides it. [S2] [S4] [S9]

## 1. Evidence before expression

Start with the product, customer, market, and current sales motion. FletchPMM's public sprint uses an intake, website, demonstration, sales recordings, and competitor research to develop alternatives before writing the page. Its client team supplies customer expertise; the service does not promise to conduct customer interviews itself. Reuse available customer material or identify what the team needs to validate. Do not imply interviews happened because the method would benefit from them. [S1] [S2]

Capture:

- What users can actually do today, including limits, prerequisites, and unavailable capabilities.
- Who notices the problem, uses the product, advocates internally, and approves spending.
- The task's trigger, current steps, tools, failure points, and consequences.
- Which alternatives appear in actual deals and why customers consider changing.
- Acquisition channels, sales resources, adoption patterns, and evidence of customer fit.

As an agent adaptation, tag each item as observed, interpreted, or assumed and attach an evidence identifier. Competitor messaging establishes what competitors claim, not proof of their product performance. A feature demo establishes capability, not customer demand. Keep those distinctions visible.

**Decision rule:** Missing evidence permits a provisional recommendation, not invented certainty. If the missing input would change the champion, job, comparator, or category, identify it as a strategic uncertainty. Missing testimonial permissions or screenshots are proof gaps that can be resolved after a coherent draft exists.

## 2. Champion and segment

Choose the person likely to arrive on the page and recognize the problem. The champion is close enough to the work to understand the product's contribution and advocate for adoption. A budget approver may be a different person. The public guidance cautions against writing exclusively for executives just because executive approval is needed later. [S6] [S8]

Compare candidate segments using the actual activity, department or persona, and company context. Check whether the team can reach the segment and whether the product meaningfully fits its work. Current revenue share is useful evidence, but should be examined alongside retention, sales friction, referrals, competitive pressure, and product fit. A large historical cohort alone need not be the next target. [S8] [S9]

**Decision rule:** Name one primary champion and the task they own. Explain any firmographic boundary through a difference in workflow, fit, or reach. A list containing nearly every industry and company size does not create an actionable priority. [S5] [S9]

**Edge case:** A founder or executive can be the champion in a small company or in a proven executive-led motion. The warning concerns assumptions about reach and task ownership, not a ban on senior buyers. Check the actual deal origin rather than inferring it from the final signature. [S6]

## 3. Market state and category route

Market state describes this audience's relationship to the solution. It is not a label for the company or a universal maturity score. Start with behavior, then layer company attributes onto the chosen group. [S5]

| State | Evidence to look for | Homepage argument | Wrong argument |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | Wants an outcome but is not doing the associated activity because of barriers | Show a feasible way to start and address the barrier | Assume an established workflow or competitor |
| M2 | Performs the activity through manual work or another inferior method, without this type of solution | Show how the product improves the existing workflow | Lead with vendor differences in an unfamiliar category |
| M3 | Uses this type of product or has evaluated it deeply | Explain why this vendor is preferable for this segment | Explain that the basic activity is possible for the first time |

Prioritize one state for the main page. Several states can be addressed through separate campaigns and pages if the resources exist. Blending them often produces generic messaging that no group recognizes. The expert's budget prioritization advice is a strategic illustration, not permission for an agent to allocate funds. [S5]

Category route is a related, separate choice. [S3]

| Route | Why choose it | Required argument and principal risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mature category | Buyers already understand the category and spend money in it | Win a shortlist through credible differentiation or underserved-segment fit; leaders have distribution and resources |
| Immature category | A category exists, but the priority buyers do not understand or use it | Educate buyers about adoption and replace their old way; education consumes time and category reputation can help or harm |
| New category | Existing labels would misrepresent the product more than they help explain it | Establish a real job and distinct tool; adoption takes sustained education and incumbents can copy success |

An established category can be unfamiliar to a particular audience. M2 therefore does not automatically mean the category is new, and M1 is not a synonym for category creation. Assess both dimensions separately. Enterprise buyers initially educated about a category may later require a vendor comparison in procurement. Prepare that secondary argument without forcing it into the hero. [S3] [S5]

The category-strategy article suggests asking a language model to name vendors as a quick existence check. Treat this as discovery, not verification: inspect buyer vocabulary and real vendors before adopting the label. That verification rule is this package's agent adaptation. [S3]

## 4. Workflow and value map

Describe activities rather than starting with distant business outcomes. List what the champion does before the product enters the workflow. Mark steps the product does not address, then detail the failures in steps it does improve. Do not imply end-to-end coverage by silently dropping unsupported steps. [S8] [S9]

Keep three layers distinct:

- **Feature:** The product element that enables an action, such as an assignment rule.
- **Capability:** What the user can do with that element, such as route a flagged inspection to the responsible supervisor.
- **Benefit:** The direct improvement to the current situation, such as eliminating the manual task of reassigning that issue.

The argument runs from a specific old limitation through the enabling feature and user action to a credible benefit. Benefits remain useful; the warning is against abstract outcomes without an understandable mechanism. Product capabilities make the promised change believable. [S8] [S9]

For prioritization, Pierri describes scoring problem severity and the strength or interest of the corresponding product features separately from 1 to 5, then adding them to identify strong intersections. [S9]

Use these practical scoring anchors, an adaptation rather than a published rubric:

- Severity 1: minor irritation; 3: recurring rework; 5: consequential blockage or failure.
- Product strength 1: little improvement; 3: useful improvement; 5: unusually strong or distinctive improvement for this task.

The sum ranges from 2 to 10 with both scales starting at 1. Record the reason for each score and any missing evidence. Do not let a total hide a weak mechanism: a severe problem with a poor solution remains a poor headline promise. Similar totals can be resolved through evidence quality, segment importance, and credible differentiation. This is prioritization for discussion, not a predictive conversion model.

**M3 rule:** Map the incumbent's actual workflow limitations rather than assuming the buyer uses spreadsheets. When features are broadly comparable, investigate segment-specific fit or a stronger implementation of the capability instead of inventing exclusivity. [S9]

## 5. Positioning bets and selection

Develop meaningful alternatives before choosing a headline. The public sprint offers four to six viable strategies, ranked by risk and accompanied by product and go-to-market implications. Several wordings of the same position are copy alternatives, not strategic bets. [S1]

For each option record champion, activity, market state, category route, familiar reference point, current alternative, product difference, direct value, evidence, risks, and a sample hero. Risk includes reach, competitive strength, education burden, product scope, and resource requirements. Avoid a universal rule that mature categories are always safer: strong incumbents may make that route demanding. [S3]

Select a primary recommendation with a rationale and explicit de-emphasis of other segments or jobs. Keep a record of the decision owner and whether the choice is accepted or provisional. The official sprint separates option presentation, the client's internal choice, strategy refinement, and copy review. An agent can recommend a bet; it should not fabricate stakeholder consensus. [S1]

Document the selected position in a compact internal record covering audience, product identity, problem, alternative, differentiation, and value. The sprint describes a six-slide deck; this package's text template organizes equivalent decisions without pretending to reproduce its proprietary deck. [S1]

**Category creation gate:** Name the job and owner, show the genuinely different tool, and check the scope it can handle. A new category requires more than a new label for existing products. If no shared job exists, either create and teach that job through a substantial market effort, present the products as a suite, or lead with the product buyers already recognize. For a homepage assignment, record the first route as a strategic burden rather than silently expanding into a category-building campaign. [S7]

## 6. Hero and homepage argument

Use one of the two public reference-point patterns as a starting point:

- A familiar category plus the relevant difference from other vendors.
- An exact job plus how the product changes the present way of doing it.

The headline should let the visitor place the offer immediately. The supporting line can explain the champion, scope, or mechanism without forcing every detail into the H1. Pierri treats these as strong starting patterns, acknowledges rhetorical simplification, and distinguishes clarity from the credibility or persuasiveness of the difference. A clear claim can still be untrue or unconvincing. [S4]

For multiple products, choose a lead product, a shared category or job, or an explicit suite presentation. A shared job must match the real offer; combining several products does not prove there is one buyer responsibility spanning them. [S4] [S7]

Build the core sequence from hero to current problem, solution introduction, and value propositions. Add proof and secondary paths when they strengthen that argument. This sequence comes from the public summary; it is not a requirement to pad every page with an artificial problem section. For category-aware buyers, the problem can be the incumbent's constraint. [S8] [S9]

Each value section should answer what the user can now do, what makes that possible, and what limitation is removed. Include a relevant product view or demonstrable example when available. Avoid an exhaustive feature catalog and keep unrelated personas off the main argument. Choose an action suited to the sales motion; this package's CTA guidance is an operational adaptation. [S8] [S9]

## 7. Review and iteration

Separate strategic correctness, comprehension, and copy style. First verify the position and workflow map. Then ask an unfamiliar reader what the product is, whom it serves, what it replaces, and why the difference matters. Ask a relevant champion to check the workflow if access is available. Record these as pending checks if nobody was consulted; an agent's desk review is not a customer test.

Use the checklist to trace every public claim to product evidence. Method citations validate reasoning patterns, not customer results. When objections reveal a wrong comparator or unsupported scope, revisit the strategic decision and update all affected copy. When the issue is wording alone, retain the selected bet. This follows the sprint's separation of strategy and copy stages. [S1]

Finish with review status, assumptions, proof gaps, and specific validation questions. Greater clarity may repel unsuitable visitors and lower aggregate conversions while reducing mismatched expectations. Pierri explicitly limits the promise to clarity, because revenue, conversion, and retention depend on the wider acquisition and product system. Do not guarantee a commercial lift from a rewritten page. [S9]

## 8. Explicit warnings and conditional cases

| Expert warning, paraphrased | Practical decision |
| --- | --- |
| Firmographic matches do not prove a need for the product. [S5] | Require evidence of the activity or blocked desire before choosing a segment. |
| A message that fits one market state can fail with another. [S5] | Match the old way and starting argument to the primary audience. |
| Curiosity, jargon, vague automation, and lofty revenue promises can conceal product identity. [S4] | Replace the abstract anchor with a recognizable category or owned job. |
| A sprawling platform can require executive access that the company cannot repeatably obtain. [S6] | Inspect real deal origins and consider a reachable departmental entry point. |
| Inventing a category name does not create its underlying job. [S7] | Verify job ownership, product coverage, and the education commitment. |
| First arrival in a category does not ensure eventual leadership. [S3] | Include incumbent response and distribution risk in the bet. |
| Copying a famous brand's broad story can fail when buyers do not know the startup's product. [S9] | Explain the offer before relying on a brand narrative. |
| Outcomes without features and capabilities undermine believability. [S8] [S9] | Show the causal mechanism, then state the direct benefit. |
| A homepage cannot serve as every segment's pitch, full feature catalog, and investor deck at once. [S9] | Choose its primary acquisition audience and send secondary needs to dedicated assets. |
| Isolating software as the cause of revenue growth overstates a complex system. [S9] | Use direct product evidence and carefully scoped customer outcomes. |

**PLG plus enterprise:** The interview suggests prioritizing the PLG audience on the homepage when it drives the main acquisition flow and enterprise prospects have tailored sales material. Apply this to the actual traffic and sales motion rather than as an unconditional rule. [S9]

**Horizontal products:** One underlying technical problem can appear as different surface workflows across industries. Broad market potential does not prove a small team can execute many industry-specific sales motions. A focused entry segment can change later as evidence and resources improve. [S9]

**Pre-product-market-fit:** Recommend a testable bet, disclose weak evidence, and name a falsifying signal. Narrowing focus helps learning; it does not establish product-market fit by assertion. [S9]

**Technical platforms:** The platform warning concerns the marketing claim of a large multi-department solution. A developer platform on which others build is a different meaning. If that is the product, explain the developer's job and real capabilities instead of treating the word itself as prohibited. [S6]
