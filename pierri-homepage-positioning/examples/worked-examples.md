# Worked Examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. Company names, research inputs, features, scores, and page copy are fictional. Citations support the rules being demonstrated, not the invented product facts or expected performance. Neither example claims measured conversion improvement.

## Illustration 1: Replace paper inspections with a visible workflow

### Brief and weak first draft

Imagine FieldLedger, software for facilities teams managing inspections across several buildings. In the scenario, the supplied demo shows scheduled mobile checklists, a dashboard of incomplete inspections, and assignment of flagged issues. The product does not manage spare parts, purchasing, or all maintenance work.

The first draft reads:

> H1: Unlock the future of operational excellence.
>
> Supporting line: An intelligent platform that empowers every team to maximize productivity and transform performance.
>
> Value sections: Work smarter. Improve collaboration. Accelerate growth.
>
> CTA: Discover more.

This draft offers no recognizable product or owned task. Its platform claim overstates scope, and its benefits contain no mechanism. [S4] [S6] [S8]

### Evidence and audience decision

The invented input set contains:

- E1: A demo showing recurring checklist scheduling, mobile submissions, and issue assignment.
- E2: Notes from facilities managers describing paper forms, spreadsheet logs, and calls to chase missing inspections.
- E3: Deal notes showing managers initiate evaluation while an operations director approves purchasing.

Choose the facilities manager as champion. Technicians are users; the director is the approver. The priority buyers already conduct inspections through paper and spreadsheets, so the segment is M2. Their need is a better way to complete existing work, not an explanation of why inspections matter. [S5] [S8]

### Workflow map and prioritization

Scores below are illustrative discussion judgments using the package's 1 to 5 anchors.

| Activity and old limitation | Feature and capability | Direct benefit | Severity | Strength | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare and distribute recurring paper forms | Recurring schedules assign the next mobile checklist | Avoid preparing each recurring form manually | 3 | 4 | 7 |
| Reconcile paper forms with the spreadsheet | Submission dashboard shows completed and missing inspections by site | Identify missing submissions without reconciling paper stacks | 5 | 4 | 9 |
| Call around to assign a flagged issue | Issue assignment sends the item to its responsible supervisor | Avoid manual reassignment calls | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| Order replacement components | Unsupported | No claim | Not scored | Not scored | Not scored |

The higher priorities address specific failures with visible capabilities. The unsupported procurement step stays outside the promise. [S8] [S9]

### Strategic options

| Bet | Sample anchor | Risk and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full facilities platform | Manage every aspect of your buildings | High scope risk: the product lacks broad maintenance and purchasing coverage |
| Mobile checklist app for any team | Digital checklists for any recurring task | Moderate focus risk: broad users require different proof and acquisition motions |
| Inspection software for facilities managers | Replace paper inspections across your facilities | Best-supported recommendation: aligns with the scenario's workflow, product, and reachable champion |

Three options suffice for this small illustrative brief; a full assignment should seek four to six genuinely viable alternatives when inputs support them. This recommendation favors fit rather than declaring one category route inherently safe. [S1] [S3]

Use an existing inspection-software category, but explain it through the paper workflow because these buyers have not adopted that type of solution. M2 describes the buyers, not a claim that the category was newly invented. [S3] [S5]

### Improved result

**H1:** Inspection software that replaces paper checklists across your facilities.

**Supporting line:** Schedule mobile inspections, see which sites have submitted their forms, and assign flagged issues to the responsible supervisor.

**Primary CTA:** See the inspection workflow.

**Problem section:** Paper forms make missing inspections hard to spot. When checklists come back from several buildings, you have to compare them with a spreadsheet before you know which teams need follow-up.

**Solution introduction:** FieldLedger keeps scheduled checklists and submitted inspections in one manager view while technicians complete forms on mobile.

**Value section 1:** Find missing inspections without sorting paper forms. The submission dashboard shows completed and outstanding checklists by site.

**Value section 2:** Give flagged issues a clear owner. Assign an issue from its inspection record so the supervisor receives the item without a round of reassignment calls.

**Value section 3:** Keep recurring checklists ready for the next inspection. Schedule repeated assignments instead of preparing a new paper form each time.

**Proof placement:** Show the actual completion dashboard beside section 1 and an assignment walkthrough beside section 2. Customer outcome proof remains a gap in this illustration; no percentage or testimonial is invented.

**Secondary path:** Link technicians to a mobile walkthrough below the manager's primary argument.

### Rules that made the difference

The familiar category establishes what is sold; the paper comparator matches M2. A reachable champion replaces the vague all-team audience. Capability mapping converts broad productivity language into explainable tasks. Product limits prevent the platform claim. Prioritization gives the most consequential workflow failure the first value section. [S4] [S5] [S6] [S8] [S9]

The recommendation is provisional until managers confirm the terminology and importance of missing submissions. The desk review can assess clarity and internal consistency; it cannot claim customer validation.

## Illustration 2: Compete with an incumbent without inventing a platform

### Brief and weak first draft

Imagine TraceHarbor, a website analytics product for privacy-conscious marketing teams. In the scenario, it offers campaign reporting without cookies and excludes personally identifying visitor fields from reports. Existing prospects use a conventional analytics vendor. A newly released survey add-on lacks shared adoption evidence and does not cover every customer research activity.

The first draft reads:

> H1: Your autonomous customer intelligence universe.
>
> Supporting line: Unify analytics, feedback, and every growth signal to increase revenue across the enterprise.
>
> Value sections: Finally understand your traffic. Transform customer experiences. Build your growth engine.
>
> CTA: Unlock intelligence.

The proposed category hides the recognizable analytics offer, bundles distinct jobs without evidence of one owner, and speaks as if buyers had never measured traffic. [S4] [S5] [S7]

### Evidence and audience decision

The invented inputs are:

- E1: A demo showing campaign reports and the absence of cookies and identifying visitor fields.
- E2: Evaluation notes showing marketing managers already use analytics and ask about a privacy-focused replacement.
- E3: Product documentation confirming the limited survey add-on and absence of revenue-attribution functionality.

Choose the marketing manager comparing analytics vendors. This is M3. The current alternative is another analytics product, so a manual-work argument would mismatch the buyer. Website analytics is a mature category in this scenario; the bet targets a segment with a particular privacy requirement. [S3] [S5]

### Strategic options and value map

| Bet | Risk | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enterprise intelligence platform | Unproven shared job, overstated scope, difficult executive reach | Reject for this brief |
| All-in-one analytics and surveys suite | Both products are describable, but shared purchase demand is weak | Keep as a later route if adoption supports it |
| Privacy-focused website analytics | Clear category and observed segment need; switching effort remains a risk | Recommend as the primary position |

The category is familiar enough to use directly. Lead with analytics and give surveys a secondary product link instead of manufacturing a new umbrella category. [S4] [S7]

| Buyer constraint | Product mechanism | Capability and direct value | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wants campaign reports without visitor cookies | Cookie-free event collection and campaign report | Review campaign traffic without collecting cookies | Severity 5 + strength 4 = 9 |
| Does not want identifying visitor data in marketing reports | Report fields exclude personally identifying information | Share campaign summaries without those visitor fields | Severity 4 + strength 4 = 8 |
| Needs detailed revenue attribution | Not provided | No value proposition | Exclude |

This establishes differentiation relevant to the segment. It does not claim that every competing vendor lacks the same functionality. The difference must be checked against the alternatives appearing in actual deals. [S9]

### Improved result

**H1:** Website analytics for campaign reporting without cookies.

**Supporting line:** TraceHarbor helps privacy-conscious marketing teams compare campaign traffic while keeping personally identifying visitor fields out of their reports.

**Primary CTA:** See a campaign report.

**Problem section:** You already measure website traffic. Your next analytics tool needs to preserve campaign reporting while fitting your team's approach to visitor data.

**Solution introduction:** TraceHarbor reports campaign activity through cookie-free collection and reports designed without identifying visitor fields.

**Value section 1:** Keep campaign reporting without visitor cookies. Compare campaign traffic in the reporting view using cookie-free event collection.

**Value section 2:** Share reports without identifying visitor fields. Give colleagues a campaign summary containing the reporting fields your team needs.

**Proof placement:** Show a real report and link the collection documentation. Provide a factual comparison against the buyer's named incumbent after checking its current documentation. Include migration guidance only after the supported process is confirmed.

**Secondary path:** Present the survey add-on as a separate product below the primary analytics story.

### Rules that made the difference

The hero supplies a category and a concrete product distinction. M3 changes the page from teaching basic traffic measurement to explaining a reason to switch. Leading with a known product avoids the unsupported umbrella job. Mechanism-based language avoids an invented revenue effect, and narrower claims avoid equating cookie-free collection with guaranteed legal compliance. [S4] [S5] [S7] [S9]

The remaining strategic risk is whether the segment considers the privacy distinction worth switching for. Clear copy alone cannot settle that question. Validate buyer importance and migration concerns before treating this provisional position as accepted.
