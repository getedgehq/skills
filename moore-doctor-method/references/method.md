# The Doctor Method in depth

Based on Richard Moore's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Richard Moore.

## Scope and provenance

Moore's public sales analogy asks the seller to establish expertise, understand the buyer's problem, recommend a suitable course, and check whether it works. The buyer should receive useful expert guidance rather than be left to choose from unexplained services. His LinkedIn version combines helpful content with a meaningful next action. [S1][S2]

**Published guidance** below is paraphrased from the sources. **Agent adaptations** convert it into operational rules for drafting and review; they are not claims that Moore published these exact rules. The framework concerns commercial problems. A diagnosis here is a reasoned sales assessment, not a clinical judgment.

Read the numbered section matching the current task. The warnings table and edge cases apply when reviewing any output. Source identifiers resolve in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Select the mode

**Published guidance:** Moore connects content, personal conversation, and a call for substantial service purchases. He favors warming a relationship before an offer and moving beyond messages when an engaged exchange needs more depth. Lower-priced offers can use a more automated purchase path. [S5][S8]

**Agent adaptation:** Choose the smallest useful deliverable at the buyer's current stage:

| Mode | Purpose | Evidence needed | Appropriate next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public content | Help a buyer recognize a problem and a useful path | Audience knowledge and support for general claims | Reply, relevant resource, or consultation invitation |
| First outreach | Open a relevant conversation | A real shared context or observed signal | Answer one contextual question |
| Discovery plan | Learn the situation and test causes | Brief, offer boundaries, known buyer facts | Confirm understanding or collect missing evidence |
| Recommendation | Guide an informed decision | Confirmed buyer account and supported fit | Review scope, approve a diagnostic step, or decide |
| Objection response | Understand and address a concern | The buyer's actual concern | Clarification, a revised plan, or respectful closure |
| Follow-up | Check a decision or the work's results | Prior agreement and present status | Agreed check-in or adjustment |

Do not force every asset through every mode. If a warm buyer explicitly asks about an offer, answer directly and investigate only the material unknowns. Connection-building is not a reason to evade a legitimate buying question.

## 2. Understand the buyer

**Published guidance:** Content aimed at industry peers can generate praise without reaching buyers. Moore asks sellers to understand the buyer's needs, concerns, desired results, and language. Seller revenue boasts can impress the wrong audience while failing to establish relevance for a premium buyer. [S3]

Outcomes explain why an offer matters. Product specifications and service components become persuasive when connected to changes in the buyer's work or life. Moore encourages listening to the desired outcome and illustrating the before-and-after through relevant experience. [S6]

**Agent adaptation:** Build this chain before writing:

`Observable symptom -> possible cause -> desired outcome -> suitable intervention -> evidence of progress`

Keep each link explicit. A buyer who says staff spend evenings correcting invoices has named a symptom. Poor data entry, unclear approvals, and an unsuitable tool are competing causes. Selling a new tool before distinguishing them skips diagnosis.

Translate features in two stages: what the feature changes operationally, then why that change matters to this buyer. A shared approval queue might reduce unresolved handoffs; the buyer may value predictable close dates rather than merely having a new dashboard. Do not infer personal aspirations the buyer has not expressed.

Label inputs as **confirmed**, **assumed**, or **unknown**. In a public post, present a plausible cause as a possibility, not as knowledge about every reader. If a missing fact changes the recommendation, ask for it or make the output conditional. If it affects only minor wording, use a visible working assumption.

## 3. Establish credible authority

**Published guidance:** Moore recommends demonstrating expertise through useful content, cases, testimonials, industry understanding, and knowledge of the buyer's specific challenges. He also emphasizes listening, making the buyer feel understood, and giving a clear roadmap as foundations for trust. [S1][S9]

**Agent adaptation:** Choose evidence in this order when available:

1. An approved, relevant case with its actual context and limitations.
2. Verifiable experience or a concrete example from the seller's work.
3. A transparent explanation of the mechanism and how it will be tested.

Relevance matters more than a large but unrelated achievement. If the seller has no case evidence, do not substitute a fictional success story. An honest diagnostic approach can establish competence without pretending the outcome is proven. Distinguish a demonstrated result from a target and an estimate. Do not transfer an earlier client's outcome into a guarantee for this buyer.

Moore's posts contain promotional success claims and broad statements about buying behavior. Use their practical lessons without presenting them as independently validated research. This package does not adopt the unsupported purchase percentage in [S6] or treat the broad price claims in [S9] as universal facts.

## 4. Create content and open conversations

**Published guidance:** Moore warns that purely educational content can leave readers without a route to becoming clients, while constant pitching can damage trust. He recommends recognizing audience problems, providing useful help, and guiding readers toward a relevant action. [S2]

For LinkedIn conversations, he advises engaging with what someone actually shared, approaching quiet engagement thoughtfully, and avoiding an offer as the opening of a new relationship. Once chat has momentum, he suggests an easy, short call rather than endless messages. [S4][S5]

**Agent adaptation:** For a post, use four moves:

1. Name a symptom the intended buyer recognizes.
2. Explain one useful distinction or a plausible cause.
3. Offer a practical way to inspect the issue, with proof if available.
4. Invite one proportionate next action.

The post should still help a reader who declines the invitation. Avoid manufacturing a problem to justify the offer. A conversion-focused post needs a clear route forward; a user-requested educational post need not be turned into a pitch.

For outreach, use an actual contextual observation and a question that can be answered without committing to a call. A view or like is a weak signal of possible relevance, not proof of need, intent, or permission for repeated follow-up. If the buyer responds substantively and the issue warrants discussion, explain what a short call would clarify. Offer an easy decline. Do not simulate prior familiarity or pretend to have seen unavailable activity.

## 5. Diagnose and confirm

**Published guidance:** Moore urges open questions, active listening, and investigation of the cause beneath surface symptoms. Rushing this stage undermines trust and the relevance of the prescription. [S1] He also stresses reflecting the buyer's problem clearly enough that the buyer feels understood. [S9]

**Agent adaptation:** Explore the following only as deeply as needed:

- **Pattern:** What is happening, to whom, how often, and since when?
- **Attempts:** What has already been tried, and what happened afterward?
- **Impact:** What does the problem interrupt or cost? Which effects are measured?
- **Outcome:** What change matters, and how would the buyer recognize it?
- **Constraints:** What timing, budget, capacity, access, or approval limits apply?
- **Cause:** What evidence supports the leading explanation, and what would disprove it?

Follow answers with questions that distinguish causes. If signups do not activate, examine both user fit and onboarding friction before prescribing a rewrite. Avoid a long fixed questionnaire when two well-chosen questions resolve the uncertainty.

Reflect back the current state, desired change, constraints, and leading explanation. Invite correction. If the buyer disputes the account, update it. If there is insufficient evidence, retain the uncertainty rather than claiming expertise eliminates it. A call plan includes question branches and what each answer would change; it must not fill in imaginary buyer responses.

## 6. Prescribe a clear course

**Published guidance:** After diagnosis, Moore recommends a tailored course of action in clear language. Too many unexplained options transfer the expert's job back to the buyer. The prescription should show how it addresses the buyer's problem and goals. [S1] Lead with the outcome that matters rather than a feature inventory. [S6]

**Agent adaptation:** A recommendation is ready when the buyer's account is sufficiently confirmed, the probable cause has support, and the seller can credibly address it under the known constraints. A sales conversation rarely proves causation fully; explain confidence and relevant uncertainty in proportion to the decision.

Include:

- The primary course and why it fits the observed pattern.
- The outcome it is intended to support, without unsupported certainty.
- Scope, relevant commercial terms, and what is excluded when useful.
- What the seller and buyer each do first.
- Preconditions and a way to check whether the intervention helps.
- One next decision with an understandable path forward.

If evidence is thin, prescribe a bounded investigation with a defined question, scope, and stopping point. If fit is poor, explain why and recommend another route without inventing a referral. A smaller scope is appropriate only when it still addresses a useful part of the problem.

Where genuine alternatives exist, explain the conditions that favor each and recommend one for the facts currently known. A buyer who explicitly requests a comparison should receive it; the method's warning concerns unexplained choice, not a prohibition on alternatives. Do not imply the buyer must obey the seller or cannot seek another opinion.

## 7. Handle objections without defensiveness

**Published guidance:** Moore frames objections as feedback about trust and the sales interaction, not automatically rejection. He advises listening without interruption, validating the concern, and responding to the underlying issue rather than arguing. [S7] He warns that discounts cannot repair missing confidence in the seller or plan. [S9]

**Agent adaptation:** Use this sequence:

`Hear the full concern -> clarify its meaning -> acknowledge it -> address the relevant gap -> check whether it is resolved`

Distinguish uncertainty about value, confidence in delivery, affordability, internal approval, timing, and poor fit. Ask a question when the wording is ambiguous. If the buyer has already explained the constraint, respond to it directly rather than repeating discovery.

For a price objection, understand the comparison and available budget before modifying terms. If the buyer doubts the diagnosis, revisit the evidence. If they cannot afford the scope, respect the limit and consider a viable smaller intervention or closure. Never interpret every budget issue as a trust problem. A clear refusal is a stopping condition, not a challenge to overcome.

## 8. Follow through and improve

**Published guidance:** Moore includes checking satisfaction, concerns, and results after a sale. [S1] He prioritizes actual conversations and clients over followers or views alone. [S2][S3][S5]

He warns that booking links, landing pages, and delegated outreach can add friction to substantial service purchases when personal contact is needed. He still allows automation for low-priced offers and increased volume. [S8]

**Agent adaptation:** Match the buying path to price, complexity, buyer preference, relationship, and seller capacity. Offer personal scheduling help when it reduces effort; retain a booking link if the buyer prefers it. Do not turn Moore's strong preference into a universal ban on tools.

For agreed work, define a baseline, a result indicator, who checks it, and when. If results disappoint, investigate implementation and the initial hypothesis before prescribing more services. For a pending decision, follow up only within the agreed context and applicable authorization; silence is not agreement to a recurring sequence.

Review the conversion path using available data. High engagement with few relevant conversations suggests checking audience fit and next action. Frequent calls with weak decisions suggests inspecting diagnosis, credibility, fit, and recommendation clarity. These are investigation prompts, not proof of causation. Record unknowns when tracking is incomplete.

## Explicit warnings and practical repairs

| Moore's published warning | What it can look like | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leaving the buyer with unexplained choices [S1] | A package list with no recommendation | Give a primary course and explain the conditions for alternatives |
| Rushing diagnosis [S1] | Naming a cause after one vague symptom | Ask the question that distinguishes competing explanations |
| Helpful content with no action, or relentless pitching [S2] | A lesson that stops abruptly, or a product-only post | Connect useful insight to a proportionate next step |
| Writing for peer approval [S3] | Insider jargon or revenue boasting | Start from the buyer's situation and language |
| Pushy tactics and volume-first outreach [S4] | Pressure, manufactured scarcity, generic mass messages | Build a relevant exchange before proposing help |
| Pitching before connection, or staying in messages indefinitely [S5] | A call demand in the first DM, or a stalled long thread | Use context first; suggest a focused conversation once engaged |
| Feature-heavy selling [S6] | Lists of components without buyer consequences | Connect the component to the operational and personal outcome |
| Interrupting or arguing with objections [S7] | A rebuttal before the buyer finishes | Listen, clarify, and address the actual concern |
| Automation that removes needed personal presence [S8] | A substantial-service buyer abandoned to a form | Offer direct help arranging the next conversation |
| Reflexive discounts [S9] | A fee cut before understanding hesitation | Identify whether the gap is confidence, value, affordability, or fit |

## Edge cases and stopping rules

These are agent adaptations to the published sequence, not additional attributed doctrine.

- **Only a product brief is available:** Draft with explicit audience and problem assumptions. Ask for the buyer fact that would change positioning; do not invent research.
- **A public symptom has several causes:** Teach how to distinguish them. Keep the recommendation conditional until individual discovery supports it.
- **The buyer's diagnosis differs from the seller's:** Explain the evidence and invite correction. Do not use authority as a substitute for confirmation.
- **A buyer wants immediate pricing:** Supply known pricing or scope limits, then identify the material unknowns. Do not withhold a straightforward answer to force a call.
- **No credible proof exists:** Explain the proposed investigation and its limits. Avoid fabricated testimonials or statistics.
- **A recommendation is outside the offer's competence:** Stop prescribing that offer. Suggest further investigation or an appropriate type of specialist.
- **A buyer requests alternatives or self-service:** Respect the preference and explain fit conditions; retain clear guidance.
- **The task is only a draft:** Produce the requested asset. Do not publish, send, schedule, or make commitments from template placeholders.
- **The buyer declines or a prerequisite fails:** Respect the decision. Reopen only with a new request or an agreed reason to reconnect.
