# Review checklist

Review the finished artifact and its preparation notes. Mark each applicable item **PASS** or **FAIL** with a brief reason. Use **N/A** only when the item belongs to a different mode, and record why. The gates are agent adaptations of the cited guidance, not Moore's own published checklist.

An output is ready when all applicable items pass or any unresolved limitation is plainly disclosed without presenting an unsupported recommendation as complete. A fictional example must remain labeled as an illustration.

## Checks for every output

- [ ] **Buyer relevance:** A specific buyer can recognize the situation and language. Peer approval or seller status is not the main argument. [S3]
- [ ] **Task and stage fit:** The artifact answers the user's request and asks for a level of commitment appropriate to the relationship. [S2][S5]
- [ ] **Facts separated:** Supplied facts, assumptions, and material unknowns are distinguishable. This is an agent adaptation of diagnosis before prescription. [S1]
- [ ] **Outcome clear:** The buyer's intended improvement is explained; features connect to why it matters. [S6]
- [ ] **Credibility honest:** Relevant evidence or reasoning supports authority. There are no fabricated cases, testimonials, claims of prior contact, or guarantees. [S1][S9]
- [ ] **Next step clear:** One primary action is understandable, proportionate, and easy to respond to. Alternatives have a reason. [S1][S2]
- [ ] **Buyer choice respected:** The language avoids pressure and manufactured urgency. A refusal or real constraint is accepted. [S4][S7]
- [ ] **Scope preserved:** The draft does not imply that sending, publishing, purchasing, or scheduling has occurred. This is an operational agent rule.

## Mode-specific checks

### Content and outreach

- [ ] **Useful content:** A post offers a concrete insight or inspection step as well as its invitation. It does not read solely as a pitch. [S2]
- [ ] **Cause qualified:** Public content does not pretend to have diagnosed an individual reader. This adapts Moore's diagnostic sequence. [S1]
- [ ] **Real context:** Outreach references available context and opens with curiosity. A like or view is not treated as certain intent. [S4][S5]
- [ ] **Conversation progression:** A call invitation follows meaningful engagement and has a useful purpose; the plan does not remain in messages indefinitely when deeper discussion is needed. [S5]

### Discovery and recommendation

- [ ] **Diagnosis sufficient:** Questions or evidence distinguish a symptom from plausible causes and consider attempted fixes and relevant constraints. [S1]
- [ ] **Understanding confirmed:** The summary invites correction. Planned discovery does not fabricate buyer answers. This adapts Moore's listening guidance. [S1][S9]
- [ ] **Fit established:** The seller can address the supported cause. Weak evidence leads to a conditional or bounded diagnostic step rather than a confident full prescription. [S1]
- [ ] **Course justified:** The primary recommendation names why it fits, what it includes, what happens first, and what decision is needed. [S1][S6]
- [ ] **Alternatives explained:** Requested comparisons are answered through fit conditions; unexplained menus do not replace guidance. [S1]

### Objections and follow-through

- [ ] **Concern understood:** The response listens and clarifies rather than interrupting, arguing, or discounting reflexively. [S7][S9]
- [ ] **Constraint respected:** Affordability, timing, approval, or a real no is addressed as stated, not automatically reinterpreted as lack of trust. This qualifies the application of [S9].
- [ ] **Friction reduced:** The buying path suits buyer preference, service complexity, and seller capacity; automation does not remove needed personal help. [S8]
- [ ] **Results checked:** Agreed work has a proportionate review of outcome and concerns, with a way to revise the plan. [S1]
- [ ] **Measures meaningful:** Relevant conversations, decisions, or service outcomes are distinguished from impressions and applause. [S2][S3][S5]

## Common failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Typical sign | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peer applause | Jargon and achievements dominate | Rewrite the opening around the buyer's observable situation [S3] |
| Empty education | Useful advice ends without direction in a conversion asset | Add one relevant next action [S2] |
| Premature pitch | First DM pushes an offer or call | Use actual context and a genuine question [S4][S5] |
| Thin diagnosis | The same recommendation could suit any buyer | Investigate the cause and confirm the summary [S1] |
| Forced fit | The offered service cannot address the cause | State the mismatch and propose investigation or another route [S1] |
| Choice overload | Several packages appear without guidance | Recommend a primary course and explain alternatives [S1] |
| Feature inventory | Scope is clear but its value is not | Connect components to the desired buyer change [S6] |
| Invented authority | Unsupported case, result, or certainty | Remove it and use transparent reasoning [S1][S9] |
| Defensive response | Rebuttal or discount arrives before clarification | Let the buyer explain, then address the issue [S7][S9] |
| Budget denial | A stated ceiling is treated as distrust | Respect the limit; revise viable scope or stop |
| Automation friction | A complex purchase disappears into a form | Offer direct assistance if it helps this buyer [S8] |
| No follow-through | A closed deal is treated as the final outcome | Agree how to inspect delivery and results [S1] |
| Vanity reporting | Views are reported as proof of sales success | Inspect qualified conversations and actual decisions [S2][S3] |
