# Method and evidence boundaries

## How to read this method

The core is an operating discipline: define the business outcome before buying media, align the customer story, make lead quality observable and give the agency the information it needs to improve downstream results. These principles are directly supported by complete public posts. [S1][S2][S3][S4]

Public indexed profile excerpts distinguish LinkedIn's future demand role from search's existing intent role and caution against allocating budgets solely by last-click acquisition cost. They also describe creative addressing a specific buying problem as a way to improve buyer fit beyond platform filters. These claims rely on partial indexed evidence. [S5]

The sections marked **Implementation guidance** convert these principles into reusable decisions. Their fields, formulas, thresholds and examples are package-author additions. There is no verified Leeuwis-owned framework name, required persona count, budget ratio or universal conversion benchmark in the fetched evidence.

## Baseline and diagnosis

When inheriting a paid media account, Leeuwis proposes reviewing roughly 90 days of history, speaking with Sales, repairing measurement and then testing one change. His example orders these activities across four weeks. The underlying priority is understanding the system before replacing it; the calendar is contextual rather than a mandatory waiting period. [S3]

**Implementation guidance:** Record spend and campaign purpose alongside submitted leads, accepted SQLs, rejected leads, opportunities and pipeline. Compare equivalent markets and mature cohorts. Document offer changes, seasonal effects, sales capacity and tracking changes that could explain apparent improvements. Preserve the baseline before edits.

Classify the bottleneck:

| Observation | Investigate first | Appropriate next move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Many submissions, few accepted SQLs | Fit, offer, message and qualification | Review rejection samples with Sales |
| Accepted SQLs, few opportunities | Buying problem, readiness and handoff | Inspect calls, routing and stage entry |
| Untraceable downstream outcomes | CRM identifiers and conversion mapping | Repair measurement before declaring a winner |
| Qualified demand but weak conversion | Landing-page promise, friction and proof | Test one conversion-path hypothesis |
| Unclear current campaigns | Ownership and account documentation | Inventory before rebuilding |

Avoid using a decline in CPL as proof of better business performance. A campaign can efficiently deliver exactly the wrong brief. [S1]

## Alignment and ownership

Product should solve a specific ICP problem; Marketing should communicate the solution consistently; Sales should continue that story in conversations. Feedback must travel both ways. More advertising spend cannot compensate for disconnected teams or an unclear customer problem. [S2]

The client owns direction and the agency executes it. Assign one accountable person who can see campaign activity and CRM outcomes. Sales rejection information must reach the agency and affect decisions. Agency reviews should explain pipeline and acquisition cost outcomes rather than merely list work completed. [S4]

**Implementation guidance:** Write a shared problem statement and confirm that the advertised promise is something Product can deliver and Sales can substantiate. Name separate owners for strategy, execution, CRM integrity and qualification, with one person accountable for the combined result. A disagreement becomes a brief dependency with an owner, rather than an invented consensus.

## Persona and message design

**Implementation guidance:** Keep account ICP and buying persona separate. The ICP describes eligible organizations and purchase circumstances. A persona describes a person's role in the buying decision and the problem they need solved. Base both on customer interviews, accepted opportunities, rejection reasons and product fit. A job title alone does not explain why anyone buys.

For each priority persona, document:

- Account context and buying role: practitioner, champion, economic buyer or evaluator, as applicable.
- Job and current problem: the work they must accomplish and the obstacle.
- Trigger: the circumstance that makes the problem worth addressing.
- Desired result: a business or workflow outcome they care about.
- Objection and proof: why they might reject the solution and what could resolve that doubt.
- Message and next action: a specific promise with an appropriate destination.
- Evidence status: observed, inferred or untested, with the supporting business artifact.

Map observable account and role attributes to platform audiences; address less observable purchase circumstances through specific copy and qualification. Problem-based self-selection can help, but it cannot guarantee that every click fits the ICP. Confirm fit downstream.

Build a message hypothesis such as: this role, experiencing this trigger, will recognize this problem and value this demonstrated outcome. Use real product proof. Keep proposed ad language separate from verified customer language.

Do not mechanically create one campaign per persona. Start with the highest-priority audience and a coherent hypothesis when budget or audience size limits learning. Expand when roles need meaningfully different messages and each test can receive useful exposure.

## Qualification and pipeline

A usable agency brief defines ICP precisely, puts SQL quality ahead of MQL volume, states a pipeline goal and exposes what happens after submission. Asking for lead volume alone encourages optimizing form fills while Sales absorbs unsuitable contacts. [S1]

**Implementation guidance:** Agree the CRM meanings before forecasting:

| Term | Required definition |
| --- | --- |
| Lead | Submitted contact or inquiry; specify source and deduplication |
| MQL | Marketing qualification, if used; specify criteria rather than assuming intent |
| SQL | Sales-accepted contact with agreed fit and a credible buying problem or intent |
| Opportunity | Deal accepted into the agreed sales stage, with an accountable owner |
| Pipeline | Sum of qualifying opportunity amounts created in the stated period, under a declared value basis |
| CAC | Declared acquisition cost basis divided by acquired customers from a comparable cohort |

Do not promote a download to SQL automatically. Record acceptance, rejection, unresolved qualification and stage timestamps. Example rejection categories include wrong company size, wrong industry, unsupported use case and absent buying need. Separate contact counts from account and deal counts.

For an unweighted pipeline-created target P and average opportunity amount V:

- Required opportunities = P / V, rounded up.
- Required accepted SQLs = required opportunities / SQL-to-opportunity rate, rounded up.
- Required inquiries, if useful = required SQLs / inquiry-to-SQL rate, rounded up.

These are planning formulas, not published Leeuwis formulas. Use coherent cohorts and a consistent annual contract value or total contract value basis. State whether the target is total company pipeline or the requested program's contribution. Do not add influenced pipeline to sourced pipeline: one opportunity can appear in both views.

Revenue goals need a separate win-rate and timing model. Pipeline is not revenue. If rates or deal values are unknown, provide scenarios and request validation rather than manufacturing a forecast. Check whether Sales can qualify the projected SQL input.

## Demand generation and demand capture

**Implementation guidance:** Assign campaigns a job by buyer readiness:

| Program | Buyer state and purpose | Message and destination | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Demand generation | Suitable buyers who need problem awareness, understanding or confidence | Persona problem, useful explanation, product value and relevant proof; accessible educational destination | Qualified audience exposure and message response first, downstream SQL and pipeline as cohorts mature |
| Demand capture | Buyers expressing an active need and evaluating solutions | Specific commercial promise and qualification; relevant product, comparison or demo destination | Accepted SQLs, opportunities, pipeline, conversion cost and eventual acquisition cost |

LinkedIn can support either job. Search queries can be educational or commercial. Classify actual audience intent and offer, rather than treating channel names as sufficient. Give the plan separate budgets, immediate measures and outcome windows for the two jobs.

Choose an allocation from the bottleneck, commercial demand available, runway, reachability, sales cycle and measurement confidence. Keep enough capture coverage to convert existing intent while deliberately testing future demand development. A limited reserve can support subsequent decisions. No fixed ratio is attributed to Leeuwis.

Do not interpret engagement as buying readiness or causally created pipeline. Do not stop education solely because its same-period CPL looks worse than capture. Conversely, protect no channel indefinitely without a learning hypothesis and an outcome review.

## Measurement and attribution

Leeuwis explicitly recommends UTMs reaching the CRM, offline conversion imports and SQL conversion visibility by channel before campaign optimization. [S3] He also calls for rejection data to flow into campaign decisions and for accountability that bridges media and CRM outcomes. [S4]

**Implementation guidance:** Specify identifiers for source, campaign, audience and experiment; preserve first-touch and later-touch records where available. Capture qualification and opportunity timestamps. Verify a sample inquiry from campaign through CRM disposition and back to the qualified conversion event. Deduplicate contacts and opportunities and document the attribution window.

Report sourced and influenced outcomes separately. Platform conversions, CRM accepted SQLs and opportunities are different measures. Reconcile mismatches before claiming improvement. Offline feedback requires usable, timely and correctly mapped events; do not promise that importing sparse data will make automated bidding reliable.

Where attribution is weak, triangulate campaign cohorts, buyer interviews and controlled comparisons when feasible. Clearly state that an observed association is not causal proof. Retargeting conversions can include prospects first introduced by another program, so last touch alone cannot justify eliminating upstream activity.

## Testing and budget decisions

The complete inherited-account post favors one hypothesis and a clear success measure over an immediate wholesale rebuild. [S3]

**Implementation guidance:** Test the message in a modest format before committing to expensive creative. Choose the priority audience, a single problem hypothesis and a small set of distinguishable angles. Hold the offer and audience comparable where practical. Define spend limits, the immediate signal, the quality check and the later outcome review before launch.

Read results in order: did the intended people see it, did the message elicit relevant interest, did responding buyers fit, and did qualified cohorts progress? High clicks with poor qualification call for a message or offer diagnosis. Good SQL fit with delayed pipeline calls for handoff and maturity analysis. A small sample supports a directional next test, not a confident universal winner.

Scale after quality and economics support the case, taking attribution and buying-cycle lag into account. Revise or stop when the wrong audience repeatedly responds, measurement fails, delivery saturates, or spend reaches the pre-agreed limit without informative evidence. Each review must record what changes in the next brief.

## Edge cases and warnings

**Implementation guidance:** The following rules extend the sourced principles to common planning situations.

- **No customer evidence:** Label personas provisional. Gather interviews and qualification samples before presenting buyer pains as established facts.
- **Little commercial search demand:** Do not force capture spend into broad irrelevant queries to spend the allocation. Reduce the capture plan and test audience education if feasible.
- **Tiny reachable audience or budget:** Consolidate tests and avoid excessive persona segmentation. If the paid test cannot generate informative exposure, propose a lower-cost message test and disclose the limitation.
- **Immediate quarter-end pipeline gap:** State what can realistically mature within the remaining time. Separate existing opportunity acceleration from future acquisition; never guarantee that a newly launched campaign repairs the quarter.
- **Broken qualification feedback:** Make CRM repair and Sales participation explicit dependencies. Retain available diagnostic reporting but withhold pipeline performance conclusions.
- **Misaligned product promise:** Fix or narrow the claim before increasing spend. Advertising should distribute a credible solution story. [S2]
- **Agency blamed for weak leads:** Compare the agreed brief with delivered outcomes, then inspect the shared operating model. Fix missing quality definitions and feedback before assuming replacement solves the problem. [S1][S4]
- **Different markets:** Treat language, proof, buying conditions and qualification rates as market-specific hypotheses. Do not carry a winning message across markets without validation.

The method's strongest directly verified warnings concern volume-led briefs, unaligned teams, rebuilding without a baseline, optimizing before measurement is reliable and agency relationships without full-funnel responsibility. [S1][S2][S3][S4] Its detailed persona workflow remains an explicit package implementation, not a claimed transcript of Leeuwis's complete practice.
