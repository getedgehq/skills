# Review checklist

Mark each item PASS or FAIL with a short piece of evidence from the finished output. Treat unresolved unknowns as explicit limitations. If a criterion genuinely does not apply, record PASS with the reason; do not quietly omit it. Repair failed items before presenting the output as ready.

This checklist is a package review convention. Citations indicate the method principle each check implements.

## Pass/fail checks

- [ ] **Purpose:** The output preserves the user's requested decision, deliverable, and constraints; it does not expand the assignment into an unrequested campaign or launch.
- [ ] **Reframing:** The customer outcome is distinct from the organization's proposed means and names a specific occasion. [S5]
- [ ] **Essentials:** Broken requirements are identified and repaired or explicitly made prerequisites; delight is not offered as a substitute. [S3]
- [ ] **Journey:** The diagnosis considers relevant stages and explains why the selected moment matters more than another measurable subsystem. [S5]
- [ ] **Hypotheses:** The conventional explanation and plausible alternatives remain visible, with evidence separated from speculation. [S2]
- [ ] **Discrimination:** At least one observation could distinguish or weaken each shortlisted explanation; plausibility is not presented as proof. [S6]
- [ ] **Context:** Audience, occasion, expectations, alternatives, and meaningful downside are specified. [S7]
- [ ] **Comparison:** The customer's likely reference point is examined, rather than inferred solely from the provider's category. [S3]
- [ ] **Neglected value:** Any reverse benchmarking claim identifies an actual overlooked dimension or is labeled a discovery question. [S6]
- [ ] **Portfolio:** Candidates include a conventional option and distinct psychological approaches; any contextual opposite has a stated use condition. [S3] [S7]
- [ ] **Concrete intervention:** Each candidate specifies what a customer would encounter and the inference or behavior expected, enough to prototype.
- [ ] **Credible signal:** Claimed reassurance has operational support; decorative assertions are not mistaken for demonstrated care. [S2]
- [ ] **Trade-offs:** Cost, delivery feasibility, unintended effects, and reversal conditions are visible; missing estimates are labeled unknown.
- [ ] **Test design:** The comparison preserves the question, bounds exposure, names a primary outcome, and sets a decision criterion before results. These fields are package conventions implementing experimentation. [S3]
- [ ] **Consequences:** Review includes completion and relevant downside, not just initial clicks or uptake. [S7]
- [ ] **Uncertainty:** The recommendation distinguishes worth testing from proven, and observed effects from an established mechanism. [S3] [S6]
- [ ] **Integrity:** Copy is truthful, total price and conditions are clear, and urgency or endorsements are not invented. This is a package guardrail.
- [ ] **Attribution and status:** Expert claims use matching [S#] references; invented examples and package conventions are labeled; proposed actions are not described as already executed.

## Common failures and fixes

| Failure mode | Why it fails | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discount by reflex | Assumes price explains the behavior | Preserve the price hypothesis and compare it with coordination, uncertainty, or another evidenced barrier. [S2] |
| Clever slogan without a mechanism | Cannot distinguish novelty from useful change | Specify stimulus, inference, behavior, and a disconfirming observation. [S3] [S6] |
| More service for everyone | Attention can be unwelcome in a different context | Consider privacy or unobtrusive service and identify the occasion for each. [S7] |
| Delight over broken basics | A pleasant detail cannot perform the missing essential | Repair the basic service, then compare performance and delight options. [S3] |
| Bigger budget treated as strategic | Ignores neglected details and alternative definitions of value | Give the expensive plan a creative challenge and inspect the whole experience. [S4] [S5] |
| Average performance treated as sufficient | Conceals variance or severe individual downside | Evaluate reliability, failure consequences, and escape routes. [S7] |
| Unverifiable reassurance | The cue may create expectations operations cannot meet | Use accurate ranges, actual policies, or a demonstrable action. [S2] |
| Competitor imitation called reverse benchmarking | Copies strengths instead of finding neglected value | Inspect disappointments and underserved customers, then test whether they matter. [S6] |
| Several changes bundled into one test | Cannot tell which explanation the result supports | Isolate the relevant change or state that only the bundle is being evaluated. |
| Bookings celebrated while completion worsens | The chosen metric rewards an incomplete outcome | Add completed service and failure measures to the decision criterion. |
| Failed test explained away | Replaces evidence with another plausible story | Reopen the mechanism or context and compare another candidate. [S6] |
| Small sample declared conclusive | Confidence exceeds evidence | Report uncertainty and use discovery to refine the next test. |
