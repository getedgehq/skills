# Worked Examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. All company names, commercial terms, observations, and results below are fictional. Proposed experiments have not been run. The examples demonstrate the public method using this package's output conventions.

## Illustration 1: A free pilot mistaken for a sales motion

### Scenario

DispatchLoop routes delivery exceptions for regional operators. Three friendly pilot customers use it without paying. Two report fewer unresolved exceptions, but there is no agreed baseline. The founder wants an outreach email and a job description for a VP of Sales.

### Weak first draft

> DispatchLoop is an AI-powered logistics platform for all transportation companies. Our pilots love it and we save every operator 50% of dispatch costs. We are hiring a VP of Sales to establish our go-to-market strategy and close enterprise accounts. Our outreach will say: Want to see the future of logistics? Book a demo of our intelligent platform.

The draft turns sentiment into an outcome claim, defines an unusably broad market, skips the willingness-to-pay question, and assigns discovery of the motion to a new leader.

### Improved working packet

**Stage assessment:** Value creation remains incompletely evidenced. Free adoption is observed, customer reports of improvement are reported, and willingness to pay is unknown. Validate a business result with the existing pilots while preparing founder conversations about a paid engagement. Do not call the motion repeatable or publish the 50% claim. [S2]

**Initial profile hypothesis:** Regional delivery operators with dispatch staff manually assigning recurring exceptions across several routes. Start with the operations manager responsible for resolution and establish who approves software spending. Route expansion is a possible timing signal, subject to discovery. Exclude operators with rare exceptions or an existing system that resolves the problem satisfactorily. [S3]

**Value argument:** Dispatchers currently coordinate exceptions through calls and spreadsheets. Delays can leave ownership unclear. DispatchLoop proposes routing an exception to an owner and surfacing unresolved work. The prospect must confirm the current process, cost, and gap before the founder presents the solution. [S3]

**Illustrative economics:** If discovery validates 40 monthly hours of avoidable coordination and a $30 hourly labor rate, the capacity-value estimate is $1,200 monthly. At a proposed $400 monthly price, the gross capacity value is three times the fee. This is not $1,200 of cash savings: released time, adoption, and implementation effort remain unverified. The calculation is a package convention for making the business case inspectable.

**Outreach draft, assuming the expansion signal is verified:**

> Subject: Exception handoffs after your route expansion
>
> Hi Maya, your announcement describes three added delivery routes. Are dispatchers still assigning delivery exceptions through calls and spreadsheets? DispatchLoop routes each exception to an owner and shows unresolved work. We are testing whether that reduces coordination time. Would a 20-minute conversation about your current exception process be useful next week?

**Discovery branches:**

- If coordination is manual, ask for the last exception that remained unresolved and who handled it.
- If the current tool works well, ask what remains unsatisfactory. Stop the pitch if there is no relevant gap.
- If ownership is unclear, ask what the delay caused and how frequently that happens.
- If the pain matters, ask who would evaluate a change and approve payment. [S3]

**Demo and commitment:** Walk through detection, assignment, escalation, and resolution of one exception matching the prospect's account. Summarize the acknowledged gap, then ask whether a defined paid evaluation would be appropriate. Confirm procurement and implementation responsibilities before treating agreement as a sale. [S2] [S3]

**Next experiment:** The founder and pilot operations leads establish a baseline and measure coordination time and unresolved exceptions over an agreed four-week window. In parallel, the founder conducts six conversations with independently qualified non-beta accounts. The six-call batch and four-week window are illustration choices, not Kazanjy thresholds. Review whether customers validate useful improvement and whether any qualified buyer agrees to a paid engagement. If outcomes disappoint, investigate delivery before adding more prospects. [S2] [S3]

**Hiring decision:** Defer the VP role as a mechanism for inventing the motion. If appointment setting or onboarding constrains learning, consider scoped support while the founder keeps ownership of selling and method development. [S2]

### Rules that made the difference

Separating value creation from value exchange removed the false claim that free pilots proved sales readiness. Narrowing organization and persona improved qualification. Discovery preceded screens. The economics separated capacity from cash. The next experiment paired payment learning with delivered value instead of using a hire to conceal uncertainty. [S2] [S3]

## Illustration 2: A founder bottleneck mistaken for permission to scale

### Scenario

MetricBridge helps sales managers identify stalled opportunities. Over eight months, the founder engaged 48 non-beta prospects, closed 16 paid customers, and obtained customer validation of improved pipeline follow-up from 13. The remaining three are still implementing. The founder documented calls and onboarding. No non-founder has sold it. The request is a first-seller hiring and ramp plan.

### Weak first draft

> We have product-market fit because we won a third of our prospects. Hire five enterprise account executives from famous software companies and a VP immediately. Give them the deck and a target of $100,000 each per month. The founder should join every late-stage call to guarantee wins. Put all new hires on any leads available so they get practice.

This invents universal hiring targets, treats employer prestige as stage fit, and lets founder intervention hide whether transfer works. It also ignores unfinished implementations and the supply of suitable opportunities.

### Improved working packet

**Stage assessment:** The record supports a small-scale repeated-exchange hypothesis and readiness to test transfer. It does not yet prove that other sellers can execute. Keep the three implementing customers visible and review their eventual outcomes. The count resembles the presentation's examples, but the decision also depends on documented execution and customer success. [S2]

**Role profile:** Start with one or two sellers comfortable selling early products with incomplete resources. Prioritize relevant sales-manager buyers and a comparable sales cycle, technical burden, and contract value. Assess their appetite to execute and refine an existing motion. Famous employers alone do not answer those questions. [S2] [S3]

**Illustrative work sample:** Give the candidate a fictional qualified account brief, narrative, and product summary. Ask for a discovery opening, response to a budget objection, selected demo path, and follow-up request. Provide feedback and observe the revision. This exercise is a package recommendation for evaluating the source-backed fit criteria, not an expert-prescribed test.

**Training and management:** The founder owns the initial transfer. Teach the customer problem, narrative, product, qualification, buying process, CRM, and onboarding handoff. Use practice, supported calls, and explicit review of recorded execution. Reduce intervention as capability grows. Reserve coaching time rather than personally handling every late-stage call. [S2]

**Comparable execution criteria:** The sellers should independently explain the value, qualify fit, progress opportunities, manage procurement, and hand off customers who achieve the intended outcome. Compare their activity and conversion with the founder's relevant cohort while accounting for opportunity quality and ramp time. Log founder assistance so assisted deals do not masquerade as independent proof. [S2]

**Review measures:** Inspect first meetings, next-step progression, proposal-to-close progression, time to implementation, and customer-validated outcomes. Report 16 wins out of 48 engaged prospects as that cohort's historical result, not as a promised future win rate. The 13 validated customers and three pending implementations remain separate categories. [S2] [S3]

**Next experiment:** Train a small seller cohort and review its first complete selling and implementation cycle. Advance only when non-founder execution is efficient and produces suitable successful customers. If progress is weak, inspect calls, lead comparability, and training before adding headcount. Once transfer works, establish balanced prospecting and success capacity before expanding or handing the unit to professional leadership. [S2]

### Rules that made the difference

The stage ladder changed the objective from adding capacity to proving transfer. Stage and buyer fit replaced credential shortcuts. Founder coaching became visible work, and assisted closes stopped inflating independence. Balanced handoffs and outcomes extended the test beyond booked revenue. [S2] [S3]
