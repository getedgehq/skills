# Review Checklist

Review the finished sales working packet, not just its prose. Mark each applicable item PASS or FAIL and record the evidence. Use N/A only with a scope or stage reason. A polished asset cannot compensate for a failed stage assessment or unsupported customer claim.

This checklist is a package review instrument derived from the public method. The exact labels and fields are not an expert-published scoring system.

## Pass/fail criteria

| Criterion | PASS when | FAIL when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | The requested deliverable and intended use are clear; omitted sections have a reason | A narrow asset request becomes an unrelated transformation program |
| Maturity | The earliest missing proof is identified using customer and sales evidence [S2] | Funding, confidence, pilot sentiment, or headcount defines readiness |
| Evidence integrity | Observed, reported, hypothesized, and unknown information are distinguishable | Invented customer outcomes or assumptions appear as established facts |
| Next experiment | Owner, action, review date, and observable exit condition address the missing proof | The plan consists of generic activity without a learning objective |
| Customer profile | Organization conditions and relevant people identify a narrow initial target [S3] | An industry label or impressive logo is the sole fit test |
| Timing | Urgency is confirmed or labelled as a hypothesis [S3] | Fit is treated as proof of immediate buying intent |
| Narrative | The current process, consequences, alternatives, solution mechanics, and evidence form a coherent value case [S3] | Generic product claims replace an acknowledged customer problem |
| Economics | Inputs, units, period, price, and uncertainty are inspectable; hidden costs support a credible budget case [S3] | Convenient arithmetic or overlapping benefits inflate the argument |
| Discovery | Research supports questions about process, incidents, consequences, and priority; branches respond to answers [S3] | The call starts with a pitch or runs a fixed fact checklist |
| Solution framing | The product demonstration addresses the acknowledged gap after value is framed [S3] | Screens appear without context or feature volume replaces relevance |
| Commitment | The requested next step is specific and appropriate to the maturity stage [S2] [S3] | Enthusiasm is counted as payment or actual progression |
| Record quality | Opportunity evidence, objections, next action, owner, and date can support later execution [S2] | Knowledge remains only in memory or vague notes |
| Customer success | Procurement and implementation ownership, baseline, and validated business result are visible [S2] | A signature or product usage alone is counted as delivered value |
| Diagnosis | Activity, progression, qualitative responses, and outcomes support a bottleneck hypothesis [S3] | Every failure is assigned to messaging or every shortfall to effort |
| Metrics | Counts retain cohort, denominator, and period; small samples retain uncertainty | A tiny sample proves causality or illustration ranges become universal quotas |
| Transfer readiness | Repeated founder execution and successful customers justify testing another seller [S2] | A first leader is asked to invent an unproven motion |
| Seller fit | Stage, buyer, and motion complexity inform the role profile [S3] | Prestigious employers or seniority substitute for relevant execution |
| Coaching and independence | Training and inspection exist; assisted deals are distinguishable from independent execution [S2] | The founder closes everything while declaring transfer complete |
| Balanced scale | Opportunity supply, selling, success, management, and economics are considered together [S2] | One function expands without the capacity of the others |
| Attribution | Method claims cite the source registry; operational conventions and fictional examples are identified | Package advice is attributed to Kazanjy without support or book text is reproduced |

## Most common failure modes and fixes

1. **Free-pilot inflation.** Free users may support a value-creation experiment, but do not establish exchange. Repair the packet by separating outcome validation from an explicit paid offer and procurement plan. [S2]

2. **Premature executive hiring.** Replace a plan to hire someone to find the initial motion with founder experiments. Once repeatability exists, test transfer with a small seller group and founder coaching. [S2] [S3]

3. **Broad targeting.** Replace a market category with observable organization conditions, relevant personas, and exclusion criteria. Revisit interviews if the pain hypothesis does not hold. [S3]

4. **New category with no budget case.** Show the labor or opportunity loss currently absorbing the cost, substantiate it, and prepare the champion to explain the case to the budget authority. [S3]

5. **Feature-first discovery.** Move product screens after questions about current practice and concrete failures. If time runs out, schedule the relevant demonstration instead of compressing an unexplained tour. [S3]

6. **Friendly calls with no progress.** Inspect whether the buyer acknowledged a meaningful problem and accepted a specific next action. Revise discovery or explanation before declaring the narrative effective. [S3]

7. **Bookings hide delivery problems.** Add implementation and outcome records. Diagnose product capability, onboarding, and expectation gaps before scaling acquisition. [S2] [S3]

8. **Cash-savings overclaim.** Reclassify released labor time as capacity unless spending actually changes. Retain arithmetic, adoption assumptions, implementation costs, and validation needs. This is a package accuracy convention supporting a credible business case. [S3]

9. **Founder rescue hides transfer failure.** Record assistance, reserve coaching time, and test whether sellers can reproduce efficient execution and successful delivery themselves. [S2]

10. **Misreading source numbers.** Restore context around prospect counts, customer counts, ramp periods, and staffing examples. Choose criteria that fit the actual motion instead of using illustrative numbers as automatic gates. [S2] [S3]

11. **Discarding a suitable new-category prospect too early.** Distinguish absence of fit from slow category adoption or poor timing. For confirmed pain with delayed readiness, record a real reason and condition for re-engagement rather than assuming every first-pass loss is final. [S3]

## Completion rule

Resolve applicable failures before labelling the packet ready for use. If missing customer evidence cannot be resolved within the assignment, clearly mark the affected content as a hypothesis and supply the needed validation action. A draft can be usable for learning while its underlying claims remain unproven.
