# Sales Working Packet Template

Fill only the sections needed for the request and the next missing proof. Mark others as outside scope or premature with a brief reason. Bracketed fields are prompts to replace, not facts. This format and its evidence labels are package conventions; the stages, value argument, discovery, and transfer logic follow [S2] and [S3].

## 1. Assignment and evidence

- **Requested deliverable:** [What the user needs and who will use it.] Limit work to this assignment.
- **Product and price:** [What it changes, current functionality, commercial terms, and unknowns.] Separate shipped capabilities from plans.
- **Available evidence:** [Customer interviews, sales records, measured results, and their dates.] Identify the source of each claim.
- **Evidence labels:** [Observed / reported / hypothesized / unknown.] Use these labels where uncertainty affects a decision.
- **Constraints:** [Founder time, target geography, implementation limits, and sales-cycle information.] Identify constraints that change the experiment.
- **Missing inputs:** [Only the unanswered questions material to this task.] Use stated assumptions for a draft when useful.

## 2. Stage and next proof

- **Current demonstrated stage:** [Earliest incomplete stage, plus evidence for completed stages.] Use the stage table in [../references/method.md](../references/method.md#1-diagnose-maturity). [S2]
- **Missing proof:** [The single uncertainty this packet should help resolve.] A payment, an outcome, and independent selling are distinct proofs. [S2]
- **Next experiment:** [Owner, target segment, action, and artifact to test.] Specify whose behavior will produce evidence.
- **Exit condition:** [Observable outcome and evidence required to proceed.] Explain why it is appropriate; do not import a universal customer-count gate.
- **Review date and fallback:** [When to inspect results and what to revisit if the condition fails.] Allow time for the relevant stage to mature.

## 3. Ideal customer and account qualification

- **Organization conditions:** [Size, relevant user count, workflow, systems, and pain indicators.] Make fit observable rather than naming a broad industry. [S3]
- **Exclusions:** [Conditions that make this solution unsuitable.] Avoid selling customers unlikely to achieve value. [S2]
- **People:** [User, champion, operational buyer, budget authority.] Mark unconfirmed roles. [S3]
- **Current approach:** [Tool, manual work, or accepted opportunity loss.] Establish what would change. [S3]
- **Timing:** [Verified event or hypothesis that could make the problem urgent.] Separate timing from fit. [S3]

## 4. Master narrative and economics

- **Current state:** [How the work happens now, in the buyer's terms.]
- **Problem and consequences:** [Concrete failures, business effects, and relevant personal stakes.] Ground these in discovery. [S3]
- **Alternatives and gap:** [Why present solutions leave an unmet need.] Include labor or doing nothing where applicable. [S3]
- **Proposed change:** [How the product changes the work and why that addresses the gap.]
- **Evidence:** [Measured customer result, comparable case, or explicitly untested hypothesis.]
- **Why act now:** [Confirmed priority or event, or state that timing is unknown.]
- **Commercial proposal:** [Price, evaluation scope, buyer responsibilities, and next decision.] Tie it to the expected value. [S2]

| Business measure | Baseline and source | Proposed change | Calculation and period | Costs and assumptions | Validation plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Metric] | [Observed or estimated] | [Measured or hypothesized] | [Units and arithmetic] | [Implementation, adoption, overlap] | [Owner and measurement date] |

Classify each benefit as cash savings, released capacity, incremental revenue, or qualitative improvement. Do not add overlapping benefits or present forecasts as achieved results. These calculation conventions support a credible economic-buyer case. [S3]

## 6. Discovery and commitment plan

- **Pre-call facts and rapport:** [Research completed and relevant context.] Do not spend discovery collecting facts already available. [S3]
- **Meeting objective:** [The agreement or information needed to progress.]
- **Opening question:** [How the relevant work happens today.]
- **Branches:** [If current tool / manual workaround / no pain / low priority / no authority, ask ...] Follow answers rather than reading every branch. [S3]
- **Concrete incident:** [Ask for a recent example, consequence, frequency, and cost.]
- **Problem summary:** [What the buyer should confirm before the solution is framed.]
- **Demo selection:** [Which workflow addresses the acknowledged problem.]
- **Objection response:** [Clarifying question, available evidence, and honest limitation.]
- **Commitment request:** [Specific meeting, evaluation, procurement action, or purchase appropriate to the stage.]
- **If no commitment:** [What to record and the condition for revisiting, if suitable.]

## 8. Motion review and revision

- **Alternative explanation:** [What would change this diagnosis.]

- **Next review:** [Owner, date, and decision to make.]

## 9. Transfer plan, only when justified

- **Readiness evidence:** [Repeatable founder selling and successful customers, plus documented execution.] [S2]
- **Seller profile:** [Early-stage comfort, buyer familiarity, and motion complexity.] [S3]
- **Evaluation:** [Relevant account or call exercise and response to feedback.]
- **Training:** [Customer context, narrative, product, tools, process, and handoff.]
- **Founder coaching:** [Time reserved, inspection cadence, and support available.] [S2]
- **Independent execution criteria:** [Efficient progression and suitable customers reaching success.] [S2]
- **Capacity and scale decision:** [Opportunity supply, success capacity, economics, and conditions for adding headcount.] [S2]
