# Method in depth

Based on Kaz Tahara-Edmonds's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Kaz Tahara-Edmonds.

## Reading map

Sections 1 through 3 cover opportunity selection and product design. Sections 4 through 7 cover creator pilots, learning, measurement, and economics. Section 8 covers reallocation and portfolio value. Sections 9 and 10 separate explicit expert warnings from package adaptations and explain boundary cases. Source identifiers resolve in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Scope and evidence

The central hypothesis is that cheaper software production makes audience understanding and efficient distribution more decisive. Kaz starts with a short video niche and builds features that belong inside its stories, rather than finishing an app and then searching for promotion. The company describes its purpose as consumer apps that solve everyday problems and spread naturally. These are strategic claims, not universal laws about every software business. [S1][S2][S3]

The deliverable is a decision memo. For discovery, recommend an opportunity and a bounded test. For an existing app, identify the limiting part of its acquisition or retention system. For a portfolio, compare incremental allocation and reusable learning. Do not force an existing product into a new niche solely to follow a fixed sequence.

Use three evidence labels throughout:

- **Observed:** Dated content examples, measured funnel data, actual quotes from creators, or other supplied evidence.
- **Reported:** The expert's public accounts or a company's product claims, attributed to their source.
- **Hypothesized:** Proposed causal explanations, budget estimates, thresholds, and tests still to run.

This labeling and the memo structure are package adaptations. Do not present them as a named framework invented by Kaz. Historical interviews establish the method; current niche viability needs current evidence. If live research is unavailable, deliver a provisional hypothesis and specify the observations needed to validate it.

## 2. Screen the niche

Kaz describes three selection factors: emotionally resonant viral formats, formats that creators can recreate readily, and limited competition from brands. Analyze them together. A niche with compelling stories may still fail if making those stories requires rare talent, expensive settings, or creators already priced for large sponsors. [S1][S2]

Build a compact evidence table with audience, geography, post URL and date, creator archetype, hook, emotional tension, story structure, product integrations, comments indicating intent, and reproduction requirements. Sample across creators and dates. Looking across the niche matters because individual creators may not see its broader patterns. The package does not prescribe a universal sample size; choose enough variety to distinguish a pattern from a single exceptional post. [S1]

Use these decisions:

- **Advance:** Multiple independent creators use recognizable structures; a useful app interaction could strengthen the story; audience access and costs appear plausible.
- **Hold:** Emotion is clear but creator supply, customer intent, or price is unverified. Obtain those observations before building a large product.
- **Reject or change channel:** Evidence consists of one viral outlier, generic market excitement, or formats the team cannot reproduce at sustainable cost.

The three-factor screen is sourced; these labels and evidence requirements operationalize it. A one-sentence thesis should name the customer, recurring content behavior, useful app interaction, and why that interaction belongs in the story. Broad categories such as wellness or productivity are insufficient without a concrete audience tension.

## 3. Design the product moment

Choose one narrow problem and one action that a creator can show naturally. Specify the input, visible interaction, output, and reason the viewer wants to try it. Kaz's relationship-app example uses a shared drawing canvas: the interaction supplies entertainment while demonstrating the app's value. The feature improves the video itself. [S1]

A product moment passes when a viewer can recognize the problem and the useful result without a separate explanatory pitch. Suitable shapes include a collaborative interaction, a routine, a reveal, a decision, or a comparison. Record why the app is needed: if the story would work just as well without its functionality, the integration may be decorative rather than persuasive.

Scope the first version around the demonstration, core user experience, and route to payment. Kaz reports rapid MVP development with AI, but his profile also documents prior mobile and AI projects. Set a deadline from actual skills, platform constraints, and quality needs. Fast shipping is a learning tactic, not evidence of demand or permission to skip functional checks. [S1][S5]

Lume's product page illustrates scans, progress tracking, and product checks as visible interactions. It also says the product does not replace medical advice. Treat its claimed capabilities as marketing descriptions, not proof of accuracy. For wellbeing-related features, the package recommends checking output reliability, privacy, and the exact claims creators may make before using a dramatic demonstration. This is an implementation safeguard, not a general clinical method attributed to Kaz. [S4]

## 4. Test creators and formats

Select creators whose geography, audience needs, emotional style, and execution fit the product. Follower counts alone do not establish customer value. Kaz reports that comparable views from different audiences produced substantially different value for the same app. [S2]

Use a small pilot with varied but interpretable test cells. A cell identifies creator, audience, format, product moment, expected cost, and posting window. Include more than one person capable of performing the chosen format. Test spending and duration must fit the user's available budget; four creators or a two-week window can be useful illustrations, not expert-prescribed constants.

A brief should supply audience tension, hook pattern, story beats, necessary product action, supported claims, and desired next action. Preserve the creator's own writing and performance. A test should reveal whether the underlying structure works across people, not whether everyone can recite an identical script. [S1][S2]

Record sourcing cost, fees, variable payouts, usable posts, audience composition, and constraints on further supply. Creator rights and permission to reuse content as advertising must be resolved before such reuse. This rights check is a package implementation rule, not a reported part of the expert's interview.

Do not assume cheap creators will discover the strategy independently. In Kaz's Tethered account, isolated creator experimentation underperformed a centrally informed system that shared successful patterns and responded to niche changes. [S1]

## 5. Operate the learning network

Maintain a shared library of hooks, story structures, pacing, sounds, visuals, product placements, audience context, and outcomes. Record failures alongside successes so that a popular structure is not mistaken for a universally useful one. The expert describes distributing patterns across the network and adapting as trends change. [S1][S2]

Software can assist with sourcing examples, coordinating assignments, tracking posts, gathering audience data, and calculating agreed payouts. Human review remains necessary for whether a video feels forced, awkward, emotionally accurate, or convincing. Direct relationships matter: creators need responsive people who understand their work and support their success. Kaz explicitly describes underestimating this human layer. [S2]

Assign one person to creative feedback and one contact for creator questions; these can be the same person in a small team. A process document should identify the work software actually performs. Do not describe a spreadsheet or a proposed future tool as an autonomous operating system.

Transfer structures, not surface mimicry. When adapting a format to another audience, test whether its tension, product interaction, and emotional payoff still fit. Creator reuse can reduce sourcing cost and improve rates, but an audience fit test remains necessary for each product. [S2]

## 6. Measure the whole funnel

Judge the route from repeatable content to retained paying customers. Kaz includes content virality and sustainable replication alongside store conversion, trial conversion, paid conversion, and customer value. Strong product-level metrics cannot compensate automatically for an expensive or scarce creator supply. Cheap reach can sometimes justify improving a weak funnel. [S2]

Define each cohort, time window, and denominator before interpreting it:

| Stage | Useful observation | What it helps diagnose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content | Views, engagement, watch behavior where available, audience geography | Reach, attention, and plausible audience fit |
| Intent | Relevant comments, bio clicks, branded search changes | Whether attention contains demand |
| Store | Product page views and installs | Presentation and search-to-install friction |
| Activation | First useful action and trial starts | Whether users reach the promised value |
| Payment | Paid customers, net proceeds, refunds | Monetization and realized customer value |
| Retention | Continued usage, renewals, cancellations | Whether the demonstration led to ongoing value |
| Cost | Creator fees and other defined acquisition costs | Acquisition economics and cash exposure |

Activation and the precise table schema are package adaptations. Do not combine stage rates with incompatible denominators or cohorts. For example, install-to-paid and trial-to-paid answer different questions.

Expect indirect journeys. Kaz says many viewers search Google or the App Store rather than using a tracked link. His approach combines partial click evidence with audience screenshots, demographic information, engagement, and comment sentiment. Perfect creator-level attribution is unavailable in that system. [S2]

Operationalize uncertainty with direct tracked counts, estimated ranges, background demand, overlapping posts, and the assumptions connecting them. Compare pre/post store or search lift where available, but call it association unless the design supports incrementality. Do not give every install after a post to that creator or sum overlapping estimates as independent customers. An inconclusive result calls for a more discriminating test, not a fabricated precision score.

Keep organic and paid decisions distinct. Kaz observes that organic's total reach can outweigh a lower conversion rate, whereas paid growth must account for click and conversion efficiency and customer value. Organic winners may supply paid creative candidates, but engagement alone does not establish paid profitability. Test the paid funnel and costs separately. [S1]

## 7. Set economic decision rules

Kaz's bootstrapped experience emphasizes short payback, conversion gains, and retention because growth depends on cash available to reinvest. Derive thresholds from the user's finances and evidence, not a copied CPM or reported portfolio outcome. [S1]

The following calculations are package planning aids:

- **Acquisition cost per paid customer:** Defined acquisition costs divided by attributable new paying customers. State whether the figure includes only creator fees or also production, sourcing, and operations.
- **Payback:** The first period when cumulative realized contribution from an acquired cohort covers its acquisition cost. Contribution means proceeds after the variable costs included in the analysis, such as platform fees, refunds, and serving costs.
- **Affordable acquisition cost:** Conservative expected customer contribution within the user's acceptable payback window, reduced by any stated margin or uncertainty reserve.
- **Affordable cost per thousand views:** Affordable acquisition cost multiplied by plausible new paid customers per thousand views. This is a locally derived ceiling, not an expert benchmark.

If attributed customers are a range, calculate a corresponding cost range. If the lower customer estimate is zero, cost may be unbounded; do not force a finite value. If retention data are immature, use conservative realized contribution and mark projected renewals as assumptions.

Specify a spend cap, decision date, and conditions before expansion. Expand when repeatability, audience fit, and conservative economics support additional spend. Revise when a specific bottleneck can be tested within the remaining cap. Stop when the cap is reached without credible improvement, adequate creators cannot reproduce the format, or conservative economics exceed the user's tolerable payback. These gates implement the method; their numerical values belong to the user's situation.

## 8. Review bottlenecks and portfolio value

Kaz describes weekly reallocation toward effective formats and creators, with underperformers cut. Use the following diagnostic matrix to avoid solving the wrong problem. [S1][S2]

| Observed pattern | Next useful decision |
| --- | --- |
| Strong app conversion, expensive creator supply | Test a cheaper archetype or an adjacent audience before discarding the app |
| Strong conversion, only one effective performer | Test execution across additional creators before scaling |
| Cheap qualified reach, weak activation or payment | Test promise-to-product alignment, onboarding, or the payment path |
| Large view counts, little relevant intent | Recheck audience fit and product integration |
| Ambiguous attribution under overlapping launches | Reduce overlap or improve measurement before ranking creators confidently |
| Profitable trend, little durable advantage | Continue within a controlled budget; separate near-term harvest from long-term investment |

LumeSkin is the critical counterexample. Kaz reports strong install-to-paid performance, but skincare creators competed for large-brand budgets. A later test reused effective relationship-app creators at bundled rates on new skincare accounts. A few worked, yet too few could execute skincare formats consistently. Lower cost for some posts did not solve the scalability constraint. [S2]

Portfolio reuse succeeds when creators, audience emotions, or structural content knowledge transfer. Kaz reports strong overlap between Tethered's relationship content and Closure's breakup journaling audience. That is evidence for an adjacent transfer, not a rule that any winning creator can sell any app. [S2]

Separate immediate profit from compounding value. A trend-dependent app may remain worth operating while profitable. Larger commitments need a hypothesis about lasting demand and advantages such as brand, network effects, relationships, or reusable content intelligence. Specify how the hypothesis could fail and what evidence would strengthen it. [S1][S2]

## 9. What the expert explicitly warns against

These cautions paraphrase direct statements or retrospective lessons in the interviews:

- **Product first, distribution later:** Months of building do not guarantee launch traffic; low-volume feedback may misrepresent the eventual channel or audience. [S1]
- **Abrupt promotional insertion:** A detached advertisement can interrupt the content rather than make the app watchable. [S1]
- **Cheap creators left to invent the whole strategy:** Individual creators lack the network's collective view; shared content intelligence improved the Tethered approach. [S1]
- **Assuming creator growth scales like ad spend:** More output requires additional formats, people, and coordination, not merely a higher budget. [S1]
- **Perfect attribution claims:** Search-led journeys and uncontrolled organic distribution prevent complete creator-to-customer tracking. [S2]
- **A fully software-driven human layer:** Creative judgment and creator trust were less replaceable than the team expected. [S2]
- **App benchmarks as the entire scale decision:** Viral potential, creator competition, and execution replicability can defeat an otherwise strong funnel. [S2]
- **One creator's success as proof of scale:** The LumeSkin account distinguishes isolated wins from enough creators reproducing results cheaply. [S2]
- **Treating trend profits as a lasting position:** Continue profitable opportunities as appropriate, but reserve larger commitments for advantages that compound. [S2]

## 10. Edge cases and boundaries

**No niche research yet:** Offer a research plan and provisional concepts. Mark them unvalidated. Do not claim that an invented video pattern is currently popular.

**Tiny budget or no creator access:** Reduce pilot size and spend, but retain the question of reproduction across people. Founder-produced content can test a demonstration; it does not establish an available scalable creator supply. This distinction follows the replication constraint. [S2]

**Long retention horizon:** Separate early acquisition and activation signals from a mature payback decision. Set an interim cap and a later cohort review rather than inventing lifetime value from a few days of activity.

**Cross-geography expansion:** Revalidate language, cultural resonance, willingness to pay, platform availability, and creator economics. Audience geography affects customer value in Kaz's attribution example. [S2]

**Sensitive personal experiences:** Avoid demonstrations that expose private relationship, skin, or journal data without suitable permission. Support only claims the product can substantiate. These are package safeguards for implementing the method responsibly; [S4] supplies a concrete product limitation, not a general endorsement of sensitive-content tactics.

**Existing profitable app:** Improve its limiting channel, format, or funnel stage within the assignment. Portfolio infrastructure and additional apps are optional investments, not mandatory outcomes.

**Public action:** The memo can propose outreach, publishing, contracts, and spend. Execute them only within the user's actual authorization. Completing the plan does not itself grant those permissions.
