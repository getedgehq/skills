# Worked examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. All niche observations, creators, product names, budgets, dates relative to launch, and results below are fictional. They demonstrate how to apply the method, not actual market research or expected performance. Rule citations refer to [the fetched sources](../references/sources.md); the package's detailed implementation appears in [method.md](../references/method.md).

## Illustration 1: A night-shift meal app

### Weak first draft

Build ShiftFuel, an AI wellness platform for every shift worker. Include calorie targets, sleep tracking, exercise plans, recipe generation, and a social feed. Hire the biggest nursing influencer available, have them say the app changed their life, and target a million views. If installs rise after launch, attribute them to the influencer and double the marketing budget.

### Why the draft fails

The feature list precedes the channel evidence. The customer is broad, the story is a sponsorship claim, one creator carries the forecast, and view counts replace cash economics. An install spike without a baseline is not reliable attribution. These problems correspond to the distribution-first principle, the replication constraint, and the incomplete-attribution warning. [S1][S2]

### Improved opportunity memo

**Decision and audience:** Test a meal-choice tool for English-speaking hospital staff working overnight shifts in one target country. Decide after a 14-day pilot whether to run a second cohort; reserve a separate 30-day contribution review before major scaling.

**Illustrative content evidence:** In this fictional scenario, a review of 18 posts across six creators finds a recurring structure: show the packed meal, show what was actually eaten at 3 a.m., then describe the practical frustration. Ten posts use variations of that structure. Comments request simple meal alternatives. Two creators have frequent food-brand sponsors, leaving their prices uncertain. In a real memo, each observation needs a dated link.

**Distribution thesis:** Night-shift creators can make a familiar food-choice story more useful by showing three practical meal options matched to a shift schedule and available preparation time.

**MVP:** The user enters shift timing, available preparation time, food preferences, and equipment. The app supplies three meal options and a packing list. The creator selects one and shows it in their actual routine. Exclude sleep and exercise features from this pilot. Do not make clinical or energy-performance promises.

**Concepts:**

1. Open with a packed lunch left untouched. Show the app generating an option that can be eaten during a short break, then show the practical choice.
2. Open with ten minutes before leaving home. Generate three packing options from available ingredients and show which one fits the time.
3. Open with a fridge after a shift. Enter tomorrow's schedule, choose an easy preparation option, and show the packed result.

**Creator pilot:** Recruit four relevant small creators with different hospital roles. Each produces two posts, with two shared structures tested across more than one person. Budget at most $800 in creator fees and $200 in other defined acquisition costs. A named founder reviews story clarity and claims; creators retain their own phrasing. No outreach or spending is implied by this memo.

**Measurement:** Track each post and audience profile, relevant comments, bio clicks, store visits, installs, first generated plan, trials, paid customers, and cohort contribution. Compare store search activity against a pre-launch baseline. Report overlapping search-led installs as uncertain rather than assigning them all to one creator.

**Local economics:** The team hypothetically sets an affordable acquisition cost of $20 based on conservative 30-day customer contribution and its cash limits. At the full $1,000 pilot cap, it needs at least 50 incremental paying customers to meet that ceiling. If plausible attribution is only 30 to 55 customers, acquisition cost ranges from about $18.18 to $33.33. The conservative end fails; another round requires a cheaper design or clearer evidence, not automatic expansion.

**Review decision:** Expand only if several creators reproduce useful content, the conservative customer estimate supports the cost ceiling, and early retention supports the contribution assumption. If views are strong but few people generate a plan, revise the promise and activation path. If credible creator supply exceeds the budget, stop this distribution hypothesis.

### Rules that changed the result

- The niche screen selected an existing emotion and reproducible story before features. [S1]
- One visible app action replaced a broad platform and inserted testimonial. [S1]
- Multiple creator executions replaced dependence on one influencer. [S2]
- Attribution ranges and contribution-based limits replaced views as the scale trigger. These calculations operationalize the expert's whole-funnel and payback discipline. [S1][S2]

## Illustration 2: A journaling app with misleading viral success

### Weak first draft

AfterWords has a 12% install-to-paid rate, and one celebrity breakup video got 500,000 views. The app is validated. Offer every lifestyle creator $1,000 per post, reuse the winning script word for word, and automate all feedback. Forecast paid subscribers by applying the 12% rate to every view.

### Why the draft fails

Install-to-paid conversion has an install denominator, not a view denominator. The exceptional performer does not establish creator supply. Literal script copying loses natural voice, and software cannot fully replace feedback or trust. The product's journaling value may be unrelated to celebrity entertainment traffic. [S1][S2]

### Improved diagnostic memo

**Decision:** Keep the existing journaling app. Test whether inexpensive reflective-story creators reach people actively seeking a private writing routine after a breakup. Do not build a new app from a single viral result.

**Known versus uncertain:** The fictional 12% rate is measured for an older install cohort. It does not establish conversion for a new audience. The 500,000-view video's geography and search-led conversions are unclear. The memo records these gaps rather than calling the launch validated.

**Product moment:** A creator describes a difficult evening, opens a short private writing prompt, and shows completion using fictional text. The useful reveal is finishing a small routine, not a promise of emotional recovery. Personal journal entries stay private unless deliberately and appropriately shared.

**Two concepts:**

1. Start with hovering over an unsent message. Demonstrate choosing a writing prompt and completing a private reflection instead. Avoid claims that the app treats distress.
2. Start with a list of activities planned for a quiet evening. Show the app supplying one small reflection task and marking it complete.

**Pilot:** Four creators produce two posts each. Total creator fees are $720 and defined support costs are $180. A human reviewer checks emotional fit and unsupported claims; one contact handles creator questions. Two related story structures are shared across creators, with different personal delivery.

**Illustrative results:** Creator A gets 210,000 mostly entertainment-led views and little relevant intent. Creator B gets 24,000 views and creator C gets 31,000, both with comments about private reflection. Creator D gets 18,000 views but struggles to show the prompt naturally. Direct links yield 17 paid customers. A separate search-led analysis suggests 28 to 43 additional paid customers, after accounting for background demand and overlap. The estimated total is 45 to 60, not 17 plus every new install.

**Economics:** At $900 total defined acquisition cost, the estimated cost per paid customer is $15 to $20. Hypothetical contribution data show $11 per acquired customer by day 14 and a conservative $24 by day 45. Immediate payback has not occurred: the first 14 days produce only $495 to $660 against $900 spent. Day-45 payback remains a projection until observed. The team's locally chosen acquisition ceiling is $18.

**Action:** Do not scale the entire cohort because the conservative $20 estimate exceeds the ceiling. Plan a smaller capped follow-up focused on B and C's audience style, recruiting two additional performers to test replication. Give D a revised demonstration brief only if a specific execution fix is plausible. Measure the older cohort's day-45 contribution before raising the overall cap. If B and C cannot be replicated or retention misses assumptions, stop expansion.

**Portfolio option:** If the team already operates an adjacent relationship app, invite consideration of creator reuse only after checking audience fit and existing permissions. Kaz's Tethered-to-Closure account motivates an adjacent test, not guaranteed transfer. [S2]

### Rules that changed the result

- Audience value replaced the ranking by total views. [S2]
- The whole funnel preserved a strong product while investigating distribution. [S2]
- Shared story patterns and human review replaced rigid scripts and automated relationships. [S1][S2]
- Replication and conservative payback prevented scaling from one viral outlier. [S1][S2]
