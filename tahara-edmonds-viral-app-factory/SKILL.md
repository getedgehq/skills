# Viral App Factory

Based on Kaz Tahara-Edmonds's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Kaz Tahara-Edmonds.

## When to use

Use this skill when a user wants to find a consumer app opportunity, shape an app around short video distribution, plan creator tests, diagnose a weak launch, or decide whether to scale a small app portfolio. The method is most useful when TikTok or Instagram Reels can show the product naturally in an emotionally resonant format. Treat the public interviews as an operating model, not a promise that any particular view count, build speed, or revenue result will transfer to the user's situation. [S1][S2]

## Procedure

### 1. Begin with the content niche

Map the audience's existing short video behavior before proposing features. For each candidate niche, collect recent posts and record recurring hooks, story arcs, visuals, sounds, comments, creator types, and visible app or brand integrations. Select niches where the emotion is easy to recognize, formats are simple enough for many creators to reproduce, and competing brands have not already made creator prices prohibitive. Reject a niche if its only evidence is a broad market trend or one exceptional creator's viral post. Kaz describes the niche screen as emotional resonance, repeatable formats, and low brand competition. [S1]

Write a one sentence content thesis for each surviving niche, then show two or three concrete video concepts using formats already present there. Ask whether a viewer could understand the product's value from the first scene and whether the app can create a visible reveal, decision, comparison, or result inside the video. If a concept needs a lengthy explanation or a detached sponsorship segment, revise the feature or change the niche. The product is designed to fit the channel's native storytelling. [S1][S3]

### 2. Build a feature that belongs in the video

Choose one narrow user problem and one demonstrable action for the first version. Describe the input, the on screen transformation, the outcome a creator can show, and the reason the viewer would want the same outcome. Build only enough app to test that loop and its payment path; Kaz reports moving from idea to an MVP in less than a week with AI tools, so speed is a tool for learning rather than proof of demand. Check the user's actual build capacity before adopting that pace: Kaz's personal profile documents earlier mobile and AI projects, which gave him experience a new team may lack. Include a clear quality and trust check for outputs that affect a user's wellbeing. [S1][S4][S6]

Prefer features that create content naturally: a result a creator can react to, a routine they can show, or a useful comparison they can narrate. Lume Skin's public product page, for example, describes a skin scan, progress view, and product scanner, all actions with visible before and after or decision moments. This is an illustration of product demonstrability, not evidence that its distribution economics work; Kaz later described LumeSkin's expensive creator niche and difficult content replication. [S5][S2]

### 3. Test creators as a system

Recruit a small, varied pilot of creators whose audiences match the intended customer in geography, demographics, and emotional context. Capture each creator's expected fee or payout, likely audience value, posting capacity, and ability to make the format feel natural. Do not select by follower count or views alone: Kaz reports similar view totals producing very different customer value because audience fit differed. Test enough creators to learn whether a format travels across people, while keeping the spend within a payback period the user can tolerate. [S2][S3]

Give creators a brief that specifies the audience tension, product moment, proven format, and essential claim, then let them write and perform in their own voice. Have a human review whether the video feels awkward, scripted, or emotionally false. Use software to collect examples, assign briefs, track posts, calculate payouts, and summarize patterns; preserve direct human feedback and relationships with creators. Kaz says central content intelligence corrected the assumption that cheap creators could invent the whole strategy independently, while human creative judgment and trust remain hard to automate. [S1][S2][S3]

Record every post as a test of a format, creator, audience, and product feature. Keep a shared library of hooks, pacing, visuals, narrative structures, and product placements, with the outcome and context of each attempt. Send the best current patterns across suitable creators and update briefs as trends change. Adapt an underlying format to adjacent niches only when the audience tension and product demonstration still make sense. This turns isolated creator work into a learning network. [S1][S2]

### 4. Measure the whole route from attention to cash

For each content cohort, report views and engagement alongside app search lift, store page views, installs, trials, paid conversions, retention, customer value, creator cost, and time to recover acquisition spend. Judge a niche on the full route from a repeatable video to a retained paying customer. Strong install to paid conversion alone does not justify scaling if creators are too expensive or only one person can make the content work. Likewise, weak conversion may still be worth improving if the content repeatedly reaches qualified viewers at low cost. [S1][S2]

Expect attribution to be incomplete because many viewers search for an app later rather than tapping a tracked link. Treat creator level revenue as an estimate using link clicks, audience screenshots, geographic mix, engagement, comment intent, search or store lift, and timing. Mark uncertainty rather than assigning every install to the nearest post. A creator with fewer views but a more valuable audience can deserve more budget than a larger account. [S2]

Review the operating economics weekly. Compare effective creator cost with the revenue each qualified view can plausibly generate, and check payback before expanding spend. Kaz's bootstrapped experience made short payback, incremental conversion gains, and subscription retention central to reinvestment. Do not present a universal CPM target or copy his reported portfolio economics into a new market; derive a ceiling from the user's own funnel and cash constraints. [S1][S3]

### 5. Reallocate and decide what compounds

Each review, expand formats that repeatedly perform across creators, retrain or replace creators whose audience or execution misses the target, and stop formats whose product moment does not convert. If a strong app funnel sits behind expensive creators, test a different creator archetype or content account before discarding the product. Kaz's LumeSkin case shows that a later bundled creator test can reduce cost for some posts yet still fail the larger requirement of repeatable execution by enough creators. [S1][S2]

Separate a profitable short trend from an enduring growth asset. Continue a trend based app while its economics work, but reserve long term investment for products whose brand, network effects, creator relationships, or content intelligence improve with use. Kaz contrasts a trend dependent app with categories such as relationships and mental health that may support longer lived demand. Record what would change this judgment, since durability is a hypothesis to test. [S2]

For a portfolio, reuse strong creators and content knowledge across related audiences where the same emotional style applies. Offer suitable creators additional opportunities across apps and test whether a proven narrative structure transfers. Kaz describes carrying creator knowledge from a long distance relationship app into a breakup journaling app. The company site presents its work as a portfolio of everyday consumer apps that should spread naturally; the reusable distribution system is the strategic asset. [S2][S4]

## Failure checks

- If a plan starts with a finished app and adds creator posts as generic promotion, return to niche formats and identify a feature viewers can understand inside the content. [S1][S3]
- If one viral post is used to forecast scale, require evidence that several creators can reproduce the format at an acceptable cost. [S2]
- If a dashboard ranks creators by views alone, add audience geography, intent, conversion evidence, and attribution uncertainty. [S2]
- If automated scripts sound unnatural or creators are managed only through software, restore human creative review and prompt, responsive creator support. [S2][S3]
- If a launch is called successful from app funnel benchmarks alone, add content replicability, creator supply, acquisition cost, payback, retention, and durability to the decision. [S1][S2]

## Required output

Deliver a short opportunity memo with the chosen niche and evidence from current formats; a user problem and one video native product moment; two or three sample concepts; a creator pilot with audience fit, cost, and human review criteria; a funnel and attribution plan; and a weekly expand, revise, or stop rule. State assumptions and missing evidence explicitly. If the user already has an app, center the memo on the bottleneck and the next test rather than inventing a new product. This output mirrors the method's sequence of finding attention, building a fitting app, distributing through creators, and learning from the entire funnel. [S1][S2][S3]

## Worked example, illustration

A team proposes a meal planning app for people who work night shifts. The agent first studies current night shift food videos and finds a repeated format in which creators show what they packed, what they actually ate, and how they felt later. The agent chooses a feature that turns a shift schedule and food constraints into three practical meal options, then briefs creators to show one choice in their own routine. This is an invented application of Kaz's content first niche and feature test. [S1]

The pilot uses four creators with different locations and shift types. The team tracks post engagement, comments asking for the meal plan, app search lift, installs, trials, payments, and creator cost. A high view creator with few qualified users gets a revised brief; two smaller creators whose audiences install and retain get another round. The app earns more investment only if the format can be repeated by additional creators and the subscription payback fits the team's budget. These thresholds are set from the team's evidence, not from Kaz's reported results. [S2][S3]
