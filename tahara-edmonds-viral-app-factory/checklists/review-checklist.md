# Review checklist

Review the completed [opportunity memo](../templates/output-template.md) against [the detailed method](../references/method.md). Mark each applicable gate PASS or FAIL and add evidence or a correction. Use N/A only with a reason. The checklist operationalizes the public method; its format and pass criteria are package adaptations. [S1][S2]

A recommendation is ready when all applicable gates pass. A clearly labeled provisional research plan may pass without completed market observations, but it must not claim validation or recommend scaling on invented evidence.

## Scope and evidence gates

- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** The recommendation addresses the actual assignment: opportunity, existing-app bottleneck, or portfolio allocation.
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Audience, geography, budget, build capacity, cash limits, owner, and decision date are explicit or listed as unresolved.
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Observed evidence, expert-reported claims, and hypotheses are distinguishable; historical interviews do not stand in for current market evidence.
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Source identifiers resolve to the fetched register, and proposed operational rules are not falsely attributed to the expert.
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Illustrations and estimates are labeled; missing data are not filled with fabricated posts, prices, or performance.

## Niche and product gates

- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** The niche screen covers emotional resonance, ease of reproduction, and brand competition. [S1]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** The evidence demonstrates a pattern across creators or explicitly requests that validation; one viral outlier is insufficient. [S1][S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** A distribution thesis connects a specific audience, familiar format, and useful app interaction. [S1]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** The MVP has one narrow problem, clear input, visible action, useful output, and a payment test; its deadline fits real capacity. [S1][S5]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Two or three concepts show the app's value inside the story and identify execution constraints. [S1]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Claims and sensitive demonstrations respect product limitations; marketing descriptions are not treated as validation. [S4]

## Creator and operating gates

- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Creator choices account for geography, customer intent, emotional fit, cost, and execution ability, rather than followers or views alone. [S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** The pilot tests reproduction across people, with defined cells, posts, spend cap, and review window. [S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Briefs share strategy while allowing a creator's natural voice; a human checks whether execution feels forced or emotionally false. [S1][S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** A named person handles creator feedback and questions; software capabilities are described accurately. [S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** The learning library records structures, contexts, outcomes, and failures; proposed transfer includes an audience-fit test. [S1][S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Any planned publishing, creator contact, reuse, or spending stays within actual user authorization and necessary rights.

## Measurement and allocation gates

- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Metrics cover attention, intent, store traffic, installs, activation, trials, payment, retention, and defined acquisition costs, with relevant denominators. [S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Direct measurement is separate from estimated attribution; search-led discovery, background demand, and overlapping posts are addressed. [S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Customer estimates and cost ranges use compatible cohorts and do not double count customers or apply install conversion to views.
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Payback uses defined contribution, distinguishes realized from projected value, and reflects retention maturity and the user's cash constraints. [S1]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Expand, revise, and stop rules include economics, replication, creator supply, and spend or time limits. [S1][S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** The next test targets the actual bottleneck; strong app conversion does not conceal expensive or scarce distribution. [S2]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Organic and paid tests use appropriate separate economics; an organic hit does not automatically authorize or validate a paid campaign. [S1]
- [ ] **PASS / FAIL:** Short-term profit is separate from a durability hypothesis, with evidence that could change the investment decision. [S2]

## Common failure modes and fixes

| Failure mode | Diagnostic sign | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product first, promotion later | Large feature list followed by generic sponsorship | Reconstruct the niche thesis and select a feature that strengthens a native story. [S1] |
| Exceptional post mistaken for scale | Forecast depends on one performer | Test the structure across additional suitable creators within a cap. [S2] |
| Cheap creators expected to invent strategy | Each creator experiments without shared learning | Share network-level patterns and update briefs from observed outcomes. [S1] |
| Views mistaken for customer value | Biggest account always gets more budget | Add audience fit, relevant intent, customer evidence, and conservative economics. [S2] |
| Attribution made artificially exact | Every post-launch install assigned to a creator | Show direct counts and indirect ranges, baseline demand, overlap, and uncertainty. [S2] |
| Wrong denominator | Install-to-paid rate multiplied by views | Rebuild stage rates from matching cohorts and denominators. |
| Strong app funnel hides distribution failure | Conversion passes but sponsors cost too much | Test a cheaper archetype or adjacent audience, then verify sufficient repeatable supply. [S2] |
| One cheaper creator interpreted as rescue | An isolated win substitutes for a network | Require reproduction by enough suitable creators before expansion. [S2] |
| Automated feedback loses emotional credibility | Videos sound scripted and questions go unanswered | Restore human creative review and a responsive creator contact. [S2] |
| Future renewals treated as cash today | Payback declared from immature lifetime-value estimates | Separate realized contribution from projections and schedule a mature cohort review. [S1] |
| Portfolio transfer assumed | Winning creator assigned an unrelated app | Test audience tension and demonstration fit before reusing relationships or formats. [S2] |
| Trend profit mistaken for durability | Allocation grows without a lasting advantage | Separate controlled profit harvesting from investment in a compounding position. [S2] |
| Research unavailable but conclusions sound certain | Invented formats or prices presented as facts | Label hypotheses and deliver an evidence collection plan before a scale conclusion. |
