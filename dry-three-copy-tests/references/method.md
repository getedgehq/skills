# Method: concrete, credible, specific copy

## Attribution and operating conventions

Dry's three tests ask whether a sentence creates an image, makes a claim open to verification, and expresses something particular to the advertiser. Three failures signal a weak idea; three successes signal promise. The interview clip explains these as prompts for deeper attention to the product, not a measured predictor of conversion. [S1]

This package operationalizes that advice with an evidence inventory, competitor substitution, explicit review statuses, and a handoff template. These are package conventions, not a claim that Dry published this exact workflow. The numbered sources document writing advice; product claims require separate evidence supplied for the assignment.

## Build the evidence base

Dry's customer-language example starts with a question about design frustrations, records replies, and uses recurring answers to address recognizable customers. The implication is to listen before guessing at a clever line. [S4]

For the assignment, create a small inventory:

| Fact category | Record | Decision it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reader | Role, situation, desired action | Which benefit matters |
| Offer | Product, service, terms, exclusions | What can be promised |
| Mechanism | What actually happens during use | How the benefit becomes believable |
| Proof | Document, demonstration, measurement, customer account | Whether the claim can be supported |
| Language | Repeated phrases with their original context | How to sound familiar |
| Objection | A documented hesitation | Which hook may earn attention |
| Difference | Offer-specific fact and known alternatives | Whether the promise belongs to this brand |

Mark each entry as supplied, verified, assumed, or missing. Prefer the smallest missing fact that unlocks an honest draft. When no proof exists, describe the demonstrated operation or narrow the claim. Do not invent precision, quotations, customer counts, or results. This evidence discipline implements Dry's preference for honest, specific claims. [S2][S6]

A customer phrase expresses experience, not automatically a universal product result. Keep attributable comments separate from factual promises. One enthusiastic customer does not establish that all users achieve the same outcome.

## Choose the selling angle

Start with the reader's outcome, then identify the mechanism or circumstance that makes it meaningful. Dry emphasizes value to the customer and an identifiable audience. For unfamiliar offers, plain explanation may be the best headline. For familiar ones, a substantiated objection can provide the hook. [S2][S3]

Package decision rule: choose the angle with the best combination of relevance, factual support, and explanatory power. Do not choose an odd company fact just because competitors cannot repeat it. A difference matters only if it helps this reader understand or decide.

## Test 1: Picture

Dry contrasts image-producing language with abstractions that leave little to remember. [S1]

Ask what a reader could point to, enact, or describe as a moment. Replace category words with the concrete activity they imply: a forgotten invoice, a drill borrowed for a weekend, or a shift appearing on a calendar. Those are invented diagnostic examples, not quotations from Dry.

Package decision rules:

- Pass when the line evokes a recognizable object, action, or use situation tied to the benefit.
- Fail when it offers only an aspiration such as transformation or excellence.
- If the scene is vivid but the offer is unclear, add a direct explanation or change the scene.
- For intangible services, show the observable work or artifact rather than inventing a guaranteed outcome.

Concrete does not mean literal at every word. A metaphor can help if the surrounding copy explains the actual offer. On a landing page, favor a real product view or demonstration; Dry explicitly rejects decorative imagery that keeps the reader distant from reality. [S3]

## Test 2: Check

Dry favors claims whose truth can be examined over blanket assertions without a usable standard. [S1][S2]

Separate two questions: could this claim be checked, and do we have enough evidence to publish it? A claim can satisfy the first while failing the second. Evidence is a publication requirement, not a fourth rhetorical flourish.

For each factual clause, record the evidence, scope, and possible contradiction. For measurements, preserve units, period, sample, baseline, and relevant conditions. For product behavior, identify a demonstration or documented term. For comparisons, specify what is compared and under which conditions. These are package evidence rules.

Prefer an observable capability when a promised business outcome is unproven. A calendar integration may be demonstrable; a reduction in missed shifts requires outcome evidence. Do not convert a mechanism into a guaranteed result. Keep qualifications close enough to prevent a misleading reading.

## Test 3: Belong

Dry uses a brand-specific contrast in the interview to show why a writer must examine what is actually being sold. [S1]

Package diagnostic: place a plausible competitor's name above the line. If it remains equally natural and supported, investigate a useful audience, process, location, service term, customer pattern, or combination of facts. Adding the brand name alone does not fix generic copy.

Treat specificity as earned fit, not an instruction to claim sole ownership of a category. If alternatives are unknown, record "distinctiveness unverified." If alternatives share the feature, concede that and seek a more meaningful angle. Do not infer market exclusivity from a lack of research or call an offer the only option without evidence.

## Context and channel

Dry's landing-page guide connects the title's value to a subtitle explaining delivery, a visual showing the product, nearby credibility, and an easy next step. Later sections substantiate the promise and handle objections. He also describes this as adaptable guidance rather than a rigid manual. [S3]

Package applications:

| Channel | Review unit | Key decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad | Selling line plus visible explanation | Can an unfamiliar reader identify the offer? |
| Landing page | Headline, subtitle, visual, proof, CTA | Does each element support the same promise? |
| Email | Subject, opening, offer, next step | Does the subject match what the email delivers? |
| CTA | Button plus surrounding context | Does clicking provide the stated next step? |
| Critique | Exact supplied copy and available evidence | Which failure requires facts rather than wording? |

A short button can be plain navigation. Do not force all three tests onto every functional label. Likewise, do not insert a testimonial or founder story into a tiny ad just to replicate a page structure.

## Voice and explicit warnings

Dry's conversational guide warns against talking at readers, imitating stock language, using ornate vocabulary to impress, artificial personality, and disrespectful treatment of competitors. It encourages reader involvement, familiar language, and reading aloud. Its tolerance for informal grammar serves natural speech, not confusion. [S5]

His general tips explicitly warn against exaggeration, empty promotional verbs, indirect passive constructions, vague modifiers, long sentences, and cleverness that displaces selling. He also argues for preserving personality and making lines lead naturally to the next. [S6]

Operationally, remove wording that contributes neither meaning nor useful tone. Preserve specific modifiers that carry facts and conditions. A conversational tone need not be jokey, intimate, or grammatically careless; match the reader and channel.

## Edge cases and stopping rules

- **Three failures:** Return to the proposition and evidence. Synonym changes cannot create a missing idea.
- **One or two successes:** Repair the failed test or supply a supporting sentence. Record any remaining limit.
- **No meaningful difference:** Deliver clear, supported copy and mark specificity as unresolved. Avoid a fabricated proprietary claim.
- **Missing evidence:** Use a narrower factual line. Keep unsupported alternatives in internal notes rather than finished copy.
- **Necessary specialist terminology:** Keep the term if the reader needs it, then explain its visible consequence where useful.
- **Personality versus accuracy:** Preserve voice only while the offer and truth conditions stay clear.
- **Unsupported performance expectations:** Passing the writing tests does not establish improved conversion. Any performance conclusion needs actual evaluation.

These stopping rules are package conventions derived from the three tests and Dry's emphasis on honesty and the selling purpose. [S1][S6] Finish when the requested output is understandable, relevant, and supported, with unresolved specificity disclosed. Never let a three-pass aspiration justify invented facts.
