# Worked examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. Every company, capability, term, and evidence item below is hypothetical. Do not reuse their claims for a real product without verifying that product's facts. Rule citations refer to the method, not evidence for these fictional offers.

## Illustration 1: neighborhood tool library

### Brief and weak first draft

A tool library wants residents tackling occasional home repairs to check membership options.

> Empowering sustainable home improvement with unrivaled convenience.

The reader cannot picture a specific activity. Sustainability and convenience lack defined claims here. Another lender could use the same language, and no evidence establishes superiority.

### Facts available within the illustration

- Members may borrow a drill for seven days under the hypothetical loan policy, evidence E1.
- The fictional pickup desk is inside Alder Street Library and opens Saturdays from 9 a.m. to noon, evidence E2.
- No savings calculation or environmental measurement exists.
- Competitor policies have not been researched.

### First repair and remaining weakness

> Need a drill for one Saturday? Borrow ours for the week.

This creates a weekend scene and a checkable loan term. It still could fit many lenders. The next revision uses the real pickup arrangement within this scenario instead of inventing a claim about being the only lender.

### Improved result

**Headline:** Hang the shelf this Saturday. Borrow a drill from Alder Street Library.

**Supporting line:** Members can pick up Saturday, 9 a.m. to noon, and keep the drill for seven days.

**CTA:** See membership and borrowing terms

### Audit and difference-making rules

| Test | Result | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picture | Pass | A shelf, drill, and Saturday task form a recognizable use moment. |
| Check | Pass within the illustration | E1 supports seven days; E2 supports location and pickup hours. The task is an invitation, not a guarantee of completion. |
| Belong | Partial | The named pickup arrangement belongs to this offer, but market exclusivity remains unverified. |

The benefit became visible through an ordinary job rather than an abstract label. The promise stayed within actual service terms, and the location supplied earned context. The CTA accurately offers information before membership. These choices apply the three tests and Dry's value-and-context advice. [S1][S2][S3]

**Open item:** Confirm current terms before publication. Research local alternatives only if competitive differentiation matters to the assignment. Clear, honest copy is usable without declaring exclusivity.

## Illustration 2: shift scheduling software

### Brief and weak first draft

A fictional product, ShiftDock, targets cafe managers who must coordinate weekend staffing. The requested deliverable is a landing-page opening, leading to a sample rota.

> Supercharge workforce efficiency with the fastest scheduling platform.

There is no visible work moment. Efficiency has no defined measure; fastest is an unsupported comparison. The line could belong to any scheduling tool.

### Facts available within the illustration

- E1 is a hypothetical recorded demonstration: publishing a rota emails each employee a calendar link containing their assigned shifts.
- E2 is a hypothetical product specification: when a swap is approved, the original rota records the change and its approval.
- E3 is a hypothetical accessible sample showing the publish and swap flow.
- There is no measured evidence of time saved, fewer missed shifts, or superiority over competitors.

### Rejected revision

> Build next week's rota in 30 seconds and never lose a shift swap.

The number is testable but invented. The absolute promise exceeds what the demonstration establishes. Adding precision made the draft more risky, not more credible.

### Improved result

**Headline:** Publish next week's cafe rota. Send each employee their shifts as a calendar link.

**Supporting line:** ShiftDock emails the links when you publish and records approved swaps on the original rota.

**Proof cue:** Watch the sample rota move from publishing to an approved swap.

**CTA:** View the sample rota

### Audit and difference-making rules

| Test | Result | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picture | Pass | A manager publishes a rota; employees receive links to shifts. |
| Check | Pass within the illustration | E1 and E2 show the two operations; E3 makes the proof cue available. |
| Belong | Partial | The combined workflow is product-grounded; alternatives may provide it too. |

The revision replaces broad praise with demonstrated operations. It names the reader's work, explains delivery, and places relevant proof beside the promise. It drops invented speed and reliability claims. The specificity test remains partial because no competitive research exists. [S1][S2][S3][S6]

**Open item:** If stronger differentiation is needed, ask which verified workflow choice cafe managers value that comparable products do not offer. Do not add "only" merely to force a pass.
