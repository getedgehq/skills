# Negotiation Strategy and Negotiation Dynamics

Based on Luís Almeida Costa's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Luís Almeida Costa. Sources: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md).

## Output contract

Read this first. It decides what the user receives.

- **The deliverable is a prep sheet the user can take into the room:** what they want and where they walk away, what to find out first (the exact questions), what to offer in exchange for what (linked packages), the words to say at the hard moments, and the traps to avoid. For a review or debrief, the deliverable is the verdict on the terms or round and the next move.
- **Length and format:** one to two pages (roughly 400 to 900 words), scannable: short headings, bullets, and sample lines in quotes. Numbers in a small table only if the input has numbers. If you save a file, the chat reply is two or three lines.
- **What the method changes:** the choices, not the volume. Diagnose the setting first (one-off deal or a round in an ongoing relationship, single issue or package) and let it shape the tactics. Ask before arguing: the critical unknowns go first, each with what you would do under each answer. Limit, target and opening are separate and defensible. Concessions are traded inside packages, never given away one issue at a time. Every move is checked against what it does to the next round: credibility, information and goodwill.
- **Facts:** use only facts from the request and the files provided. Never invent the counterpart's numbers, alternatives, market rates, comparable deals, deadlines or quotes. Where a number is missing, give the rule for setting it ("your walk-away is the best alternative you actually have, e.g. [your other offer]") rather than a made-up figure.
- **Never output:** a recap of negotiation theory or the method's sections, setting-classification tables, relationship-state records, inquiry plans with consequence columns, agreement-architecture sections, rehearsal plans, round records, review results, source tags like [S2], or the expert's name. Do that thinking silently; the sheet shows the decisions.
- **Make it concrete:** include the meeting sequence (what to say first, what to find out, when to propose), the concession plan as trades, and the mechanics the decision turns on (for an investment, how the terms on the table change ownership, worked on the user's numbers or on clearly labeled illustrative numbers). General knowledge of how such deals and terms usually work is welcome when stated as general; what is forbidden is inventing facts about this counterpart or this deal.
- **Open questions:** at most two, at the end, only if the answer would change the plan.

## The method, as decisions

Each rule is sourced in [references/method.md](references/method.md), which also covers detail and edge cases.

1. **Diagnose the setting before choosing tactics.** Auction, single-issue bargaining, linked package, internal allocation, one-off or repeated: the right opening and concession pattern differ. A changed situation (new decision maker, deadline, capacity) resets the plan.
2. **Every round changes the next one.** Today's agreement, unilateral moves and behavior change tomorrow's options, information and willingness to cooperate. Prefer moves that keep credibility and information flowing.
3. **Information before argument.** Identify the few unknowns that would move your limit, agenda or package, and ask about them early with genuine questions.
4. **Separate limit, target and opening.** Limit = the boundary for an acceptable whole deal; target = the ambitious but feasible outcome; opening = a defensible, deliverable first package. Revise an offer only for a substantive reason, or it costs credibility.
5. **Trade on differences.** Find terms that are cheap for one side and valuable to the other (timing, volume, scope, risk, payment terms, information rights). Link concessions to reciprocal terms; negotiating issues one by one keeps the fight distributive.
6. **Prevent escalation.** When tension rises, ask a courteous question, reset the agenda or clarify; hold the boundary without threats or personal attacks.
7. **Debrief to learn.** After a round, note what was agreed, what each side did unilaterally and what to improve next time.

## Before you hand it over (silent)

Check without writing it down: every number is from the input or clearly a rule for the user to fill; concessions are tied to something received; the first questions come before any proposal; the sheet fits the meeting the user described; nothing the user would delete before walking in.
