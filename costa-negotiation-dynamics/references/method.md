# The Method in Depth

Based on Luís Almeida Costa's public method. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Luís Almeida Costa.

## Scope and reading guide

The core idea is to analyze a negotiating situation, identify the information that matters, create exchanges around differences, and manage the process so the agreement and relationship support future dealings. The source materials describe an integrated approach rather than a fixed script applicable to every setting. [S2][S3]

Sections 1 to 4 cover diagnosis and preparation; sections 5 and 6 cover value creation; sections 7 and 8 cover process and learning. Section 9 handles edge cases. The rules below translate published principles into agent actions. Tables, decision gates, alternative packages and scenario tests are practical adaptations. Do not describe them as Costa's official worksheet or claim to calculate either simulator's score. The older sources describe Negotiation Dynamics: The Game; the current program uses the name Deal Maker. [S1][S2][S7]

## 1. Situation diagnosis

Costa's syllabus separates competitive bidding and basic negotiation analysis from complex packages, long-term arrangements, process management and repeated relationships. Tactics depend on the setting, and moves can change the setting itself. Begin with the situation rather than a favorite technique. [S3]

Prepare a concise diagnosis:

- **Decision:** What must be agreed, by whom, and by when?
- **Structure:** Auction, single-issue bargaining, linked package, internal allocation, or some combination?
- **Relationship:** Is this one transaction or a round in an ongoing relationship?
- **Constraints:** What is already committed, operationally feasible or outside the user's authority?
- **Changeable structure:** Can the agenda, parties, timing or variables change in a useful way?

These fields are an implementation aid. Use them to choose the next inquiry, not to delay a simple task with unnecessary analysis. If a draft price concession is the assignment, diagnose enough to understand its dependencies and return the relevant recommendation. If it is an auction, identify the actual format, rules, objective and circumstances before discussing bidding behavior; the syllabus does not disclose a single optimal bidding formula. [S3]

**Decision rule:** When the situation changes materially, revisit the diagnosis before continuing the old concession plan. A loss of supply capacity, new decision-maker or changed deadline can make yesterday's opening inappropriate. This applies the public emphasis on evolving options and constraints. [S2]

## 2. Relationship state

An ongoing relationship has memory. Earlier agreements, unilateral decisions by either side, external changes and interpersonal behavior alter the environment of later negotiations. Information availability can also depend on those earlier choices. Do not assess a transaction solely by its current financial result. [S2]

Maintain a state record with four distinct categories:

| Category | What to record | Why the distinction matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joint agreements | Accepted terms and remaining obligations | Defines what both sides actually committed to |
| Unilateral decisions | Changes made by one side, including a refusal or capacity allocation | Can alter options without being an agreement |
| External changes | Demand, availability or other relevant conditions | Prevents attributing every change to bargaining behavior |
| Process and information | What was learned, access lost or gained, cooperation observed | Shapes what can be understood or achieved next |

The categories operationalize the interview's account; they are not simulator data-entry rules. For every important move, record an immediate effect, a plausible next-round effect and the evidence supporting each. Use scenarios when the effect is uncertain. A low price may create value today while a rigid commitment limits flexibility later; neither effect should disappear from the recommendation. [S2]

**Decision rule:** A future benefit is not a reason to ignore today's boundary. Compare the whole feasible agreement and its consequences against the user's actual constraints. The sources motivate considering future dealings, but do not provide a formula that trades credibility or flexibility for a particular price. [S2][S3]

## 3. Information before argument

The effectiveness article emphasizes diagnostic inquiry and identification of critical information over argumentative performance. Experienced negotiators can still make damaging mistakes; strong preparation focuses on the unknowns capable of changing a decision. [S4]

Separate three evidence states:

- **Observed:** A documented term, a reported constraint or a direct counterpart response. Note who supplied it and whether it has been verified.
- **Hypothesized:** An inference about costs, priorities, motives or likely behavior.
- **Unknown:** Missing information that could change the agenda, boundary or package.

Rank unknowns by consequence. For each, write a question, the plausible answers and the action each answer would change. For example, if predictable volume is valuable to a supplier, the package might exchange a forecast commitment for reserved capacity. If it has no production value, abandon that trade rather than insisting that the supplier ought to appreciate it. This is an implementation of inquiry and respect for different priorities. [S4][S6]

Useful question forms include: Which part of the request reflects a fixed constraint? What changes if delivery can be scheduled earlier? Which of these terms matters most, and why? What did the last agreement make harder? Ask without embedding an accusation or treating a hypothesis as established.

**Decision rule:** If a material answer contradicts the brief, update the state record and package. Do not convert a failed hypothesis into a more forceful argument. Track how prior decisions enabled or restricted access to the information, because the relationship also shapes what can be learned. [S2][S4]

## 4. Boundaries and credibility

The syllabus identifies separate preparation questions: limit, target, opening, concession pattern, response to offers and resistance to concession demands. It does not prescribe numeric distances between these positions. [S3]

For a practical brief, use these definitions and keep their dependencies visible:

| Field | Working definition | Review rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Limit | The user's boundary for an acceptable whole agreement | Identify evidence, authority and relevant nonprice conditions |
| Target | The outcome the user intends to pursue | Explain why it is ambitious yet feasible |
| Opening | The first proposed package or term | Make it defensible and deliverable |
| Concession response | A planned conditional adjustment after a specific demand or new fact | State what must be received or learned in return |

These definitions are implementation aids, not quotations from the syllabus. If the user has supplied no limit, mark it unresolved and explain what information determines it. Do not manufacture a monetary threshold or disguise a target as a limit. The viability of a proposal depends on the complete package, not just a headline number. [S3]

The conflict article explicitly reports warnings against commitments that cannot be honored, lying, an outrageous opening and withdrawing an offer without a strong reason. It also explains that lost credibility obstructs trust and constructive value creation. Apply the warnings before recommending tactical aggression. [S5]

**Decision rules:**

- If the user cannot authorize or fulfill a commitment, do not present it as firm. Mark the dependency or find a feasible substitute.
- If an opening has no defensible connection to interests, facts or plausible terms, revise it before use.
- If a prior offer must change, explain the actual new condition or substantive reason. Do not invent a pretext.
- If the proposed package violates the user's boundary, recommend revision or non-agreement; courteous communication does not require a concession.

The first three rules implement the reported credibility warnings. The last operationalizes the syllabus's separate limit and concession questions. [S3][S5]

## 5. Interests and negotiable variables

Costa's authored article distinguishes a price-only contest from a negotiation with potential to create joint value. The opportunity comes from differences in cost structures, capabilities, objectives, preferences or expectations. Convincing the counterpart to adopt the same preferences can remove the very difference that makes an exchange attractive. [S6]

Map issues with the following implementation table:

| Issue | User interest and priority | Counterpart interest and priority | Evidence status | Possible difference to exploit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Term] | [Reason it matters] | [Known or hypothesized reason] | [Observed, hypothesized or unknown] | [Cost, capability, preference or expectation] |

Expand the agenda only with feasible variables tied to interests. Delivery timing, volume predictability, payment timing, scope, review cadence and risk allocation may matter, depending on the task. The interview describes creative variables and self-created agendas, but does not endorse a universal list. [S2]

**Decision rule:** Retain a new issue when it changes feasibility, value or risk for at least one side. If every issue has identical priorities and opposing preferences, acknowledge limited room for integrative trade-offs. Multiple topics create potential, not proof, of mutual gain. Do not label any enlarged package a win-win without explaining the exchange. [S5][S6]

## 6. Linked packages and agreement architecture

Build exchanges in which a concession relatively inexpensive to one side obtains something that side values more, while making the other side's plausible benefit explicit. Consider compatible interests as well as disagreements. Evaluate terms together: the conflict article warns that even an expanded agenda can preserve distributive conflict when issues are negotiated separately. [S5][S6]

For each proposed package, specify:

1. All linked terms and the condition connecting them.
2. What each side gives and receives.
3. Which differences explain the possible benefit.
4. Feasibility and authority to deliver.
5. Unresolved assumptions and the questions that test them.
6. Immediate outcome and effects on later options, information and cooperation.

Alternative packages are useful when they test priorities or accommodate distinct constraints. They are an optional implementation aid, not a requirement to generate a fixed number of offers. Avoid fragmenting a package into independent promises during discussion. If a counterpart accepts one term but rejects its reciprocal term, reassess the package before accepting. [S5][S6]

For a long-term arrangement, the syllabus raises agreement structure under uncertainty and differences in expectations. Translate this into concrete questions: What happens if demand changes? Who measures performance? When is a review triggered? Who can authorize a change? What remains committed if the condition is unmet? The questions are implementation aids; the syllabus does not provide a universal contingent-contract design. [S3]

**Decision rule:** Test a favorable scenario and a plausible adverse scenario whenever uncertainty changes the recommendation. Leave unsupported probabilities and simulator scores out. If the mechanism cannot be measured or implemented, keep it unresolved rather than calling the package finished. [S2][S3]

## 7. Process and escalation

The conflict article describes a supplier-retailer dispute in which successive refusals, withdrawn discounts, product removals and retaliatory advertising destroyed value. The underlying mistakes included narrow framing, isolated issue discussions and persistence in a contest of wills. [S5]

Watch for counterpressure, retaliatory measures, personal affront, attachment to winning and inability to acknowledge that an existing course is failing. Diagnose both the agreement structure and the interpersonal process. Softer language alone will not repair a package that leaves no meaningful exchange. [S5]

A practical reset sequence is:

1. Name the shared decision and acknowledge the counterpart's concern without agreeing to the demand.
2. Clarify facts or ambiguous terms with a courteous question.
3. Reopen useful linked variables instead of adding another punitive move.
4. Restate a feasible package and the user's boundary calmly.
5. If no acceptable package exists, record non-agreement and its consequences.

This sequence adapts the public communication and agenda guidance. The article recommends courteous language, listening, appreciation, acknowledgment of the other perspective and prevention of misunderstanding. It warns against maximum pressure as a default, aggressive or disrespectful language and misleading the other side. Satisfaction depends on process as well as outcome. [S5]

**Decision rule:** Evaluate a response by what it does to both substance and process. Avoid trading away the user's constraint merely to relieve tension; also avoid taking a worse deal simply to defeat the counterpart. The former follows prepared boundaries, while the latter reflects the article's warning about destructive persistence. [S3][S5]

## 8. Rehearsal, recording and learning

The program's public cycle includes studying instructions, obtaining additional information, planning the agenda, discussing team strategy, negotiating, recording agreement or non-agreement, entering decisions and process evaluation, and debriefing. Adapt the sequence to the real task; do not simulate inaccessible software feedback. [S7]

Rehearse a likely acceptance, an objection, a refusal and a material new fact. Check whether the user can explain the package and whether the response protects reciprocal terms. Discuss with available team members; a missing team is not a reason to invent observers.

After the round, separate agreed terms from rejected proposals, unilateral decisions and open questions. Record who will do what and by when as a practical way to preserve the state. Ask an observer or the user for one concrete behavioral observation if available. Label self-assessment as such rather than presenting it as external feedback. [S2][S7]

Costa's public teaching descriptions emphasize discovering habitual weaknesses and avoiding major errors. Choose one consequential habit and one observable adjustment. For example, replace arguing about an assumed priority with asking which delivery constraint matters most. A good outcome does not prove a good process; inspect whether avoidable risks were taken and whether useful information was missed. [S4]

## 9. Edge cases and stopping rules

These applications follow the principles above; they are not additional expert claims.

| Situation | Response | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genuine single-issue negotiation | Prepare the boundary and response plan; test whether any real additional variable exists, then accept limited scope if none does | [S3][S6] |
| Internal negotiation with no price | Map workload, timing, access, risk and authority where relevant; analyze future cooperation just as carefully | [S2][S7] |
| One-off transaction | Scale the relationship record down; still assess credibility, feasibility and any continuing obligations | [S3][S5] |
| Material information unavailable | Use conditional recommendations and explicit unresolved fields; prioritize the question that changes acceptability | [S2][S4] |
| Counterpart refuses to discuss interests | Work from observable constraints and label inferences; do not treat pressure as evidence of priorities | [S4][S6] |
| Claimed mutual gain rests on matching priorities | Test the alleged cost or preference difference; withdraw the claim if unsupported | [S6] |
| Deadline before all uncertainty is resolved | Identify the essential missing fact, present feasible conditional options and record non-agreement if needed | [S7] |
| Newly infeasible promise or prior offer | Explain the substantive change and revise transparently; do not silently withdraw or overpromise | [S5] |
| No acceptable whole package | State the reasons and record non-agreement; preserve a factual state for later dealings | [S3][S7] |
| Auction request without rules | Obtain the format, rules and objective; withhold an allegedly optimal bid until the setting is understood | [S3] |

## 10. Explicit warnings to carry into review

The following are reported warnings or clearly stated contrasts in the fetched material, not invented formulas:

- Do not equate negotiating skill with innate flair alone or possession of a toolkit alone. Diagnosis, judgment and experience matter. [S3][S4]
- Do not assume experience prevents serious mistakes. Identify consequential errors and recurring habits. [S4]
- Do not focus on convincing the counterpart so completely that you overlook useful differences. [S6]
- Do not reduce the agenda to a price contest when real exchanges are available; adding issues but discussing them separately can preserve the same problem. [S5][S6]
- Do not persist in retaliation or a battle of wills after it destroys value. [S5]
- Do not substitute pressure, aggression, disrespect or deception for a constructive process. [S5]
- Do not make commitments you cannot honor, lie, use an outrageous opening or withdraw an offer without a strong reason. [S5]

Apply the [review checklist](../checklists/review-checklist.md) to the finished brief. Repair the most consequential defect first, then return an output that makes the next decision and the next round easier to prepare. [S4]
