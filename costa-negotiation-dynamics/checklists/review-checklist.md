# Review Checklist

Internal review: run it silently on your draft and fix what fails. Never put this checklist, PASS/FAIL marks, a review note or a list of limitations in the deliverable; a gap that matters to the user becomes one short open question at the end (three at most). The gates are package adaptations of the public method, not a scoring system published by the expert.

A material failure affects acceptability, feasibility, credibility, decision-changing information or the recommended next action. Repair it before presenting a proposal as ready to use. If the missing evidence cannot be obtained, deliver a clearly conditional brief and name the unresolved gate. A review-ready conditional brief is not an agreement-ready package. This is the package's review convention, not a simulator grading system.

## Pass/fail gates

| Gate | PASS when | FAIL when | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Situation diagnosis | Decision, parties, authority, setting and real deadline explain the chosen approach | A stock tactic replaces diagnosis or auction rules are missing from a bid recommendation | [S3] |
| Relationship state | Relevant prior agreements, unilateral decisions and external changes are distinguished | The current transaction is isolated from known history or proposed terms appear as agreements | [S2] |
| Critical information | Facts, hypotheses and unknowns are separate; key questions specify what the answer changes | Counterpart motives or priorities are invented, or the inquiry is only disguised argument | [S4] |
| Preparation positions | Limit, target, opening and concession responses are distinct and supported | A single unexplained price stands in for all four, or unsupported limits are treated as facts | [S3] |
| Credibility | Promises are feasible, openings defensible and offer revisions substantively explained | The proposal relies on deception, impossible commitments, an outrageous opening or unexplained withdrawal | [S5] |
| Interests and variables | Terms connect to interests and meaningful differences or compatible priorities | The agenda remains unnecessarily price-only or additional issues have no value mechanism | [S2][S6] |
| Linked exchanges | Reciprocal terms and their dependencies remain visible; the whole package meets the boundary | Issues are settled independently or concessions survive after reciprocal terms are removed | [S5][S6] |
| Claimed mutual gain | Both sides' plausible benefits are explained, with assumptions marked | More issues or a friendly tone are treated as proof of mutual gain | [S6] |
| Uncertainty and future effects | Material adverse conditions and next-round consequences are considered; unresolved mechanics are explicit | Favorable outcomes are assumed or future effects are expressed as invented simulator scores | [S2][S3] |
| Process and escalation | Reset language is courteous, clarifies the problem and opens useful linked discussion | Retaliation, disrespect or a contest of wills is the response to pressure | [S5] |
| Rehearsal and actionability | Likely objections, refusal and new information have usable responses and a next action | A persuasive script has no decision logic or stops at general advice | [S7] |
| Round record | Agreement or non-agreement, unilateral decisions and process observations are separate and accurate | Rejected proposals become commitments, or only the achieved price is recorded | [S7] |
| Learning | Feedback identifies one consequential habit and an observable next-round adjustment | A favorable result is treated as proof of sound behavior, or external feedback is invented | [S2][S4] |
| Attribution | Method claims use matching citations; adaptations and illustrations are labeled accurately | The brief claims official simulator formulas or credits an invented aid to Costa | [S1][S2][S3][S7] |

## Common failure modes and fixes

| Failure mode | Practical fix |
| --- | --- |
| “They obviously care most about price.” | Reclassify the assertion as a hypothesis; ask which term changes their decision and rebuild the issue map. [S4][S6] |
| “Let us meet halfway.” | Check both boundaries and seek a real difference in priorities before treating a midpoint as appropriate. A midpoint is not itself a value-creation mechanism. [S3][S6] |
| A larger agenda with sequential concessions | Present a conditional whole package and identify the reciprocal term attached to each concession. [S5] |
| A promise made to soothe tension | Verify capacity and authority; replace an infeasible promise with a feasible conditional proposal. [S5] |
| An aggressive opening without evidence | Rebuild the opening around a defensible exchange; explain its relation to the target and boundary. [S3][S5] |
| A concession motivated by embarrassment or winning | Recheck acceptability and future effects; use a courteous reset or record non-agreement if no feasible deal exists. [S3][S5][S7] |
| A revised offer with no explanation | State the actual changed fact or substantive reason; if none exists, reconsider the revision. [S5] |
| A low price hiding a rigid future obligation | Assess the whole commitment under an adverse condition and name the lost flexibility. [S2][S3] |
| Precise future values without evidence | Replace fabricated numbers with qualitative scenarios or supported calculations whose assumptions are explicit. [S2][S3] |
| Blaming motives after information sharing worsens | Record observed behavior and sequence; ask a clarifying question and rebuild a useful information process. [S2][S4][S5] |
| Debriefing only the outcome | Add process observations, changed hypotheses, separate unilateral decisions and one behavioral improvement. [S4][S7] |

## Final review note

Fill in this short note at the end of the brief when a review summary is useful:

- **Status:** [PASS, or conditional with named unresolved gates.]
- **Most damaging plausible error:** [Specific error, not a generic warning.]
- **Prevention or repair:** [Evidence or action that addresses it.]
- **Next action:** [Question, package revision, user decision or state update.]

Prioritize consequential errors before polishing rhetoric. Strong preparation should improve the user's decision and preserve an accurate basis for the next negotiation. [S4]
