# Worked examples

Both scenarios are invented illustrations. Their events, voices, and outcomes are fictional. They demonstrate editorial decisions, not workplace research, autobiographical evidence, or measured reader satisfaction.

## Illustration 1: The meeting that concealed a missing decision

### Brief and exploratory choice

A manager wants a short essay for team leads about stalled decisions. The initial objective is to advocate fewer meetings. Exploration finds a more useful question: why might a decision remain stalled after its recurring meeting disappears? The objective changes to diagnosing missing decision authority. This makes the opening consequential and gives the piece an unexpected mechanism. [S2] [S3]

### Weak first draft

> Meetings are often inefficient and unproductive. In today's fast-paced organizational environment, managers should optimize their calendars and prioritize asynchronous communication. We canceled our weekly launch meeting, but there were still coordination deficiencies creating organizational latency. It is important to communicate clearly and assign responsibilities. Meetings can be useful too, so leaders should strive for a balance and think carefully about whether each meeting adds value.

### Revision decisions

- Replace the generic efficiency thesis with the specific unresolved event and its consequence. [S2]
- Separate discussion from authority, then show how one failure leads to the next. [S3] [S4]
- Replace abstract organizational language with visible actions; include a contrasting useful meeting. [S4]
- Remove broad advice about balance and end with a diagnostic step that fulfills the opening. [S4] [S6]

### Improved result

> We canceled the weekly launch meeting. The launch still waited.
>
> Everyone had commented on the plan. Nobody knew whose approval would let the team proceed. The meeting had given that uncertainty a place to hide: each week, we discussed the decision without making it.
>
> We tried a smaller fix than another calendar overhaul. We named one person who could approve the launch and wrote down which questions still needed answers. The next conversation had a purpose: resolve those questions so that person could decide.
>
> This does not mean every recurring meeting conceals a problem. A review with a named approver and unresolved risks can move work forward. Removing it may simply postpone the review.
>
> Before canceling a meeting, ask which decision it serves and who can make that decision. An empty calendar will not settle an unanswered question about authority.

### Why the result works

The opening promises an explanation for a concrete puzzle. The body answers it instead of offering generic productivity advice. The peak reframes the meeting as a place where uncertainty persisted. The counterexample prevents an absolute claim against meetings. The ending supplies a practical test. [S2] [S4]

The compression pass removed repeated abstractions without deleting the causal explanation. A single short post could express the thesis, but the fictional event and contrasting case justify this longer treatment. [S4]

### Review status

Passes a self-review for fulfilled promise, understandable mechanism, retained nuance, and a useful ending. Intended-reader reactions remain pending. No feedback score is claimed.

## Illustration 2: Learning that failed outside the example

### Brief and exploratory choice

A hobbyist wants a personal blog post about learning photo-editing software. The weak idea is to recommend more practice. The stronger direction is an honest account of confusing recognition with independent action, a distinction readers may recognize in themselves. This illustration uses resonant articulation rather than claiming a new scientific theory. [S2] [S7]

### Weak first draft

> I have always believed that learning is a lifelong journey. Recently I watched several tutorials about photo editing and learned many techniques, but applying them was difficult. There are many factors involved in skill acquisition, and it is essential to remain motivated. Tutorials are useful, but practice is also useful. I realized I needed to practice more and embrace mistakes. I encourage everyone to keep learning and never give up.

### Revision decisions

- Locate a scene where the problem becomes visible rather than opening with a universal slogan. [S2] [S5]
- State the distinction in ordinary language, then show a small attempt at changing the process. [S4]
- Keep the narrator's limited experience as the claim boundary; do not invent general learning data. This is an agent safeguard.
- Preserve an understated voice and one telling detail instead of adding inspirational flourishes. [S5]
- Build the ending around the discovered distinction, with a specific next step. [S4] [S6]

### Improved result

> In the tutorial, I knew exactly which button came next. When I opened my own photograph, I stared at the toolbar.
>
> I had become good at following someone else's decisions. I had not practiced making those decisions myself.
>
> So I changed one part of my routine. After watching a lesson, I closed it and tried the same kind of edit on a different photograph. Where I got stuck, I wrote down the question before reopening the video.
>
> One question was smaller than I expected: should I brighten the whole picture, or only the face? I had watched the instructor make that choice without noticing there was a choice to make.
>
> The video still helped. It gave me a worked example and a place to return when I needed an explanation. But this time, I brought a question rather than another hour to fill.
>
> My next lesson will end with a different photograph. I want to find out what I can do when nobody is pointing at the next button.

### Why the result works

The first scene gives the reader a reason to care about a familiar frustration. The strongest insight comes early enough to orient the practical example. The specific brightness decision makes the distinction observable. The ending returns to the toolbar problem with a changed intention, providing payoff without promising universal results. [S2] [S4] [S5]

The revision earns its length through an example and a boundary: tutorials remain useful. It removes repeated encouragement while retaining the author's discovery. It does not adopt an expert persona or decorate the narrator's modest experience. [S4] [S5] [S8]

### Review status

Passes a self-review for coherent discovery, concrete example, scoped claims, and authentic voice. Reader feedback and any real-world effectiveness assessment remain pending.
