# Worked examples

These are invented illustrations, not Social Cooks clients, observed campaign results, or forecasts. All budgets, thresholds, metrics, names, and dates below are invented account decisions. They illustrate how to apply the published principles in [method.md](../references/method.md). They are not default settings for other accounts.

## Illustration 1: Finding a first winner for a lunch container

### Situation

Fictional brand DeskLunch has a leak-resistant lunch container, authentic product demonstration footage, and permission to use one customer interview. Its account has no adequately evidenced winning concept. Five existing ads use the same studio image with different overlays. The team can produce three new videos this week and has EUR 1,500 available for this test wave.

### Weak first draft

Make ten ads with different colors. Use emotion, product benefits, and reviews in every video. Try different audiences and a discount too. Keep the one with the highest CTR after two days and scale it.

### Why it fails

The asset count hides a single concept. Several changes make results difficult to interpret. Each creator must convey too many ideas, and CTR alone cannot establish purchase efficiency. Two days is an arbitrary verdict period. There is no supported parent for iteration. [S1] [S2]

### Improved result

**Inventory:** Five exports, one unproven concept, zero supported winners. Aim to build toward the published 8 to 12 distinct-creative guideline over subsequent waves; do not pretend the first three concepts fulfill that inventory goal.

**Cadence:** Brief Monday, batch shoot Tuesday, edit and review Wednesday, launch Thursday, review the following Thursday. The launch wave contains three Explorations-Creatives. Reserve the next production session for both fresh exploration and iteration if a winner emerges.

| ID | Lane and angle | Hypothesis and reason | Opening and body |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DL-E01 | Exploration, Emotion | A calmer lunch break may resonate with rushed desk workers; this is an untested assumption | Open on a rushed desk lunch, then show a prepared meal and a calmer break |
| DL-E02 | Exploration, Funktion | Demonstrated leak resistance may address a known customer objection | Open with an inverted closed container over a sink, show the actual seal test, then packing and CTA |
| DL-E03 | Exploration, Social Proof | A real customer's account may reduce uncertainty about everyday use | Open with the permitted customer's lunch-bag routine, then their authentic experience and CTA |

**Brief for DL-E02:** Communicate one idea: the demonstrated seal. Show the inversion in the opening two seconds, using genuine footage and supportable claims. Follow problem, demonstration, everyday use, CTA. The creator chooses natural phrasing and kitchen setting. Do not add an invented testimonial or an unsupported absolute guarantee. Capture alternate openings in the same shoot for a later hook test, but keep them out of this discovery comparison.

**Comparison:** Keep the product, price, destination, conversion event, and audience settings consistent. Compare complete concept packages. Because creators and executions differ, the result cannot isolate angle alone.

**Invented decision contract:** Target CPA at most EUR 25. Review only after seven elapsed days, a further 72 hours for attribution maturation, and at least 20 attributed purchases for a candidate winner. These are this fictional team's provisional evidence gates, not a statistical guarantee. EUR 1,500 is the total spend ceiling. If it is reached before sufficient evidence, stop spending and report uncertainty. A mature asset with at least EUR 300 spend and no purchases qualifies for the agreed failure rule. A near-target result can receive one additional seven-day retest capped at EUR 250.

### Illustrative review

| ID | Mature spend | Purchases | CPA | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DL-E01 | EUR 300 | 0 | Undefined | Loser under the agreed no-purchase rule |
| DL-E02 | EUR 600 | 30 | EUR 20 | Winner candidate, economics check passes in this fictional account |
| DL-E03 | EUR 120 | 3 | EUR 40 | Inconclusive due to sparse delivery |

The three assets received unequal spend. DL-E03 does not prove Social Proof is ineffective. The team proposes a bounded follow-up comparison for it. DL-E02 enters Iteration: test two opening hooks against its existing opening while retaining the body, offer, and CTA. Remaining wave capacity goes to a new exploratory customer situation.

**Rules that made the difference:** Substantive diversity, one idea per brief, hook priority, honest concept-level inference, evidence before routing, and exploration before iteration without a supported winner. [S1] [S2]

## Illustration 2: Refreshing a tired winner without losing the learning

### Situation

Fictional clothing brand WeekdayWear has a supported functional video showing one shirt styled for work and dinner. It has nine distinct concepts in its library and can release four variants weekly. For the unchanged winning motif, three comparable weeks show CPM moving from EUR 12 to EUR 17, frequency from 2.0 to 3.1, and CTR from 1.8% to 1.1%. Its purchase CPA also deteriorates.

### Weak first draft

CPM rose, so pause everything now. Recolor the winning footage into eight ads. Script every line for the creator. Duplicate the campaign into five audiences and scale the ad with the highest platform ROAS.

### Why it fails

A single warning becomes an automatic shutdown. Cosmetic exports are counted as fresh exploration. The script removes creator freedom, audience duplication adds confounders, and platform ROAS becomes the only scaling test. [S1] [S4] [S5]

### Improved result

**Diagnosis:** Compare matching periods and check auction competition, promotions, placements, spend, and tracking. In this invented account, no major external change is identified, and the combined CPM, frequency, CTR, and CPA trends support probable fatigue. Keep that interpretation conditional.

**Four-variant weekly wave:** Two iterations, WW-I01 and WW-I02, change only the opening of parent WW-W07. Two explorations, WW-E10 and WW-E11, introduce an emotional evening-use scene and a genuine customer story. The 2/2 allocation is a capacity choice, not the method's fixed ratio. Two hook iterations do not count as two additional concepts in the inventory.

**Iteration brief:** Keep the product, creator, body, duration, CTA, destination, and offer from WW-W07. I01 opens with the office-to-dinner transition. I02 opens with the styling problem. Test the hook only. Capture both openings in one session and let the creator choose natural phrasing.

**Exploration brief:** E10 communicates the confidence of arriving comfortably dressed after a busy day. Begin with the evening arrival, then show the actual outfit transition, then the CTA. E11 communicates credible everyday use through the permitted customer's own experience. Neither exploration is used to infer that its opening alone caused a result.

**Invented decision contract:** Purchase CPA at most EUR 30, consistent attribution definition, at least 25 mature purchases, and ten observation days for winner review. Individual test ceiling EUR 900. One borderline retest is allowed for seven further days up to EUR 300. Low delivery or broken tracking yields inconclusive status. These account gates do not establish a universal confidence level.

### Illustrative review and next cycle

I01 produces 36 mature purchases on EUR 900 of spend, giving EUR 25 CPA. It meets the predeclared winner gates at the spending ceiling, so the team stops further test spending and proposes focused iteration. I02 has mature evidence above EUR 30 CPA and is stopped under its agreed rule. E10 passes at EUR 28 CPA with sufficient signal. E11 is under-delivered and receives no verdict.

At business level, illustrative weekly MER falls from 4.0 to 3.3 while platform ROAS rises. A promotion and lower-margin product mix changed during that week. The team investigates margin, returns, and other channel spending before proposing a larger budget. It does not attribute the MER movement solely to I01 or E10.

The next wave retains I01's learning, iterates E10's supported concept, introduces another substantive exploration, and assigns a bounded follow-up to E11. The review log records the spend ceiling, actual evidence, confounders, and next owners.

**Rules that made the difference:** Combined fatigue signals, market-change checks, continuous replenishment, one-lever iteration, native briefs, economics alongside attribution, and explicit spending boundaries. [S1] [S2] [S5]
