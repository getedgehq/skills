# Method in depth

Credit: Based on Denys Lichtenstein's public creative testing method at Social Cooks. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Denys Lichtenstein. Additional agency guidance and publisher context retain their separate attribution in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Scope and evidence

The operational objective is a repeatable supply of tested ads, not a one-time winner. Lichtenstein's framework separates discovery from refinement, establishes a production rhythm, and connects briefs to evidence. The agency presents creative production as a central growth constraint under increasingly automated delivery. Use that as the method's working rationale, not as an independently verified account of every Meta ranking mechanism. [S1] [S2] [S4]

Keep the German method names where useful: Exploration, Iteration, Explorations-Creatives, Iterations-Creatives, Emotion, Funktion, Social Proof, and wöchentliche Creative-Wellen. Creative Engineering names the agency's broader production and testing service. These labels do not denote an additional proprietary statistical technique. [S1] [S3]

Distinguish three levels of evidence in the output:

- **Published principle:** Explicit instruction or rule of thumb from a cited source.
- **Account decision:** Budget, target, timing, evidence requirement, or allocation chosen for this account.
- **Implementation adaptation:** A proposed schema or safeguard making the principle executable, such as asset IDs, a retest deadline, or a comparison design.

The field schema, ranking rubric, review statuses, and example thresholds in this package are implementation adaptations. Do not describe them as verbatim or proprietary Lichtenstein rules. [S1] [S2]

## 2. Measurement and business context

Start with the business decision: which product and offer can be promoted profitably, and what constitutes worthwhile acquisition? Establish the conversion event, attribution window, spend source, revenue source, contribution margin, returns assumptions, and target CPA or equivalent account objective. Clean measurement is a prerequisite for trustworthy creative decisions. A shorter attribution window can make an otherwise useful creative appear unsuccessful. [S2] [S5]

The supporting setup article favors consolidated delivery and clean browser and server-side conversion signals. It gives an Event Match Quality guideline above 7, but this is supporting setup guidance, not a universal creative test pass mark or sufficient proof of correct tracking. Check event integrity and duplicate reporting in the actual account; that integrity check is an implementation adaptation. Do not restructure an account merely to follow this skill. [S4]

Use three complementary lenses:

- Ad-level CPA, purchases, and platform ROAS help evaluate delivered creatives under a consistent reporting definition.
- MER = total revenue / total ad spend, using all channels for the same period, gives a business-level efficiency view.
- Margin, returns, and variable costs determine whether revenue growth is commercially useful. [S2] [S5]

MER cannot identify which individual hook caused a change. A platform winner with deteriorating business efficiency deserves investigation, not automatic scaling. Weekly and monthly MER readings reduce the temptation to make business decisions on daily fluctuations. Directional incrementality checks are possible, but require their own comparison plan; a routine creative result does not establish incrementality. [S5]

Historical context reinforces the economics focus: OMR reports that Denys used margin, conversion, cart activity, and returns for product selection. The ChefTreff episode description emphasizes data and risk management. Neither source supplies the creative framework's weekly quotas or a modern universal product-score formula. [S9] [S10]

## 3. Two separate testing lanes

**Exploration:** Discover new substantive approaches. Compare new customer messages, angles, or formats, such as an emotional use scene, a functional demonstration, and a real testimonial. Expect many exploratory ideas to fail. The purpose is discovering a viable approach, not polishing every speculative asset. [S1] [S2]

**Iteration:** Start with an evidenced winner and change a specific lever: opening hook, edit pacing, CTA, or format. Keep the winning core idea recognizable. Test hooks before spending effort on production polish because the opening determines whether viewers engage with the rest. Attention is an early diagnostic; it does not override the purchase or profit objective. [S1] [S2]

Both lanes must appear in the pipeline. Only iteration can trap a team in minor improvements of mediocre work. Only exploration wastes established winners by never extending them. The sources give no fixed 70/30 or other split. Choose a mix based on winner inventory, fatigue, unknowns, and feasible evidence, and label the split as an account decision. [S1] [S2] [S3]

If there is no supported winner, emphasize exploration now and reserve an iteration follow-up once a concept qualifies. Do not relabel an unproven asset as a winner merely to fill both lanes. This is a practical application of the two-lane distinction. [S1] [S2]

## 4. Hypotheses and substantive diversity

Maintain a prioritized agenda instead of uploading whatever happens to be available. Each hypothesis should identify a customer situation, an angle, the expected response, and the reason for expecting it. Support the reason with account evidence, real customer information, or an explicitly untested assumption. Move the most useful hypotheses into the next weekly wave. [S2]

Use the three-angle grid as a creative prompt:

- **Emotion:** The feeling or identity the product supports.
- **Funktion:** A concrete problem the product solves.
- **Social Proof:** Credible evidence that other people trust or value it. [S1] [S3]

One video should communicate one clear idea. The grid is not permission to invent testimonials or product evidence. If proof is missing, propose obtaining authentic material or choose a supportable functional or emotional concept. This evidence boundary implements the method's preference for credible creator content. [S1] [S6]

Count distinct propositions and execution concepts separately from production variants. Recoloring one image, changing an overlay, or exporting several aspect ratios does not create equivalent substantive variety. An iteration can be valuable without becoming a new exploratory concept. The agency specifically warns that near-identical imagery may supply insufficient diversity and associate with higher CPMs. Treat this as its practical warning, not a precise classifier rule. [S1]

Implementation adaptation: rank the backlog by the importance of the uncertainty, credible supporting evidence, difference from existing concepts, and production feasibility. No numerical ranking formula is claimed by the sources.

## 5. Discovery versus variable isolation

The framework article compares materially different exploration executions; the systematic guide asks teams to isolate variables. Preserve both instructions by making the unit of inference explicit. [S1] [S2]

For **concept discovery**, label the tested unit as a complete concept package. A testimonial beating a product demo supports preferring that execution in this setting. It does not prove that its hook, creator, or format individually caused the difference. Keep audience settings, offer, optimization event, and other surrounding conditions stable where practical. This reconciliation is an implementation interpretation, not an explicitly named submethod.

For **an isolated learning question**, change one meaningful lever. If the question is which hook wins, retain the body, creator, offer, CTA, and comparable delivery conditions. If the question is angle alone, hold the format and surrounding execution as similar as feasible. Document changes that cannot be held constant. [S2]

Ordinary campaign delivery can distribute spend unevenly. An under-delivered asset is not automatically a loser. The package adds a delivery check and, where needed, a controlled comparison proposal. Never describe a campaign's unequal allocation as a randomized experiment or infer causal certainty from it.

## 6. Cadence, volume, and capacity

Published starting points are 8 to 12 unique creatives per campaign and wöchentliche Creative-Wellen of 3 to 5 new variants. These express inventory diversity and replenishment rate respectively. They are not an instruction to launch 8 to 12 new tests every week, nor a requirement to spend equally on all exports. [S1]

Fix a repeatable production, launch, and evaluation schedule. Publish each wave with a clear learning purpose, keep fresh exploration available, and build a library of supported winners. Intentional smaller waves are preferable to arbitrary output made solely to hit a production number. Actual volume depends on budget and testing needs. [S1] [S3]

Capacity edge case: if the planned tests would each receive too little evidence, reduce concurrent launches, prioritize the backlog, or lengthen observation. Preserve the review rhythm even when an asset needs more than one week to mature. Explain the deviation and avoid fragmenting limited spend into many weak comparison cells. These are implementation choices consistent with sufficient-signal and consolidation principles. [S2] [S4]

## 7. Clear UGC briefs and efficient production

Every creator brief needs four core elements:

1. **Angle:** One idea and intended customer response.
2. **Hook:** A concrete opening image or action and the message delivered in the first seconds.
3. **Structure:** A rough problem, solution, and CTA progression.
4. **Freedom:** Where creators may choose their natural phrasing or presentation. [S1]

Specify the desired opening rather than asking for a generally engaging video. Preserve natural delivery rather than scripting every syllable. Expensive production is not the default answer; the sources favor native UGC and smartphone footage where appropriate, without guaranteeing they always win. [S1] [S3]

Batch related hooks and shots in one session to reduce cost per variant. Identify which shots belong to which concept, then assemble the planned comparisons. AI can assist ideation, editing, and variants, but human strategy and brand review remain essential. The AI workflow is supporting guidance authored by Roxana Lichtenstein, not an additional Denys-authored requirement. [S1] [S6]

## 8. Predeclared review and routing

Write the decision rules before launch. The sources require enough time, budget, and conversion evidence, but provide no universal observation duration or statistical threshold. The systematic guide explicitly rejects treating a handful of purchases as sufficient certainty. Its FAQ's reference to several purchases is directional, not a numerical pass rule. [S1] [S2]

For each test, define the primary KPI, commercial target, evidence requirement, attribution maturation period, observation dates, spend boundary, and retest conditions. These fields implement the published principle. Choose their values from account economics, conversion delay, baseline variability, and expected delivery. If inputs are absent, leave the decision unresolved and request them; do not fabricate certainty.

Recommended implementation statuses:

| Status | Evidence interpretation | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winner | Target met with the agreed sufficient signal and acceptable economics | Preserve the concept; schedule a focused iteration and a separately specified scaling proposal |
| Loser | Enough valid evidence supports failure against the declared rule | Stop under the agreed rule and document the failed hypothesis |
| Borderline | Evidence is usable but too close or mixed to resolve the decision | One bounded retest with a clear question, deadline, and spending limit |
| Inconclusive | Tracking, delivery, or immature attribution prevents a valid decision | Resolve that issue or collect evidence; make no performance verdict |

Scaling winners, stopping losers, and retesting borderline cases come from [S2]. The explicit four-status table and retest fields are package adaptations.

The case studies describe immediate stopping of losing ads and real-time monitoring, while the guides warn against premature shutdown. Apply quick action once a predefined failure or spend boundary is met; do not turn case-study language into stopping after a few impressions. SASSYCLASSY's reported 1-day-click ROAS target above 4 is brand-specific and cannot be exported to every account. [S1] [S2] [S7]

## 9. Fatigue and the next wave

The framework puts rising CPM first in its fatigue warning system, supported by rising frequency and falling CTR on the same motif. It also qualifies the interpretation by asking whether the market changed. A single metric is not enough to condemn an ad. [S1]

Compare consistent periods and delivery definitions. Investigate promotions, seasonal competition, audience or placement changes, budget changes, and tracking issues before assigning the entire movement to creative wear. This comparison discipline is an implementation adaptation of the market-change caveat and clean-testing principle. [S1] [S2]

When the evidence supports fatigue, activate genuinely different exploratory concepts from the prepared pipeline. Focused iteration can extend a winner, but another text overlay is not a substitute for a fresh concept. Continue weekly replenishment to avoid emergency production after performance deteriorates. [S1]

## 10. Learning loop and warnings

Record the concept, hook, parent winner, test question, dates, delivered signal, result, limitations, decision, and next action. The source requires documenting angle, hook, and performance; the additional fields make that log actionable. Feed findings back into the ranked agenda and subsequent briefs. [S1] [S3]

Avoid these method-breaking patterns:

- Continually polishing an unproven motif instead of testing new concepts. [S1] [S2]
- Discarding every winner to chase novelty. [S1] [S2]
- Changing several levers and then claiming one caused the result. [S2]
- Mistaking tiny samples or short observation for decisive evidence. [S1] [S2]
- Daily fundamental account changes that interrupt learning. [S4]
- Inventing volume without learning intent or production feasibility. [S1] [S3]
- Overly rigid creator scripts that remove native expression. [S1]
- Scaling attractive platform figures without business economics. [S5]
- Presenting agency-reported client growth as a causal promise. [S7] [S8]

The LinkedIn example contains several simultaneous business and campaign changes. It illustrates why business outcomes matter, not which creative test produced the reported growth. The public profile establishes Denys's role and provenance; neither a profile nor a podcast description proves the framework's efficacy. [S8] [S9] [S11]
