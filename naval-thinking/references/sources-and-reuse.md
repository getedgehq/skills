# Sources, attribution, and reuse

Primary starting point: Eric Jorgenson, *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* (2020), [official PDF](https://navalmanack.s3.amazonaws.com/Eric-Jorgenson_The-Almanack-of-Naval-Ravikant_Final.pdf), [official website](https://www.navalmanack.com/).

The PDF's copyright page credits Eric Jorgenson, states all rights reserved, and announces free digital downloads. The checked PDF and homepage do not establish an open license for repackaging the text. Free access should not be represented as permission to redistribute an extracted book or license it as one's own.

This skill contains original instructions, concise idea-level paraphrases, and source links. It does not bundle the PDF, extracted chapters, illustrations, or a quotation database. Its practical reuse approach relies on the distinction between ideas and expression, not on claiming the book is public domain or assuming blanket fair use. The [U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html) explains that copyright protects expression rather than ideas, concepts, systems, or methods. This is a design rationale, not a legal clearance opinion.

For public attribution, describe this as an independent Naval-inspired decision framework informed by Eric Jorgenson's compilation and Naval's published material. Do not suggest approval by either person. Do not apply a software or Creative Commons license to the source book on the basis of this skill.

The book's introductory notes explain that interview material was edited and rearranged and recommend checking primary sources before quoting Naval. For an exact quotation, locate the original interview, essay, or post and verify its wording and context. If it cannot be verified, paraphrase and label the interpretation. Never manufacture a quote from the lens wording.

If a later request involves reproducing substantial book text, images, or a full-text distribution, check for an explicit rights grant covering that use. Continue ordinary application of these original instructions without requiring permission from the author for every use of an idea.
